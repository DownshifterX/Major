Delhi District Court

Sh. Ashok Kumar vs Sh. Kanshi Ram on 5 July, 2014

                        IN THE COURT OF SH. CHANDER SHEKHAR 
                       DISTRICT & SESSIONS JUDGE (NORTH­WEST) 
                          ROOM NO. 401 : ROHINI COURTS: DELHI.

ARCT No. 20/12

Sh. Ashok Kumar
s/o Sh. Desh Raj
r/o H.No. 1, Sahipur Village
Shalimar Bagh, Delhi­110088                                                                                          ........Appellant
         
                     Vs.

1.          Sh. Kanshi Ram
            s/o Sh. Amar Singh
            r/o SE­8, Village Singalpur
            Near Shalimar Bagh
            Delhi­110088


2.          Sh. Bal Kishan
            s/o Late Sh. Surat Singh
            r/o Village & Post Office Ghoga
            New Delhi.                                                 ..........Respondents

Date of institution                                            :             16.03.2012

Date of clarifications                                         :             28.05.2014

Date of Judgment                                               :          05.07.2014


                                                                JUDGMENT

1. This Judgment shall govern the disposal of an Appeal filed by ARCT No. 20/12 Page 1 of 17 the appellant (JD before the Ld. Trial Court) u/s 38 of Delhi Rent Control Act against the Order dated 21.04.2011 passed by the Ld. Addl. Rent Controller (N/W), Rohini Courts, Delhi, in the execution petition bearing E. No. 02/210 whereby the Ld. Trial Court dismissed the objections of the appellant/JD filed under provisions of Order XXI Rule 99, 100 and 101 CPC seeking restitution of the suit property. However, later on, on the pointing out of the Ld. Counsel for the respondent, the appellant/JD also moved an application u/s 5 r/w Section 14 of the Limitation Act seeking condonation of delay in filing the present appeal.

2. Firstly I will take up the application u/s 5 r/w Section 14 of the Limitation Act for determination and adjudication in as much as it will decide the determination and adjudication of the present appeal.

3. It is stated in the application u/s 5 r/w Section 14 of the Limitation Act by the appellant/JD that after passing the impugned Order dated 21.04.2011 by the Ld. Trial Court, the applicant/appellant/JD being aggrieved by the said Order, by mistake approached the Hon'ble High Court of Delhi vide CM(M) No. 80/2012, however, upon realizing his mistake in not approaching this Court and going to the wrong Court, the applicant/appellant/JD chose to withdraw the said petition i.e. CM(M) No. 80/2012 after seeking liberty to file the present appeal and upon considering the said plea of the applicant/appellant/JD, the Hon'ble High Court was pleased to ARCT No. 20/12 Page 2 of 17 grant liberty to the applicant/appellant/JD to withdraw the said petition and thereupon, the applicant/appellant/JD chose to approach this Court by way of the present appeal. Accordingly, it is stated that the delay in filing the present appeal is neither intentional nor deliberate as the same has been caused because of the reasons mentioned herein above. Accordingly, the applicant/appellant/JD has prayed to condone the delay in the filing the present appeal which was caused under the bonafide mistake.

4. Respondent no. 1 filed the reply to the said application wherein, apart from other facts, it is stated that the applicant/appellant/JD has concealed the material facts and misrepresented this Court as the applicant/appellant/JD has contended that the Hon'ble High Court has granted the liberty to file the present appeal, whereas a perusal of the Order dated 13.02.2012 of the Hon'ble High Court shows that, in fact, the applicant/appellant/JD himself has withdrawn the petition i.e. CM(M) No. 80/2012 with liberty to file the present appeal, as per law. It is further stated in the reply that it is well settled law that once the limitation starts to begin, it will not stop till the filing of the application for condonation of delay moreover by explaining therein the delay for each and every day, however, the application under reference is only filed when the Ld. Counsel for respondent no. 1 took a specific plea regarding limitation and the same has been filed after almost 2 years.

5. It is further stated in the reply that the said application is neither properly verified nor the same is supported by a proper legal affidavit as required under the law. Accordingly, respondent no. 1 has prayed that the application under reference along with the present appeal may be dismissed with heavy costs.

6. I have Ld. Counsel for both the parties and have also gone through the record carefully.

7. It is a well settled law that to obtain extension of time by invoking the provisions of Sec. 5 of the Limitation Act, 1963, the party seeking extension has to satisfy the Court that he had sufficient cause for not filing the appeal, revision or objections, within the prescribed time. [reliance is placed upon Rishi Prabhat vs. DDA, (1994) 1 CCC 719 (Del)].

8. However, so far as Sec. 14 of the Limitation Act is concerned, when a party has been bonafide pursuing a wrong remedy, it would be a fit case for applying the principle embodied in Sec. 14 of the Act. [reliance is placed upon Vaditho Anantharao Naik vs. Bhoomisetty Rajaiah, (1994) 2 CCC 45 (AP)].

9. Sections 5 and 14 of the Limitation Act, though independent, are not mutually exclusive. Even in a case when Sec. 14 of the Act applies, Sec. 5 is not excluded.

10. Where there is ground for excluding certain periods u/s 14 of the Act, in order to ascertain what is the date of the expiration of the ARCT No. 20/12 Page 4 of 17 prescribed period, the days excluded have to be added to what is primarily the prescribed period. Only the time during which the suit was pending in the wrong Court can be deducted.

11. The main factor which would influence the Court in extending the benefit of Sec. 14 to a litigant would be whether the applicant's conduct would satisfy the test of prosecuting the suit in good faith and due diligence. Hon'ble Supreme Court of India has held that the party prosecuting the suit in good faith in a Court having no jurisdiction is entitled to exclusion of that period.

12. Section 14 has to be liberally construed. Unless there is sufficient material to come to the findings that the plaintiff had acted dishonestly and with lack of good faith he cannot be denied the benefit of Sec. 14 of the Limitation Act.

13. It is also a well settled law that the applicant, who has moved an application for condonation of delay, has to give the explanation regarding each day's delay in filing the application or for the cause for the delay in moving the application.

14. It is also a well settled preposition of law that a discretionary jurisdiction as conferred upon this Court, cannot be exercised when a party conceals the material fact and does not come to the Court with clean hands.

15. The contents of the application u/s 5 r/w Section 14 of the Limitation Act demonstrate that the applicant/appellant/JD has failed ARCT No. 20/12 Page 5 of 17 to disclose the dates particularly when CM(M) bearing no. 80/2012 was filed before the Hon'ble High Court of Delhi. However, copy of the Order which is found placed in the main file of the Ld. Trial Court demonstrates that the aforesaid CM(M) No. 80/2012 was filed on 20.12.2011 assailing the Order of the Ld. Trial Court dated 21.04.2011. There is no explanation on the record, why the applicant/appellant/JD assailed the impugned Order dated 21.4.2011 after such a long delay before Hon'ble High Court of Delhi by filing the petition on 20.12.2011 i.e. almost after a lapse of 8 months. The applicant/appellant/JD has failed to give any justifiable reason for filing the CM(M) No. 80/2012 after a lapse of 8 months before Hon'ble High Court of Delhi, which was, later on, withdrawn by the applicant/appellant/JD on 13.02.2012.

16. It seems that the applicant/appellant/JD mischievously had filed the aforesaid petition before the Hon'ble High Court of Delhi and withdrawn the same on 13.02.2012. It further seems that the applicant/appellant/JD was aware before filing the aforesaid petition in the Hon'ble High Court that the time period for the filing of the appeal before this Court is 30 days from the date of the impugned Order, has expired and in case, he will file a petition before Hon'ble High Court of Delhi as CM(M), he may get the relief from this Court while praying for condonation of delay and exclusion of time in filing the appeal, as prayed in the present application. But in my opinion, ARCT No. 20/12 Page 6 of 17 the applicant/appellant/JD just to circumvent the rigours of the Limitation Act filed the CM(M) before the Hon'ble High Court of Delhi, as discussed herein above. The applicant/appellant/JD has failed to give any justifiable reason for not filing the appeal before this Court within prescribed period of 30 days or not preferring the CM(M) before the High Court of Delhi within a period of limitation and/or reasonable period. Had the reasons been given for the condition of delay of 8 months in the filing the CM(M) before the Hon'ble High Court and in filing of the present appeal regarding these 8 months, it would have carried weight, in the light whereof this Court could have appreciated those reasons for not filing the CM(M) before the Hon'ble High Court of Delhi and not preferring the appeal before this Court well in time. When a special procedure and limitation has been conceived of, it should not be allowed to be circumvented and ignored on palpably flippant grounds.

17. The applicant/appellant/JD has failed to disclose any sufficient cause for condonation of delay in filing the appeal. Even if I take it that there is a ground for excluding certain periods u/s 14 of the Limitation Act, in order to ascertain what is the date of the expiration of the prescribed period, the days excluded have to be added to what is primarily the prescribed period. Only the time during which the CM(M) was pending in the wrong Court can be deducted, not the earlier period of 8 months for which no cause has been submitted by ARCT No. 20/12 Page 7 of 17 the applicant/appellant/JD. The applicant/appellant/JD had not been prosecuting with due diligence and in good faith CM(M) before the Hon'ble High Court of Delhi. Hence, in my opinion, applicant/appellant/JD has failed to disclose any sufficient cause regarding 8 months for the condonation of delay in filing the present appeal.

18. The act of the applicant/appellant/JD clearly demonstrates that it is a case wherein in­action, want of bonafide, concealment of facts and negligence is writ large on the part of the applicant/appellant/JD in approaching this Court and in the delay. The whole of the conduct of the applicant/appellant/JD speaks voluminous about the negligence, gross negligence, in­action, want of bonafide on his part. It is not a fit case for applying the principles embodied u/s 5 r/w Sec. 14 of the Limitation Act and for these reasons, the application u/s 5 r/w Sec. 14 of the Limitation Act filed by the applicant/appellant/JD seeking condonation of delay in filing the appeal, is dismissed.

19. Since the application u/s 5 r/w Sec. 14 of the Limitation Act is dismissed, the present appeal is also liable to be dismissed. However, in the interest of justice, I have given my thoughtful consideration, hereas under, to the impugned Order dated 21.04.2011 passed by the Ld. Trial Court.

20. The brief facts, as stated, which led to the filing of the present appeal are that the appellant/JD had been occupying the premises ARCT No. 20/12 Page 8 of 17 bearing shop no. 24­A, SE­9, Village Singalpur, Shalimar Bagh, Delhi­110088 (herein after referred to as the tenanted property) for the last more than 8 years in the capacity of a tenant of respondent no. 1 and was paying the monthly rent of Rs. 800/­. It is further stated that as the tenanted premises was being used as a cement godown, the appellant/JD did not use to be present at all the time at the tenanted premises and appellant/JD or his representatives used to open the same only intermittently for the purposes of loading or unloading the stocks therein.

21. It is further stated in the appeal that on 25.04.2008, respondent no. 1, who has been living in the adjacent plot, filed an eviction petition u/s 14(1)(a) of the DRC Act on the ground of non­payment of the rent through his son Deepak Rathore purposely against respondent no. 2 in respect of the tenanted premises by relying upon a rent agreement which did not even had the address and the parentage of respondent no. 2, which clearly demonstrates that respondent no. 2 is a dummy tenant, if not a fictitious person.

22. It is further stated in the appeal that the service of the said eviction petition was very bizarrely affected upon respondent no. 2, moreover, the summons were served to him at village Ghogha, Delhi, whereas the address of respondent no. 2 on the memo of parties has been mentioned as of the tenanted property in the eviction petition.

23. It is further stated in the appeal that as there was a collusion ARCT No. 20/12 Page 9 of 17 between respondent no. 1 and respondent no. 2, respondent no. 2 purposely did not appear before the Ld. Trial Court and consequently, Ld. Trial Court initiated ex parte proceedings against respondent no. 2 since 22.10.2008 and in furtherance to the same, also allowed the petition filed by respondent no. 1. However, considering the facts that it was the case of first default, Ld. Trial Court gave the benefit of Sec. 14(2) of the DRC Act and adjourned the matter for 04.08.2009 for compliance of Order u/s 15(1) of the DRC Act. On 04.08.2009, the Ld. ARC passed an eviction order considering the facts that the benefit u/s 14(2) of the DRC Act cannot be granted to respondent no. 2 as he has not deposited the arrears of rent despite opportunity.

24. It is further stated in the appeal that in pursuant to the eviction petition, respondent no. 1 filed an execution petition before the Ld. ARC wherein on 08.01.2010, the Ld. ARC issued the warrants of possession in respect of the tenanted premises, but the same were returned unexecuted as the premises was found locked and pursuant to the same, Ld. ARC on 26.02.2010 ordered to break open the locks of the tenanted premises and to execute the warrants as per law.

25. It is further stated that on 15.03.2010 i.e. on Monday which is a weekly holiday for the shops in the locality, respondent no. 1chose to invade the tenanted premises in the grab of warrants of attachment which were procured by playing a fraud upon the Court and the appellant/JD was left stunned and scandalized when he received a call ARCT No. 20/12 Page 10 of 17 from the neighbourhood of the tenanted premises that some persons had gathered at the tenanted premises and they had broken the lock and thrown out the articles belonging to the appellant/JD out of the tenanted premises. It is further stated that the appellant/JD immediately reached the tenanted premises and was shocked to see that his goods and belongings were lying on the road and when appellant/JD tried to enquire about the same, one person calling himself to be the Bailiff of the Court, disclosed the appellant/JD that the possession of the tenanted premises had been handed over to respondent no. 1 in furtherance of the Orders of the Ld. ARC.

26. It is further stated that the appellant/JD immediately called the police, but the police personnel showed their helplessness in view of the orders of the Ld. ARC and when the police left the tenanted premises, the Bailiff told the appellant/JD that all the goods and articles which were lying in the tenanted premises were to be seized as respondent no. 2 was not present, however, later on when respondent no. 1 identified the appellant/JD as the owner of the said articles, the same were released to the appellant/JD under the signatures of respondent no. 1 and on issuance of the receipt by the appellant/JD.

27. It is further stated that on further enquiry by the appellant/JD, it was revealed to the appellant/JD that the landlord of the appellant/JD i.e. respondent no. 1 had filed an eviction petition against respondent ARCT No. 20/12 Page 11 of 17 no. 2 who apparently does not have any relation with regard to the tenanted property and in the said eviction petition, the orders for handing over the possession of the tenanted property were passed in favour of respondent no. 1 by the Ld. ARC. Thereafter, the appellant/JD immediately approached his Counsel and thereupon filed an application under Order XXI Rule 99, 100 & 101 CPC r/w Sec. 151 CPC, as discussed herein above, seeking the restitution of the suit/tenanted property wherein it is stated that the suit property was once sealed by the Municipal Corporation of Delhi when the same was in the possession of the appellant/JD, whereafter when the appellant/JD approached the concerned Department of the MCD and filed an appropriate application seeking permanent de­sealing of the suit property and the same was de­sealed in favour of the appellant/JD after verifying the possession of the appellant/JD. It was also mentioned in the application for restitution of the suit property that when the appellant/JD came to know that the seals imposed by the MCD on the said property had been broken by somebody, he immediately approached the PS Shalimar Bagh and filed a complaint regarding the same. It was also elucidated in the said application that being in settled and undisputed possession of the suit/tenanted property and in view of the fact that the appellant/JD shared an amiable relation with respondent no. 1, the appellant/JD had also applied for conversion of the said property from 'residential' to ARCT No. 20/12 Page 12 of 17 'commercial' in the record of the MCD as the appellant/JD was using the same as a godown for storing the cement.

28. It is further stated that after the notice was issued to respondent no. 1, Counsel for respondent no. 1 kept on lingering on the matter and filed reply at a very belated stage and to the utter shock of the appellant/JD, respondent no. 2 appeared for the first time through his Counsel and filed an application under Order IX Rule 13 CPC which on its face appeared to be a mere eye wash and a blatant attempt on the part of respondent no. 1 to linger on the proceedings.

29. It is further stated that the said suspicion of the appellant/JD further strengthened by the fact that the Counsels representing respondent no. 2 and respondent no. 1 are practising together in association since long and have been appearing for each other in various cases before the different forums and said Counsels have appeared in the matter in which the Counsel for the appellant was representing the opposite side. It is further stated that the appellant/JD has also filed a complaint against the said Counsels before the Bar Council of Delhi and notices have been issued to them. It is further stated that after filing the said application, Counsel for respondent no. 2 never turned up to argue the application.

30. After hearing the arguments on behalf of the appellant/JD and on behalf of respondent no. 1, the Ld. Trial Court dismissed the application under Order XXI Rule 99, 100 & 101 CPC vide Order ARCT No. 20/12 Page 13 of 17 dated 21.04.2011 and being aggrieved by the same, the appellant/JD preferred a petition bearing CM(M) no. 80/2012 before the Hon'ble High Court of Delhi which was later on withdrawn by the appellant/JD to approach this Court and thereafter, the applicant/appellant/JD filed the present appeal.

31. I have gone through the impugned Order dated 21.04.2011 passed by the Ld. Trial Court and have perused the entire record.

32. It is well settled proposition of law that under Section 38 of the DRC Act, the Addl. Rent Control Tribunal is to act as an Appellate Court only on a question of law. In this regard, in 'Ram Dulari thru. L.Rs'. Vs. Om Parkash Gupta and Anr. MANU/DE/1017/2010, our Hon'ble Delhi High Court has been pleased to observe as under :

"12. I also consider that the learned Additional Rent Control Tribunal exceeded its jurisdiction by entering into an area which was prohibited. It is prohibited for Additional Rent Control Tribunal to re­appreciate the facts and change the finding of fact given by Additional Rent Controller."

33. Also, in Kulwant Singh and Anr. Vs. Arjun Singh­ MANU/DE/9677/2006, our Hon'ble Delhi High Court has been pleased to observe as under :

"7. If the matter is examined in terms of aforesaid, it will be found that it is not the function of this Court to re­ appraise the evidence. There are concurrent findings by both the courts below, ARCT No. 20/12 Page 14 of 17 i.e., the ARC and the ARCT. In fact, under Section 38 of the said Act, the ARCT is to act as an Appellate Court only on a question of law."

34. Furthermore, in Dr. Mrs. Sushil Puri and Ors. Vs. Shri Jai Gopal and Ors.­MANU/DE/9637/2006, our Hon'ble Delhi High Court has been pleased to observe as under :

"9. An important aspect which cannot be lost sight of is that the legislature in its wisdom has amended the said Act whereby the appeal to the Tribunal under Section 38 of the said Act has been confined to only a question of law."

35. And also, in Smt. Kiran Sajjan and Ors. Vs. Smt. Swarnkanta Mahajan­ MANU/DE/9022/2006, our Hon'ble Delhi High Court has been pleased to observe as under :

"5. It cannot lose sight under the provisions of the said Act that the appeal lies to the Tribunal under Section 38 of the said Act only on a question of law. This was in terms of the legislative intent as a conscious decision in December, 1988 prior whereto the appeal was both on an error of law. These aspects have been emphasised to set forth the contours within which the impugned orders have to be scrutinised."

36. In the present appeal, there is no question of law involved and what is involved in the present Appeal are mere facts and the reappraisal of the same, hence, the same is liable to be dismissed on ARCT No. 20/12 Page 15 of 17 this sole ground. However, even otherwise, I do not find any flaw or infirmity in the impugned Order dated 21.04.2011 passed by the Ld. ARC (N/W), Rohini Courts, Delhi. The Ld. Trial Court has taken into consideration all the documents placed on record in its right perspective as per law.

37. I do not find any flaw in the Order of the Ld. Trial Court holding that all the documents attached by the appellant/objector are after the date of passing the ex parte Judgment in favour of the petitioner. Ld. Trial Court has further correctly held that the appellant/objector has failed to place on record even a single document to show his possession before the date of the passing of the Judgment or eviction order. Ld. Trial Court has further correctly held that even though as per the report of the Bailiff, some of the articles of the appellant/objector were found in the suit premises, but since the objector has failed to prove his independent possession or the title to the suit premises, even as per the Sec. 25 of the DRC Act, 1958, the eviction order is binding on all the persons who may be in occupation of the suit premises, therefore, the appellant/objector is not entitled for the restitution of the suit/tenanted property.

38. In view of the above observations, I do not find any flaw in the impugned Order dated 21.04.2011 passed by the Ld. ARC (N/W), Rohini Courts, Delhi, and I do concur with the findings of the Ld. Trial Court.

39. Hence, I do not find any reason to interfere in the findings of the Ld. Trial Court. I see no substance in this appeal as nothing could be pointed out from the contentions of the appellant.

40. In the result, I hold that this appeal is devoid of any merits, and is, accordingly, dismissed. However, parties are left to bear their own costs. Record of the Trial Court be sent back along with an attested copy of the Judgment passed today. Appeal file be consigned to Record Room, after completing the necessary formalities. Announced in the open Court today on 5th day of July, 2014 (CHANDER SHEKHAR) Distt. & Sessions Judge (North­West) Rohini Courts, Delhi ARCT No. 20/12 Page 17 of 17
