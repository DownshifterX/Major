Delhi District Court

Smt. Bimla Devi W/O Late Ram Bir vs Dr. U.R. Biswas on 12 December, 2014

       CIVIL JUDGE-CUM-ADDITIONAL RENT CONTROLLER
               (WEST), TIS HAZARI COURTS, DELHI.

                              JUDGMENT

1. By filing the present petition, petitioner is seeking eviction of respondent in respect of a shop at the ground floor forming part of property bearing no. 2088/9-F, Nai Basti, Main Road, Baljeet Nagar, New Delhi as shown in red colour in the site plan (hereinafter referred to as suit premises) under section 14(1)(a) of DRC Act for non-payment of rent.

2. The facts in brief as narrated in the petition are that the E. No. 115/2008. Rambir vs. U.R. Biswas Page 1 of 15 tenancy of the suit premises commenced on 07/03/2002 and the same was up to 06/02/2004 only. That there was no written agreement for the same. That rate of rent was Rs. 1,500/- per month excluding electricity and water charges. That after the expiry of such period instead of paying rent and rent arrears, the respondent threatened the petitioner, including in December 2004, and did not vacate the tenanted premises. It is further stated that respondent neither paid nor tendered the monthly rent to the petitioner, hence the petitioner even issued a legal notice dated 29/12/2004 through UPC. But still the respondent neither replied nor complied with such notice. It is further disclosed that petitioner even filed a complaint to Local SHO PS. Anand Parvat and in reply thereto to the police, the respondent desired to take legal recourse.

It is further stated that as the respondent failed to pay the arrears of rent within the stipulated period of two months from the date of legal demand notice, as such the present petition is filed for the eviction of the respondent.

3. The respondent filed Written Statement dated 10/10/2005 to the eviction petition wherein he stated that the petitioner has deliberately given incorrect address of respondent's clinic and it is claimed that correct address of respondent's clinic is 2087/11, Prem Nagar, Gali no.21, Patel Nagar, Delhi. It is further claimed that respondent is running his clinic from front portion and whereas his residence is at back portion of the same. It is further claimed by the respondent that he has no concern or connection with suit property i.e. property bearing no. 2088/9-F, Nai Basti, Main Road, Baljeet Nagar, New Delhi nor he is in occupation of the same. Consequently, it is claimed by respondent that he is E. No. 115/2008. Rambir vs. U.R. Biswas Page 2 of 15 not a tenant in respect of suit premises nor there exists any relationship of landlord and tenant between the present parties. Respondent further submitted that the petitioner did not serve any legal demand notice on the respondent before filing the present petition. Respondent further submitted that the petitioner has filed the present petition based on forged and fabricated documents. And based on such documents has unnecessarily dragged that respondent in present litigation. Further, the respondent denied in detail the facts on merit of present petition, in his WS.

4. Replication dated 07/12/2005 was filed and in the replication the petitioner denied the stand taken by the respondent and reiterated his original stand taken in the petition.

5. In this case arguments were heard U/s 15(1)of DRC Act. But in view of the stand taken by the respondent and on the basis of material on record, no interim order U/s. 15 (1) DRC Act was passed vide detailed order dated 28/05/2007.

6. It is further pertinent to mention that during the trial original petitioner Sh. Rambir expired and as such on application made, his 4 LRs i.e. Smt. Bimla Devi (widow), Ms. Rubi (daughter), Ms. Shashi (daughter) and Master Vijay Kumar (son) were replaced vide order dated 05/07/2014.

7. Evidence was produced by both the parties. The original petitioner examined himself as PW-1 and he tendered his evidence by way of affidavit dated 11/10/2007 as well as additional affidavit dated 16/07/2009 (which was allowed vide order dated 16/07/2009 by my Ld. Predecessor). Further E. No. 115/2008. Rambir vs. U.R. Biswas Page 3 of 15 petitioner examined one Smt. Tara as PW-2. Further the petitioner sought to examine witness from police station Anand Parbhat regarding complaints made in police station by the parties but it is stated by the Head Constable Mukesh that such record has already been destroyed under the direction of DCP concern. Both PW-1 & 2 deposed on the line of main petition of the petitioner. And both of them were cross examined by the respondent side.

8. Respondent examined himself as RW-1 by way of affidavit dated 05/01/2013 Ex. D-1 as well as additional affidavit dated 04/04/2013 Ex. D-1/14. RW-1/ respondent deposed on the line of defense taken in his WS and he was cross examined by Ld. Counsel for petitioner.

Further respondent got examined one public witness, Sh. Balbir Singh, LDC, Record Keeping, ER Branch, Chief Election Office, Kashmiri Gate, Delhi as RW-2, who is a summoned witness. He was examined regarding name of the respondent in Electoral Role of 2002 and of 2003. He is just a witness regarding documents only.

9. Arguments were heard and record perused. Further, I have gone to the written arguments/submissions filed by both the sides.

10. To prove the case under section 14(1)(a) of DRC Act, the petitioner is required to prove the following ingredients:-

(I) That there is relationship of landlord and tenant between the parties;
II) Rate of rent;
E. No. 115/2008. Rambir vs. U.R. Biswas Page 4 of 15
III) That there were arrears of legally recoverable rent at the time of issuance of legal demand notice;
IV) That a valid legal demand notice was duly served upon the respondent; and V) That the respondent has neither paid nor tendered the entire arrears of legally recoverable rent within two months of date of receipt of legal demand notice.
I. Relationship As Landlord and tenant :
11. Before proceeding further it is pertinent to note that in this case a specific defence/ objection is taken by the respondent that the petitioner has deliberately given incorrect address of respondent's clinic and it is claimed that correct address of respondent's clinic is at property bearing no. 2087/11, Prem Nagar, Gali no. 21, Patel Nagar, Delhi. It is further claimed that respondent is running his clinic from front portion of such property and whereas his residence is at back portion of the same. It is further claimed by the respondent that he has no concern or connection with suit property i.e property bearing no. 2088/9-F, Nai Basti, Main Road, Baljeet Nagar, New Delhi nor he is in occupation of the same.

Therefore, it has to be decided whether the respondent is in physical occupation of suit property bearing no. 2088/9-F, Nai Basti, Main Road, Baljeet Nagar, New Delhi or not. Further in view of contrary stand taken by parties, the core issue is from where the respondent is running his commercial activities/clinic.

12. It is also pertinent to note that there is no dispute about the residential address of the respondent nor that is the issue in E. No. 115/2008. Rambir vs. U.R. Biswas Page 5 of 15 present petition. The petitioner nowhere claimed that suit premises is the "residential address" of the respondent. Further according to the petitioner, the suit premises is let out for commercial purpose only. Therefore, there is no dispute as far as respondent's claim that his "residence" is at property no-2087/11, Prem Nagar, Gali no. 21, Patel Nagar, Delhi is concerned nor same is the issue in present petition.

13. Further as the present petition is filed by the petitioner, therefore, burden of proof regarding the same is on him unless there is admission on the part of the respondent.

14. In this background, on a bare reading of evidence by way of affidavit of the petitioner/ PW-1, it is clear that he deposed on the line of his main petition that respondent occupied that suit premises bearing no. 2088/9-F on 07/03/2002 and there was no written agreement regarding the same. Further, PW-2 Smt. Tara also supported the petitioner claim in her examination in chief by way of affidavit. Further the petitioner also relied heavily on the document Ex. PW-1/6 which is a photocopy of the letter written by respondent to the SHO of local police station. Although it is a photocopy but from record it is clear that no objection was taken by respondent side regarding mode of proof of this document Ex. PW-1/6, as such same can be considered in evidence.

Contrary stand is taken by respondent in the document Ex. PW-1/6 in comparison to his stand in WS and his evidence in present case. In such document Ex. PW-1/6 the respondent admits that he is running his clinic at suit premises bearing no. 2088/9F. Respondent further admitted that he has taken the same on rent from the petitioner. Further the respondent has E. No. 115/2008. Rambir vs. U.R. Biswas Page 6 of 15 mentioned his residential address as 2087/11, Gali no.21, Prem Nagar, Hanuman Chock. Thus, it appears that although the residential address mentioned in such complaint/reply Ex. PW-1/6, execution of which is not denied by the respondent as well as by petitioner in present petition, is same as is claimed by respondent at present, but the respondent is claiming a different address of his clinic than what is mentioned in such document EX. PW-1/6.

But at the same time, on perusal of cross examination of PW-1 and PW-2, it is correct that it is admitted by PW-1 as well as PW-2 themselves that only last two sentences from point A to A in such document Ex. PW-1/6 were written by the respondent and rest of the document was written by police official of PS Anand Parbat. But having held so, it is also pertinent to note that it is not the case of the respondent himself that the document Ex. PW-1/6 was blank when he wrote last two sentences from point A to A in document Ex. PW-1/6. Further in his cross examination the respondent admitted that he signed some paper in police station although he claimed that same were written by police official and he was asked merely to sign the same. And he further admitted his signature at point A on document Ex. PW-1/6. Further it is clear from the evidence on record that the respondent is educated and a practicing Ayurvedic Doctor. Therefore, it can be safely inferred that he must have read the contents of such documents before writing last two sentence and signing the same. Therefore, from the totality of these facts and circumstances it is clear that respondent did sign the document Ex. PW-1/6 and same was signed after the content of the same were already written by the police official, including the clinic's address of the respondent as the suit premises bearing no.

E. No. 115/2008. Rambir vs. U.R. Biswas Page 7 of 15

2088/9F in question. Further the respondent is a educated person and he did not file any complaint against such facts mentioned in such document Ex. PW-1/6, in case he had any grievance regarding such facts. Even in his WS or in his evidence by way of affidavit, no objection or grievance is raised by the respondent regarding such document Ex. PW-1/6 although the same was filed along with main petition, this was very much in his knowledge.

15. But this issue does not rest here. It is stated by the petitioner/PW-1 in his cross examination that property bearing no. 2087/11, Prem Nagar, Gali no. 21, Patel Nagar is old number of his property/suit premises. But he failed to point out what is the new number of the same, although as it is petitioner's defence/explanation to such two property numbers under consideration, therefore, it was petitioner's duty to prove the same through cogent evidence. But petitioner did not place on record any material in support of his contention that property no-2087/11 is the old number of the suit property number 2088/9A. In fact, not even a suggestion is put to the respondent/RW-1 that such two property numbers are of the same property/suit property.

16. It is the cardinal rule that a court can not go beyond the evidence on record in deciding a case. In thus settled position of law, based on evidence on record, it is clear that petitioner failed to prove that these two properties numbers are one and the same that too of suit premises.

17. On the other hand in support of his contention, that he is running his clinic from 2087/11 (and not from suit premises E. No. 115/2008. Rambir vs. U.R. Biswas Page 8 of 15 bearing no-2088/9A), the respondent in his evidence by way of affidavit placed many documentary evidence on record including Adhoc Application Form of professional activity establishment dated 08/01/2007 Ex. D1/9 and a certificate issued by MCD dated 22/01/2007 Ex PW-1/10. Further, such documents are part of evidence as the same were permitted to be placed on record by my Ld. Predecessor. But having said so it held that such documents are of the year 2007 i.e. after filing of the present case. Further such documents appear to be based on the information furnished by the respondent himself, as Ex. D-1/9 itself indicates that it is based on the electricity bill and ration card provided by the respondent himself.

18. It is to be noted at this stage that another cardinal rule of civil cases is the rule of preponderance of probabilities. Therefore, although petitioner failed to prove that property bearing no-2087/11 is the old number of suit premises bearing no-2088/9A, still in the light of such cardinal rule and based on the facts and circumstances of present case, particularly, the admission in document Ex. PW-1/6 (which can be legally admitted, although the same is before the police official but as no criminal proceedings were pending against the respondent) by the respondent that he is running his clinic from the suit premises in question bearing no. 2088/9F, it is held that he is in physical possession of the same and that premises bearing no. 2087/11 is at best his residential address only. Further, it is hard to believe that if the respondent is not occupying the suit premises of the petitioner then why the petitioner would file the case in court at all against the respondent that too not under easier general Act i.e. Transfer of property Act but under a relatively harsher law for E. No. 115/2008. Rambir vs. U.R. Biswas Page 9 of 15 landlord side i.e. DRC Act.

19. Now coming to the issue of landlord tenant relationship, the burden of proof that there exists relationship of landlord and tenant between the parties is on the petitioner. Further the creation of such relationship is a matter of contract between the parties, which can be expressed or implied.

In present petition, the petitioner claims that tenancy agreement was oral. On the other hand, the respondent asserted that there was no such relationship at all. But in view of above mentioned detailed discussion and relying on the admission by the respondent in the same document Ex. PW-1/6 that he is a tenant under the original petitioner, it is held that there exist the relationship of landlord and tenant between the parties.

II) Rate of Rent:

20. As per the petitioner, the rate of rent agreed with the respondent was Rs. 1,500/- per month excluding electricity and water charges. On the other hand, the respondent stated that as he is not a tenant at all under the petitioner, therefore, there is no question of any rate of rent agreed by him. As such, the rate of rent is vehemently disputed by the respondent.

21. PW-1/ petitioner as well as PW-2 deposed on line of main petition regarding rate of rent @ Rs. 1,500/ per month. It is further deposed by PW-1 that there was no written agreement in this regard. PW-1/ petitioner was cross examined by respondent side but nothing material is extracted in favour of respondent in this regard. In fact, not even a suggestion was put to PW-1 by respondent that rate of rent is not Rs. 1,500/- per month. It is so E. No. 115/2008. Rambir vs. U.R. Biswas Page 10 of 15 as the stand of the respondent is that he is not a tenant at all. But such stand of the tenant is not accept as discussed in detail above. Therefore, it appears that even in his evidence, the respondent did not lead any evidence to show that rate of rent was not Rs. 1,500/- per month. In fact there is no evidence by respondent side in this regard. Thus, from the evidence on record it is concluded that rate of rent agreed with the respondent was Rs. 1,500/- per month excluding electricity and water charges.

III) Arrears Of Rent:

22. The question before the court is whether the respondent was in arrears of rent on the date of legal demand notice dated 29/12/2004 Ex. PW-1/3 ?

23. It is the case of the petitioner that the respondent had defaulted in payment of rent and failed to pay rent w.e.f. 06/02/2004 despite the service of legal notice dated 29/12/2004 Ex. PW-1/3.

24. In reply to the contention raised by the petitioner, the respondent denied that he has ever defaulted in payment of rent. In fact according to him there is no question of payment of rent at all in view of the stand taken by him.

25. It is settled law that when the petitioner alleges non- payment of rent under section 14(1)(a) of DRC Act, the onus lies upon the respondent/tenant to prove that he has paid the rent. It has been held in "Sukhanand Vs. IVth Additional District E. No. 115/2008. Rambir vs. U.R. Biswas Page 11 of 15 Judge, Bulendshahar & Ors." [1994(2) AIRCJ 27] that the onus to show payment of rent lies on the tenant and oral testimony of tenant in regard to the payment of rent claiming discharge of liability in this regard cannot be admitted to be worth reliance at all. It is also held in "Raghubir Prasad Vs. Rajendra Kumar Gurudev", [1993(2) R.C.R. (Rent) 234] that on default in payment of rent, the onus to show payment of rent lies on tenant.

26. It is held by Hon'ble Supreme Court of India in "Karamchand Deojee Sanghavi Vs. Tulshiram Kalu Kumawat", [1992(1) RCR 118] that in a case of eviction on arrears of rent, the onus would always be on tenant to prove that he has paid the rent.

27. Thus, the onus was upon the respondent to prove that he had made upto date payment of rent and that there were no arrears of rent due against him as claimed in the legal demand notice Ex. PW1/3.

28. The respondent can prove the said fact either through oral or documentary evidence.

29. But the stand of the respondent, which is not accepted by this court as observed above, is that there is no landlord tenant relationship between him and the petitioner and as such petitioner has no right to claim rent from the respondent.

30. Thus, it is an admitted fact that no rent was paid or received after 06/02/2004 and as such the respondent is in arrears of the same as on the date of legal demand notice Ex.

E. No. 115/2008. Rambir vs. U.R. Biswas Page 12 of 15

PW-1/3.

IV) That a valid legal demand notice was duly served upon the respondent:

31. The court is required to decide whether the legal notice dated 29/12/2004 Ex. PW-1/3 was served upon the respondent or not, as it is the service of this notice that gives rise to cause of action for filling of the present petition.

32. The presumption of service as per Section 114 of the Indian Evidence Act and Section 27 of General Clauses Act is applicable if the petitioner is able to prove on record that Legal demand notice Ex. PW-1/3 was sent through UPC in present case.

33. In K. Bhaskaran v. Sankaran Vaidhyan Balan, (SC) [1999 A.I.R. (SC) 3762 ] it was observed by Honourable Apex Court that the principle incorporated in Section 27 can profitably be imported in a case where the sender has dispatched the notice by post with the correct address written on it. Then it can be deemed to have been served on the sendee unless he proves that it was not really served and that he was not responsible for such non-service. It was further observed that when a notice is returned by the sendee as unclaimed such date would be the commencing date in reckoning relevant period.

34. The said judgment was relied upon by The Full Bench of the Honourable Apex Court in C.C. Alavi Haji v. Palapetty Muhammed, (SC)[2007(6) S.C.C. 555] and it was observed that E. No. 115/2008. Rambir vs. U.R. Biswas Page 13 of 15 when a notice is sent by registered post and is returned with a postal endorsement "refused" or "not available in the house" or "house locked" or "shop closed" or "addressee not in station", due service has to be presumed.

35. But in present case as per the case of petitioner, he had sent legal notice dated Ex. PW-1/3 through ordinary post only vide UPC and not through any registered post/courier etc. Although service only through UPC is also a valid service, but in present case the petitioner even failed to prove the photocopy of receipt of such UPC in his evidence in chief by way of affidavit. Further in his cross examination the PW-1/petitioner deposed that such legal demand notice was sent through police. Further not even a suggestion is put to the respondent during his cross examination about receiving of such notice by the respondent. As such, as per evidence available on record there is no proof of even sending, what to talk of receiving, the legal demand notice Ex. PW-1/3 to the respondent. As such even the presumption of service as per Section 114 of the Indian Evidence Act and Section 27 of General Clauses Act can not be raised in favor of petitioner and against the respondent.

36. As such, in view of above mentioned position of law coupled with facts and circumstances of present case and evidence on record, it is concluded that the petitioner failed to prove the service of legal demand notice Ex. PW-1/3 upon the respondent.

V) That despite the service of the legal demand notice the respondent has neither paid nor tendered the entire arrears of legally recoverable rent:

E. No. 115/2008. Rambir vs. U.R. Biswas Page 14 of 15

37. As already discussed above, the respondent denied the relationship of landlord and tenant between the parties and according to him there is no question of payment of arrears of rent at all in view of the stand taken by him. Thus, it is concluded the respondent has neither paid nor tendered the entire arrears of legally recoverable rent.

Conclusion:

38. From the above said discussion, it is clear that the petitioner has failed to prove one of the essential condition of section 14(1)(a) of DRC Act i.e. the service of legal demand notice Ex. PW-1/3 upon the respondent. As a result, the present petition is dismissed .

39. No order as to costs. File be consigned to Record Room.

Announced in the open court on 12/12/2014 (This judgment contains 15 pages).

(Naveen Kr. Kashyap) Commercial Civil Judge-cum Additional Rent Controller, West District Courts, Delhi.
