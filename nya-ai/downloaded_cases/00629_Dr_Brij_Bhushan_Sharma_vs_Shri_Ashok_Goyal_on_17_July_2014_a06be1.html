                   SUIT FOR  POSSESSION AND MESNE PROFITS

Suit No.41/10                 Dr. Brij Bhushan Sharma & Ors. Vs. Sh. Ashok Goyal                             1/17
 JUDGMENT:

1. The plaintiffs have filed the present suit for possession and mesne profits against the defendant claiming to be the joint owners/co­parceners of the premises bearing no. 3784 and 3805 Nai Basti, Pahari Dhiraj, New Delhi

06. The plaintiffs contend that the defendant is a very old tenant (a tenant for more than 30 years) under the plaintiffs in respect of two rooms, one hall, one toilet and one covered gallery forming part of property no. 3784, Nai Basti, Pahari Dhiraj, New Delhi - 06 situated on the ground floor (hereinafter referred to as 'the suit premises').

2. The plaintiffs aver that since the defendant is a very old tenant so initially the rate of rent of the suit premises was Rs. 260/­ per month. Thereafter the rent was increased to Rs. 3,405/­ per month vide notice dated 26.04.2002 which was replied to by the defendant but the defendant failed to make the payment of the increased/enhanced rent. As per the plaintiffs, the present rate of rent of likewise premises prevalent in the surrounding areas was Rs. 15,000/­ per month but they claim Rs. 10,000/­ per month towards rent.

3. It is stated further that despite service of the demand notice the defendant did not clear the arrears of rent w.e.f. 01.06.2002. The plaintiffs ultimately terminated the tenancy of the defendant vide notice dated Suit No.41/10 Dr. Brij Bhushan Sharma & Ors. Vs. Sh. Ashok Goyal 2/17 09.11.2009 sent by registered post and UPC U/s 106 of Transfer of Property Act. The defendant neither complied with the notice nor did he hand over the possession of the suit premises or clear the arrears of rent. The defendant only sent a reply dated 03.12.2009. The plaintiffs contend that the tenancy of the defendant had already been terminated therefore the defendant was a rank trespasser and they were entitled to recovery of damages at the market rate which was about Rs. 15,000/­ per month but were claiming damages at Rs. 10,000/­ per month from the defendant.

4. The plaintiffs through the present suit have thus sought the reliefs of possession of the suit premises against the defendants along with mesne profits/damages @ Rs. 10,000/­ per month.

5. The defendant filed the written statement. Preliminary submissions were made. It was averred that the suit was not maintainable and had been filed to pressurize the defendant to bow to the illegal demands of the plaintiffs for exorbitant increase in the rent. The defendant averred that he received one legal notice dated 26.04.2000 purported to be U/s 8 of the Delhi Rent Control Act 1958 from and on behalf of Sh. Yograj Sharma son of Late Ghanshyam Das Sharma (predecessor in interest of the plaintiffs no. 4 and

5) demanding payment of enhanced/increased rent from Rs. 260/­ to Rs. 3,450/­ per month without any legal basis. Thereafter, an another legal notice Suit No.41/10 Dr. Brij Bhushan Sharma & Ors. Vs. Sh. Ashok Goyal 3/17 dated 12.09.2006 was served upon the defendant under the instructions and on behalf of Sh. Yograj Sharma wherein the rent at the rate of Rs. 286/­ per month was claimed from the defendant after enhancing the last paid rent of Rs. 260/­ per month by 10% as per the law in terms of Section 6A of the Delhi Rent Control Act w.e.f. 01.11.2006. The defendant alleges that again a notice dated 09.11.2009 was received by the defendant from the plaintiff wherein monthly rent @ Rs. 10,000/­ was claimed from him w.e.f. 01.11.2009 contrary to the notice dated 12.09.2006. The defendant avers that the only intention of the plaintiffs is to seek illegal enhancement in rent.

6. It is further a preliminary submission of the defendant that he is/was a tenant under the plaintiffs no. 4 and 5/their predecessor in interest only and had no concern with the plaintiffs no. 1 and 3 and the plaintiffs no. 1 to 3 had no right/title or interest in the suit property and the suit was bad for misjoinder of plaintiffs no. 1 to 3. The defendant never attorned to plaintiffs no. 1 to 3 as his landlords and there was no landlord tenant relationship between the defendant and plaintiffs no. 1 to 3. It was further contended that the suit was barred by the principles of estoppel and acquiescence and demand made in notice dated 09.11.2009 @ Rs. 10,000/­ per month was not sustainable.

7. Preliminary objections were also taken by the defendant. It was averred inter alia that the suit was hit by Section 50 of the Delhi Rent Control Act Suit No.41/10 Dr. Brij Bhushan Sharma & Ors. Vs. Sh. Ashok Goyal 4/17 since the agreed rate of rent was below Rs. 3,500/­ per month and the tenancy was for commercial purpose. The defendant on merits admitted the rights of the plaintiffs no. 4 and 5 in respect of the tenanted premises but contended that the plaintiffs no. 1 to 3 had no right/title or interest in the tenanted premises. It was submitted that the rent was unilaterally increased from Rs. 260/­ to Rs. 3,450/­ vide notice dated 26.04.2000 without any basis and the defendant was not bound to comply with the legal notice dated 26.04.2000. It was also contended that the reply to the legal notice dated 26.04.2000 had been given which the plaintiffs had malafidely concealed. It was contended that the tenancy was terminated illegally. It was averred that even after the alleged termination of tenancy of the defendant had acquired the status of the statutory tenant protected under the provisions of Delhi Rent Control Act 1958. Further, it was contended that the notice dated 09.11.2009 was duly replied and rent for the period up to 31.12.2009 had been deposited by filing a petition U/s 27 of the Delhi Rent Control Act at the agreed rate of rent. It was prayed that the suit be dismissed.

8. The plaintiffs filed the replication. In reply to the preliminary submissions inter alia it was contended that the notices dated 26.04.2000 and 09.11.2009 were admitted but the sending of notice dated 12.09.2006 was denied. It was submitted that the defendant was an old tenant and was Suit No.41/10 Dr. Brij Bhushan Sharma & Ors. Vs. Sh. Ashok Goyal 5/17 making the payment of rent to the plaintiffs at a fixed rate for decades and the rate of rent of the property had increased manifold therefore the rent of the premises was enhanced to Rs. 3,450/­. It was averred that when the defendant did not make the payment of the said amount his tenancy was terminated vide notice dated 09.11.2009 and rent was increased to Rs. 10,000/­ despite the fact that rent of similar properties in the neighbouring area was Rs. 15,000/­ per month. It was denied that the tenancy was covered under the Delhi Rent Control Act. The allegations contained in the written statement were denied and those contained in the plaint were reiterated.

9. On 26.10.2010 the following issues were framed in the present suit­

1. Whether on the basis of plaint allegations plaintiff is entitled for decree of possession as prayed for? OPP

2. Whether on the basis of plaint allegations plaintiff is entitled for any sum towards damages/mesne profit. If yes, at what rate? OPP

3. Whether the suit is hit by Section 50 of DRCA and not maintainable in the present form? OPD Relief.

10. The matter was initially fixed for arguments on the issue no. 3 since it was observed that the issue no. 3 was of preliminary nature. On 07.04.2011 Suit No.41/10 Dr. Brij Bhushan Sharma & Ors. Vs. Sh. Ashok Goyal 6/17 relying on the judgment of Hon'ble Delhi High Court in 155 (2008) DLT 1445 wherein it was held that the issue of jurisdiction on the ground of bar of Section 50 of the Delhi Rent Control Act was not a pure issue of law and it was held by this court that findings on this issue would be given at the time of final adjudication and the case was fixed for plaintiffs' evidence.

11. The plaintiffs in evidence examined PW1 Sh. Brij Bhushan Sharma (plaintiff no. 1), PW2 Ankit (plaintiff no. 5) and PW3 Swaran Kumar Sharma (plaintiff no. 2) who were all subjected to cross examination. The defendant in evidence examined himself as DW1 and was subjected to cross examination and on 29.05.2014 DE stood closed. The matter was then fixed for final arguments.

12. Counsel for the plaintiffs advanced final arguments. Despite opportunity to address oral arguments or file written arguments the defendant chose not to do so however certain citations were filed by the defendant. Ld. counsel for plaintiff relied on the judgment of the Hon'ble Delhi High Court in Pearey Lal Workshop Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Raghunandan Saran Ashok Saran CM(M) 2696 of 2005 decided on 27.11.2008 equivalent citation­(2008) 155 DLT 145 while the defendant has relied on 2009 (107) DRJ 58 'Model Press Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Mohd. Saied' and 2012 (128) DRJ 392 'Santosh Vaid & Anr. Vs. Uttam Chand.' Suit No.41/10 Dr. Brij Bhushan Sharma & Ors. Vs. Sh. Ashok Goyal 7/17

13. I have heard the final arguments advanced by counsel for the plaintiff, perused the record and the relevant case law.

14. My issue­wise findings are as follows­ Issue No. 3 Whether the suit is hit by Section 50 of DRCA and not maintainable in the present form? OPD

15. This issue is taken up first since the discussion on this issue is pivotal for determining whether the plaintiffs are entitled to the relief or not. Admittedly, the initial rate of rent for the suit premises which the defendant used to pay was Rs. 260/­ per month. The plaintiffs' case is premised on the ground that the notice dated 26.04.2002 Ex. PW1/A was sent to the defendant but the defendant who replied to the said notice did not make the payment of the increased rent and was in arrears of rent, and ultimately notice dated 09.11.2009 i.e. Ex. PW1/B was sent to the defendant who did not comply with the same and only sent a reply to the same Ex. PW1/F, but neither did he hand over the peaceful possession of the suit premises nor did he clear the arrears of rent. The site plan Ex. PW1/J was admitted by the defendant. It was argued by the plaintiffs that the tenancy having been terminated vide the notice U/s 106 Transfer of Property Act, the defendant was liable to vacate the premises. It was also argued that the rate of rent @ Rs. 260/­ per month was the rate agreed upon by the parties at the time of commencement of Suit No.41/10 Dr. Brij Bhushan Sharma & Ors. Vs. Sh. Ashok Goyal 8/17 the tenancy, and more than 30 years had elapsed since then, therefore the plaintiffs had sought to increase the rent to Rs. 3,450/­ per month vide the notice dated 26.04.2002 in keeping with inflation and market rates. The valuation report of the suit property was exhibited as Ex. PW1/I, though the valuer was not called in evidence by the plaintiffs. Ld. counsel for the plaintiff relied on Pearey Lal's case (supra) for the observations made therein by the Hon'ble Delhi High court that there was no justification for keeping rents frozen and not allowing the landlords to reap the present value of the rent originally agreed.

16. There is no dispute that at the time of letting of the suit premises the rent agreed between the parties was Rs. 260/­ per month. The rent being admittedly less than Rs. 3,500/­ per month at the time of letting, the premises would be governed by the provision of the Delhi Rent Control Act 1958 and the enhancement of rent can accordingly be done only as per the provisions of the said Act.

17. Now, as per Section 6A and Section 8 of the Delhi Rent Control Act, the rate of rent can only be increased by way of a notice of enhancement of rent and enhancement can only be up to 10% every three years. The notice dated 26.04.2002 Ex. PW1/A is captioned as one U/s 8 of the DRC Act. Merely terming the notice as one U/s 8 of the Delhi Rent Control Act does Suit No.41/10 Dr. Brij Bhushan Sharma & Ors. Vs. Sh. Ashok Goyal 9/17 not suffice, it has to be seen whether the said notice actually complies with the legal requirements of Section 8 of the Delhi Rent Control Act. In para 3 of the said notice it is stated that the rate of rent of such like premises had increased up to Rs. 6,000/­ per month but the plaintiff Sh. Yog Raj Sharma (on whose instructions the notice was issued) was claiming Rs. 3,450/­ per month as rent vide the said notice. I note here that PW1 in his evidence admitted that in the said notice it was stated that it was issued on behalf of Sh. Yog Raj Sharma but PW1 volunteered that since they were all brothers therefore it was issued on behalf of all of them. From a rental amount of Rs. 260/­ per month suddenly the plaintiffs sought enhanced rent @ Rs. 3,450/­ per month in accordance with the market rental and the enhancement is not @ 10% of the agreed rent. Thus, clearly Ex. PW1/A notice which though was admittedly received by the defendant does not comply with the provision of Section 8 of the Delhi Rent Control Act and does not constitute a valid notice for enhancement of rent as per law.

18. Interestingly, the plaintiffs in the replication had denied the issuance of notice dated 12.09.2006 which was exhibited as Ex. PW1/D1 however, during the cross examination of PW1, he deposed that he met Sh. Ravinder Singh Advocate alongwith Sh. Yog Raj Sharma for issuance of the said notice which was issued on the instructions of the plaintiffs. PW1 denied the suggestion Suit No.41/10 Dr. Brij Bhushan Sharma & Ors. Vs. Sh. Ashok Goyal 10/17 that the notice was issued only on the instructions of Sh. Yog Raj Sharma. It must be observed at this stage that during the witness's cross examination realizing perhaps that the witness had deposed contrary to the pleading of the plaintiffs that no such notice was issued by the plaintiffs, the counsel for the plaintiffs instructed PW1 to read the contents of the notice, whereafter apparently the witness got wise, and stated that though he met Sh. Ravinder Singh, Advocate however the contents of the notice were not drafted as per the instructions of the plaintiffs. No such plea has been taken by the plaintiffs in the plaint or even the replication and is only an afterthought of PW1 who made the above feeble and unconvincing attempt to cover up the admission he had made regarding the sending of the notice Ex. PW1/D1.

19. The counsel for the plaintiffs put a suggestion to the defendant in cross examination that Sh. Yog Raj Sharma never issued any notice demanding Rs. 286/­ per month towards the rent of the suit premises to which he initially responded that he did not know but volunteered that his counsel would be having the knowledge about the same and that DW1 remembered that a notice was sent in the year 2002 by Sh. B.B. Sharma demanding rent @ Rs. 3,450/­ per month and thereafter in the year 2006, he (DW1) received a notice from Sh. B.B.Sharma demanding rent @ 10% increase upon the earlier rent of Rs. 260/­ per month. The notice Ex. PW1/D1 Suit No.41/10 Dr. Brij Bhushan Sharma & Ors. Vs. Sh. Ashok Goyal 11/17 is mentioned as being upon the instructions of Sh. Yog Raj Sharma, but as observed above, PW1 admitted meeting Sh. Ravinder Singh, Advocate along with Sh. Yog Raj Sharma for issuance of the said notice and though he sought to withdraw the admission made by him in the cross examination that the notice was issued on their instructions however he did not deny the sending of the said notice to the defendant. The defendant was thus able to elicit from the plaintiff's witness PW1 the fact that the notice Ex. PW1/D1 which is dated 12.09.2006 was sent to the defendant which mentions the same to be on the instructions of Sh. Yog Raj Sharma. Vide this notice, which was sent about four years after the notice Ex. PW1/A was sent, the defendant was called upon to pay the increased rent by 10% as per law w.e.f. 01.11.2006, i.e. Rs. 286/­ per month.

20. DW1 testified in his cross examination that it was correct that he did not pay rent @ Rs. 10,000/­ per month, volunteering that he had been making payment as per the 2006 notice (i.e. the notice Ex. PW1/D1). In fact Ex. DW1/P1 application for rent deposit specifically mentions the amount for rent being deposited by the defendant w.e.f. 01.11.2006 till 31.12.2009 to be @ Rs. 286/­ per month total amounting to Rs. 13,988/­ and the plaintiffs have not seriously challenged the claim of the defendant that he has not been depositing rent @ Rs. 286/­ per month. They claim that their entitlement is to Suit No.41/10 Dr. Brij Bhushan Sharma & Ors. Vs. Sh. Ashok Goyal 12/17 monthly rent @ Rs. 10,000/­ as per the notice Ex. PW1/B as per the prevalent market rent, and to possession of the suit premises.

21. In the case titled 'Santosh Vaid Vs. Uttam Chand' 2012 (188) DLT 29, the matter came up before the Hon'ble Division Bench of the Delhi High Court upon a reference by the Hon'ble Single Judge of the Delhi High Court regarding correctness of the view taken in M/s. Pearey Lal Workshop Pvt. Ltd. (supra) and a legal question was framed as follows­ "Whether in the case of premises fetching rent of less than Rs. 3,500/­ per month, the owner/landlord can claim increase in rent other than as provided under Sections 6A & 8 of the Act or have the rent increased in proportion to the rate of inflation or devaluation of money and if so on what basis and/or to what extent?"

22. This question was answered by the Hon'ble Division after a detailed discussion as under­ "A landlord of a premises governed by the Delhi Rent Control Act 1958 is entitled to have increase(s) in rent only in accordance with Section 6A and 8 thereof and not otherwise; such a landlord cannot approach the Civil Court contending that the rent stands increased or should be increased in accordance with the inflation or cost price index, the jurisdiction of the Civil Court in this regard is barred by Section 50 of the Delhi Rent Control Act." Suit No.41/10 Dr. Brij Bhushan Sharma & Ors. Vs. Sh. Ashok Goyal 13/17 Further, is pertinent to note that in para 17 of the aforesaid judgment it was observed that Pearey Lal (supra) could not be said to be an authority in favour of the right of a landlord to have the rent increased to bring it at par with the consumer price index or to account for the rate of inflation.

23. The judgment of the Hon'ble Delhi High Court in Santosh Vaid (supra) is the authority which is squarely applicable to the present case. The originally agreed rate of rent being Rs. 260/­ per month, the enhancement of the rent could only have been done in accordance with Section 6A read with Section 8 of the Delhi Rent Control Act and not unilaterally by the landlords as was sought to be done vide the notices of the years 2002 and 2009 Ex. PW1/A and Ex. PW1/B respectively and it was only the notice Ex. PW1/D1 which correctly sought enhancement @ 10% of the existing rate i.e. Rs. 286/­ per month which enhanced rent the defendant testified he was paying. The rate of rent thus can neither be pegged at Rs. 3,450/­ per month nor Rs. 10,000/­ per month and the notices Ex. PW1/A and Ex. PW1/B are contrary to the provisions of Section 6A & Section 8 of the Delhi Rent Control Act and the rent payable by the defendant is Rs. 286/­ per month in view of the notice Ex. PW1/D1.

24. The landlord­tenant relationship is admitted by the defendant, at least with respect to plaintiffs no. 4 and 5 however the plaintiffs contend to be Suit No.41/10 Dr. Brij Bhushan Sharma & Ors. Vs. Sh. Ashok Goyal 14/17 coparceners of the suit premises. Plaintiff no. 5 infact denied in his cross examination the suggestion of the defendant that the plaintiffs no. 1 to 3 had nothing to do with the tenancy rights of the defendants in the suit property. Further, Ex. DW1/P1 i.e. the application for rent deposit filed by the defendant himself before the Ld. Rent Controller which the defendant admitted was drafted at his instructions and names of the parties (therein) were supplied by him, mentions the names of the plaintiffs no. 1 to 3 herein in addition to the plaintiffs no. 4 and 5 as respondents in the memo of parties while in column no. 3, pertaining to the names and addresses of the landlord or persons claiming to be landlords, the defendant herein has mentioned, 'As that of the respondents' meaning thereby all the plaintiffs herein had been impleaded as landlords. The defendant also testified in his cross examination that after the death of Sh. Yog Raj Sharma when he received a notice from plaintiff no. 1 stating therein that he along with plaintiffs no. 2 and 3 had become owners, the defendant sent a money order to Sh. Brij Bhushan Sharma which was refused therefore he deposited the money in court.

25. It is the landlord­tenant relationship and not ownership which has to be established by the plaintiffs. The defendant himself admitted to tendering the rent to plaintiff no. 1 upon the death of Sh. Yog Raj Sharma apparently without any protest and also filed the rent deposit application U/s 27 of Delhi Suit No.41/10 Dr. Brij Bhushan Sharma & Ors. Vs. Sh. Ashok Goyal 15/17 Rent Control Act impleading all the plaintiffs herein as respondents instead of just the plaintiffs no. 4 and 5 showing his acquiescence and acceptance of the plaintiffs as his landlords. The defendant then cannot be heard to say that only plaintiffs no. 4 and 5 are his landlords, the plaintiffs on a preponderance of probabilities have shown that the plaintiffs no. 1 to 3 are also landlords of the suit premises qua the defendant.

26. The fact of the matter is that there is a landlord tenant relationship between the parties. The rate of rent of the suit premises being below Rs. 3,500/­ per month, the present suit for recovery of possession by the landlords against the tenant does not lie in the Civil Court in view of the express bar of Section 50 of the Delhi Rent Control Act. The relief of ejectment/recovery of possession against the defendant can only be sought from the Ld. Rent Controller's Court. The suit of the plaintiff is thus held to be barred U/s 50 Delhi Rent Control Act and is held to be not maintainable before the Civil Court or in the present form.

Issue decided against the plaintiffs and in favour of the defendant. Issues No. 1 and 2 Whether on the basis of plaint allegations plaintiff is entitled for decree of possession as prayed for? OPP and Whether the suit is hit by Section 50 of DRCA and not maintainable in the present form? OPD

27. Even if for the sake of arguments, it be considered that the plaintiffs have validly terminated the tenancy of the defendant U/s 106 Transfer of Suit No.41/10 Dr. Brij Bhushan Sharma & Ors. Vs. Sh. Ashok Goyal 16/17 Property Act, since as I have already held above that the bar of Section 50 of Delhi Rent Control Act is attracted in the present case, the status of the defendant may be of a statutory tenant however the plaintiffs are not entitled to the relief of recovery of possession from the Civil Court. Moreover, even if the status of the defendant is that of a statutory tenant since the eviction is prohibited, the possession cannot be said to be unauthorized and the question of mesne profits does not arise. As held in Santosh Vaid's case (supra) in absence of a provision in the statute it cannot be held that a landlord is entitled to market rent from a protected tenant. The plaintiff is thus not entitled to the reliefs of possession and mesne profits as sought.

Issues decided against the plaintiffs.

Relief.

28. In view of the above issue­wise discussion, the suit of the plaintiffs stands dismissed. No order as to costs.

Decree sheet be prepared accordingly.

File be consigned to the Record Room.
