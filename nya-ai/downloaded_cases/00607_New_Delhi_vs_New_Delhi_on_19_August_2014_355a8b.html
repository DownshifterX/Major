Delhi District Court

New Delhi vs Sh. Rakesh Basra on 19 August, 2014

JUDGMENT:

The present appeal has been filed on behalf of the appellant­Sh. H. L. Kohli against the impugned judgment/decree dated 29.08.2013 passed by the Ld.Trial Court in Suit No. 235/12 titled as 'Sh. Rakesh Basra Vs. Sh. H.L. Kohli'.

2. Brief facts necessary for the disposal of the present appeal are that a suit for possession, recovery of Rs.57,030/­ and damages/mesne profits was filed on behalf of the plaintiff­ Sh. Rakesh Basra (respondent herein) against the defendant Sh. H.L. Kohli (appellant herein) before the Ld. Trial Court, wherein it has been stated that the plaintiff is the owner of Ist Floor of property bearing no. 48/2, East Patel Nagar, Delhi and the defendant was granted a lease in respect of the said suit property by the father of the plaintiff i.e. late Sh. K.L. Basra and the plaintiff vide legal notice dated 20.5.2011 called upon defendant to pay to the plaintiff the rent of the suit property @ Rs.3,195/­ per month from the period 01.04.2011 to 31.05.2011 as also all other arrears of rent, if any due and payable by the defendant, to the plaintiff alongwith interest @ 15% per annum on belated payments within one week of the receipt of notice inter­alia of demand. It is further stated that the defendant was further called upon to pay to the plaintiff the rent of the suit property at the escalated/enhanced rate in accordance with Section 6A r/w Section 8 of Delhi Rent Control Act w.e.f 01.07.2011 onwards on a monthly rent of Rs.3,515/­ exclusive of electricity and water charges. It is stated that the defendant sent a frivolous reply dated 10.06.2011 to the said notice. It is further stated that tenancy of the defendant in respect of the suit property do not have the protection of Delhi Rent Control Act RCA No. 43/2013 2/14 3 and the plaintiff did not wish to keep defendant as a tenant any longer and accordingly a notice dated 23.07.2011 was served upon the defendant intimating that his tenancy in respect of suit property shall stand determined on 31.08.2011 and the defendant was called upon to deliver actual, physical, vacant and peaceful possession of the suit property to the plaintiff on or before the said date, however the defendant has failed to hand over peaceful and vacant possession of the suit property despite service of legal notice dated 23.07.2011 which resulted in filing of the present suit, wherein it has been prayed that a decree of possession in respect of the suit property may be passed in favour of the plaintiff and against the defendant. It is further prayed that a decree for a sum of Rs.57, 030/­ alongwith interest @ 15% per annum till recovery and a decree of mesne profits @ Rs.25,000/­ per month from the date of filing of the suit till the date of vacation of the suit property may also be passed in favour of the plaintiff and against the defendant.

3. The defendant contested the above said suit and filed the W.S, wherein it has been stated that the plaintiff has not come to the court with clean hands and suppressed the material facts. It is stated that plaintiff had earlier filed a civil suit for possession and mesne profits against the defendant on the same cause of action at the alleged rate of rent of Rs.3,515/­ per month and the said suit was also contested by the defendant, wherein the defendant has taken preliminary objection that the said suit was barred u/s 50 of Delhi Rent Control Act as the rate of rent was Rs.3,195/­ and the said suit was dismissed vide judgment/decree dated 04.04.2011 passed by the Ld. Civil Judge, Delhi merely on the ground that the suit was hit by RCA No. 43/2013 3/14 4 Section 50 of Delhi Rent Control Act . It is stated that there is no cause of action in favour of the plaintiff to file the present suit as the rate of rent has not been increased by the defendant either by agreement or by virtue of law and as such the present suit of the plaintiff is barred by Section 50 of Delhi Rent Control Act. It is further stated that the suit of the plaintiff was also hit by the doctrine of res­judicata as contemplated u/s 11 of CPC. It is stated that no valid notice has been sent by the plaintiff to the defendant before filing the present suit and that the suit of the plaintiff is also liable to be rejected u/o 7 rule 11 CPC as the same has been filed without any cause of action. All other averments made in the plaint have also been denied on behalf of the defendant and it has been prayed that the suit of the plaintiff may be dismissed with exemplary cost.

4. Thereafter, on the basis of the pleadings of the parties, issues were framed by the Ld. Trial Court and after trial, aforesaid suit was decreed with costs for recovery of possession and suit qua recovery was partly allowed and was decreed to the tune of Rs.14,220/­ alongwith interest @ 6% per annum from the date of filing of the suit till its realization and the suit for damages/mesne profits was also partly decreed and it was held that the plaintiff was entitled to the damages @ Rs.10,000/­ per month from the date of filing the suit till its recovery, but the sum of Rs.3195/­ deposited by the defendant in the account of plaintiff till 28.01.2012 shall be adjusted from the total claim of damages.

Aggrieved by the said impugned judgment/decree, the appellant/ defendant has filed the present appeal, wherein it has been prayed that the aforesaid RCA No. 43/2013 4/14 5 impugned judgment/decree dated 29.08.2013 passed by the Ld. Trial court may be set aside.

5. Upon filing of the present appeal, notice of the appeal was issued to the respondent and accordingly, the respondent entered the appearance and contested the present appeal.

Trial Court Record (TCR) was also summoned and the same has been received.

6. I have heard the arguments put forward by Ld. counsels for the appellant and respondent and have carefully gone through the record of the case.

7. It has been submitted on behalf of the appellant that the impugned judgment/decree passed by the Ld. Trial Court is against the law and facts on record. It is further submitted that the Ld. Trial Court wrongly observed that mere acceptance of rent does not amount to waiver of notice to quit, unless there is no other evidence to prove or establish that the landlord so intended. It is submitted that the Ld. Trial Court erred in observing that appellant himself continued to deposit rent @ Rs.3,195/­ per month in the bank account of the respondent as he was well aware of the account number and that it will not amount to waiver of notice to quit and no consent of the respondent was required for depositing the cheque in the account of the respondent. It is further submitted that the Ld. Trial Court also wrongly observed that unilateral act of the depositing rent @ Rs.3,195/­ by the RCA No. 43/2013 5/14 6 appellant will not amount to waiver of notice. It is submitted that the respondent did not have any access to the account number of bank of the respondent at any point of time and the Ld. Trial Court has failed to appreciate the fact that appellant has been continuously sending the rent @ Rs.3,195/­ per month by account payee cheques to the respondent and respondent used to give receipts of the same and this shows that the respondent has been accepting the rent @ Rs.3,195/­per month without any demur. It is submitted that the Ld. Trial Court has failed to appreciate that the respondent could not have unilaterally increased the rent by issuing legal notice on 20.05.2011 and could not have thereby terminated the tenancy as fresh tenancy was created month to month on 04.04.2011 when the earlier suit of the plaintiff was dismissed by the Ld. Civil Judge, Delhi and that the respondent could not have increased the rent of the suit premises u/s 6A r/w 8 of Delhi Rent Control Act as three years have not passed. It is further submitted that the Ld. Trial Court has failed to appreciate that the rate of rent proved on record was Rs.3,195/­ per month and not Rs.3,515/­ and thus the suit was hit by Section 50 of Delhi Rent Control Act. It is further submitted that Ld. Trial Court erred in granting the damages/mesne profits @ Rs.10,000/­ per month merely on the ground that the said rate was prevailing rate of rent of similar property in the same vicinity, although the plaintiff/respondent has failed to discharge onus in this regard on record.

It has been further submitted on behalf of the appellant that impugned judgment/decree passed by the Ld. Trial Court was based upon conjecture & surmises and was in contradiction to the fact, law, documents and evidence on record. It has also been submitted that the impugned judgment/decree passed by the RCA No. 43/2013 6/14 7 Ld. Trial Court was based upon the presumption & assumptions and was not sustainable in law and it has been prayed that the impugned judgment/decree dated 29.08.2013 passed by the Ld. Trial Court may be set aside.

8. On the other hand, it has been submitted by Ld. counsel for the respondent that there was no illegality or irregularity in the impugned/decree dated 29.08.2013 passed by the Ld. Trial Court. It is further submitted that Ld. Trial Court has properly appreciated the material on record and have correctly passed the impugned judgment/decree in the fact and circumstances of the present case. It is submitted that the appellant has failed to disclose any substantial question of law and as such the appeal filed on behalf of the appellant was not maintainable. It is further submitted that in the instant case, relationship of the landlord and tenant between the parties is admitted and the rate of the rent has also been proved as Rs.3,515/­per month consequent to the enhancement of the rent in accordance with the provisions Section 6 A & 8 Delhi Rent Control Act. It is submitted that unilateral act of depositing of rent @ Rs.3,195/­ by the appellant in the bank account of the respondent without his consent does not amount to the waiver of notice to quit. It is further submitted that dismissal of earlier suit cannot act as res­judicata for the institution of the present suit as the earlier suit was dismissed merely on the ground that the same was hit by provisions of Delhi Rent Control Act as the rate of rent in respect of suit premises at that time was Rs.3,195/­ per month. It is submitted that the Ld. Trial Court has correctly granted the mesne profits @ Rs.10,000/­ per month in accordance with prevailing the rate of rent of the similar situated properties in the RCA No. 43/2013 7/14 8 same area or vicinity. It has also been submitted that there was no illegality or irregularity in the impugned judgment/decree dated 29.08.2013 passed by the Ld.Trial Court and it has been prayed that the present appeal filed on behalf of the appellant may be dismissed.

9. I have carefully considered the submissions made on behalf of the appellant and respondent and have carefully gone through the record of the case. I have also carefully perused the TCR.

10. The present appeal has been filed on behalf of the appellant against the impugned judgment/decree dated 29.08.2013 passed by the Ld. Trial Court in Suit No. 235/12 titled as 'Sh. Rakesh Basra Vs. Sh. H.L. Kohli', whereby the present suit was decreed with costs for recovery of possession and suit qua recovery was partly allowed and was decreed to the tune of Rs.14,220/­ alongwith interest @ 6% per annum from the date of filing of the suit till its realization and the suit for damages/mesne profits was also partly decreed and it was held that the plaintiff was entitled to the damages @ Rs.10,000/­ per month from the date of filing the suit till its recovery, but the sum of Rs.3195/­ deposited by the defendant in the account of plaintiff till 28.01.2012 shall be adjusted from the total claim of damages.

11. It has been submitted on behalf of the appellant that the Ld. Trial Court wrongly observed that mere acceptance of rent does not amount to waiver of notice to quit, unless there is no other evidence to prove or establish that the landlord RCA No. 43/2013 8/14 9 so intended. It is submitted that the Ld. Trial Court erred in observing that appellant himself continued to deposit rent @ Rs.3,195/­ per month in the bank account of the respondent as he was well aware of the account number and that it will not amount to waiver of notice to quit and no consent of the respondent was required for depositing the cheque in the account of the respondent. It is further submitted that the Ld. Trial Court also wrongly observed that unilateral act of the depositing rent @ Rs.3,195/­ by the appellant will not amount to waiver of notice. It is submitted that the respondent did not have any access to the account number of bank of the respondent at any point of time and the Ld. Trial Court has failed to appreciate the fact that appellant has been continuously sending the rent @ Rs.3,195/­ per month by account payee cheques to the respondent and respondent used to give receipts of the same and this shows that the respondent has been accepting the rent @ Rs.3,195/­per month without any demur, however the said submissions made on behalf of the appellant are devoid of any merits and are contrary to the record as the perusal of the record reveals that Ld. Trial Court has properly dealt with all the averments and contentions raised on behalf of the appellant and have come to the just and reasonable conclusions on the basis of the material available on record. In the instant case, it has been correctly observed by the Ld. Trial Court that the defendant had to prove that the plaintiff had accepted a sum of Rs. 3,195/­ p.m as the complete monthly rent from the defendant even after service of notice Ex. PW­1/C, however no suggestion in this regard has been given in the cross examination of PW­1 i.e the plaintiff . Further, no evidence, documentary or otherwise, have been produced on record by the defendant which RCA No. 43/2013 9/14 10 could prove that the plaintiff/respondent had intentionally and willfully accepted the rent @ Rs. 3,195/­ as the complete rent. It is well settled law that mere acceptance of rent does not amount to waiver of notice to quit unless there is any other evidence to prove or establish that the landlord so intended and further that rent received after termination of tenancy can be taken as charges towards use and occupation because after all the tenant is bound to pay charges till he vacates the tenanted premises.In these circumstances, it has been rightly held by the Ld. Trial Court that unilateral act of depositing the rent @ Rs. 3195/­ p.m by the defendant will not amount to waiver of notice to quit in this case.

12. It has been further submitted on behalf of the appellant that the Ld. Trial Court has failed to appreciate that the respondent could not have unilaterally increased the rent by issuing legal notice on 20.05.2011 and could not have thereby terminated the tenancy as fresh tenancy was created month to month on 04.04.2011, when the earlier suit of the plaintiff was dismissed by the Ld. Civil Judge, Delhi and that the respondent could not have increased the rent of the suit premises u/s 6A r/w 8 of Delhi Rent Control Act as three years have not passed. It is further submitted that the Ld. Trial Court has failed to appreciate that the rate of rent proved on record was Rs.3,195/­ per month and not Rs.3,515/­ and thus the suit was hit by Section 50 of Delhi Rent Control Act. It is further submitted that Ld. Trial Court erred in granting the damages/mesne profits @ Rs.10,000/­ per month merely on the ground that the said rate was prevailing rate of rent of similar property in the same vicinity, although the plaintiff/respondent has failed to RCA No. 43/2013 10/14 11 discharge onus in this regard on record, however the said contentions raised on behalf of the appellant are devoid of any merits and are contrary to the record as the perusal of the record reveals that Ld. Trial Court has properly dealt with the said contentions and have come to the just and reasonable conclusions on the basis of the material available on record.

In the instant case, it is pertinent to note that relationship of land lord and tenant between the parties is not disputed and it is also admitted that earlier rate of rent was Rs. 3195/­ The said rate of rent was enhanced to Rs. 3515/­ by the plaintiff vide legal notice Ex.PW­1/C and the service of the said notice on defendant is also not disputed . Further from the material on record, it is evident that above said rate of rent has been enhanced/increased by the plaintiff in accordance with the provisions of Section­6A and Section ­8 of Delhi Rent Control Act and the plaintiff was entitled to do so. Section ­6A of Delhi Rent Control Act deals with revision of rent and it provides that notwithstanding anything contained in that Act, the standard rent or where no standard rent is fixed under the provisions of this Act in respect of any premises, the rent agreed upon between the landlord and the tenant may be increased by ten percent every three years. Further, Section ­8 of Delhi Rent Control Act deals with notice of increase of rent and it provides that where a landlord wishes to increase the rent of any premises, he shall give the tenant notice of his contention to make the increase and in so far as such increase is lawful under this Act, it shall be due and recoverable only in respect of the period of the tenancy after the expiry of thirty days from the date from which notice is given. In these circumstances, in view of the provisions of section­6A RCA No. 43/2013 11/14 12 and section­ 8 of Delhi Rent Control Act, the rent in this case stood enhanced to Rs. 3515/­ in terms of legal notice Ex.PW­1/C and as such the present suit filed on behalf of the plaintiff before the Ld. Trial Court was not barred by Section ­50 of Delhi Rent Control Act .

It has been submitted on behalf of appellant that the plaintiff/ respondent could not have unilaterally increased the rent by issuing the legal notice dated 20.5.2011 and could not have thereby terminated the tenancy as fresh month to month tenancy was created on 04.4.2011, when earlier suit filed on behalf of the plaintiff was dismissed by the Ld. Civil Judge , however the said contention raised on behalf of the appellant does not hold water as admittedly the previous suit for possession filed on behalf of the plaintiff/respondent was dismissed by the court merely on the ground that the rate of rent was Rs. 3,195/­ p.m and the plaintiff could not have terminated the tenancy which was protected by the statute. In this regard, it has been rightly observed by the Ld Trial Court that since no fresh tenancy was created and the present defendant continued to be a statutory tenant as the rate of rent was less than Rs. 3500/­ on the date of decision i.e 04.04.2011 in the previous case. It has been further correctly observed by the Ld. Trial Court that there can not be estoppel on the question of law and that fact that tenancy of a statutory tenant could not have been terminated by way of serving a notice required u/s­ 106 of TP Act is a question of law and hence the plaintiff was not estopped in any manner from filing the present suit. It has been rightly held by the Ld. Trial Court that the plaintiff has proved on record that rate of rent was increased to Rs. 3515/­ w.e.f 01.7.2011 and the tenancy became month RCA No. 43/2013 12/14 13 to month which was terminated vide notice Ex. PW­1/E, dated 23.7.2011 w.e.f. 01.9.2011 and as such the defendant was in unauthorized occupation of the suit property and the plaintiff was entitled to the possession of the said suit property. In addition to this, it has also been rightly observed by the Ld. Trial Court, the present suit filed on behalf of the plaintiff was also not barred by the principle of res­judicata as matter directly and substantially in issue in the present suit was not directly and substantially in issue in the above said earlier suit between the parties. It has also been rightly observed by the Ld. Trial Court that the matter which was directly and substantially in issue in the previous suit was, whether the parties had increased the rent to Rs. 3515/­ by mutual agreement, whereas the matter directly and substantially in issue in the present suit is whether the plaintiff had enhanced the said rent as per law and whether he was competent to do so and therefore the matter directly and substantially in issue in the present suit is different from the matter directly and substantially in issue in the former suit.

13. It has been submitted on behalf of the appellant that the impugned judgment/decree passed by the Ld. Trial Court is against the law and facts on record. It has been further submitted on behalf of the appellant that impugned judgment/ decree passed by the Ld. Trial Court was based upon conjecture & surmises and was in contradiction to the fact, law, documents and evidence on record. It has also been submitted that the impugned judgment/decree passed by the Ld. Trial Court was based upon the presumption & assumptions and was not sustainable in law, however the said contentions put forward on behalf of the appellant do not hold RCA No. 43/2013 13/14 14 water and are contrary to the record as perusal of the impugned judgment/decree reveals that it is based upon the sound, cogent and just reasoning and the same has been passed on the basis of the proper appreciation of the material on record.

14. Thus, in view of the above discussion & observations and having regard to the fact and circumstances of the present case, I am of the considered opinion that there is no illegality or infirmity in the impugned judgment/decree dated 29.8.2013 passed by the Ld. Trial Court.

Accordingly, the present appeal filed on behalf of the appellant against the aforesaid impugned judgment/decree dated 29.8.2013 passed by the Ld. Trial Court in Suit No. 235/12 titled as 'Sh. Rakesh Basra Vs. Sh. H.L. Kohli' is hereby dismissed.

Decree sheet be prepared accordingly.

TCR alongwith copy of this judgment be sent back to the Ld.Trial Court.

Appeal file be consigned to the record room.
