JUDGMENT

1. Plaintiffs have filed the suit for declaration, permanent and mandatory injunction and for damages. Plaintiff no. 1 is widow of Late Sh. Hazura Singh and the other plaintiffs are his children. In brief the case of the plaintiffs is that in the year 1960 Late Sh Hazura Singh constructed a tin shed and an office. The property is known as WZ-15C, Kailash Park, Najafgarh, Delhi. Late Sh. Hazura Singh was running an auto workshop from the aforesaid property under the name and style of M/s. New Vishwakarma Garage. Plaintiff no. 2 and 3 also joined him and continued to run Suit No. 109/13 Page 2 of 21 pages 3 the firm after his death. On 12.12.2001, the officers of defendant visited the area and started demolishing property no. WZ-15A and 15B. They also damaged property no. WZ-15D. Late sh. Hazura Singh filed WP (C) No. 4850/2002 before the Hon'ble Delhi High Court. Defendant admitted the number of the property but claimed that it was situated in Khasra No. 2778/738 of Village Basai Darapur which had been acquired vide award no. 1724 and physical possession of the land had been handed over to them by the LAC on 19.08.1964. The writ petition was dismissed. Late Sh. Hazura Singh filed LPA. In the LPA, the defendant filed a wrong demarcation report. The LPA was also dismissed. Late Sh. Hazura Singh moved a review application and it was dismissed. He preferred SLP before the Hon'ble Supreme Court which was disposed of vide order dt 26.09.2008. Hon'ble Supreme Court granted liberty to Late Sh. Hazura Singh to approach the civil court or any other appropriate forum which would decide the matter without being influenced by the observations and findings of the Hon'ble High Court. In the year 2002 during the proceedings before Suit No. 109/13 Page 3 of 21 pages 4 the Hon'ble High Court, the plaintiffs came to know about the notifications under section 4 & 6 of the Land Acquisition Act 1894. A general notification under Section 4 of the Act was issued on 13.11.1959 and the areas of Tilak Nagar, Bali Nagar, Kailash park and Lakshmi Garden had been exempted. The suit property falls in Kailash Park and therefore it was exempted from acquisition. The notification under Section 6 of the Act was made vide notification no. F. 1(35)/63-L & H dated 21.09.1963 and it did not mention Khasra no. 2778/738 Village Basai Darapur. Award no. 5/1964-65 was passed in pursuance to notification no. F. 15(III)/50-LSG dated 13.11.1959 under Section 4 & notification no. F. 1 (35)/63-L & H dated 21.09.1963 under Section 6 of the Land Acquisition Act. The award mentioned that the built up area upto the west side of the "Nala" and constructed portion measuring 7 bigha and 10 biswas was not covered by the acquisition. Defendant has no right to dispossess the plaintiffs as they have remained in possession of the suit land for more than 30 years.

2. Defendant no. 2 contested the suit. Hereinafter defendant no. 2 shall be referred to as a defendant. According to the defendant, the suit land falls in Khasra no. 2778/738 which was acquired vide award no. 1724 and possession of the land was handed over to them on 19.08.1964 by the LAC / L & B deptt. The land was notified under Section 22 (I) of DD Act on 03.01.1968 vide notification no. F.8 (49) 63/L & H. The land was illegally occupied by the plaintiffs claiming it to be WZ-15C, Kailash Park. On 25.09.2004 demarcation was carried out and report was filed before the Hon'ble High Court. It is stated that the suit land falls between Kailash Park and Lakshmi Garden and it forms part of the acquired land which was handed over to DDA.

Ld counsel for the plaintiffs has also filed written arguments and I have perused the same. The issues are decided as Suit No. 109/13 Page 6 of 21 pages 7 hereunder.
5. Issue no. 1:- Whether the suit is not maintainable in law ?

OPD (5.1) This issue was framed vide order dated 08.07.2013 in view of the oral submissions made by ld counsel for the parties. Ld counsel for the defendant submitted that the area where the suit property is situated had been duly acquired vide award no. 1724. According to the plaintiffs the suit property is a part of Kailash Park Colony. In the plaint, it is stated that the area of Kailash Park was kept out of the acquisition as mentioned in the notification dated 13.11.1959 under Section 4 of Land Acquisition Act (hereinafter referred to as the LA Act). Defendants claim that the suit property is part of Khasra no. 2278/738, Basai Darapur. In this regard it is stated in the plaint that the notification dated 21.09.1963 under Section 6 of the LA Act did not include Khasra No. 2278/738. It is further stated in the plaint that on the basis of the notifications under Section 4 & 6 of the Act, award no. 5/ 1964-65 was passed.

(5.2) The stand taken by the plaintiffs in the plaint is two fold. Firstly they claim that the suit property was left out of acquisition Suit No. 109/13 Page 7 of 21 pages 8 and secondly they claim that even if the stand taken by the defendant regarding the Khasra number is accepted, defendant has no right over the suit property as the particular khasra number was not acquired. It is pertinent to note that in the plaint, reference was made to award no. 5 of the year 1964-65 but the actual award on which the defendant relies is award no. 1724. Khasra No. 2778/738 is mentioned in the award no. 1724 copy of which is Ex.D2W1/1. Copy of the award has also been exhibited by the plaintiffs as Ex.PW1/37. The fact that Khasra no. 2778/738 was acquired vide award no. 1724, goes against the stand taken by the plaintiffs. Ld counsel for the plaintiffs tried to defend the case put up by the plaintiffs by making the submission that even if award no. 1724 covers Khasra no. 2778/738, it has no bearing on the case as the acquisition proceedings were invalid. No argument can be put forward beyond the scope of the suit. The plaintiffs could not have challenged the validity of the acquisition proceedings before a Civil Court and they have not challenged it in the present suit. In the prayer for declaration, it is stated that a decree of declaration be Suit No. 109/13 Page 8 of 21 pages 9 passed declaring that the suit property which is part of Kailash Park has never been acquired and belongs to plaintiffs. There is difference between a property being not acquired and a property becoming free due to the acquisition proceedings being invalid. There is no prayer in the plaint to declare the acquisition proceedings as null and void and there is no issue framed regarding the validity of the acquisition proceedings. Therefore the submission made by ld counsel for the plaintiffs regarding the validity of the acquisition proceedings has to be ignored and no issue regarding maintainability of the suit arises. The issue is accordingly deleted.

6. Issue no. 2 :- Whether the plaintiff has not come to the Court with clean hands having suppressed the material facts regarding the acquisition of the land in question in view of the Notification dated 13.11.1959 pursuant to which the land was handed over to the DDA on 20.08.1964 ? (OPD-2) (6.1) Defendant claims that the suit property is part of Khasra no. 2778/738 which was acquired vide award Ex.D2W1/1. It also Suit No. 109/13 Page 9 of 21 pages 10 claims that possession of the land was taken vide the possession proceedings (Kabza Karwahi) Ex.D2A1/2 and it was placed at the disposal of the defendant with other land under Section 22 of Delhi Development Act 1957 vide notification no. F.8(49)/63-L & H (II) dated 03.01.1968, copy of which is Ex.D2W1/3. To prove that the suit property is part of Khasra no. 2778/738, defendant relies upon the demarcation report Ex.D2W1/4. In the site plan Ex.D2W1/5 the suit property is shown as part of portion of land show in Purple hedged lines.

(6.2) Plaintiffs claim that the suit property is part of Kailash Park Colony. The notification under section 4 of the Act is Ex.PW4/A. At page 629 of Ex.PW4/A it is mentioned that the area covered by Tilak Nagar, Bali Nagar, Kailash Park and Lakshmi Garden is excluded. According to the plaintiffs, Ex.PW4/A makes it clear that the suit property was never acquired. Notification under section 6 of the LA Act has been exhibited by the plaintiffs as Ex.PW1/36. The relevant notification which the plaintiffs want to rely upon is at page 517 of Ex.PW1/36. It is pointed out by the plaintiffs that Khasra No. Suit No. 109/13 Page 10 of 21 pages 11 2778/738 is not mentioned in the specifications of the land under the notification. Validity of the demarcation report Ex.D2W1/4 is challenged by the plaintiffs. Correctness of the site plan Ex.D2W1/5 is disputed by the plaintiffs. The Kabza Karwahi is also disputed by submitting that Ex.D2W1/2 is prepared on a letter head of the year 1982 whereas the defendant claim that possession of the land was taken in the year 1964 and land was placed at the disposal of the defendant in the year 1968. It has also been argued by ld counsel for the plaintiff that the suit property falls between Kailash Park and Lakshmi Garden. The defendant has failed to explain why a small strip of land situated between two colonies would be acquired. (6.3) The demarcation report is Ex.D2W1/4. This demarcation was done in pursuance of the order passed by the Hon'ble High Court in the writ petition filed by the plaintiffs. It is mentioned in the report that the demarcation was carried out in presence of the ld counsel for the plaintiffs. Ld counsel for the plaintiffs raised the objection that Hon'ble High Court had directed to carry out the demarcation of plot no. 15A, 15B and 15C, Kailash Park and not of Suit No. 109/13 Page 11 of 21 pages 12 the Khasra number. In the suit the plaintiffs did not apply for fresh demarcation of the property and they have not led any evidence to challenge the correctness of the demarcation report Ex.D2W1/4. The demarcation report clearly mentions that the disputed piece of land falls in Khasra No. 2778/738 Basai Darapur, Delhi. Plaintiffs dispute the correctness of the site plan Ex.D2W1/5 and at the same time reliance is also placed on the site plan to argue that the defendant has failed to explain why a small strip of land between two colonies would be acquired.

(6.4) Plaintiffs have not mentioned the khasra number of the land in the plaint. They have also not mentioned the khasra number of Kailash Park colony. They have not disclosed the number of houses or residential/ commercial plots which existed in Kailash Park colony when the land was acquired. Plaintiffs have not explained that how and when property no. WZ-15C which comes after WZ 15, came into existence. Ld counsel for the plaintiffs submitted that the number of the property is admitted by the defendant. This is not true. The defendant always maintained that Suit No. 109/13 Page 12 of 21 pages 13 the suit property which plaintiffs call WZ-15C is not a part of Kailash Park Colony. In the demarcation report Ex.D2W1/4 also it is mentioned that in the plan of Kailash Park colony available with the defendant, properties no. WZ-15A, B and C did not exist. The demarcation report is accompanied by site plan which shows that the suit property is part of land falling between the two colonies. (6.5) Defendant has been successful in showing that the suit property falls in Khasra No. 2778/738 Basai Darapur which was acquired vide award Ex.D2W1/1. Ex.D2W1/1 mentions the notifications under Section 4 & 6 of the LA Act. The award shows that land falling in Khasra No. 2778/738 was acquired and this is sufficient to support the stand taken by the defendant. As discussed above, the validity of the acquisition proceedings is not to be decided by this court and therefore the argument that the land in khasra no. 2778/738 was not notified under Section 6 of the LA Act, can not be entertained. In the plaint reference to the notification under Section 6 of LA Act was made in relation to award no. 5/1964-65. Reference was not made in relation to award no.

Suit No. 109/13 Page 13 of 21 pages 14 1724 which covers Khasra no. 2778/738. This is another reason why the ground taken by the plaintiffs under Section 6 of the LA Act is not relevant because plaintiffs are relying upon a notification applicable to some other award. Question has also been raised about the authenticity of Ex.D2W1/2 on the ground that it is prepared on a letter head of the year 1982. Ex.D2W1/2 is a photocopy of the Kabza Karwahi and the document is written in Persian Script. The date of the proceeding is mentioned as 20.08.1964 against the signatures of the officer. Defendant has not explained how this document has been prepared on the letter head of the year 1982. However it is possible that the photocopy has been prepared by using a blank paper from the letter head of the year 1982. Plaintiffs have not been able to dispute the correctness or authenticity of the notification Ex.D2W1/3. If the land was placed at the disposal of the defendant vide notification Ex.D2W1/3, at least paper possession must have been taken before that. Once the award is made and Government claims that possession has been taken, plaintiffs have no locus standi to raise dispute Suit No. 109/13 Page 14 of 21 pages 15 regarding the Kabza Karwahi. The arguments put forward to assail the evidence adduced by the defendant have no merit. (6.6) In his cross examination dated 10.09.2009 at page 1, PW-1 had admitted that the correct khasra number of the suit property is 2778/738. The evidence of the other PWs examined by the plaintiffs are of no help to them to support the stand taken by them in the plaint. In many of the documents such as Ex.PW1/24, 1/25, 1/26, 1/27, 1/28, 1/29 and 1/33 the number of the property is mentioned as WZ-15 and not as WZ-15C. The other documents where the property is described as WZ-15C are not of such nature which can support the case of the plaintiff that the suit property is covered and protected by the notification dated 13.11.1959. The admission by PW-1 and the discussion above on the present issue shows that the plaintiffs withheld vital information regarding the suit property. The land on which Late Sh. Hazura Singh allegedly constructed the tin shed in the year 1960 must have belonged to somebody. The plaint is silent about the owner of the property. Plaintiffs have also not explained whether late Sh. Hazura Singh Suit No. 109/13 Page 15 of 21 pages 16 occupied the property as an owner or as a tress passer. They have not explained how and when WZ-15C became part of Kailash Park colony. They tried to project that the suit property was a part of Kailash Park Colony and exempted from the acquisition proceedings in view of the notification dated 13.11.1959 under Section 4 of the LA Act. Accordingly the issue is decided against the plaintiffs and it is held that they have not approached the court with clean hands.

7. Issue No. 3:- Whether the plaintiff is in legal possession of the suit land and the defendant no. 2 has no right over the property in question ? (OPP) (7.1) The discussion on issue no. 2 shows that possession of the plaintiffs over the suit land is not legal. Plaintiffs claim that they have been in possession of the property for more than 30 years and therefore defendant can not displace them. Copy of memo of parties of CW (P) No. 4580/2002 titled Hazura Singh Vs. DDA has been filed alongwith copy of order dated 29.04.2003 passed by Hon'ble High Court and they are collectively exhibited as mark A. Suit No. 109/13 Page 16 of 21 pages 17 The date of filing on the memo of parties is 27.07.2002. I shall examine the evidence to see whether the plaintiffs were in possession of the suit property for more than 30 years prior to filing of writ petition. If the plaintiffs are successful in proving this fact, I shall examine its effect.

(7.2) The documents exhibited in the statement of PW-1 as Ex.PW1/6 to Ex.PW1/34 have no value as PW-1 is not competent to prove these documents. Even some of these documents do not support the plaintiffs. For example, Ex.PW1/26 is an electricity bill issued for shop situated at WZ-15/GF. In this electricity bill the property is not described as WZ-15C. The discrepancy regarding the address in the documents has already been mentioned while deciding issue no. 2. Plaintiffs have examined PW-2 Sh. Trilok Singh who is brother of Late Sh. Hazura Singh. He has deposed that the suit property i.e WZ-15C, Kailash Park has been in the possession of Late Sh Hazura Singh since 1954-55. This statement does not match with the claim of the plaintiffs as it is stated in the plaint that late Sh. Hazura Singh came in possession of the Suit No. 109/13 Page 17 of 21 pages 18 property in the year 1960. Further the oral testimony of PW-2 has no value as it is not supported by documentary evidence. Plaintiffs have examined PW-3 from MTNL who has produced the record as Ex.PW3/A (colly). The telephone connection was installed in the year 1982 and the documents produced by PW-3 are of no help to the plaintiffs in proving that they have been in possession of the suit land for more than 30 years before filing of the writ petition in the year 2002. Plaintiffs have also examined PW-5 from NDPL. PW-5 has produced the record as Ex.PW5/A, 5/B and 5/C. In his examination in chief, he has stated that he had brought the summoned record pertaining to K No. 33100610712 and K No. 33100610713 installed in the name of Sh. Hazura Singh at premises bearing no. WZ-15C (shop) Kailash Park, Najafgarh road, New Delhi-15. In Ex.PW5/A which is dt 20.06.1972, only the name of the applicant (Sh. Hazura Singh) is mentioned and the address is not mentioned. In Ex.PW5/B, the address is mentioned as WZ-15/GF, Kaila, N. Delhi. It is not mentioned as WZ-15C. In Ex.PW5/C the address is mentioned as shop at WZ 1KC GF, Kaila, Suit No. 109/13 Page 18 of 21 pages 19 N. Delhi. It is again not mentioned as WZ-15C, Kailash Park colony. It appears that PW-5 deliberately mentioned the address as WZ-15C in his examination in chief to help the plaintiffs though the address was not mentioned as WZ-15C in the documents produced by him. Plaintiffs also summoned the record from the factory licensing department and department of labour but the said departments could not produce the record as it was not traceable. From the above discussion it is clear that plaintiffs have not been able to prove that they have been in possession of the suit property for more than 30 years before filing of the writ petition. The length of possession is also not important as there is no evidence of the possession being adverse to the defendant. The plaint lacks the necessary averments for adverse possession. Issue no. 3 is also decided against the plaintiff and it is held that plaintiffs have not been in legal possession of the suit property and they have no right over the suit property.

8. Issue No. 4:- Whether the plaintiff is entitled to the relief of declaration as asked for in the plaint ? (OPP) Suit No. 109/13 Page 19 of 21 pages 20 Plaintiffs have prayed that it be declared that the suit property i.e WZ-15C, Kailash Park, Najafgarh road, New Delhi which is part of Kailash Park has never been acquired and that it belongs to the plaintiffs. The discussion on issue no. 2 and 3 covers the present issue also. Plaintiffs have failed to prove that the suit property is part of Kailash Park colony and they are also not entitled to be declared its owner. Issue no. 4 is also decided against the plaintiffs.

9. Issue No. 5:- Whether the plaintiff is entitled to the relief of permanent and mandatory injunction as asked for in the plaint ? (OPP) and Issue No. 6:- Whether the plaintiff is entitled to the relief of damages as asked for in the plaint ? (OPP) In view of the decision on issue no. 2 to 4, plaintiffs are not entitled to the relief for permanent and mandatory injunction and they are also not entitled to any amount as damages. Issue no. 5 and 6 are also decided against the plaintiffs.
