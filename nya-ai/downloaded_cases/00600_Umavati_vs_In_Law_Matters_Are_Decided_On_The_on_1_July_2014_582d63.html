2. Petitioner Smt. 10. Before appreciating the evidence on record, I must mention here the contents of the said provision which is as follows:

11. Section 14(1)(b)DRC Act says that an order of recovery of possession of the premises can be made by the controller if the tenant has, on or after the 9th day of June, 1952, sublet, assigned or otherwise parted with the possession of the whole or any part of the premises without obtaining the consent in writing of the landlord.

12. As per the aforesaid provision the landlord is entitled to eviction on the ground of subletting if he establishes (1) that the tenant has sublet or assigned or parted with the possession of the whole or any part of premises and (2) that such subletting or assigning or parting with the possession has been done without the consent in writing of the landlord. But once the landlord proves that the subtenant is in exclusive possession of the suit premises , the onus shifts to the tenant to prove that it was not a case of subletting. Reliance in this regard is made on the case Vaishakhi Ram and others Vs Sanjeev kumar bhatani (2008) 14 SCC

356.

13. In the present case , Petitioner had alleged in the petition that the shop in question were rented out to respondent who in turn was doing the business under the name and style of M/s. Capital Tailors and Drapers. As per petitioner, the said business is no more in existence and instead of the same, respondent had sublet, assigned or parted with the possession of shop in question to a sub tenant and it is the sub tenant who is doing business under the name and style of M/s. Capital Telecom and M/s. Hari Om Communications. In addition to the said change in the user of the shop in question, petitioner alleged that one person namely Sunil is also running the business of flowers and bouquets in the shop in question. No consent in oral and writing Page no. 6 Umawati Vs. H.R. Maini E No. 56/09/05 had been obtained by the respondent while subletting the premises and therefore she alleged that the respondent must be evicted out in the premises on the said ground. 14 Petitioner examined herself as PW­2 and deposed the aforesaid facts on oath in her evidence by way of affidavit. Respondent did not cross examine petitioner on the aforesaid aspect. There was no suggestion to the effect that shop in question was not subletted, parted or assigned to any sub tenant by respondent. There was no suggestion that shop in question are still in the possession of respondent. In his written statement respondent has stated that the earlier business of respondent under the name and style of M/s. Capital Tailors and drapers is no more in existence and instead of the said business, respondent had started doing the business of ladies boutique and business relating to Tele Communications under different names and styles in the tenanted premises. Respondent had not suggested the said version to the petitioner, while cross examining her. Respondent had also not stated in his written statement that the factum of change of business was told by him to the petitioner orally or in writing or by some other means. He asserted that he had not subletted or parted with possession of shop in question to any sub tenants. As per respondent, he along with his younger brother is continuously and without interruption is in possession of the tenanted premises. Respondent during cross examining the petitioner has also not put the said version to the petitioner for checking its veracity. The net result was from the pleadings on record and from the evidence on record, I found that petitioner had no occasion or means to know the alleged change of business of respondent, if version of respondent is believed for a moment. It was never the case of respondent that he had informed the petitioner about the change of business from M/s. Therefore petitioner was within her right to observe, find and then claim the eviction of Page no. 7 Umawati Vs. H.R. 15. In his written statement, respondent had stated that he had put two shutters in the shop in question for the purpose of safety and had admitted the fact that he had put a kacha wooden partition, for managing his two businesses, as mentioned by me in my preceding paragraphs. The said version, at the very outset was never put to the petitioner while cross examining her. Again it was never the case of respondent that he had apprised the petitioner about the said change in the shop in question. In such circumstances when respondent himself had admitted the factum of closure of earlier business, which was known to the petitioner, it was his responsibility to have at least apprise the landlady about the commencement of two businesses, admitted by him. In the absence of any such communications, landlady/petitioner rightly found that the shop in question were sublet, parted with or assigned by the respondent to some sub tenant. Even otherwise, under normal circumstances, landlady could not have known the internal arrangement of respondent and his family members with regard to change of business in shop in question. Based on the pleadings on record and the evidence of petitioner, I found that the case of petitioner with regard to subletting was partly admitted by respondent and partly was not challenged by him. There was no evidence to the effect that shop in question was still occupied by respondent and his family members, as on date. There was no evidence to the effect that respondent was running the two businesses alleged by him. There was no evidence to the effect that brother of respondent was assisting respondent in running the businesses. There was no evidence to the effect that petitioner knew about the said change of business. There was no evidence to the effect that respondent was running business of ladies boutique under the Page no. 8 Umawati Vs. H.R. Maini E No. 56/09/05 name and style of "F. Capital" as alleged by the respondent. Further there was no evidence on record to the effect that a person namely Sunil was running the business of flowers and bouquets in shop in question. Therefore petitioner was able to discharge her initial onus of proving the case of subletting in the background of admission by respondent and respondent was not able to prove his case, by way of probabilities or evidence. Hence case of petitioner stands proved U/s. 14(1)(b)DRC Act.

H.R. Maini E No. 56/09/05 challenge the manner in which petitioner had obtained the said document, during trial. He did not raise the said argument by way of suggestion to petitioner during trial. Further, as per respondent, Ex.PW2/R1 required registration U/s. 17 of Registration Act. Section 17 of Registration Act, talks about those documents which should be registered. It says that all instruments of gifts of immovable property, other non testamentary instruments which proposed or operate to create, declare, assign, limit or extinguish any right, title or interest, in present or future, whether vested or contingent, of the value of Rs. 100/­ or upwards, to or in immovable property or which acknowledge the receipt of payment of any consideration on account of the creation, declaration, assignment, limitation or extinction of any such right, title or interest, among other documents(not relevant for our purpose), requires compulsory registration. In this case, document Ex.PW2/R1 did not require any registration as this is not a case of ownership of premises in question. Further the argument that petitioner failed to show specific position of shop in question being sublet was a farce as respondent himself had admitted that two new businesses were being run after closure of earlier businesses. Onus was on respondent to have raised that argument during trial. It was an afterthought. It was discarded by me.

23. Therefore, based on aforesaid appreciation of evidence before me, I found that petitioner was able to prove his case U/s. 14(1)(b) of DRC Act.

24. Section 14(1)(j)DRC Act says that the tenant has, whether before or after the commencement of this Act, caused or permitted to be caused substantial damage to the premises.

25. Justice Mehmood Ali Khan of Hon'ble Delhi High Court summarized the law on this point in case titled as Suraj Prakash Chopra Raj Kumar Vs. Baijnath Dhawan & Ors. 103(2003) DLT 645. He observed that

i) The onus of proving that the tenant has caused substantial damage to the demise premises is upon the landlord.

ii) Landlord must prove that addition and alteration in the tenancy premises is carried out by the tenant.

iii) Tenant has made the construction without the consent of landlord.

Page no. 11 Umawati Vs. H.R. Maini E No. 56/09/05

iv) The said construction has materially effected the tenancy premises and further that the construction which had been carried out by the tenant had materially altered the premises.

v) Court must determine the nature, character of the construction and the extent to which they make changes in the structure of the premises having regard to the purpose for which the premises have been let out.

vi) Landlord has to prove it by cogent evidence and wherever necessary expert witness should be examined.

vii) An eviction order under clause(i) could be passed if the tenant has carried out such additions or alterations and structural changes in the tenancy premises which had brought about material impairment in the value and value utility of premises.

viii) Every construction or alteration does not impair the value and utility of the building and hat construction must be of material nature which should substantially diminish the value of the building either from commercial and monetary point of view o from utilization aspect of the building.

ix) A temporary alteration o addition which can be easily repaired without causing damage to the structure is not substantial damage to the tenancy premises.

x) Every change, addition or alteration in the tenancy premises will not invite eviction of the tenant under clause (I) and that each case would depend upon its own facts; and Page no. 12 Umawati Vs. H.R. Maini E No. 56/09/05

xi) The impairment of the value and utility of the building is to be seen from the point of view of the landlord and not tenant.

26. Case of petitioner failed with regard to aforesaid ground of eviction as there was no evidence with regard to the substantial damage in the shop in question, which had occurred due to any act of respondent. There remained the mere bald assertion to the effect that tenant had caused substantial damage to the shop in question but in the absence of any evidence in support thereof, I did not found the said version of petitioner proved.

27. Section 14(1)(k)DRC Act says that the Controller can order for eviction of tenant if the tenant has, notwithstanding previous notice, used or dealt with the premises in a manner contrary to any condition imposed on the landlord by the Government of the Delhi Development Authority or the Municipal Corporation of Delhi while giving him a lease of the land on which the premises are situate.

Purpose of enacting clause(k) :­ On a plain reading of clause (k) of the proviso to section 14(1) it is clear that the stress is laid on the conduct of the tenant, who has misused or dealt with the premises in a manner contrary to any condition imposed on the landlord by the Government while giving him the lease of the land on which the premises are situated. The provision in clause (k)is intended to protect the interest of the landlord who may face termination of the lease and lose the property for breach of conditions imposed by the superior lessor on him while granting the lease of the land. Faced with such situation the landlord is given the right to move the Controller for eviction of the tenant and for recovery of possession of the premises so that he may be saved from the consequences of misuser of the premises and breach of conditions lf lease. An opportunity is provided to the tenant to protect himself against Page no. 13 Umawati Vs. H.R. Maini E No. 56/09/05 threatened eviction from the premises if he complies with the condition imposed on the landlord by any of the authorities referred to in clause(k) of the proviso to section 14(1) and pays to that authority such amount by way of compensation as the Controller may direct. Provision for the purpose is made in sub­section (11) of section 14 of the Act. Neither of the aforementioned two statutory provisions mentions execution of a document of lease in any particular form. The stress is o n compliance with the conditions subject to which lease of the property was given to the lessee who is the landlord of the tenant in occupation of the premises.

The policy of the legislature in enacting clause (k) seems to be to put an end to unauthorized use of the leased lands rather than merely to enable the authorities to get back possession of the leased lands. This conclusion is fortified by a reference to sub­section (11) of section 14. The lease is not forfeited merely because the building put upon the leased land is put to an unauthorized use. The tenant is given an opportunity to comply with the conditions imposed on the landlord by any of the authorities referred to in clause (k). As long as the condition imposed is complied with there is no forfeiture. It then enables the Controller to direct compensation to be paid to the authority for a breach of the condition. The anxiety of the legislature is to prevent unauthorized user rather than protection of the tenant or strengthening of the hands of the authorities referred to in clause (k) in effecting forfeiture.

Clause (k) has been enacted to serve the purpose of the Government and not of the landlord at all. It is because the Government is not a party to the contact between the landlord and the tenant that the statutory remedy had to be provided by clause (k). Where the landlord lets out the premises for a purpose contrary to the terms of the lease granted to him and then seeks eviction under clause (k), in such a case he does not act to serve any interest of his. His interest is, on the contrary, the same as that of the tenant. For the landlord has already agreed Page no. 14 Umawati Vs. H.R. Maini E No. 56/09/05 for the user of the premises by the tenant and would like to continue the lease because he is getting rent. But such an agreement between the landlord and the tenant is irrelevant inasmuch as it is the interest of the Government which is to be safeguarded under clause (k).

28. The legislature in his own wisdom has introduced clause 14(1)(k) DRC Act, taking note of the fact that enormous extent of land has been given on lease by three authorities i.e. the Government of India or Delhi Development Authority or the Municipal Corporation of Delhi. While creating lease, these authorities have put certain conditions about the user of the premises so leased out but are violated by the lessee or his tenant and sometime by both of them. To meet out this exigency, this clause has been introduced to safeguard the interest of the Government and to stop the misuser of the premises. The landlord in order to bring his case under this clause has to prove that the tenant has violated any condition of the lease executed by the superior lessor in favour of the landlord and secondly despite notice has not stopped the misuser.

29. It was the case of petitioner that respondent had raised unauthorized construction of 277 sq. ft. in the shop in question in violation of the terms of lease as per lease deed Ex.PW1/1 between the superior lessor and the petitioner. Regarding the said unauthorized construction petitioner had called upon respondent to remove the said construction regarding which respondent had given an undertaking that he would do the needful and would be responsible for any liability, expenses, damages, penalty if any levied on that account. That despite intimation and notice about the said unauthorized construction, respondent had not removed the said construction and respondent must be evicted out from the shop in question.

30. Petitioner in her evidence as PW­2 had reiterated the said version. So far as unauthorized Page no. 15 Umawati Vs. H.R. Maini E No. 56/09/05 construction in the shop in question was concerned, PW­1 Smt. The said reports categorically mentioned the factum of unauthorized construction in the shop in question. Further notice of the said unauthorized construction was sent to petitioner by L & DO office vide letter Ex.PW1/2A and Ex.PW1/7. That completely proves two facts viz. that there was unauthorized construction in the shop in question and subsequently petitioner was notified by the L & DO office about the said unauthorized construction.

31. Petitioner on her part had served respondent with notice dated 08.11.2004, Ex.PW2/4 in which she had apprised about the unauthorized construction in the shop in question to the respondent and had also requested respondent to remove the said unauthorized construction of 277 Sq. ft. She had sent the said notice through Post and Registered Envelope. She had also sent the said notice through Courier and UPC. Envelope with AD Card and Postal receipts Ex.PW2/5, Receipt of Courier Ex.PW2/6, Receipt of UPC Ex.PW2/7 corroborated the version of petitioner that the said notice was duly sent by her to the respondent. Section 114 Illustration E of Notice Evidence Act says that Court may presume unless rebutted factum that official acts are regularly performed. Therefore aforesaid notice which was sent to respondent through various official notes was presumed by me to be duly served upon respondent. More so where respondent did not challenge the said version of petitioner. The net result was that respondent was duly informed about alleged unauthorized construction in the shop in question by the petitioner. If that was the case, then respondent should have stopped using the shop in question, immediately. They continuously used it and therefore breached the directions of the superior lessor, within the domain of aforesaid provision of eviction of tenant.

32. Another noteworthy fact mentioned in the evidence of the petitioner was that after receiving the aforesaid notice, respondent had given an undertaking that she would do the needful and would be responsible for any liability, expenses, damages or penalty if levied by L & DO office on that account. Respondent did not controvert the said deposition of the petitioner. I had no occasion to disbelieve the said version of petitioner. Therefore I found that respondent not only knew about the alleged unauthorized construction in the shop in question but also had undertaken to pay the penalty in case the same arise in future, by the L & DO office. By not removing the said unauthorized construction and continuously using the shop in question, he had used the premises in a manner contrary to the condition imposed by the superior lessor.

33. Stand of respondent qua the unauthorized construction was not clear from the pleadings. In his written statement respondent had replied that there was no unauthorized construction in the premises is on. In the same breath he had also stated that the said unauthorized constructed loft was present since the inception of his tenancy and was compoundable and condonable, as per several circulars issued in this regard. So he admitted and denied presence of loft, as alleged by petitioner and as alleged by PW­1 and PW­2 in their testimonies. Said confusing stand of respondent only wakened the case of respondent qua the presence of unauthorized construction in premises in question.

34. Moving further, respondent did not reply in clear unequivocal terms as to what were the circulars, based on which he was saying that the said alleged unauthorized construction was compoundable and condonable. He failed to reply as to why he did not remove the said alleged unauthorized construction despite knowing it to be so, though it was constructed by petitioner, Page no. 17 Umawati Vs. H.R. Maini E No. 56/09/05 so to say. He failed to mention as to what bonafide steps he had took to remove the said alleged unauthorized construction despite knowing that the same was in existence since the date of inception of his tenancy. Respondent in my view played a very safe game. He continued to be the said unauthorized construction despite knowing or in ignorance, that it was so. But when the was given notice of the removal of the said structure, he shirked away from his responsibility of paying requisite compensation/penalty to L & DO office. He rather tried to shift the onus of paying the said amount on petitioner thought it was he who was using premises in question for his personal gain. Aim of the aforesaid provision, as stated above is to put an end to unauthorized use of the leased premises rather than merely to enable the authorities to get back the possession of the leased premises. So blaming petitioner, was not the proper cause, for the respondent.

35. The argument that petitioner was estopped from raising the ground of eviction under clause(k), for the reason that the said unauthorized construction was present from the day of tenancy inception was not a plausible ground as their cannot be any estoppel against statute. Moreover landlord is not barred from raising the said ground for eviction even if he had leased out premises, which were contrary to the condition imposed by superior lesser. Reliance in this regard is made on Rashida Begum VS. General Sales Ltd. (2002) 6 SCC 60 and Faquir Chand Vs. Ram Rattan Bhanot, 1973 RCR 221.

36. Respondent agreed that petitioner was not able to prove the status of unauthorized construction in the premises in question. He agreed that all the inspection reports laked about loft which as per testimony of PW­1, was removed by 06.06.1983, when inspection was carried Page no. 18 Umawati Vs. H.R. Maini E No. 56/09/05 out by officials of L & DO. The said arguments were not tenable. So far as unauthorized construction was concerned, I found that there were inspection reports placed on record, which were prepared by L & DO clarified in her testimony that description of said unauthorized construction were not mentioned in the letters used by his department. She was a witness to the record . She was not the person who had inspected the premises or had prepared the reports. So she was within her rights and capability to have spoken so. It was not expected of her to have given a detailed account of unauthorized construction in premises in question. Petitioner had examined her so as to prove the inspection reports prepared by official of L & DO office. She had proved the said reports by bringing original record and lacing on record their photocopies. Respondent on his part had not objected to the said reports also. Further their was presumption U/s. 114 Illustration (E) in favour of petitioner to the effect that the said reports were prepared in ordinary official course. The net result was that based on the said inspection reports concluded the presence of unauthorized construction in premises in question. Even otherwise respondent had admitted presence of the said construction since inception of his tenancy.
