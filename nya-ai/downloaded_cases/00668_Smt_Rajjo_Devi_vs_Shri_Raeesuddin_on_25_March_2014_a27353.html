                                     J U D G M E N T

1. Plaintiff has averred that she is the owner of a property described as plot measuring 200 sq. yards bearing no. 250, Pushta Road, Karawal Nagar, Delhi-110094 (hereinafter referred to as the suit property) in which the defendant is her tenant at a monthly rent of Rs.12,000/-, excluding electricity and other charges for the past many years. It is averred that a rent agreement between the parties was executed only on Civil Suit No. 125/2009 Page 1/31 14.11.2006. Plaintiff has averred that the defendant is using the suit premises for commercial purposes and is in the business of manufacture and repair of picture tubes of televisions as well as repair and cleaning of used picture tubes. Plaintiff has averred that the defendant defaulted tendering of rent since December, 2006. Plaintiff has averred that the defendant expressed his inability to pay the arrears due to losses, illness of relatives and several other fictitious grounds but since the husband of the plaintiff was a friend of the defendant, no pressure was exerted on him to make payment of arrears.

2. Plaintiff has averred that she came to know that the defendant had not paid electricity bills of the suit premises which he was using through commercial meter no. 125108060027 having a sanctioned load of 12KW. Plaintiff has averred that the said electricity meter was disconnected and meter removed by M/s BSES, Yamuna Power Ltd. due to non-payment of arrears. Plaintiff has averred that after disconnection of electricity, the defendant started using a big electricity generator to generate electricity causing nuisance and became a health hazard for the locality. Plaintiff has averred that she asked the defendant several times to stop using the generator but he did not refrain from doing so and she lodged a complaint with the SDM against defendant under Section 145 of the Code of Criminal Procedure.

3. Plaintiff has averred that when despite repeated reminders, defendant failed to pay arrears of rent and electricity she served a legal notice dated 21.01.2009 upon him asking him to pay the same. It is averred that instead of complying with the terms of the said notice, brother of the defendant Sh. Hakimuddin sent a notice to the plaintiff alleging that he himself was the tenant of the plaintiff in the suit property at a monthly rent of Rs.3,000/- and alleged that the plaintiff was not issuing any rent receipt to him. The said notice was replied by the plaintiff through reply dated 05.02.2009 denying that Sh. Hakimuddin was her tenant in the suit property.

4. Plaintiff has averred that Sh. Hakimuddin being brother of the defendant was working with the defendant in the suit property and he himself had signed as a witness to the rent agreement dated 14.11.2006 executed between the plaintiff and the defendant. Plaintiff has averred that through another notice dated 05.02.2009, she Civil Suit No. 125/2009 Page 2/31 called upon the defendant to pay the arrears of dues failing which the tenancy would be terminated w.e.f. 28.02.2009 and defendant would be liable to hand over peaceful possession of the suit property to the plaintiff. Plaintiff has averred that the defendant failed to comply with the terms of the said notice and has become liable to be ejected from the suit property and to pay arrears of electricity dues. Plaintiff further averred that the defendant is liable to pay damages for the use and occupation of the suit property after the termination of the tenancy at the rate of Rs.25,000/- per month till the date of receiving possession of the suit property. In these circumstances, plaintiff has filed the present suit praying for reliefs as under:-

"a) to pass a decree of ejectment of the defendant from the premises bearing plot no. 250, Pushta Road, Karawal Nagar, Delhi as shown Red Colour in the site plan annexed with this suit;
b) to pass a decree of Mandatory Injunction issuing mandate to the defendant to pay the entire electricity bill to the BSES Yamuna Power Ld. and to get restored the electricity connection in the premises bearing Plot No. 250, Pushta Road, Karawal Nagar, Delhi-110094;
c) to pass a decree of Injunction restraining the defendants, his agents, servant, colleagues, attorneys etc. from creating any third party interest in the premises in suit bearing Plot No. 250, Pushta Road, Karawal Nagar, Delhi;
d) to pass a decree of arrears of rent to the tunes of Rs.3,24,000/- rent from the month of December, 2006 to February, 2009 @ Rs.12,000/- per month in respect of the premises in suit bearing plot no. 250, Pushta Road, Karawal Nagar, Delhi;
e) to pass a decree of mesne profits for the use and occupation by the defendant in respect of the suit premises @ Rs.25,000/- per month; in favour of the plaintiff and against the defendant in the interest of justice;
f) to pass such or further order or directions which this Hon'ble Court deems fit and proper under the above mentioned facts and circumstances of the case in favour of the plaintiff alongwith costs."

5. Upon summons of the suit being issued, the defendant filed his written statement raising preliminary objections to the effect that the suit was bad for mis- joinder of parties there was no relationship of landlord and tenant between them; that Civil Suit No. 125/2009 Page 3/31 the suit has been filed against a wrong person only to put pressure on the actual tenant who is brother of the defendant; no cause of action has arisen against the defendant and in favour of the plaintiff; that the defendant has never been in possession of the suit property. On merits, the defendant denied that he is a tenant of the plaintiff or that he is in occupation of the suit property. Defendant averred that his brother Sh. Hakimuddin is the real tenant of the defendant and there is a dispute between them. Defendant has averred that he was called by them to settle their disputes and after the settlement was reached, signature of the defendant was taken on a blank paper for the purposes of reducing in writing the terms of the settlement upon which signature of the defendant would be used as a witness. Defendant has averred that he signed the said blank paper as he was in a hurry to leave for some urgent work and was not in a position to remain on the spot. Defendant has denied that he had any agreement with the plaintiff. Defendant has denied that he is in the business of manufacturing and repairing of picture tubes of televisions and has averred that the same is the business of his brother Sh. Hakimuddin.

6. On the pleadings of the parties, by order dated 10.09.2010, the following issues were framed:-

"1. Whether there is a relationship of landlord and tenant between the parties? (OPP)
2. Whether the plaintiff is entitled to possession of the suit premises? (OPP)
3. Whether the plaintiff is entitled to mesne profits against the defendant? If so, at what rate and for what period? (OPP)
4. Whether the plaintiff is entitled to recover any arrear rent from the defendant? If so, at what rate and for what period? (OPP)
5. Whether the plaintiff is entitled to mandatory injunction as prayed for? (OPP)
6. Whether the plaintiff is entitled to permanent injunction as prayed for? (OPP)
7. Relief."

48. It is, however, to be seen that the term of the rent agreement Ex.PW1/6 is for period of three years, that is more than one year. As per Section 107 of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882, a lease for a period in excess of one year can only be effected by a registered agreement. The rent agreement Ext.PW1/6 is unregistered. Section 49 of the Registration Act, 1908 lays down the effect of non-registration of documents which are compulsorily required to be registered under the Registration Act or under the provisions of the Transfer of Property Act. The relevant portion of Section 49 reads as under:-

"49. Effect of non-registration of documents required to be registered.--No document required by Section 17 [or by any provision of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882 (4 of 1882),] to be registered shall--

(a) affect any immovable property comprised therein, or
(b) confer any power to adopt, or
(c) be received as evidence of any transaction affecting such property or conferring such power, unless it has been registered:
Civil Suit No. 125/2009 Page 20/31
Provided that an unregistered document affecting immovable property and required by this Act or the Transfer of Property Act, 1882 (4 of 1882), to be registered may be received as evidence of a contract in a suit for specific performance under Chapter II of the Specific Relief Act, 1877 (1 of 1877), or as evidence of any collateral transaction not required to be effected by registered instrument."
49. Section 49 of the Registration Act thus provides that a document which is compulsorily required to be registered but it is so registered will not affect any immovable property which is subject matter of such document nor can the same be received in evidence of any transaction affecting such property. The provisio to Section 49 lays down that such document however can be received as evidence of any collateral transaction not required to be effected by such registered instrument.

The provision of Section 49 of Registration Act have been interpreted by the Hon'ble Supreme Court in a catena of judgments. In the case of K.B. Saha & Sons (P) Ltd.v. Development Consultant Ltd., reported in (2008) 8 SCC 564 the Hon'ble Supreme Court was pleased to hold as under:-

"34. From the principles laid down in the various decisions of this Court and the High Courts, as referred to hereinabove, it is evidence that:
1. A document required to be registered, if unregistered is not admissible into evidence under Section 49 of the Registration Act.
2. Such unregistered document can however be used as an evidence of collateral purpose as provided in the proviso to Section 49 of the Registration Act.
3. A collateral transaction must be independent of, or divisible from, the transaction to effect which the law required registration.
4. A collateral transaction must be a transaction not itself required to be effected by a registered document, that is, a transaction creating, etc. any right, title or interest in immovable property of the value of one hundred rupees and upwards.
5. If a document is inadmissible in evidence for want of registration, none of its terms can be admitted in evidence and that to use a document for the purpose of proving an important clause would not be using it as a collateral purpose.
35. In our view, the particular clause in the lease agreement in question cannot be called a collateral purpose. As noted earlier, it is the case of the appellant that the suit premises were let out only for the particular Civil Suit No. 125/2009 Page 21/31 named officer of the respondent and accordingly, after the same was vacated by the said officer, the respondent was not entitled to allot it to any other employee and was therefore, liable to be evicted which, in our view, was an important term forming part of the lease agreement.

Therefore, such a clause, namely, Clause 9 of the lease agreement in this case, cannot be looked into even for collateral purposes to come to a conclusion that the respondent was liable to be evicted because of violation of Clause 9 of the lease agreement. That being the position, we are unable to hold that Clause 9 of the lease agreement, which is admittedly unregistered, can be looked into for the purpose of evicting the respondent from the suit premises only because the respondent was not entitled to induct any other person other than the named officer in the same."

50. Thus, it is clear that a document required to be registered, if unregistered is not admissible in evidence. Such unregistered document can be used as an evidence of a collateral transaction. However such collateral transaction must be independent from the transaction for which the law required registration. The collateral transaction must be such which by itself does not require to be effected by a registered instrument. A particular clause in a lease which requires registration cannot be looked into by way of collateral purpose.

51. Ex.PW1/6, the lease dated 14.11.2006 entered into between the plaintiff and the defendant is for a period of more than one year. The same was required to be registered under the provisions of Section 106 of the Transfer of Property Act. Since, it is unregistered, the bar of Section 49 of the Registration Act comes into play and none of the clauses of the said lease can be looked into for any purpose to ascertain as to whether the relationship between the parties was that of landlord and tenant or the rate of rent or the period of the tenancy. Although the execution of the rent agreements Ex.DW2/1 and Ex.DW2/2 between the plaintiff and Sh. Hakimuddin has been disbelieved, it is also to be seen that both these alleged rent agreements are for a period in excess of one year. Both are unregistered. Hence, bar of Section 49 of the Registration Act is also attracted to both these agreements.

61. Question now arises is as to the course to be adopted for determining the rate of rent of the suit property. Plaintiff has averred in her plaint that the suit property is measures 200 sq. yards and was being used for commercial purposes by the defendant. There is no dispute raised by the defendant regarding the size of the suit property i.e. it being 200 sq. yards. There is also no dispute that the suit property was being used for commercial purposes as Ex.PW4/4, the business verification report reveals that the defendant himself represented to M/s Axis Bank that he was running the business of manufacture of picture tubes under the name and style of M/s Malik Picture Tube from the suit property and that the covered area available with the defendant was in excess of Rs.2600/- sq. feet.

62. In the case of M.C. Aggarwal (HUF) Vs. Sahara India reported in 183 (2011) DLT 105, the Hon'ble High Court of Delhi was considering a question as to the rate of mesne profits / damages to which a plaintiff would be entitled in a case of termination of tenancy where such plaintiff has been unable to prove the market rate of rent of similar properties in the locality. The Hon'ble High Court was pleased to hold that ordinarily a plaintiff would be required to prove the market rate of rent by leading evidence regarding rent of similar properties in the locality. However, in the absence of the same, it was held that the Court could resort to the provisions of Section 57 and 114 of the Indian Evidence Act grant appropriate relief.

63. As per the evidence by way of affidavit filed by the defendant, the rate of rent of Civil Suit No. 125/2009 Page 26/31 the suit property was Rs.3,000/- in the year 2005. The same was disputed by the plaintiff who claimed that the rate of rent was Rs.12,000/-. The suit property measures 200 sq. yards / 2600 sq.ft. and is being used for commercial purposes. Even by applying the rate of rent of Rs.4/- per sq. feet, the monthly rent would come to Rs.11,200/-. One has to note that the tenancy was of the year 2006. Even then it is inconceivable that such a large commercial property would be available on rent at the rate of Rs.3,000/- per month. In the opinion of this Court, it would, therefore be safe to presume that the rate of rent of the suit property in the year 2006 would be Rs.7,500/- per month to say the least.

64. Plaintiff in his plaint has claimed that the defendant was in arrears of rent w.e.f.

December, 2006 at the rate of Rs.12,000/- per month and has claimed a decree for recovery of Rs.3,24,000/- being arrears of rent w.e.f. December, 2006 to February, 2009 i.e. till filing of this suit. Rate of rent of the suit property has already been assessed by this Court to be Rs.7,500/- per month. Question which arises is whether the defendant is in arrears of rent w.e.f. December, 2006.

65. The case of the defendant was of absolute denial. He claimed that he was not a tenant in the suit property and therefore question of being in arrears of rent did not arise. Defendant has, therefore, not led any evidence to prove that he had paid any rent to the plaintiff. However DW-2 Sh. Hakimuddin, brother of the defendant had deposed that he had deposited rent in the Court of ARC for the months of January and February, 2009 amounting to Rs.3,000/-. In his cross-examination, he deposed that he had deposited rent in the year 2009 for a few months. Since there is no relationship of landlord and tenant between the DW-2 and plaintiff, the amount deposited by the DW-2 in the Court of ARC cannot be taken to be the payment of rent paid by the defendant to the plaintiff.

66. Thus in the absence of any evidence being led by the defendant in this regard, this Court has no option but to presume that the defendant was in arrears of rent from December, 2006. The plaintiff will, therefore, be entitled to recover of arrears of rent at the rate of Rs.7,500/- per month w.e.f. December, 2006 till February, 2009.

67. The next question which arises now is that the plaintiff is entitled to recover Civil Suit No. 125/2009 Page 27/31 damages / mesne profits at the rate of Rs.25,000/- per month w.e.f. 01.03.2009 till the date of recovery of the possession of the suit property. The plaintiff has not produced any evidence of rate of rent of similar properties in the locality to prove that the market rate of rent of the suit property would be Rs.25,000/- per month. Rate of rent of the suit property has been assessed to be Rs.7,500/- per month w.e.f. December, 2006 to February, 2009. Since the premises are commercial in nature, I hold that the plaintiff will be entitled to damages / use and occupation charges at the rate of Rs.7,500/- + 10% per month for the first year of illegal occupation between 01.03.2009 to 27.02.2010; thereafter additional damages at the rate of 10% over and above the first year of illegal possession for the period of 01.03.2010 to 28.02.2011 and so on till such time the plaintiff gains possession of the suit property. Issues 3 and 4 are decided accordingly.

Ex.PW1/7 is the original electricity bill for the month of March, 2009 and payable by 20.03.2009. As per the same the amount payable was Rs.91,736/-. After the due date the said amount came to Rs.92,950/- with LPSC charges. The plaintiff had also deposed that after the disconnection of the electricity supply the defendant was carrying on his business with help of electricity generator. The defendant in his written statement had denied Civil Suit No. 125/2009 Page 28/31 relationship of landlord and tenant and also liability to bear electricity dues on the same ground. In his evidence by way of affidavit Ex.D1/1, the defendant has deposed in para no. 5 that electricity supply in the premises was disconnected because the plaintiff despite collecting electricity charges along with the rent did not deposit the same with the electricity department. He further deposed that on account of such disconnection his brother Sh. Hakimuddin was carrying on his business with the help of an electricity generator.

69. Thus, in view of the contents of the evidence by way of affidavit it stands admitted by the defendant that electricity connection was existing in the premises which were disconnected on account of non-payment of electricity dues and as a result of which his brother Sh. Hakimuddin was running his business with the help of electricity generators. The defence of the defendant that he was not a tenant in the suit property has already been disbelieved and it has been held that he was the tenant in the suit property. By virtue of Ex.PW4/4, the business verification report, it stands established that it was the defendant who was carrying on commercial activity from the suit property under the name and style of M/s Malik Picture Tube. Since the defendant was the tenant in the suit property and was carrying on business therein, it was he who used the electricity supply to the suit property through connection bearing no. 125108060027. Liability to bear arrears of electricity dues during the period of tenancy would be on the defendant.

70. Defendant had contended that the plaintiff while collecting rent had also collected electricity charges but the same were not deposited by her leading to disconnection of the electricity supply. It has already been found that the defendant was in arrears of rent w.e.f. December, 2006. Thus, he would also have not paid any electricity dues since then. Defendant has not led any evidence to prove that he paid any electricity dues w.e.f. December, 2006 to the plaintiff which were not deposited by her. As per Ex.PW1/7, i.e. the electricity bill for the month of March, 2009, the current dues were Rs.1561 whereas the arrears were Rs.90,174/-. The last payment receipt against the connection was for Rs.74,000/- on 26.12.2008. Thus, since 26.12.2008 no further payment had been made. Therefore, the defendant having consumed Civil Suit No. 125/2009 Page 29/31 electricity and not having paid its charges would be liable to bear the same. Defendant has not denied the electricity bill Ex.PW1/7. He is therefore liable to pay the arrears of electricity shown in the bill Ex.PW1/7 amounting to Rs.91,736/- along with LPSC accrued on the same till date and is also liable to get the electricity connection restored against connection no. 125108060027 by completing all necessary formalities with M/s BSES Yamuna Power Ltd. in accordance with their rules and regulations. Issue no. 5 is decided accordingly.

ISSUE NO.6: Whether the plaintiff is entitled to permanent injunction as prayed for? (OPP)

71. The plaintiff had prayed for a decree of permanent injunction to restrain the defendant from creating any third party interest in the suit property. Title of the plaintiff in the suit property is not in dispute. The relationship of landlord and tenant stands established. Termination of the tenancy of the defendant has been effected by the plaintiff. The defendant will therefore have no right to create any third party interest in the suit property. Plaintiff is, therefore, entitled to this relief. RELIEF

72. For the reasons recorded above, the suit of the plaintiff is decreed in the following manner:-

(i) The plaintiff is granted a decree for ejectment of the defendant from the suit property described as plot no. 250, Pusta Road, Karawal Nagar, Delhi as shown in red in the site plan Ex.PW1/8;
(ii) The plaintiff is granted a decree for mandatory injunction by which the defendant is directed to pay the electricity arrears amounting to Rs.91,736/- as shown in Ex.PW1/7 along with LPSC up to date and also to get the electricity connection against meter no. 125108060027 in the suit property restored after completing all necessary commercial formalities as required by M/s BSES Yamuna Power Ltd.;
(iii) The plaintiff is granted a decree for permanent injunction restraining the defendant from creating third party interest in the suit property in any manner whosoever;
(iv) The plaintiff is granted a decree for recovery of arrears of rent at the rate of Rs.7,500/- per month w.e.f. December, 2006 to February, 2009;
(v) The plaintiff is granted a decree for mesne profits / use and occupation charges against the defendant at the rate of Rs.7,500/- + 10% per month for the first year of illegal occupation between 01.03.2009 to 27.02.2010; thereafter additional damages at the rate of 10% over and above the first year of illegal possession for the period of Civil Suit No. 125/2009 Page 30/31 01.03.2010 to 28.02.2011 and so on till such time the plaintiff gains possession of the suit property;

(vi) The plaintiff shall also be entitled to the costs of the suit.

73. Decree sheet be drawn. However decree sheet in respect of relief (v) on mesne profits be drawn after the plaintiff deposits deficient Court fee in respect of this relief up to the date of this judgment. File be consigned to the Record Room.
