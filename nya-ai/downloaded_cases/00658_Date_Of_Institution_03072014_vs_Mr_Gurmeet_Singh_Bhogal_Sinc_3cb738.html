J U D G M E N T

1. Instant appeal under Section 96 read with Order XLI and Section 151 Code of Civil Procedure, has been preferred by appellant against the impugned judgment and decree dated 08.05.2014 passed by learned Civil Judge - 07, West District Delhi in CS No.690/2012 whereby the suit filed by appellant against the respondents was partly decreed for the relief of permanent injunction, however the claim of appellant to the extent of seeking the relief of declaration relief was dismissed.

2. The facts necessary as set out in the appeal and the impugned judgment passed by learned Trial Court are as follows:­ Appellant and respondent No.2 are brothers and respondent Nos.1(i) to 1(iii) are the LRs of deceased respondent No.1, who was also their third brother; and respondent No.3 is son of respondent No.2, whereas respondent No.4 is the builder who raised the construction over the property in question bearing No.35, Jamrudpur, Punjabi Mohalla, Kailash Colony, New Delhi - 110048 (hereinafter referred as 'property in dispute'). Deceased respondent No.1 expired during pendency of suit and his LRs RCA No.18/2014 Surinder Singh Bhogal v Gurmeet Singh Bhogal & Ors Page 3 of 18 were brought on record. Appellant had filed the suit for permanent injunction and declaration against respondents praying for restraining respondent Nos.2 & 3 from dispossessing the plaintiff from first floor of property in question and also declaring the plaintiff as absolute owner of first floor said of property. Vide the impugned judgment and decree, the suit of appellant for the relief of permanent injunction was decreed in favour of appellant but he was held to be not entitled for the relief of declaration.

3. The admitted factual matrix of the instant case in brief can be recapitulated as under:­ The property in question was acquired by Late Sardar Atma Singh, who was father of appellant, deceased respondent No.1, as well as respondent Nos.2 vide partition deed dated 18.06.1991. Late Sardar Atma Singh died intestate leaving behind three sons who are appellant, deceased respondent No.1, and respondent No.2 as well as two daughters namely Gurdev Kaur and Kulwant Kaur. Some property dispute arose but the same was reconciled between all five legal heirs of deceased Sardar Atma Singh and both daughters of late Atma Singh relinquished their share in the property of their father. All three brothers of late Sardar Atma Singh mutually agreed that property in question would be re­built and each one of them would have one floor each to be determined by draw of lots, the said oral partition agreement dated 22.12.2000 was stated to have reduced into writing. Subsequently, three sons of late Sh Atma Singh entered into a RCA No.18/2014 Surinder Singh Bhogal v Gurmeet Singh Bhogal & Ors Page 4 of 18 collaboration agreement with a builder i.e. respondent No.4 herein for raising construction over the plot in question after demolishing existing structure. Respondent No.4 who is builder raised construction over the property in question as per the collaboration agreement entered into between the parties. The basement, ground and top floors were given to respondent No.4 whereas first, second, and third floor were taken by appellant, respondent Nos.2 and deceased respondent No.1 respectively. The portion of deceased respondent No.1 viz third floor was later on purchased by respondent No.2 and his son i.e. respondent No.3 who is now occupying the said floor. It is alleged by appellant that respondent Nos.2 & 3 were quarreling with him on the ground that respondent No.2 is absolute owner of entire property in question by virtue of unregistered Will and respondent No.3 allegedly claimed that they would forcibly dispossess the appellant from the first floor. On the basis of said facts, appellant instituted a civil suit for the relief of permanent injunction and declaration. The suit was contested by respondent Nos.2 & 3 as well as deceased respondent No. 1 and they had taken the objection that both daughters of late Atma Singh Bhogal are necessary parties and that all the six legal heirs are joint owners in equal share of the property and memorandum of oral partition is inadmissible in evidence. The possession of appellant in respect of first floor was admitted by them, however, respondent Nos.2 & 3 have refuted any attempt or threat by them to forcibly dispossess the plaintiff from the first floor of property in question as alleged by plaintiff. It is also averred that second floor was purchased from deceased respondent No.1 by RCA No.18/2014 Surinder Singh Bhogal v Gurmeet Singh Bhogal & Ors Page 5 of 18 respondent No.3.

4. Learned Trial Court on the basis of pleadings of parties, re­ framed the issues under Order 14 Rule 5 CPC vide order dated 20.09.2013 as under:­ "(i) Whether the plaintiff is entitled for decree of declaration, as prayed for? OPP

(ii) Whether the plaintiff is entitled for decree of permanent injunction, as prayed for? OPP

(iii) Whether the plaintiff has not paid the proper Court Fee? OPD

(iv) Relief."

5. During trial before learned Trial Court, appellant examined himself as PW1 and one of his sisters Smt.Kulwant Kaur as PW2. Respondents however, did not lead any evidence. After hearing arguments, learned Trial Court vide impugned judgment and decree, declined the relief of declaration, however suit of appellant for relief of permanent injunction was decreed in his favour. Learned Trial Court has opined that claim of appellant for relief of declaration was based on the document Ex.PW1/6 and Ex.PW1/8, which were unregistered document namely memorandum of oral partition deed/agreement dated 22.12.2000 and family settlement dated 12.02.2003 and while relying upon the decision of the Hon'ble Delhi High Court in Jai Dev Singh v Sujan Singh & Ors : (1993) 50 DLT 663 (DB) came to the conclusion that Section 49 of the Registration Act bars the admissibility of such documents as such they are of no consequence and RCA No.18/2014 Surinder Singh Bhogal v Gurmeet Singh Bhogal & Ors Page 6 of 18 cannot be relied upon to confer any title or make any declaration in favour of appellant regarding his title in the suit property and don't confer any title in favour of appellant.

6. Being aggrieved from the impugned judgment, appellant has preferred instant appeal on the grounds that since the 'family settlement' was not disputed by the respondents, the Trial Court ought to have passed the decree for declaration, as in such eventuality the appellant was not even required to prove said document in view of Section 58 of the Indian Evidence Act, 1872. Secondly, that said family settlement is not otherwise required to be registered as per Section 17 of the Indian Registration Act, appellant has also relied upon many citations as mentioned in the grounds A to D, which will be referred in latter part of this judgment.

7. Upon service of notice of appeal, respondents put appearance and Trial Court Record has also been requisitioned. On the submissions of learned counsel for appellant and learned counsel for respondent Nos.2 & 3, vide order dated 24.07.2014, the name of LRs of deceased respondent No.1 as well as that of respondent No.4 were deleted from the array of parties in instant appeal and respondent Nos.2 & 3 are only contesting this appeal.

8. Learned counsel for respondent Nos.2 & 3 without filing any reply to instant appeal, orally opposed the appeal and relied upon the same judgments as relied upon by the learned Trial Court in impugned judgment RCA No.18/2014 Surinder Singh Bhogal v Gurmeet Singh Bhogal & Ors Page 7 of 18 itself.

9. I have heard arguments of learned counsel for respondent Nos. 2 & 3 and part arguments of learned counsel for appellant. For the reason mentioned in the order sheet dated 11.09.2014, written submissions have been filed on behalf appellant, which have been perused. I have also gone through the TCR with the assistance of both learned counsel for parties.

10. In the suit filed before learned Trial Court, appellant had sought declaration of his title in respect of first floor of property in dispute on the basis of a family settlement arrived at between the appellant, deceased respondent No.1 as well as respondent No.2 namely late Sh Gurmeet Singh Bhogal and Daljeet Singh Bhogal respectively on 11.02.2003 whereby first, second and third floor of the property in dispute were partitioned among appellant, deceased respondent No.1 as well as respondent No.2. As per said family settlement available in Trial Court Record as Ex.PW1/8, appellant and deceased respondent No.1 as well as respondent No.2 had entered into a collaboration agreement with a builder i.e. erstwhile respondent No.4 in this appeal, for construction of a residential building upto third floor on the plot of land bearing No.35, Jamrudpur, Punjabi Mohalla, Kailash Colony, New Delhi - 110048 after demolishing the entire existing structure over it and as per said collaboration agreement, available in TCR as Ex.PW1/7, basement and ground floor was to be given to the builder and first, second and third of RCA No.18/2014 Surinder Singh Bhogal v Gurmeet Singh Bhogal & Ors Page 8 of 18 property in dispute came to the share of appellant, deceased respondent No. 1 and respondent No.2 respectively.

11. After completion of the entire building, aforementioned family settlement Ex.PW1/8 was entered into between the appellant, deceased respondent No.1 as well as respondent No.2 and pursuant thereto the three floors of the property came to be partitioned and came to the share of brothers in the manner as noted above.

12. Learned Trial Court while partly decreeing the suit of appellant for the relief of permanent injunction, dismissed the claim of appellant for relief of declaration. Learned Trial Court while declining the relief of declaration to the appellant, made following observations:­ " In the instant case also, the plaintiff is relying on the unregistered oral partition and family settlement deed i.e. Ex.PW1/6 and Ex.PW1/8 respectively, in order to prove his ownership regarding first floor of property No.35, Zamrudpur, Punjabi Mohalla, Kailash Colony, New Delhi, but in view of law laid down in Tek Bahadur Bhujil (supra) and Jai Dev Singh (supra) the said documents must be registered in order to prove the title of plaintiff regarding the said floor, but the said documents are of no consequences, being unregistered."

13. Both the counsels appearing for the parties had advanced verbal arguments and learned counsel for appellant has also filed the written submissions with the supporting case law.

14. The impugned judgment has been challenged mainly on the RCA No.18/2014 Surinder Singh Bhogal v Gurmeet Singh Bhogal & Ors Page 9 of 18 ground that a family settlement does not compulsorily require registration in view of Section 17 of the Indian Registration Act and therefore, learned Trial Court has committed a grave error by declining the relief of declaration on the premise that family settlement, being unregistered was of no consequence and could not be relied upon for proving the title of the appellant in the first floor of property in dispute. In support of his contentions he relied upon the judgments namely M Venkataramana Hebbar v M Rajagopal Hebbar : (2007) 6 SCC 401; Syndicate Bank v Estate Officer & Manager APIIC Ltd : (2007) 8 SCC 361; as well as Bhagwan Krishan Gupta v Prabha Gupta : (2009) 11 SCC 33.

15. The second limb of argument raised by learned counsel for appellant is that respondents/defendants had never disputed the execution of the MOU/Agreement/'Family settlement' and considering said admission of defendants, said documents were not even required to be proved in view of provisions of Section 58 of the Indian Evidence Act, 1872.

16. Learned counsel appearing on behalf of respondent refuted the aforementioned arguments and relied upon the same judgments which have been relied upon by learned Trial Court in the impugned judgment.

17. After having considered the rival submissions of both the sides and perusing the impugned judgment, the short question requiring consideration in this appeal is that "Whether a 'family settlement' does RCA No.18/2014 Surinder Singh Bhogal v Gurmeet Singh Bhogal & Ors Page 10 of 18 or does not compulsorily requires registration?"

18. Before dwelling upon the aforementioned question, it would be apt to glance through the relevant provisions of the Indian Registration Act, 1908:­ "17. Documents of which registration is compulsory: (1) The following documents shall be registered if the property to which they relate is situate in a district in which, and if they have been executed on or after the date on which, Act No.XVI of 1864 or the Indian Registration Act, 1866, or the Indian Registration Act, 1871, or the Indian Registration Act, 1877, or this Act came or comes into force, namely:­

(a) xx xx xx

(b) other non­testamentary instruments which purport or operate to create, declare, assign, limit or extinguish, whether in present or in future, any right, title or interest, whether vested or contingent, of the value of one hundred rupees or upwards, to or in immovable property;

49. Effect of non­registration of document required to be registered:­ No document required by Section 17 (or by any provisions of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882) to be registered shall:­

(a) affect any immovable property comprised therein, or

(b) confer any power to adopt, or

(c) be received as evidence of any transaction affecting such property or conferring such power, unless it has been registered. "

19. For the purpose of Section 17 (1) (b) of the Act, all that is necessary is that instrument should create or purport to create or declare, assign, limit or extinguish whether in present or in future the right, title or interest to any immovable property. For deciding whether a document requires to be registered, one has to take the document as a whole and counter it with reference to its claims. In deciding whether any instrument required registration, the crucial thing is to check whether an instrument purports to bring about a change in the legal relation in respect of some RCA No.18/2014 Surinder Singh Bhogal v Gurmeet Singh Bhogal & Ors Page 11 of 18 property.
20. As per appellant's case, the property bearing No.35, which constitutes a part of plot No.4, Khasra No.98, situated in Mauza Zamurdpur, Delhi was purchased by Sardar Atma Singh and his two brothers in their joint name vide sale deed dated 02.06.1956, and the same came to the share of appellant's father Sardar Atma Singh vide a partition deed dated 18.06.1991. The said registered sale deed and partition deed are available on record of learned Trial Court as Ex.PW1/1 and Ex.PW1/2 respectively.
21. After death of late Atma Singh, who died intestate, a family settlement/oral partition dated 22.12.2000 was entered into amongst legal heirs of late Sh Atma Singh vide which Mrs. Gurdev Kaur and Mrs Kulwant Kaur, both daughters of late Atma Singh relinquished their rights in the demise property and the three sons of late Atma Singh including the appellant herein mutually agreed to enter into a collaboration agreement with a builder to reconstruct the demise property from basement uptill third floor. It was further agreed that basement and ground floor of property shall be given to the builder and remaining three floors shall be distributed amongst three brothers. In accordance with terms of said memorandum of oral partition, appellant, deceased respondent No.1 and respondent No.2 entered into a collaboration agreement with a builder Bijender Singh (earlier respondent No.4 herein) and upon completion of construction in accordance with collaboration agreement, appellant and his RCA No.18/2014 Surinder Singh Bhogal v Gurmeet Singh Bhogal & Ors Page 12 of 18 brothers again entered into a family settlement vide family settlement deed dated 11.02.2003 vide which they distributed three floors of property i.e. first, second and third floors among themselves and first floor came to the share of appellant and second floor and third floors came to the share of deceased brother of appellant namely Gurmeet Singh Bhogal and Daljeet Singh respectively.
22. Now, for basing his claim for declaration of his title, appellant has placed reliance on family settlement deed dated 11.02.2003 by raising the contention that said document does not fall within the ambit of Section 17 (1) (b) of the Indian Registration Act, 1908 as said document does not create or declare a title or interest in immovable property,but it is a mere narration of a transaction which had taken place in past. It is argued that property came to be already partitioned vide previous memorandum of oral partition dated 22.12.2000 and in subsequent family settlement, only the terms of said previous oral partition were reduced into writing.
23. For understanding the distinction between two types of family settlement one requiring registration & other not requiring any registration, the judgment of Hon'ble Supreme Court in Kale & Ors v Deputy Director of Consolidation and Ors : AIR 1976 SC 807 can be referred wherein it was held as under:­ "The family arrangement may be even oral in which case no registration is necessary. The registration would be necessary only if the terms of the family arrangement are reduced into writing. Here also, a distinction should be RCA No.18/2014 Surinder Singh Bhogal v Gurmeet Singh Bhogal & Ors Page 13 of 18 made between a document containing the terms and recitals of a family arrangement made under the document and a mere memorandum prepared after the family arrangement had already been made either for the purpose of the record or for the information of the Court for making necessary mutation. In such a case the memorandum itself does not create or extinguish any rights in immovable properties and is, therefore, not compulsorily registrable. "

24. The aforementioned proposition of law was again reiterated by Hon'ble Apex Court in its subsequent decision in Roshan Singh v. Zilay Singh : 1988 SC 881 wherein the Court held as under:

"It is well settled that while an instrument of partition which operates or is intended to operate as a declared volition constituting of severing ownership and causes a change of legal relation to the property divided amongst the parties to it, requires registration under S. 17(1)(b) of the Act, a writing which merely recites that there has been in time past been a partition, is not a declaration of will, but a mere statement of fact, and it does not require any registration. The essence of the matter is whether the deed is a part of the partition transaction or contains merely an incidental recital of a past transaction. It is equally well settled that a mere list of properties allotted at a partition is not an instrument of partition and does not require registration. S. 17 (1)(b) lays down that a document for which registration is compulsory should, by its own force, operate or purport to operate to create or declare some right in immovable property. Therefore, a mere recital of what has already taken place cannot be held to declare any right and there would be no necessity of registering such a document. Two propositions must therefore flow:(1) A partition may be effected orally; but if it is subsequently reduced into a form of a document and that document purports by itself to effect a division and embodies all the terms of bargain, it will be necessary to register it. If it be not registered, S. 49 of the Act will prevent its being admitted in evidence. Secondly evidence of the factum of partition will not be admissible by reason of S. 91 of the Evidence Act, 1872 (2) partition lists which are mere records of a previously completed partition between the parties, will be admitted in evidence even though they are unregistered, to provide the fact of partition."

25. After having gone through the aforementioned judgments it is RCA No.18/2014 Surinder Singh Bhogal v Gurmeet Singh Bhogal & Ors Page 14 of 18 clear that in order to determine whether a document is a deed of partition or is a memorandum of partition, it is to be seen whether partition has been made by the document itself or the document contains the terms and conditions of a mutual partition arrived in a family settlement. In latter case, the document itself does not create or extinguish any right or title in an immovable property and it is not compulsorily registrable, whereas the document falling in the former category would require registration.

26. In the instant case bare reading of the document i.e. the family settlement deed Ex. PW1/8 shows that by the document itself the property has been divided by meets and bounds not only amongst the sons of late Atma Singh but a right has also been created in respect of basement and ground floor of the demise property in favour of a non family member i.e. a builder Bijender Singh i.e. originally impleaded as respondent no. 4 herein.

27. Admittedly, prior to execution of collaboration agreements, the property in question was not a multi story building and it is only after it was demolished, a new construction was raised from basement up to third floor. The property came to be partitioned vide said family settlement Ex. PW1/8 amongst the builder Bijender Singh and three sons of late Atma Singh. Hence, in said circumstances, there could be no occasion for any pre­ existing rights in respect of floors of the property in favour of appellant and his brothers.

28. Learned counsel for appellant has raised the contention that property no. 35, Zamrudpur, Punjabi Mohalla, Kailash Colony, New Delhi was actually partitioned among legal heirs of late Sh. Atma Singh vide memorandum of oral partition dated 22.12.2000 Ex.PW1/6. However, it is necessary to observe here that even said document Ex.PW1/6 is also not merely recognition of pre­existing rights of parties in the property but the recital in the document shows that document itself has declared rights in favour of sons of late Atma Singh and extinguished rights of his two daughters in favour of his sons as they relinquished their shares in the property in favour of their three brothers. In said circumstances, even said document Ex.PW1/6 also requires registration and cannot be relied upon for the purposes of evidence in view of bar of Section 49 of the Indian Registration Act, 1908. Assuming for sake arguments that at the time of execution of document Ex.PW1/8, the appellant and deceased respondent no.1 and respondent no.2 were having pre­existing rights in first, second and third floor of the property in dispute by virtue of document Ex.PW1/6, but those rights were joint as it is only through subsequent family settlement Ex.PW1/8, the different floors of the property came to be partitioned among the appellant and his brothers.

29. I have carefully gone through the judgment relied upon by learned counsel for appellant but all are distinguishable on the facts of the case. The judgment of Hon'ble Supreme Court in M. Venkataramana Hebbar (supra) is of no help to appellant as in said case, the Hon'ble RCA No.18/2014 Surinder Singh Bhogal v Gurmeet Singh Bhogal & Ors Page 16 of 18 Supreme Court only for the purpose of that partition case, assumed that deed of family settlement which was in question in that case was not required to be compulsorily registered. Another judgment in Bhagwan Krishan Gupta (supra) is also distinguishable in as much as in said case it was interpretation of a "Will" that was in question before the Supreme Court and in that context it was held that family settlement are permissible in law and the question of requirement of registration of such family settlement was not touched upon. Even in the judgment of the Hon'ble Supreme Court relied upon by learned counsel for appellant in Ramdev Food Products Pvt Ltd : Arvindbhai Rambhai Patel & Ors : (2006) 8 SCC 726 the issue of requirement of registration of a family settlement was not in question and it was only held that since family settlement are actuated by a desire to resolve the disputes, the Courts should not easily disturb them.

30. The last contention of learned counsel for appellant is that in view of Section 58 of the Indian Evidence Act, an admitted document need not be proved by the parties. Section 58 of the Indian Evidence Act reads as under:­ "58. Facts admitted need not be proved:­­ No fact need to be proved in any proceeding which the parties thereto or their agents agree to admit at the hearing, or which, before the hearing, they agree to admit by any writing under their hands, or which by any rule of pleading in force at the time they are deemed to have admitted by their pleadings.

Provided that the Court may, in its discretion, require the facts admitted to be proved otherwise than by such admissions."

Section 58 of the Indian Evidence Act, dispenses with the requirement of proof of an admitted fact. However, where a document is in­admissible RCA No.18/2014 Surinder Singh Bhogal v Gurmeet Singh Bhogal & Ors Page 17 of 18 in evidence, owing to some statutory bar, like in case of insufficiently stamped document or an unregistered document, which is compulsorily registrable as per Indian Evidence Act, mere admission of opposite party regarding contents of such document will not make such document admissible in evidence. Moreover, in the instant case, during trial respondents in their WS have taken a categoric objection regarding admissibility of the said oral partition deed/ family settlement agreement. Hence, I do not find any merit in the contention so urged by learned counsel for appellant.

31. In view of the fact that both the said documents i.e. Ex.PW1/6 and Ex.PW1/8 have created rights in respect of an immovable property worth more than Rs.1,000/­ in favour of appellant, his brothers and the builder Bijender Singh, the documents were compulsorily registrable as Section 17 (1) (b) of Indian Registration Act. As the said documents are unregistered, they cannot be read into evidence except for the collateral purposes in view of bar of Section 49 of Indian Registration Act. Considering the said position, learned Trial Court was right in holding that appellant was not entitled to claim declaration of his title on the premise of said documents.

32. Having regard to the aforementioned discussion, I find no perversity or illegality in the impugned judgment and instant appeal is liable to fail being devoid of any merits.

33. Consequently, instant appeal is dismissed with no order as to cost. Decree sheet be drawn accordingly.

34. Trial Court Record be remitted back henceforth with copy of this judgment and decree sheet. Appeal file be consigned to Record Room.
