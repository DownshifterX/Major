REFERENCE UNDER SECTION 18 OF THE LAND ACQUISITION ACT

1. Vide this award, I shall dispose of the instant reference under section 18 of the L.A. Act preferred by the petitioners against the respondents being aggrieved with the quantum of compensation awarded by the LAC in lieu of the acquisition of the land of the petitioners. It was stated by the petitioners that the church was the owner of the properties for which this reference had been filed by the church with the L.A.C. It was further averred that the land under acquisition was being used for commercial purpose much prior to the date of acquisition under section 4 of the L.A. Act. As stated, the land in question was sought to be acquired for a public purpose of constructing a grade separator at Rani Jhansi Road, Delhi. It also averred that the Land Acquisition Collector had grossly contradicted his own stand taken in two previous awards i.e 8 and 8A/LAC/M/2010-11 wherein the property bearing No. 18 Boulevard Road which is the adjacent property has been assessed at commercial rates by the Land Acquisition Collector and therefore the Land Acquisition Collector ought to have assessed the market value of the land of the petitioners on the basis of LAC-3/1/2011 Page:-2/36 commercial land use.

2. That vide Notification No. F.10(36)/06/L&B/LA/6292 dated 24.08.2009 under section 4 of LA Act, declaration vide notification No F.10(36)/06/L&B/LA/10391 dated 13.11.2009 and Notification u/s 9 of the LA Act also dated 13.11.2009, the land of the petitioners amongst others was situated at 12,14,16 Boulevard Road, in khasra No. 280, Civil Lines has been acquired by the Government and possession of the same was taken on 7.12.2009. The aportioned area was as stated in the revised statement U/s 19 L. A. Act as submitted by LAC. The Land Acquisition Collector, after completing all the formalities as provided under the Act assessed the market value of the land of the petitioners @ Rs. 27,300/- per sq meter.

3. In reference U/s 18 of LAC Act as filed by petitioner, it was pleaded that an area of 6376.21 has been acquired by the Land Acquisition Collector, District-North, 1 Kripa Narain Marg pertaining to Kh. No. 280 min at Civil Lines area for the purpose of construction of grate separator at Rani Jhansi Road. It was also averred in reference that entire area under acquisition was originally owned by the petitioner as a lessee forever. It was also averred that area under acquisition was being used for commercial purposes such as LAC-3/1/2011 Page:-3/36 running of school and various commercial activities were being carried out on the acquired land much before the date of acquisition U/s 4 of L. A. Act. It was also averred that petitioner has not accepted the award on the grounds as mentioned in reference. Hence it was prayed that petitioner be paid market value of land, value of structure, shifting charges, severance charges besides 30% solatium, additional amount and interest @ 9% for the first year from the date of taking of possession and thereafter @ 15% p.a. till the amount of compensation is tendered and paid to the petitioner or market value of land and structure.

4. The respondents No. 1 filed separate written statement to the instant reference petition wherein preliminary objections were raised stating that petitioner is claiming excessive and exorbitant market value of the land structures. On merits, it was stated that the compensation assessed by the Land Acquisition Collector was sufficient and reasonable and further stated that the petitioners were claiming exorbitant value of the land and structures. It was also averred that petitioner is not entitled to any enhancement over and above the market value assessed by the Land Acquisition Collector. It was further averred that reference under reply is misconceived and as such liable to be dismissed. It was also LAC-3/1/2011 Page:-4/36 pleaded that no ground or reason has been shown by the petitioner for any enhancement in market value of land or super structures which were correctly assessed by the LAC. Accordingly, it was prayed that the instant reference petition as filed by the petitioner be dismissed.

5. Separate written statement was also filed on behalf of the respondent No. 2 namely the MCD wherein preliminary objections were raised stating that application U/s 18 of LAC Act is not maintainable. On merits, it was contented by the MCD that there was no legal infirmity in award No 09/LAC/N-10-11 of village Civil Station, Civil Lines, Delhi. It further stated that the impugned award was based on sound reasons and was passed after taking into consideration the evidence/documents produced during the proceedings of the award and the LAC had rightly assessed the compensation amount in respect of land and structures. It was stated that the land owners are claiming exorbitant value of the land and structures. It was also averred that petitioners are not entitled to any enhancement over and above the market value assessed by the Land Acquisition Collector. Hence, it was prayed that the petition is liable to be dismissed being without any merits.

The petitioners having been aggrieved by the compensation awarded by the LAC sought enhancement through the reference made and have put forth a number of grounds on which he claims to be entitled to the enhanced compensation. Even a slight disturbance or variation in routine unsettles a person and that too depends upon the degree of impact and vulnerability of the individual, the actual impact, thus, may even be devastating. In these circumstances, unsettling someone alongwith the entire family with habitat and hearth lock, stock and barrel can very well be imagined especially when establishment is stable and running for quite sometime. The proximity not only breeds contempt but also develops into fascination, bondings and different types of LAC-3/1/2011 Page:-7/36 relations between the individual, and at times encompasses in itself, the relationship between the humans animals and even the stones and structures. One may, more often than not, feel the pain of leaving particular abode, locality, city or country.
11.Against this backdrop, un-settling someone from his abode is, without doubt, disturbing if not devastating but then it has to be coped up depending upon the circumstance especially when it is supported by the legislation (may be through a more than hundred years old 'colonial' legislation, not much amended, retaining its basic features) and when there is no escape or no way out and has limited rather no option to exercise but to toe the line or so to say that no option whatsoever except the one thrusted upon by the circumstances.
12.The requirement to broaden the road and to create/construct a grade separator at Rani Jhansi Road was the need of the hour and the creation of the same armed and empowered with the will of Government to remove all possible obstacles with all the might of the state in order to have smooth passage/path/road in place at the earliest in the City of Delhi resulted into acquisition of land even in thickly populated urban landscape under the Land Acquisition Act, 1894 and persons whose land/houses were the LAC-3/1/2011 Page:-8/36 subject matter of the notification under section 4 of Land Acquisition Act for this purpose were not even given an option to file any objections as contemplated under Section 17-A of Land Acquisition Act thereby leaving them practically with no option but to concede to the Directives of Government. In such scenario, those who were displaced could have only consoled themselves that they got suitable compensation, which may compensate them to the extent possible though at times there cannot be a compensation for such situations. To fulfill the same, the Govt. acquired the land at Boulevard Road for the purpose of grade separator at Rani Jhansi Road and in the process, some individuals were forced to surrender their houses/shops/factory and land (commercial as well as industrial) which of course was not to their liking or pleasure.
13.It was compulsory acquisition so much so that land was required on urgent basis and therefore, the formalities with regard to the provisions of Land Acquisition Act which under normal circumstances, are routinely followed were dispensed with resorting to Section 17 of LA Act. After processing the matter, the compensation was fixed by the Land Acquisition Collector at the rate of Rs.27,300/- per sq. Mtr. Which was not acceptable to the LAC-3/1/2011 Page:-9/36 claimant and therefore the reference was made under section 18 of Land Acquisition Act whereby the claimant have sought enhancement in the amount of compensation based upon various facts that it was an area located in heart of city, located very close to the prestigious Delhi University, Interstate Bus Terminal, Railway Station, Popular and big markets, on the legendary Grant Truck Road. Since the land was in possession of its owner for long and they had raised commercial structure over the same according to their own requirement, choice and taste and in the process invested handsome amount of money, was deprived of not only of the means of livelihood but he was also deprived of with the benefits which were available to the claimant on account of its situational advantages and the potential of property from commercial angle specially when metro is going near the property in question.
14.All the amenities of good life were available to the claimant at a stones throw and that too of a very high standard and better quality. To elaborate, the claimant has asserted that the land/houses surrounded by popular residential and commercial areas like Kamla Nagar, Roop Nagar, Shakti Nagar, Malka Ganj, Barf Khana and reputed Delhi University in its toe besides having LAC-3/1/2011 Page:-10/36 important commercial civic, educational hospitality and medical establishment such as Oberoi Maiden's Hotel, Tirath Ram Shah Hospital, St Stephen Hospital, District Courts and Railway Claims Tribunals etc., surrounded by lush green area located adjacent to the Northern Ridge which had become so important in the city life as it is a sort of big source of fresh air oxygen which is scarce if not exclusive and missing in his metropolis choking with various kinds of pollutions. The importance of such a place can very well be imagined. Whereas while awarding the compensation, it is asserted that the Land Acquisition Collector has not taken into account all these factors and has awarded the compensation which is not suitable to the kind of properties which have been acquired by the Govt.
15.All the above facts and circumstances coupled with documents the petitioners has also placed reliance upon the evidence led in LAC No. 05/1/11 in case titled "National Coal Storage and Refrigeration Pvt Ltd Vs UOI & Ors" which reflected the actual value of the properties in the vicinity qua the transactions which took place in or around the same time when the notification under section 4 was published in order to drive home their case that the value of the acquired property of the petitioners should be the LAC-3/1/2011 Page:-11/36 same as the property No. 18 Boulevard Road duly acquired vide award No 10-11/LAC/N wherein the petitioners therein demanded that the compensation may be given @ Rs.2,38,474/- per sq. mtr. alongwith solatium, additional amount coupled with the interest etc so far as the property at Boulevard Road, village Civil Lines belonging to petitioners is concerned.
16.In this regard, it will be appropriate to refer the broad parameters laid down by the Hon'ble Supreme Court of India. In case of "Chaman Lal Hargovind Dass Vs Special Land Acquisition Officer (1988) 2 SCC 751" wherein it was observed that the case of enhancement of compensation on account of acquisition of land are required to be considered on the following parameters :-
"4. The following factors must be etched on the mental screen : (1) A reference under section 18 of the Land Acquisition Act is not an appeal against the award; and the court cannot take into account the material relied upon by the Land Acquisition Officer in his award unless the same material is produced and proved before the court.
(2) So also the award of the Land Acquisition Officer is not to be treated as judgment of the trial court open or exposed to challenge before the court hearing the reference. It is merely an LAC-3/1/2011 Page:-12/36 offer made by the Land Acquisition Officer and the material utilized by him for making his valuation cannot be utilized by the court unless produced and proved before it. It is not function of the court to sit in appeal against the award, approve or disprove its reasoning, or correct its error or affirm, modify or reverse the conclusion reached by the Land Acquisition Officer, as if it were an appellant court.
(3) The court has to treat the reference as an original proceedings before it and determine the market value afresh on the basis of the material produced before it. (4) The claimant is in the position of a plaintiff who has to show that the price offered for his land in the award is inadequate on the basis of the material produced in the court. Of course, the material placed and proved by the other side can also betaken into account for this purpose.
(5) The market value of land under acquisition has to be determine as on the crucial date of publication of notification under section 4 of the Land Acquisition Act (dates of notifications under section 6 & 9 are irrelevant).
(6) The determination has to be made standing on the date line of valuation (date of publication of notification under Section4) as LAC-3/1/2011 Page:-13/36 if the valuer is a hypothetical purchaser willing to purchase land from the open market and is prepared to pay a reasonable price as on that day. It has also to be assumed that the vendor is willing to sell the land at a reasonable price.
(7) In doing so by the instances method, the court has to correlate the market value reflected in the most comparable instance which provides the index of market value. (8) Only genuine instances have to be taken into account.

(Sometimes instances are rigged up in anticipation of acquisition of land).

(9) Even post-notification instances can be taken into account (1) if they are very proximate, (2) genuine and (3) the acquisition itself has not motivate the purchaser to pay a higher price on account of the resultant improvement in development prospects. (10) The most comparable instances out of the genuine instances have to be identified on the following considerations :

(i) proximity from time angle.

(ii) proximity from situation angle.

(11) Having identified the instances which provide the index of market value the price reflected therein may betaken as the norm and the market value of the land under acquisition may be LAC-3/1/2011 Page:-14/36 deduced by making suitable adjustments for the plus and minus factors vis-a-vis land under acquisition by placing the two in juxtaposition.

(12) A balance-sheet of plus and minus factors may be drawn for this purpose and the relevant factors any be evaluated in terms of price variation as a prudent purchaser should do. (13) The market value of the land under acquisition has thereafter to be deduced by loading the price reflected in the instance taken as norm for plus factors and unloading it for minus factors.

(14) The exercise indicated in clause (11) to (13) has to be undertaken in a common sense manner as a prudent man of the world of business would do. We may illustrate some such illustrative (not exhaustive) factors : 

               Plus factors                 Minus factors
      1. Smallness of size              1. Largeness of areas
      2. proximity to a road            2. situation in the interior at a
                                         Distance from the road.
      3. frontage on a road             3. narrow strip of land with
                                         Very small frontage
                                         Compared to depth

4. nearness to developed area 4. lower level requiring the depressed portion to be LAC-3/1/2011 Page:-15/36 filled up

5. regular shape 5. remoteness from developed Locality

6. level vis-a-vis land under 6. some special disadvantageous acquisition factor which would deter it purchaser

7. Special value for an owner of an adjoining property to whom it may have some very special advantage.

(15) The evaluation of these factors of course depends on the facts of each case. There cannot be any hard and fast or rigid rule. Common sense is the best and most reliable guide. (16)Every case must be dealt with on its own fact pattern bearing in mind all these factors as a prudent purchaser of land in which position the judge must place himself.

(17)These are general guidelines to be applied with understanding informed with common sense.

In the present case, the acquisition was of compulsory in nature as enlisted in section 17 of the Land Acquisition Act, 1894 and the petitioners were not given an opportunity to file objections under section 5-A of the Land Acquisition Act which, in the present LAC-3/1/2011 Page:-16/36 reference was the commercial property of the petitioners who was using the said property for commercial activities in order to earn his livelihood. All the civic amenities were available to the land in question and the acquired property was completely abutted to property No 18 Boulevard Road which was acquired for the same purpose i.e. construction of a grade separator at Rani Jhansi Road and was pertaining to the same area i.e. Civil Lines area of village Civil Station and which had been acquired vide award No 10/LAC/N/10-11 wherein the date of notification under section 4 was 15.04.2010 and the date of notification in the present case is 24.08.2009 i.e. just a few months prior to the date of notification in award No 10/LAC/10-11.

18. Act came into being. In the instant matter where the LAC-3/1/2011 Page:-17/36 acquisition was of compulsory nature, one did not have even the opportunity to question it but to resign to one's fate and accept the monetary compensation. Thus, the compensation (consolation) in the form of money should be potent enough atleast to console if not compensate.

25. In P. Rama Reddi & others Vs. Land Acquit ion Officer, HUDA Hyderabad (1995) 2 SCC 305, parameters was enumerated as follows :

"(i) the situation of the acquired land vis-a-vis the city or the town or village which had been growing in size because of its commercial, industrial, educational, religious or any other kind of importance or because of its explosive population:
(ii) the suitability of the acquired land for putting up the buildings, be they residential, commercial or industrial, as the case may be;
(iii) possibility of obtaining water and electric LAC-3/1/2011 Page:-22/36 supply for occupants of buildings to be put up on that land;
(iv) absence of statutory impediments or the like for using the acquired land for building purposes;
(v) existence of highways, public roads, layouts of building plots or developed residential extensions in the vicinity or close proximity of the acquired land; (vi) benefits or advantageous or educational institution, health care centers, or the like in the surrounding areas of the acquired land which may become available to the occupiers of buildings, if built on the acquired land;
(vii) and lands around the acquired land or the acquired land itself being in demand for building purposes, to specify a few."

26. The counsel for LAC has come up with the plea that the method adopted by the LAC in assessing the compensation was appropriate and he has taken into consideration all the factors which were relevant and material. It has been correctly argued by petitioner that as compared to the evidence led on behalf of the respondent No. 1 the evidence brought on record by the petitioner reflects the correct picture and the area shown by respondent is far away and valuation of the property falling in category 'E' locality have been produced. The petitioner has also submitted that the sale deeds of LAC-3/1/2011 Page:-23/36 properties talked by respondent no. 1 are not comparable with the acquired property because the sale deeds talked by the respondent no. 1 are of properties which are extremely small residential floors ad-measuring 100 sq. mtrs. while the property in question is a four side open property ad-measuring 1183.96 sq. mtrs. The petitioner has submitted that rather in one of the case of Methodist Church Vs. Union of India this court itself had fixed the rate of residential property as Rs.28,351/- pertaining to the land acquired way back in 2000 for metro in nearby situated property in the area of Civil Lines. The instances of sale deed relied upon by the respondent cannot be taken into account for two reasons firstly admittedly the property in question falls under village 'Civil Station' as mentioned in the award which comes under Category 'C' as mentioned in the circle rates of Delhi issued vide notification no. F.1(152)/Regn. Br./Div.com./2011/780 dated 4.12.12 read with notification no. F. 2(12)/Fin.(E.I)/Part File Vol(ii)/3548 dated 18.7.07 and secondly none of such instances have been taken into account by LAC as per the award. Moreover, the LAC himself has fixed the rate Rs. 54,600/- per sq. mtr. and even if the factor of 2 is applied deeming the property as industrial property (though the same is refuted and agitated by the petitioner for the reasons mentioned herein after) LAC-3/1/2011 Page:-24/36 still the rates shown by the respondent do not correspond with the rates given by the LAC. The reasons mentioned by the LAC was that sale deed produced by the claimant pertained to freehold properties but the LAC did not take into account the order of Hon'ble High Court whereby this property was also ordered to be converted into freehold on 5.4.10. The second reason given was that the sale deed were out dated but even that is so then 12% per annum enhanced value upto the date of notice issued u/s 4 of LAC Act could have been considered. The Ld. LAC has found fault with the calculations of the petitioner but had failed to give reasons regarding his calculations and has not at all discussed as to what was the basis with the respondent to get the market value fixed at the value assessed by LAC. So reasons prevailing in the mind of LAC while passing the award in question is not available before this court. In these circumstances, the court should keep in mind the estimation and expectation of prospective buyers and sellers. Reference can be made to UOI Vs. Pramod Gupta and Ors 2005(12) SCC 1 wherein it has been held that :-

"24.-While determining the amount of compensation payable in respect of the lands acquired by the State, the market value therefor indisputably has to be ascertained. There exists different modes therefor.
LAC-3/1/2011 Page:-25/36
25.-The best method, as is well known, would be the amount which a willing purchaser would pay to the owner of the land." Reference can be made to the judgment in (Viluben Jhalejar Contractor Vs. State of Gujarat (2005) 4 SCC 789)."

27. It has been held in various pronouncements by different Forum that comparable sale value method is most relevant and appropriate method to determine the value of the land acquired so as to award compensation to the affected persons. Reference can be made to Union of India Vs. Pramod Gupta (supra) wherein it has been held that :

"The reference court, it is trite, has to apply the comparable sales method as also the situation of the land which is to be appreciated keeping in view the fact as to whether acquired land is similar to any land sold in the vicinity.
It is no doubt true that courts adopt, comparable sales method of valuation of land while fixing the market value of the acquired land. While fixing the market value of the acquired land, comparable sales method of valuation is preferred than other methods of valuation of land such as capitalization of net income method or expert opinion method. Comparable sales method of valuation is preferred because it furnishes the evidence for determination of the market value of the acquired land at LAC-3/1/2011 Page:-26/36 which a willing purchaser would pay for the acquired land if it had been sold in the open market at the time of issue of notification under Section 4 of the Act." Reference can be made to the judgment in (V. Hanu-manthua Reddy Vs. Land Officer & Mandal & ors (2003) 12 SCC 642).

28. The counsel for the petitioner has with the aid of several judgments contended that claimants are entitled to the highest value of the land and relied upon the sale deeds in this context as discussed in "National Cold Storage and Refrigeration Pvt Ltd Vs UOI and Ors" and asserted that the sale deed having highest value should be given consideration to award the compensation. In this context petitioners have relied upon the judgment in (1969) 1 MLJ (SC) 45, (1968) 2 SCJ 869, (1984) 86 Punj. RL 540.

29. The Ld. counsel for the respondent also relied upon a case of Methodist Church Vs UOI wherein this court had assessed the market value on the basis of residential land use way back in 2000 but the said judgment is also of no use since the acquisition in the present case were initiated in the year 2009 i.e. after the publication of notification of the change of land use vide notification dated 15.9.2006 duly issued by the Ministry of Urban LAC-3/1/2011 Page:-27/36 Development. Therefore, the said sale deeds are rejected in this case as well on the same reasoning as was given in case of "National Cold Storage and Refrigeration Pvt Ltd. Vs UOI and Ors".

Zone as R-2 W-1wherein the MCD also reiterated that the LAC had correctly assessed the market value as per residential land use and in support of his contention, he filed various documents. The very first document Ex. R2W-1/1 (Colly) was a copy of a list of persons affected by the grade separator at Rani Jhansi Road. The said witness stated in his evidence by way of an affidavit that the properties in question (acquired properties) were comprised of small residential units as well as some bungalows and none of them were commercial or institutional or industrial. I have gone through the said list Ex. R2W-1/1. The said list admittedly does not bear a mention of the land use in respect of the acquired properties and the witness also admitted that the said list, though prepared by the MCD, was mere a photocopy and did not bear the signatures of any official of any concerned department including the M.C.D itself. Even otherwise, the list Ex. RW-1/1 is contrary to the record available with the LAC and the LAC-3/1/2011 Page:-28/36 admissions made by the LAC in documents Ex. PW-1/1 and PW-1/3. The list is further rejected on the ground that the witness himself admitted that no documents were checked from any person before incorporating their names as owners or occupiers. The next document relied upon by the MCD was a photocopy of an alleged representation from some unknown person to the Chairman of the Minority Commission. I have gone through the said document and I hereby reject the said document since it has absolutely no relevance to the facts and circumstances of the present case. The above stated document was a mere photocopy and neither bore the property No. of the acquired property nor had any connection with the present matter.

31. The witness also stated that he did not have any knowledge of the documents which had been filed by him alongwith his affidavit and was a mere file fetcher and therefore the documents Ex. R2W-1/2 is outrightly rejected. The said witness had mainly relied on document Ex. R2W-1/5 and R2W-1/6 which are supposedly an extract of the Zonal Development Plan. The Ld. counsel for the petitioners strongly objected to the exhibiting of the above stated documents on the ground that the said documents were not LAC-3/1/2011 Page:-29/36 properly proved since the said documents were a mere extract downloaded from the web site and were not proved by way of the summoning of the original records. Even otherwise, the witness had himself admitted that all the noting/markings done on copy of the Zonal Development Plan were done by him i.e. for the purpose of giving evidence on 05.12.2013. The said noting can also not be relied by this court since the witness had himself admitted that the witness has no knowledge of the surrounding area since he was unable to state as to where property No. 18 was situated. Secondly, the petitioner has also LAC-3/1/2011 Page:-31/36 been able to prove the fact that khasra No. 280 min was abutted to khasra No. 277 by virtue of PW-1/2 & PW-1/3 which had already been acquired vide award No. 10/LAC/N/10-11 and the market value of the said area has already been assessed by this court as per commercial land use vide order dated 30.4.2013 in "National Cold Storage and Refrigeration Pvt Ltd Vs UOI and Ors" and this case is also squarely covered by that judgment. Moreover, the existence of any residential unit (if any), though the beneficiaries have failed to prove existence of any residential unit, will have absolutely no adverse effect on the market value of the land under the present reference on account of the fact that it was a commercially declared road and the same analogy has also been applied by the LAC itself in other subsequent award which have been relied by the petitioners during arguments being Awards bearing No 11/LAC/N and 12/LAC/N which were for the same project and involve the same beneficiary (MCD).
