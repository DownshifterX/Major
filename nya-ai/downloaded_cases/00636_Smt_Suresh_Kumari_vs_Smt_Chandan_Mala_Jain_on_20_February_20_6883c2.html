Smt. Suresh Kumari vs Smt. Chandan Mala Jain on 20 February, 2014

JUDGMENT

1. Plaintiff has filed the suit for recovery of possession, arrears of Suit No. 544/11 Page 1 of 18 pages 2 rent, damages and mandatory injunction against the defendants. Case of the plaintiff in brief is that she is the owner / landlady of property bearing no. 1, Nehru Kutia, Malka Ganj, Subzi Mandi, Delhi­7. Sh. A.K. Jain was inducted as a tenant in respect of a portion of the ground floor of the suit property in the year 1997 on a monthly rent of Rs. 6000/­. The tenancy was created orally. The monthly tenancy started from the first day of each calender month. Sh. A.K. Jain expired on 27.08.2006 leaving behind defendant no. 1 to 4 as his legal heirs. Sh. A.K. Jain did not tender the rent w.e.f 01.01.2003. After his death also defendants herein did not pay the arrears of rent and they also did not pay the current rent. As per the directions of the Hon'ble Supreme Court regarding polluting industries, the tenanted property was sealed on 03.01.2007. At that time the property was in the power and possession of the defendants. Plaintiff asked the MCD to de­seal the property and undertook to use it for residential purpose. MCD did not de­seal the property. The defendants assaulted the plaintiff and her family members. FIR No. 233/07 was registered against them. The defendants filed a civil suit titled Chander Mala Jain Vs. Suresh Kumari in the Court of Civil Judge, Delhi which was registered as Suit No. 11/07. In that suit, the defendants produced forged rent receipts. Plaintiff got FIR No. 349/07 registered against them at PS Subzi Mandi under Section 420/468/471 IPC. On the complaint of Suit No. 544/11 Page 2 of 18 pages 3 defendants, FIR No. 146/07 under Section 323/341/506 IPC was registered against the husband of the plaintiff. Plaintiff sent legal notice dated 02.05.2007 to the defendants through registered post and the defendants sent reply dated 10.05.2007. The defendants are in unauthorized possession of the suit property since 01.06.2007 and they have also stopped paying the electricity bills. The electricity bill dated 04.01.2007 for Rs. 6100/­ and 02.03.2007 for Rs. 5420/­ have been paid by the plaintiff to avoid disconnection. In the present suit, the plaintiff has claimed the arrears of rent at the rate of Rs. 6000/­ per month for three years upto the date of filing of the suit, a sum of Rs. 11520/­ towards the electricity charges. She has prayed for a decree of possession and she has also claimed damages at the rate of Rs. 15000/­ per month for the period 01.06.2007 to 31.07.2007. She also claims pendente lite and future damages at the rate of Rs. 15000/­ per month. MCD was impleaded as defendant no. 5 in the suit but it was deleted by the plaintiff on 27.01.2009.

2. Defendants have contested the suit. In the written statement, it is stated that Sh. A.K. Jain was a protected tenant under the Delhi Rent Control Act. The rent was Rs. 300/­ per month. After the death of Sh. A.K. Jain, the tenancy was inherited by the defendants. The suit is barred by section 50 of the DRC Act. After the death of Sh. A.K. Jain, rent was tendered to the plaintiff but Suit No. 544/11 Page 3 of 18 pages 4 she did not accept the same. The defendants have completed the formalities for de­sealing of the property but the plaintiff is preventing the same. On merit, the allegations made in the plaint are denied. Plaintiff filed replication and she has reiterated her case.

3. Issues were framed vide order dated 27.01.2009 and the same are:­ Issue No. (1):­ Whether the rate of rent was Rs. 6000/­ per month as claimed by the plaintiff or Rs. 300/­ as claimed by the defendant no. 1 to 4 ? (OPP­OPD1 to 4) Issue No. (2):­ Whether the suit is barred under Section 50 of the Rent Control Act ? OPD1 to 4 Issue No. (3):­ Whether the tenancy has been validly terminated by the plaintiff ? OPP Issue No. (4):­ Whether the plaintiff is entitled for the relief of possession ? OPP Issue No. (5):­ Whether the plaintiff is entitled for the relief of arrears of rent and damages, if so, to what extend and for which period ? OPP Issue No. (6):­ Relief.

4. Plaintiff examined herself as PW­1 and the documents have been exhibited in her statement as Ex.PW1/1 to Ex.PW1/35. She Suit No. 544/11 Page 4 of 18 pages 5 has also examined PW­2 Sh. Sanjeev Seth. He has relied upon the document Ex.PW1/20 and he has also identified his signatures on the counter foils of the rent receipts Ex.PW1/21 to Ex.PW1/35.

5. The defendants have examined DW­1 Sh. Rajkumar Jain, DW­2 Sh. Amit Jain and DW­3 Sh. Hari Chand. DW­1 and DW­2 have exhibited their affidavits as Ex.DW1/A and Ex.DW2/A respectively. Copy of FIR No. 146/07 PS Subzi Mandi has been produced as Ex.DW1/B. DW­3 has produced the documents Ex.DW3/1 and Ex.DW3/2.

6. I have heard the arguments of ld counsel for the parties and I have perused the record. The issues are decided as hereunder:­ Issue No. (1):­ Whether the rate of rent was Rs. 6000/­ per month as claimed by the plaintiff or Rs. 300/­ as claimed by the defendant no. 1 to 4 ? (OPP­OPD1 to 4) The tenancy was created in favour of Late Sh. A.K. Jain orally. Rent receipts were also not issued. It is an admitted fact between the parties that Late Sh. A.K. Jain had good relations with the family of the plaintiff. It is not the case of the defendants that the plaintiff refused to issue the rent receipts to late Sh. A.K Jain despite demand for the same. There is no documentary evidence regarding the rate of rent settled between the plaintiff Suit No. 544/11 Page 5 of 18 pages 6 and Late Sh. A.K. Jain. The plaintiff has tried to prove the rate of rent by drawing support from the statement of PW­2. PW­2 has stated that he was inducted as a tenant in the hall measuring 8' X 24' on the ground floor of the suit property for the period 09.02.1993 to 30.04.1994 on a monthly rent of Rs. 1400/­. The rent agreement executed between the plaintiff and PW­2 is Ex.PW1/20. Ld counsel for the plaintiff has argued that PW­2 was having 1/3rd area of the hall on the ground floor in the year 1993 and he was paying Rs. 1400/­ per month. Therefore the claim of the plaintiff that the rent settled between her and Late Sh. A.K. Jain in the year 1997 was Rs. 6000/­ per month is probable. Ld counsel for the plaintiff has also referred to the statement of DW­3. DW­3 has stated that he is in possession of Teh Bazari side measuring 8' X 9' and he is paying license fee to the MCD at the rate of Rs. 9500/­ per annum. Ld counsel for the plaintiff submitted that if the license fee for a site measuring 8' X 9' is Rs. 800/­ per month approximately, the rent for the hall measuring 17' X 33' could be Rs. 6000/­ per month. Ld counsel for the plaintiff also submitted that it has to be considered that the property was let out to Late Sh. A.K Jain for commercial purpose and therefore the rate of rent was not abnormal. Ld counsel for the defendants has assailed the testimony of PW­2. He submitted that PW­2 has not produced any document to show that he was doing the business of medicine from the suit Suit No. 544/11 Page 6 of 18 pages 7 property in the year 1993. The exact portion under the tenancy of PW­2 is not shown in the site plan Ex.PW1/1 and the rent receipts Ex.PW1/21 to Ex.PW1/35 can be created afterwards.

7. The Court has to apply the test of preponderance of probability while deciding the present issue. The rent agreement Ex.PW1/20 and the rent receipts Ex.PW1/21 to 1/35 were filed along with some other documents after framing of the issues. Application of the plaintiff to file the additional documents was allowed by Ld Predecessor vide order dated 24.11.2009. In the cross examination of PW­2, it is generally suggested that Ex.PW1/20 is a forged and fabricated document. It was the duty of the defendants to point out specific deficiency in the document to PW­2 which create doubt over the document. Similarly attention of PW­1 was to be drawn towards the deficiencies in the documents specifically so that she could answer the same. Unless and until the defendants draw the attention of the witnesses of the plaintiff specifically to the aspect of the documents, which are doubtful, adverse inference can not be drawn against the plaintiff. Had the defendants been specific in their allegation regarding the documents Ex.PW1/20 and Ex.PW1/21 to Ex.PW1/35, the plaintiff/PW­2 could have explained the same and the plaintiff could have also prayed for permission to lead additional evidence to remove the doubt. The Suit No. 544/11 Page 7 of 18 pages 8 defendants had the opportunity to deliver interrogatories to the plaintiff regarding the business done by PW­2 from the suit property and they could have also asked PW­2 to produce the documents in this regard but they have not done so. The rate of license fee being paid by DW­3 makes the rate of rent claimed by the plaintiff probable. The defendants claim that the rent receipts mark A to E were issued by the plaintiff but in their evidence DW­1 and DW­2 refrained from producing the original of these receipts. In fact the defendants have given up the rent receipts in their evidence. For the same receipts (mark A to E) criminal case has been registered against the defendants. In the criminal case, FSL result has also come against them. Copy of the FSL result has been filed as Ex.PW1/9 but there is no need to go into the details of the report as the defendants have themselves given up the receipts mark A to E. Adverse inference is to be drawn against the defendants for the fact that they claimed reliance on the rent receipts mark A to E but abandoned the same during their evidence. Considering the evidence adduced by the parties on the present issue, the plaintiff has proved that the rent of the premises is Rs. 6000/­ per month. The defendants have failed to prove that the rent is Rs. 300/­ per month. Accordingly the issue is decided in favour of the plaintiff and it is held that the rent of suit property is Rs. 6000/­ per month.

   8.  Issue No. (2):­       Whether the suit is barred under Section 50 of 
       the Rent Control Act ? OPD1 to 4

Section 3 (c) of the Delhi Rent Control Act 1958 provides that the provisions of the Act do not apply to the premises whose rent exceed Rs. 3500/­ per month. The defendants took the objection regarding bar to the jurisdiction of the Civil Court on the basis that the rent of the suit property is Rs. 300/­ per month. While deciding issue no. 1, it has been held that rent is Rs. 6000/­ per month. Even if, the rent was below Rs. 3500/­ per month, the suit would have been maintainable for the prayer for recovery of arrears of rent. As it has been specifically held that the rent is Rs. 6000/­ per month, the suit is not barred under Section 50 of DRC Act in respect of any part of the claim of the plaintiff in the present suit and the issue is decided against the defendants.

9. Issue No. (3):­ Whether the tenancy has been validly terminated by the plaintiff ? OPP Before filing of the suit, plaintiff gave notice Ex.PW1/10 to the defendants. This notice is dated 02.05.2007. It was mentioned in the notice that after the death of Late Sh. A.K. Jain, the defendants became tenants by operation of law. Tenancy is a heritable right. Admittedly the tenancy of Late Sh. A.K. Jain was not terminated during his life time and therefore the defendants became tenants by operation of law of succession. Vide notice Suit No. 544/11 Page 9 of 18 pages 10 Ex.PW1/10 their tenancy was terminated w.e.f 31.05.2007. Under Section 106 (1) Transfer of Property Act, 1882 , 15 days notice was required to terminate the tenancy as it was an oral tenancy. In the written statement, it is stated that the suit property comprises of one factory and one toilet. The purpose of letting out is stated to be commercial. The defendants have not specifically claimed that the property was let out for manufacturing purposes but they have stated that the suit property was used as a factory. The plaintiff has stated that late Sh. A.K. Jain was doing business of plastic. Even if Late Sh. A.K Jain was doing manufacturing activity from the suit property, the tenancy could have been terminated by a 15 days notice as it was an oral tenancy. In AIR 2001 Supreme Court 1696 it has been held by the Hon'ble Supreme Court that where oral lease is granted for manufacturing purposes, it would be a month to month lease. Since the lease in this case was oral, the tenancy was month to month which could be terminated by 15 days notice. Six months notice under Section 106 (1) TP Act was not required. Ld counsel for the defendants has failed to point out any other defect in the notice. The notice Ex.PW1/10 validly terminated the tenancy of the defendants. The issue is decided in favour of the plaintiff.

       possession ? OPP

The property is not covered by the DRC Act. After termination of the tenancy, the defendants are liable to hand over the possession of the suit property to the plaintiff. Therefore the plaintiff is entitled for the relief of possession and the issue is decided in her favour.

11. Issue No. (5):­ Whether the plaintiff is entitled for the relief of arrears of rent and damages, if so, to what extend and for which period ? OPP In the present suit, plaintiff has claimed arrears of rent, damages and also electricity charges. The claim of electricity charges is not specifically mentioned in the issue but the parties were well aware of the claim and ld counsel have also submitted their arguments on the claim regarding the electricity charges. Therefore I shall consider the claim for electricity charges also in the present suit. It would be appropriate to consider all claims under the present issue separately. I shall firstly take up the claim for arrears of rent.

12. Arrears of rent:­ Plaintiff has claimed that Late Sh. A.K. Jain had not been paying the rent since 01.01.2003. He expired on 27.08.2006. In paragraph 10 of the plaint, it is stated that the prayer for arrears of rent was being made for a period of three years only and a sum of Rs. 2,16,000/­ has been claimed under Suit No. 544/11 Page 11 of 18 pages 12 this head. The suit was filed on 13.08.2007. In the prayer clause, the period for which the arrears of rent is claimed is mentioned as 01.06.2003 to 31.05.2006. The plaintiff could have claimed the arrears of rent only for a period of three years prior to the date of filing of the suit. According to the plaintiff, the tenancy started on the first day of each calender month. Therefore the arrears beyond 01.08.2004 could not have been claimed in the present suit. The property was sealed by MCD on 03.01.2007. The defendants did not give up the tenancy after sealing of the property and there is no specific plea in the written statement regarding suspension of the rent. However the Court has to consider the fact that the property was let out for manufacturing purposes and it was sealed as per directions of the Hon'ble Supreme Court. It is not the case of the plaintiff that the property was sealed because Late Sh. A.K. Jain or the defendants were doing any activity which was beyond the scope of the purpose of the letting out of the suit property. Therefore the defendants are entitled for suspension of the rent for the period for which the suit property remains sealed. It is also to be considered that for de­sealing of the property, the plaintiff applied to the MCD and she stated that she would be using the property for residential purpose. If she has represented to the MCD that she would be using the property for residential purposes, she can not claim rent from the defendants for the period when the property has Suit No. 544/11 Page 12 of 18 pages 13 remained sealed and the defendants are entitled to suspension of the rent as mentioned above. Therefore in the present suit, the plaintiff can claim rent w.e.f 01.08.2004 to 03.01.2007 only. Having ascertained the period for which the plaintiff could have claimed the arrears of rent, I shall proceed to examine the merit of the claim.

13.Defendants relied upon the rent receipts mark A to E but abandoned the same. Therefore they have no documentary evidence to show that the rent was paid by Late Sh. A.K. Jain during his life time. They have themselves stated that they tendered the rent after the death of Late Sh. A.K. Jain but the plaintiff refused to accept the rent. The claim of the defendants that the rent was Rs. 300/­ per month has been rejected. Therefore even if they tendered the rent at the rate of Rs. 300/­ per month after the death of Late Sh. A.K. Jain, it was an invalid tender and the plaintiff can recover the arrears of rent from them with interest. Again we have to consider the merit of the claim by bifurcating the same into two periods. The plaintiff has not specified the date on which the rent was payable. The receipts Ex.PW1/21 to Ex.PW1/35 show that the rent for a particular month was paid in advance in the beginning of the month and this is the usual practice. Late Sh. A.K. Jain expired on 27.08.2006. Normally he would have paid the rent for the month Suit No. 544/11 Page 13 of 18 pages 14 of August, before his death. Therefore the first period would be 01.08.2004 to 31.08.2006 and the second period would be from 01.09.2006 to 03.01.2007 which falls under the tenancy of the defendants. If it is found that late Sh. A.K Jain had not paid the rent during his life time, the defendants would be liable to pay the same not as tenants but as legal heirs of Late Sh. A.K. Jain.

14.According to plaintiff rent receipts were not issued to late Sh. A.K. Jain as he was close to her family. Ld counsel for the plaintiff argued that Late Sh. A.K. Jain was not keeping well and therefore the plaintiff did not initiate any action during his life time for recovery of the arrears of rent. Section 2 of the Indian Evidence Act 1872 defines the term 'proved'. It is provided that a fact is said to be proved when, after considering the matters before it, the Court either believes it to exist, or considers its existence so probable that a prudent man ought, under the circumstances of the particular case, to act upon the supposition that it exists.

15.The plaintiff has not stated that from 01.01.2003 till the death of Late Sh. A.K. Jain i.e 27.08.2006, no business was conducted from the suit property due to the illness of Late Sh. A.K. Jain. If late Sh. A.K. Jain was not able to conduct any business and pay the rent to the plaintiff, the plaintiff would have normally requested him Suit No. 544/11 Page 14 of 18 pages 15 to vacate the property. She might not have initiated legal proceedings against late Sh. A.K.Jain due to personal relations but she would not have waited for almost three and half years when the rent was not being paid. If Late Sh. A.K. Jain was not able to earn from the suit property, there was no reason for the plaintiff to allow him to occupy the suit property for such a long period specially when she was losing Rs. 6000/­ per month. Rent receipts were not issued and Late Sh. A.K. Jain was the person who could have effectively answered the claim. Unfortunately he is no more. In her cross examination, Plaintiff has stated that the rent was paid by Late Sh. A.K. Jain to her father in law. PW­2 stated that the rent was paid to the plaintiff through her husband. Husband of the plaintiff has not stepped into the witness box and her father in law has expired. The plaintiff did not use to receive the rent personally and therefore her oral testimony regarding the arrears of rent is not of much value. Plaintiff has claimed that Late Sh. A.K. Jain was not paying the rent and she had to prove this allegation as required under Section 101 of the Indian Evidence Act 1872. She can not succeed in her claim on the basis that the defendants did not prove that the rent was paid by Late Sh. A.K. Jain for the aforesaid period. Therefore the claim for arrears of rent for the period 13.08.2004 to 31.08.2006 can not be granted to the plaintiff.

16. Now I shall take up the second period for the claim for arrears of rent and i.e 01.09.2006 to 03.01.2007. According to the plaintiff, the defendants did not pay the rent despite demand. Defendants claimed that the rent was tendered but not accepted. It has been seen that even if the rent was tendered by the defendants, it was invalid tender as it was not tendered according to the actual rate of rent. It may again be noted that the defendants have deliberately given up the rent receipts mark A to E and this shows that the rent was not paid by them. Therefore the defendants are liable to pay the arrears of rent at the rate of Rs. 6000/­ per month for the period 01.09.2006 to 31.12.2006 and the proportionate rent from 01.01.2007 to 03.01.2007.

17. Damages:­ Plaintiff has claimed damages at the rate of Rs. 15000/­ per month for the period 01.06.2007 to 31.07.2007. The tenancy of the defendants was terminated vide notice Ex.PW1/10 dated 02.05.2007 but they could not have surrendered the possession even if they wanted to do so as the property was sealed by the MCD. Even if the defendants did not give up their claim of tenancy, they are not liable to pay damages as the rent itself would have remained suspended. Therefore the plaintiff is not entitled for the damages as claimed.

18. Electricity charges:­ Plaintiff has claimed the electricity charges Suit No. 544/11 Page 16 of 18 pages 17 for the bills Ex.PW1/17 and Ex.PW1/18. Ex.PW1/18 is bill dated 27.12.2006. The property was sealed by MCD on 03.01.2007. Therefore the defendants are liable to pay the electricity charges against bill EX.PW1/18. Ex.PW1/17 is bill dated 27.01.2007. For the bill Ex.PW1/18 which was for the month of December, 2006, reading was taken on 20.12.2006. The defendants must have consumed some electricity from 20.12.2006 to 03.01.2007 and therefore there is a bill which is Ex.PW1/17. The defendants are liable to pay the amount of Ex.PW1/17 also. Therefore the plaintiff is entitled for a sum of Rs. 11520/­ towards the electricity charges.

19. Issue No. (6):­ Relief In view of the decision on issue no. 1 to 5, the suit is decree in favour of the plaintiff with cost. The defendants are directed to hand over the possession of the suit property i.e ground floor hall at property bearing no. 1, Nehru Kutia, Malka Ganj, Subzi Mandi, Delhi­7 as shown in site plan Ex.PW1/1 to the plaintiff. As the property is sealed by the MCD, notional possession of the suit property will be deemed to have handed over to the plaintiff from the date of the decree. In addition, the defendants are directed to pay to the plaintiff a sum of Rs. 24580/­ towards the arrears of rent for the period 01.09.2006 to 03.01.2007 and a sum of Rs. 11520/­ towards the electricity charges. The defendants Suit No. 544/11 Page 17 of 18 pages 18 shall pay pendente lite and future interest at the rate of 6 % per annum to the plaintiff on the amount of arrears of rent and electricity charges.

Decree sheet be prepared. File be consigned to Record Room.
