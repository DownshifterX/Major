23 The question which requires to be considered at this stage is as to whether the suit property is Hindu undivided property ( HUF) in the hand of defendant no. 1, in which plaintiffs have their share being the grand sons of defendant no. 1.

24 The Hindu Succession Act 1956 has brought about a sea change in the matter of inheritance and succession amongst Hindus. Section 4 of the Act contains a non obstante provision in terms where of any text, rule or interpretation of Hindu Law or any custom or usage as part of that law in force immediately before the commencement of the Act, ceased to have effect with respect to any matter for which provision is made therein. Section 8 lays down the general rules of succession that the property of a male dying intestate devolve according to the provisions of the Chapter as specified in clause (1) of the Schedule. In the Schedule appended to the Act, natural sons and daughters are placed in Class-I heirs but a grandson, so long as father is alive, has not been included. Section 19 of the Act provides that in the event of succession by two or more heirs, they will take the property per capita and not per stripes, as also tenants-in-common and not as joint tenants.

Suit no. 927-08 Page no. 8 /13

25 A Full Bench of Madras High Court in Addl. Commission of Income-tax, Madras Vs P.L. Karuppan Chettiar, reported AIR, 1979, Madras 1 has held that " under the Hindu law, the property of a male Hindu devolved on his death on his sons and grandsons as the grandsons also have an interest in the property. However, by reason of Section 8 of the Hindu Succession Act, 1956 the son's son gets excluded and the son alone inherits the property to the exclusion of his son. No interest would accrue to the grandson of P in the property left by him on his death. As the effect of Section 8 was directly derogatory of the law established according to Hindu law, the statutory provision must prevail in view of the unequivocal intention in the statute itself, expressed in Section 4(1) which says that to the extent to which provisions have been made in the Act, those provisions, shall over ride the established provisions in the texts of Hindu law. Accordingly, in that case K, alone took the properties obtained by his father, P, in the partition between them, and irrespective of the question as to whether it was ancestral property in the hands of K or not, he would exclude his son.

Further, since the existing grandson at the time of the death of the grandfather had been excluded, an after- born son of the son will also not get any interest which the son inherited from the father. In respect of the property obtained by K on the death of his father, it is not possible to visualize or envisage any Hindu undivided family".

26 The interpretation of Section 8 of Hindu Succession Act came up for consideration before the Hon'ble Supreme Court of India in Commissioner of Wealth tax, Kanpur Vs Chander Sen & Others reported as AIR 1986, SC, 1753. The Hon'ble Supreme Court of India has held that, " under the Hindu law the son would inherit the property of his father as Karta of his own family. But the Hindu Succession Act has modified the rule of Suit no. 927-08 Page no. 9 /13 succession. The Act lays down the general rules of succession in the case of males. The first rule is that the property of a male Hindu dying intestate shall devolve according to the provisions of Chapter II and Class I of the Schedule provides that if there is a male heir of Class I then upon the heirs mentioned in Class I of the Schedule. In interpreting provisions of Act it is necessary to bear in mind the Preamble to the Hindu Succession Act. The Preamble states that it was an Act to amend and codify the law relating to intestate succession among Hindus. In view of the Preamble to the Act i.e that to modify where necessary and to codify the law, it is not possible when Schedule indicates heirs in Class I and only includes son and does not include son's son but does include son of a predeceased son, to say that when son inherits the property in the situation contemplated by Section 8 he takes it as karta of his own undivided family. If a contrary view is taken it would mean that though the son of a predeceased son and not the son of the son who is intended to be excluded under Section 8 to inherit, the latter would be applying the old Hindu Law get a right by birth of the said property contrary to the scheme outlined in Section 8.

Furthermore the Act makes it clear by Section 4 that one should look to the Act in case of doubt and not to the pre-existing Hindu Law. It would be difficult to hold today that the property which devolved on a Hindu under Section 8 of the Hindu Succession Act would be HUF in his hand vis-a-vis his own son; that would amount to creating two classes among the heirs mentioned in Class I, the male heirs in whose hands it will be joint Hindu family property and vis-a- vis son and female heirs with respect to whom no such concept could be applied or contemplated. It may be mentioned that heirs in Class I of Schedule under Section 8 of the Act included widow, mother, daughter of predeceased son, etc. The express words of Section 8 of the Hindus Succession Act cannot be ignored and must prevail. The Preamble of the Act reiterates that the Act is inter alia, to 'amend' the law. With the background the express language Suit no. 927-08 Page no. 10 /13 which excludes son's son but included son of a predeceased son cannot be ignored.

27 The above legal preposition has been reiterated by the Hon'ble Supreme Court of India in Yodhishter Vs Ashok Kumar, AIR 1987 SC 558 and recently in Bhawar Singh Vs Puran reported as AIR 2008 SC 1490.

28 The above legal preposition makes it absolutely clear that after the commencement of Hindu Succession Act 1956 the property of a Hindu after his death devolved upon his Class I as per Section 8 of Hindu Succession Act and such heir acquired such property not as an ancestral property but as his separate property. In the present case, late Sh. Chuni lal, father of defendant no. 1 died intestate and after his demise, the suit property was transferred in the name of his wife Smt. Budhwanti vide lease deed Ex. PW-3/B. After the demise of Smt. Budhwanti, the said property has been acquired by defendant no. 1 and his brother being her heir as per Section 8 of Hindu Succession Act. The defendant no. 1 & his brother has subsequently, mutually divided the said property as per their share and the suit property fell into the share of defendant no. 1.

29 The defendant no. 1 has acquired the suit property being Class 1 heir of late Sh. Chuni Lal and Smt. Budhwanti and plaintiff no. 1 and 2 have not acquired any share in the suit property being the grand sons of defendant no. 1 because they are not the Class 1 heir of late Sh. Chuni Lal and Smt. Budhwanti and they cannot acquired any share in the suit property during the life time of defendant no. 1. The plaintiff no. 1 & 2 and defendant no. 2 would be entitled to acquire any share in the suit property only and only after the Suit no. 927-08 Page no. 11 /13 demise of defendant no. 1 and not during his life time. The defendant no. 1 is exclusively entitled to deal with the suit property in any manner. The plaintiffs and defendant no. 2 are not entitled for any share during the lifetime of defendant no. 1.

30 In view of the above facts and circumstances, I am of the considered view that plaintiffs have not acquired any share in the suit property and they are neither entitled for partition nor for possession of any portion of the suit property. The plaintiffs have thus failed to discharged the onus of issue no. 1, same is accordingly decided against the plaintiffs. 31 Issue no. 2: Whether plaintiffs are entitled to decree of rendition of accounts against defendant no. 1? OPP Issue no.3: Whether plaintiffs are entitled to any interest @ 18% p.a from date of filing of suit till realisation from defendant no. 1? OPP Issue no. 4: Whether plaintiffs are entitled to decree of permanent injunction as claimed? OPP In view of my finding on issue no. 1, I am of the considered view that plaintiffs are neither entitled for decree of rendition of account nor entitled for any interest over the said amount. The plaintiffs are also not entitled for a decree of permanent injunction against the defendant. The plaintiffs have thus failed to discharge the onus of issue no. 2, 3 & 4, same are accordingly decided against the plaintiffs.

32 Issue no. 5: Whether plaintiffs no. 1 & 2 are the sons and plaintiff no. 3 wife of Sh. Sanjay Rattan? OPP The plaintiffs have proved on record that plaintiff no. 1 & 2 are the sons of plaintiff no. 3 who is wife of Sh. Sanjay Rattan, son of defendant no. 1. The defendant no. 1 has also not disputed the said fact in the written statement.

33 In view of the above facts and circumstances, I am of the considered view that plaintiffs have successfully discharged the onus of issue no. 5, same is accordingly decided in favour of the plaintiffs.

34 Issue no. 6: Whether defendant no. 1 had disowned Sanjay Rattan Sharma from his movable or immovable property in 1994? OPD1 The defendant no. 1 has taken a plea that he disowned Sh. Sanjay Rattan Sharma from his movable and immovable property in the year 1994. The plaintiffs have controverted the said fact in the corresponding para of replication.

35 The onus to prove the fact that defendant no. 1 had disowned his son Sh. Sanjay Rattan Sharma from his movable and immovable property is upon defendant no. 1, but defendant no. 1 has not led any evidence on record in order to substantiate the above plea. In the absence of any evidence in this regard, I am of the considered view that defendant no. 1 has failed to discharge the onus of issue no. 6, same is accordingly decided against the defendant.

36 RELIEF.

In view of the above, the suit filed by the plaintiffs against the defendants is dismissed. No order of cost. Decree sheet be accordingly prepared. File be consigned to record room after due compliance.
