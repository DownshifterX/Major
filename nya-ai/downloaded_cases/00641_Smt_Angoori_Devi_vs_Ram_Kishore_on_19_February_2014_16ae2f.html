   SUIT FOR EVICTION & POSSESSION MESNE PROFITS AND
                       PERMANENT INJUNCTION
JUDGMENT:

­

1. Vide this Judgment, I shall decide a suit for eviction & possession, mesne profits and permanent injunction filed by the plaintiff.

2. The brief facts of the case as per the plaintiff is that plaintiff is the owner /landlady of the property N. RZ­D3/213, Street No. 9, Mahavir Enclave Dabri Road, Palam Delhi­45. Plaintiff is at Suit No. 438/2010 Page No. 1 of 19 present residing at F­222/56, Gali No. 31, Sadh Nagar, Palam Colony, Delhi 45 with her family. Plaintiff purchased the suit property vide sale deed dt. 18­07­1984. Plaintiff is an old widow and is living with her family which include her five sons out of which two are married who along with their wives and children live with the plaintiff. It is further submitted that plaintiff is suffering from various old age ailment and a separate accommodation is urgently required for her. The total living room at the disposal of plaintiff are two whereas her family member are ten. Defendant is the brother of the plaintiff and has been residing in the suit premises as tenant since 1999 when he was first inducted in the suit property. The two rooms kitchen and toilet were let out to the defendant at monthly rent of Rs. 2000/­ per month. The defendant being the brother of the plaintiff , the property was let out to afford him accommodation for few months till he would find appropriate accommodation for himself and his family and therefore, no written agreement was executed between the parties. The roof rights were not let out. Premises were let out for residential purpose only. The remaining portion of the said property apart from two rooms, kitchen and a toilet , which has been let out to the defendant are in possession of the plaintiff. The plaintiff requires the said tenanted premises for residential purpose of the family of the plaintiff. The Suit No. 438/2010 Page No. 2 of 19 present accommodation shown in the green in the site plan is highly insufficient to met the demands of residence of the family of the plaintiff. There is no electricity connection in the premises and defendant has been illegally taping the electricity and is committing theft of electricity. The plaintiff had time and again told the defendant not to indulge in such activity. At the inception of tenancy it was agreed that the defendant would pay a refundable security of Rs. 10,000/­ and after receiving the plaintiff was to apply to Delhi Viduat Board for electricity connection. The defendant kept postponing the payment of security deposit and has never paid the same , even the monthly rent has not been paid after 01­05­2000. The theft of electricity by the defendant exposes the plaintiff for criminal action since she is the owner and landlady. The defendant is liable to vacate the suit premises on the short ground that the use and occupation of the suit premises by the defendant has turned illegal and unlawful. Defendant is habitual defaulter in payment of monthly rent, despite demand made by the plaintiff. On the contrary defendant kept abusing and threatening to kill the plaintiff and her sons, if they tried to evict the defendant or any demand or rent and police complaint in this regard was made on 05­05­2003. On 28­06­2003 also the defendant came to the house of the plaintiff and threatened the plaintiff and her family Suit No. 438/2010 Page No. 3 of 19 which was also reported to the police and to avoid police action and criminal proceedings the defendant compromise the matter in the police station and promised to vacate the premises by on or before 20­07­2003. On 03­08­2003 at about 9:30 PM, the defendant came to the house of the plaintiff with one Mr. Shook Yadav and goondas associate to threaten and intimidate the plaintiff and a complaint dt. 04­08­2003 was also made in this regard at PS Dabri. The defendant was in arrear of rent and therefore , the plaintiff asked the plaintiff to pay the due amount and to vacate the premises but the defendant lodged a false and factious complaint against the plaintiff with a view to harass the plaintiff and to disrepute her . Defendant neither paid monthly rent nor vacated suit premises rather filed a suit for injunction against dispossession, which was disposed of on the statement of the plaintiff as compromise. It is further submitted that due to aforesaid a legal notice dt. 09­11­2004 under section 106 TP Act terminating the tenancy of the defendant wef 31­12­2004 was sent through the counsel for the plaintiff to the defendant. This notice was sent by regd. post as well as UPC and was served on the defendant on or about 12­11­2004 but the defendant failed to comply with the said notice and is trespasser in the suit property from 31­12­2004 and is liable to pay damages for use and Suit No. 438/2010 Page No. 4 of 19 occupation of the suit premises at Rs. 3000/­ per month from 31­ 12­2004 till he vacate the suit premises. Defendant is also liable to pay Rs. 72,000/­ as arrear of rent from 01­01­2002 to 31­12­2004 at Rs. 2000/­ per month and Rs. 4320/­ as interest thereon at 18 % p.a. and damages of Rs. 12,000/­ for use and occupation from 31­12­2004 onwards of the suit premises at Rs. 3000/­ per month i.e. total of Rs. 88,320/­ and further liable to pay damages at Rs. 3000/­ per month till vacation of the suit premises. The plaintiff has also come to know that the defendant is intending to hand over the possession of the suit property to the third party and and if defendant is not restrained from creating third party interest, the plaintiff would suffer irreparable loss and injury , hence the present suit is filed.

3. On the other hand the defendant in his written statement has contended that the court has no jurisdiction to try the present suit because the relationship between the parties is that of landlord and tenant. Thus suit is barred under section 50 of DRC, Act. Plaintiff is guilty of blackmailing and taking undue advantage of illiteracy of the defendant. Rate of Rent at the time of inception of tenancy was Rs. 800/­ per month including electricity and water charges as per the oral agreement between the parties. No notice of increase of rent was served upon the defendant. Plaintiff is guilty of Suit No. 438/2010 Page No. 5 of 19 concealment of material facts and has not come to the court with clean hands. Before the court of Sh. Sanjeev Kumar Singh in the suit filed by the defendant for permanent injunction against dispossession it was stated by the plaintiff rent was Rs. 2000/­ per month excluding other charges where as herein it is stated that there is no water and electricity connection. At the time of inception of tenancy the plaintiff had promised to provide electricity very soon after receiving monthly rent from the defendant but till date she has failed to do so. The suit barred under section 115 of the evidence act on this ground also . Plaintiff is the landlady of the defendant was not owner of the suit property vide sale deed dt. 18­ 07­1984. Plaintiff and her family never resided in the suit property nor has purchased the suit property but was purchased on behalf of mother of the defendant for the plaintiff and later on all the papers after the death of the mother of the parties were kept with the plaintiff and she declared that she had got prepared the said sale deed. Due to this reason defendant hastily out of fear of dispossession filed the said suit as plaintiff had threatened that she had got prepared sale documents in her favour from the old illiterate mother of the parties. The suit is not valued property and proper court fees has not been affixed. Further the defendant denied all the allegations of the plaintiff which are made in his Suit No. 438/2010 Page No. 6 of 19 plaint and prayed that the suit of the plaintiff be dismissed.

4. The plaintiff has filed replication to the written statement filed by the defendant. In his replication the plaintiff has re­affirmed the contents in the plaint and denied all the allegation made by the defendant in his written statement and further submitted that the suit of the plaintiff be decreed.

5. Vide order dt. 24.10.2005, following issues were framed which are as under:­

i) Whether the suit of the plaintiff is barred by section 50 of the DRC Act?

ii) Whether the plaintiff has concealed the material facts and not entitled for discretionary relief? OPD

iii) Whether the plaintiff is entitled for eviction and possession of the suit property , as prayed against the defendant? OPP

iv) Whether the plaintiff is entitled for decree towards arrear of rent, interest and damages as prayed? OPD

v) Whether the plaintiff is entitled for pendentelite and future damages/mesne profits, as claimed by the plaintiff? OPP

vi) Whether the plaintiff is entitled for the relief of permanent injunction, as prayed? OPP

vii) Relief.

6. To prove his case plaintiff has examined herself as PW1. PW1 Suit No. 438/2010 Page No. 7 of 19 has filed her evidence by way of affidavit Ex. PW1/A & PW1/B. In her affidavit the PW1 has reiterated the facts mentioned in the plaint and relied upon the following documents:­

i) Copy of sale deed dt. 18­07­1984 is Ex. PW1/1.

ii) Copy of complaint made to PS Dabari on 05­05­2003 & 04­08­ 2003 are Ex. PW1/3 & 4.

iii) Copy of legal notice dt. 09­11­2004 is Ex. PW1/5, Postal receipt and UPC of the same are Ex. PW1/6 & 7 respectively.

iv) Site plan of the suit property is Ex. PW1/X.

7. Further the plaintiff has examined Sh. Rajesh Kumar as PW2 who has also filed his evidence by way of affidavit Ex. PW2/X. Both PW1 & PW2 were also cross­examined by the Ld. counsel for the defendant. During cross­examination PW2 was confronted with the documents Ex. PW2/D1, PW2/D2 & Mark PW2/D3 which are house tax receipt for the year 1995, khatoni in respect of suit property & house tax receipts dt. 05­10­2005 respectively.

8. On the other hand the defendants have examined Sh. Ram Kishore as DW1 who has filed his evidence by way of affidavit Ex. DW1/A. In his evidence the DW1 has reiterated the facts in his written statement. During cross­examination the DW1 was confronted with documents Ex.DW1/P­1 to DW1/P­3 which are certified copies of statement of plaintiff & defendant dt. 05­06­2003 Suit No. 438/2010 Page No. 8 of 19 and copy of order dt. 03­07­2004 passed in civil suit No. 89/2003.

9. Further the defendants have examined Sh. Durga Dass, Sh. Amar Nath, Sh.Vijay Kumar as DW2, DW3 & DW4, who have filed their evidence by way of affidavit Ex. DW2/A, DW3/A & DW4/A respectively.

All the defendant's witnesses were also cross­examined by the Ld. counsel for the plaintiff.

10. I have heard the counsel for both the parties and have gone through the record carefully.

11. My issue wise finding is as under:­

12.Issue No. 1 Whether the suit of the plaintiff is barred by section 50 of the DRC Act?

The burden to prove this issue was on the defendant. The contention of counsel for the defendant is that the rent of the suit premises is Rs. 800/­ per month and thus, the provisions of Delhi Rent Control Act applies and present suit is barred under S. 50 of the DRC Act.

13.It would be worthwhile to reproduce relevant provisions of the Delhi rent Control Act in this regard, which are as under:

1. Short title, extent and commencement. -
(1) This Act may be called the Delhi Rent Control Act, 1958.
Suit No. 438/2010 Page No. 9 of 19
(2) It extends to the areas included within the limits of the New Delhi Municipal Committee and the Delhi Cantonment Board and to such urban areas within the limits of the Municipal Corporation of Delhi as are specified in the First Schedule:
Provided that the Central Government; may, by notification in the Official Gazette, extend this Act or any provision thereof, to any other urban area included within the limits of the Municipal Corporation of Delhi of exclude any are from the operation of this Act or any provision thereof (3) It shall come into force on such date as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint.

3. Act not to apply to certain premises.­ Nothing in this Act shall apply­

(a) To any premises belonging to the Government;

(b) To any tenancy or other like relationship created by a grant from the Government in respect of the premises taken on lease, or requisitioned, by the Government:

Provided that where any premises belonging to Government have been or are lawfully let by any person by virtue of an agreement with the Government or otherwise, then, notwithstanding any judgment, decree or order of any court or other authority, the provisions of this Act shall apply to such tenancy;] [(c) To any premises, whether residential or not, whose monthly rent exceeds there thousand and five hundred rupees; or
(d)To any premises constructed on or after the commencement of the Delhi Rent Control (Amendment) Act, 1988, for a period of ten years from the date of completion of such construction;
Suit No. 438/2010 Page No. 10 of 19

14.For the purpose of the case at hand, if the area wherein the suit property is situated is covered by sub­section (2) of S. 1 of the Delhi Rent Control Act, then since the rate of rent is Rs. 800/­ per month i.e. Less than Rs. 3,500/­ per month thus, this court shall have no jurisdiction. But it is also essential that the area where the suit property lies is included within the area of New Delhi Municipal Committee or the Delhi Cantonment Board or urban area of Municipal Corporation of Delhi and a further notification is issued under proviso to Sub­section (2) of Section 1 of the Delhi Rent Control Act. But as is evident from S. 1(2) of the DRC Act, unless the area is so specified in the Schedule by a notification, the provisions of the Delhi Rent Control Act cannot be made applicable to that area. The defendant has not proved that the ingredients of Section 1 of the Delhi Rent Control Act are satisfied and that the area where the suit property lies is included within the area of New Delhi Municipal Committee or the Delhi Cantonment Board or urban area of Municipal Corporation of Delhi and a further notification is issued under proviso to Sub­section (2) of Section 1 of the Delhi Rent Control Act.

Thus, clearly, the defendant has failed to discharge his burden and thus is of proving this issue. This issue is accordingly decided in favour of the plaintiff & against the defendant. Suit No. 438/2010 Page No. 11 of 19

15. Issue No. 2 Whether the plaintiff has concealed the material facts and not entitled for discretionary relief? OPD The burden to prove this issue was on the defendant. The contention of the counsel for the defendant is that the plaintiff in a suit no. 89/2003 compromised between the parties before the court of Sh. Sanjeev Kumar Singh, the then Ld. Civil Judge stated that the rent of the suit property is Rs. 2,000/­ per month excluding other charges whereas in the instant suit it is stated that the suit premises has no electricity and water connection.

However in preliminary objection no. 3 of the written statement filed by the defendant, the defendant has stated that at the inception of the tenancy the plaintiff promised to provide electricity but she has failed to do so. Further, the defendant DW1 in his cross­examination stated that in 2000 there were no electricity and MCD water connection in the area where the suit property is situated as its on the agricultural land. DW2 Durga Dass and DW3 Amarnath also stated in their cross­examination that when they came to reside in the suit property there was no electricity connection and MCD water connection . Thus, clearly, the defendant has himself contradicted his own contentions and has failed to prove this issue. Accordingly, this issue is decided in favour of the plaintiff and against the defendant. Suit No. 438/2010 Page No. 12 of 19

16. Issue No. 3 Whether the plaintiff is entitled for eviction and possession of the suit property , as prayed against the defendant? OPP In the preliminary objection no. 1 of the written statement of the defendant, the defendant, he has admitted existence of the landlord and tenant relationship between the parties. The plaintiff has also proved the registered sale deed dated 18/7/1984 as Ex. PW1/1 according to which she has purchased the suit property from Sh. Ram Roop. Plaintiff has also proved the Khatoni of the year 1995 Ex. PW2/D2 where mutation qua the suit property in the name of the plaintiff has been carried out. Admittedly, the parties had entered into a oral tenancy. Even otherwise the defendant is estopped in view of provisions under S. 116 of the Evidence Act from assailing the plaintiff to be his landlord/owner. The contention of the defendant that plaintiff/PW1 and PW2 Rajesh Kumar have stated the suit property to be owned by the husband of the plaintiff is of no assistance since defendant is estopped in view of provisions under S. 116 of the Evidence Act from assailing the plaintiff to be his landlord/owner.

17.At this juncture it would be worthwhile to reproduce S. 106 of the Transfer of Property Act, which is as under:

Suit No. 438/2010 Page No. 13 of 19

106. Duration of certain leases in absence of written contract or local usage: In the absence of a contract or local law or usage to the contrary, a lease of immovable property for agricultural or manufacturing purposes shall be deemed to be a lease from year to year, terminable, on the part of either lessor or lessee, by six months' notice expiring with the end of a year of the tenancy;

and a lease of immovable property for any other purpose shall be deemed to be a lease from month to month, terminable, on the part of either lessor or lessee, by fifteen days' notice expiring with the end of a month of the tenancy.

Every notice under this section must be in writing, signed by or on behalf of the person giving it, and either be sent by post to the party who is intended to be bound by it or be tendered or delivered personally to such party, or to one of his family or servants at his residence, or (if such tender or delivery is not practicable) affixed to a conspicuous part of the property.

Clearly, a lease of immovable property for any other purpose than agricultural or manufacturing purposes, in the absence of a written contract and local usage, shall be deemed to be a lease from month to month, terminable, on the part of either lessor or lessee, by fifteen days' notice expiring with the end of a month of the tenancy. The notice of termination of tenancy must be in writing, signed by or on behalf of the person giving it, and either be sent by post to the party who is intended to be bound by it or be tendered or delivered personally to such party, or to one of Suit No. 438/2010 Page No. 14 of 19 his family or servants at his residence, or (if such tender or delivery is not practicable) affixed to a conspicuous part of the property.

18.The plaintiff PW1 has proved the termination of tenancy by sending of legal notice dated 9/11/2004 Ex. PW1/5 and postal receipts as proof of service, Ex. PW1/6 & UPC receipt as Ex. PW1/7. The defendant has denied receipt of the said notice. In the case of notices admittedly addressed at the proper address, sent by ordinary as also by registered post, there was presumption of its service under section 27 of General Clauses Act 1897 as also under section 114 illustration (f) of Indian Evidence Act. Though, the presumption was rebutable, but, a bald statement to that effect was not enough to rebut the presumption. (See : Fitter Peera Saheb vs. K. Balachandra Rao­AIR 1972 Mysore 14 and M/s. Madan & Co. vs. Wazir Jaivir Chand­AIR 1989 SC 630) Thus, the notice Ex. PW1/5 has been clearly served upon the defendant and clearly the tenancy stood terminated on 16/11/2004.

In view of the foregoing discussion, the plaintiff is entitled to decree of possession of the suit property. Accordingly, this issue is decided in favour of the plaintiff and against the defendant.

19.Issue No. 4 Whether the plaintiff is entitled for decree towards arrear of rent, interest and damages as prayed? OPD Suit No. 438/2010 Page No. 15 of 19 Issue No. 5 Whether the plaintiff is entitled for pendentelite and future damages/mesne profits, as claimed by the plaintiff? OPP Issue nos. 4 and 5 being inter­related are being taken up and decided together.

The contention of the plaintiff is that the suit property was let out to the defendant @ Rs. 2,000/­ per month. Whereas as per the defendant the monthly rent was Rs. 800/­. The PW2 Sh. Rajesh Kumar stated that the property comprising of one room bathroom laterine and one kitchen was let out to a Bihari in 1992 @ Rs. 1,000/­ per month. He also stated that his mama Sh. Pradeep resided in the property comprising of one room, bathroom, latrine, open space and one kitchen at the backside of the suit property from 1995 to 1998 @ Rs. 1,200/­ per month. The defendants are tenants since 1999 in the suit property comprising of two room, bathroom, latrine and one kitchen. Nothing has been brought on record by the plaintiff that the defendant was a tenant @ Rs. 2,000/­ per month in the suit property. Be that as it may, since the two versions as to the rate of rent are coming forward thus the rate of rent admitted by the defendant shall prevail in the absence of any documentary evidence to this effect. Thus, it is held that the defendant was a tenant at a monthly rent of Rs. 800/­ per month in Suit No. 438/2010 Page No. 16 of 19 the suit property.

20.Similarly, since no evidence has been led as to what is the rate of rent in the area for assessing the future and pendentelite damages/mesne profits then the pendentelite and future damages/mesne profits are taken to be @ Rs. 800/­ per month.

Furthermore, no evidence has been led by the defendant that he has been paying rent since 1/5/2000.

Thus, the plaintiff is entitled to arrears of rent from 1/1/2002 to 31/12/2004 @ Rs. 800 per month i.e. Rs. 28,800/­.

21.The plaintiff has also claimed the pendente­lite and future interest on the decreetal amount @ 18 % p.a. which in my opinion is very exorbitant and unreasonable. According to me, considering the market rate the plaintiff is entitled to pendente­lite and future interest @ 9 % p.a. on the decreetal amount of Rs. 28,800/­ from 1/1/2002 till the actual realization on the decreetal amount.

22.The defendant is also entitled to damages @ Rs. 800 per month from 31/12/2004 till the vacation of the suit property.

23.The plaintiff has also claimed the pendente­lite and future interest on the decreetal amount @ 18 % p.a. which in my opinion is very exorbitant and unreasonable. According to me, considering the current market rate the plaintiff is entitled to pendente­lite and future interest @ 9 % p.a. on the decreetal amount from filling of Suit No. 438/2010 Page No. 17 of 19 the suit till the actual realization on the decreetal amount. This issue is decided accordingly.

24. Issue No. 6 Whether the plaintiff is entitled for the relief of permanent injunction, as prayed? OPP In view of the findings in issue no. 3 above the plaintiff is entitled to the decree of permanent injunction being the landlord and the owner of the suit property. Clearly, the plaintiff has a prima­facie case in his favour, balance of convenience is also in favour of the plaintiff and the plaintiff would suffer irreparable loss if the injunction is not granted in her favour and thus is entitled to the decree of permanent injunction. The defendant, his agents, associates etc. are hereby restrained from transferring, sub­letting or parting with the suit property shown in red colour in the site plan PW1/X i.e. first floor of the property bearing no. RZ D­3/213, street no. 9, Mahavir Enclave, Dabri Road, Palam, Delhi­45 in favour of any third party.

25.Relief:

In view of the foregoing discussion, a decree of possession is passed in favour of the plaintiff and against the defendant and the defendant is directed to hand over the vacant possession of the suit property shown in red colour in the site plan PW1/X i.e. first floor of the property bearing no. RZ D­3/213, street no. 9, Mahavir Suit No. 438/2010 Page No. 18 of 19 Enclave, Dabri Road, Palam, Delhi­45 A decree for the recovery of Rs. 28,800/­ along with pendente­lite and future interest at the rate of 9 % p.a. from 1/1/2002 till the actual realization on the decreetal amount.
The defendant is also entitled to damages @ Rs. 800 per month from 31/12/2004 till the vacation of the suit property along with pendente­lite and future interest @ 9 % p.a. on the said amount from filling of the suit till the actual realization on the decreetal amount.
A decree of permanent injunction is also passed in favour of the plaintiff and against the defendant, his agents, associates etc. are restraining them from transferring, sub­letting or parting with the suit property shown in red colour in the site plan PW1/X i.e. first floor of the property bearing no. RZ D­3/213, street no. 9, Mahavir Enclave, Dabri Road, Palam, Delhi­45 in favour of any third party.
Cost of the suit is also awarded to the plaintiff. Decree sheet be prepared accordingly. File be consigned to Record Room after due compliance.
