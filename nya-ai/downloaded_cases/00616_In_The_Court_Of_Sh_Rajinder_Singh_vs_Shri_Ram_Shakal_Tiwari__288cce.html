APPLICATION FOR EVICTION OF TENANT UNDER SECTION 14 (1) (a) and (f) OF DELHI RENT CONTROL ACT ORDER / JUDGMENT :

1. Eviction in respect of premises No.R-6, Naveen Shahdara, Delhi - 110 032 (henceforth referred to as 'tenanted premises') as shown in Red colour in the Site Plan attached with the petition has been sought on the ground that the respondent / tenant had defaulted in payment of rent despite service of demand notice and hence, committed 'first default', as envisaged under Clause (a) to the proviso of sub-section (1) of Section 14 of the Delhi Rent Control Act, 1958 (in short 'DRC Act').

 1.1        It is further stated that the tenanted premises is an
old construction and it is in dilapidated condition. It may collapse any time and there is every apprehension of danger to the lives and properties of the persons residing in the premises as well as the neighbours and passers-by.
2. Brief resume of the facts is desirable, same is as under:
Petitioner claims that Premises No. R-6, Naveen Shahdara, Delhi - 110 032 was let-out to the respondent many years ago (exact date not mentioned in the petition) at monthly rent of Rs.3,200/- excluding electricity and water charges. No written rent agreement was executed.
2.1 The ground which has been delineated in the petition in Paragraph No.18 (a) is that of eviction u/s 14 (1) (a) of DRC Act for non-payment of rent. The petitioners claim to be the undisputed owners of the tenanted premises. 2.2 The petitioners received the last payment of rent in cash in September 2005, thereafter, the respondent has not paid any amount towards the arrears of rent. The tenant / respondent has neither paid nor tendered the whole of the arrears of rent since October 2005 @ Rs.3,200/- despite service of the legal notice on 23/05/2008. The Reply dated 23/07/2008 to the legal Notice was given on behalf of the tenant / respondent. 2.3 It is stated that the tenanted premises is in dilapidated condition. It may collapse any time and there is every apprehension of danger to the lives and properties of the persons residing in the premises as well as the neighbours and passers-by. The landlords / petitioners do not want the respondent to be their tenant anymore because the tenant has E No. 87/11 (Old No.E No.101/09) Page 2 to 15 violated the provisions of Delhi Rent Control Act, hence respondent is liable to be evicted under section 14 (1) (a) and (f) of D.R.C. Act.
3. Written statement was filed on behalf of the respondent. It is submitted that the tenanted premises was taken on rent from Smt. Kiran Arora in the year 1999 @ Rs.

2,000/- per month. In this regard, a written agreement was prepared on a plain non-judicial stamp paper. The same is in the custody of Smt. Kiran Arora. The petitioners are not the landlords. The respondent is regularly paying the rent. Due to acute financial problem, the respondent did not pay the arrears of rent since March 2008. The respondent is ready to make the payment of arrears of rent for the aforesaid period. The respondent with the consent of Smt. Kiran Arora has spent Rs. 15,000/- on the maintenance and white wash of the tenanted premises which has not been paid by the landlady (Smt. Kiran Arora). It is further stated that Smt. Kiran Arora has taken furniture worth Rs.12,000/- from the respondent, for starting coaching classes to be conducted by her son. This amount has also not been paid.

3.1 It is denied that the tenanted premises is in dilapidated condition. Apart from the respondent, other tenants are also residing in the premises, comprising the tenanted premises. There is no danger of collapse of the tenanted premises. One Mr. Dwarka is residing as a tenant on the first floor of the premises, comprising the tenanted premises. There is a separate meter of the tenanted premises. The respondent is directly paying the electricity charges to the BSES. With consent of Smt. Kiran Arora, the respondent got constructed an outer E No. 87/11 (Old No.E No.101/09) Page 3 to 15 room in the tenanted premises. Ms. Kiran Arora has not paid the expenses of construction of that room so far.

4. Replication to the written statement of the respondent filed by the petitioners wherein the allegations to the contrary have been controverted and the averments made in the petition have been reiterated to be correct and true.

5. On behalf of the petitioners, the petitioner no.2 Sh. Honey Arora examined himself as PW 1, he deposed by way of affidavit Ex. PW 1 / A. He relied upon the documents, such as the Will dated 10/09/1991, photocopy of the same is Mark A, Site Plan is Ex. PW 1 / 1, the legal Notice dated 23/05/2008 is Ex. PW 1 / 2, the received Registered AD Card is Ex. PW 1 / 3, the UPC is Ex. PW 1 / 5, the Reply dated 23/07/2008 to the legal Notice along with Envelope are Ex. PW 1 / 5.

5.1 The petitioners examined Ms. Kiran Arora (mother of the petitioners) as PW 2. She tendered her affidavit as Ex. PW 2 / B in evidence. She relied upon the documents, SPA dated 31/07/2008 in her favour, the same is Ex. PW 2 / A. Both the PWs were cross-examined on behalf of the respondent.

Vide Statement dated 11/11/2010, ld. counsel for the petitioners closed the evidence.

6. On behalf of the respondent, the respondent examined himself as RW 1. He tendered his affidavit Ex. RW 1 / A in evidence.

6.1 The respondent examined Sh. Dwarka Nath as RW 2. His affidavit of evidence is Ex. R.2. Both the RWs were duly cross-examined on behalf of the petitioners. Thereafter, the RE was closed.

7. Arguments heard on behalf of the parties.

 7.1        It was argued on behalf of the petitioners that in the

legal Notice Ex. PW 1 / 2, the petitioners have claimed themselves to be the owners. The Reply to the legal Notice is Ex. PW 1 / 5. The respondent stated that Smt. Kiran Arora is her landlady, she was examined as PW 2. She has stated that the petitioners are now the owners of the tenanted premises which they inherited by virtue of the Will Mark A. Initially, the respondent was inducted as a tenant by Smt. Kiran Arora. Later on, by virtue of the Will Mark A, the petitioners became the owners of the tenanted premises. The change of landlordship was intimated orally to the respondent. The rate of rent is Rs. 3,200/- per month, as stated in the Petition as well as by PW 2. RW 2 is also a tenant of the petitioners for the last ten years @ Rs.2,500/- per month. The portion under his tenancy is smaller than the portion under the tenancy of the respondent. It is not possible for the respondent to be a tenant in the larger portion as compared to RW 2 at a lesser rate i.e., Rs.2,000/- per month, as claimed by the respondent. The respondent has admitted that he has not paid the rent since March 2008.

8. With regard to the ground under Section 14 (1) (f) of the DRC Act, it was stated that RW 2 in his cross-examination admitted that there is a stone in the tenanted premises with the inscription "1958". It shows that the building is an old construction, rain water gets collected in the tenanted premises since the road in front of the tenanted premises is now at a higher level. It can harm the building.

9. It was argued on behalf of the respondent that the petitioners have not produced any evidence to show that the rate of rent was Rs.3,200/- per month. During cross-examination, E No. 87/11 (Old No.E No.101/09) Page 5 to 15 PW 1 stated that Dwarka Nath (RW 2) is neither their relative nor their tenant, but RW 2 stated that he was a tenant of the petitioners. No suggestion was given to RW 2 regarding the fact that he was the tenant of the petitioners. RW 2 was inducted as a tenant in the year 2004 @ Rs.2,500/- per month. In such circumstances, it is highly improbable that the respondent would be inducted as a tenant @ Rs.3,200/- per month in the year 1994.

10. In regard to the ground under Section 14 (1) (f) of the DRC Act, it was argued on behalf of the respondent that PW 1 admitted that he has never visited the tenanted premises, so he could not have any direct knowledge about the dilapidated condition of the tenanted premises. PW 2 stated that she made several complaints to the MCD about the dilapidated condition of the tenanted premises, but the same were not placed on record. PW 2 admitted that the only reason for filing the present petition was the arrears of rent. RW 2 is also residing on the first floor in the same premises, as the respondent. But no Notice under Section 14 (1) (f) of the DRC Act has been given to him.

11. In rebuttal, it was submitted on behalf of the petitioners that the respondent has admitted his default of rent since March 2008 till the filing of the petition. He claims to have adjusted the arrears of rent against Rs.15,000/- which he has spent on the maintenance of the tenanted premises and also Rs. 12,000/- i.e., the value of the furniture which he claims to have given to the son of PW 2. The expenditure made by the tenant / respondent can be adjusted towards the arrears of rent, only as per the provisions of Section 44 of the DRC Act.

12. On of the ground for eviction in the present petition is E No. 87/11 (Old No.E No.101/09) Page 6 to 15 under Section 14 (1) (a) DRC Act. In order to be entitled to an eviction order under clause (a) of sub section 1 of Section 14 of DRC Act, the petitioner is required to prove :-

(1)relationship of landlord and tenant, (2)rate of rent and the existence of arrears of rent legally recoverable as on date of service of demand notice, (3)service of notice of demand and (4)failure of the tenant to pay/tender the entire legally recoverable arrears of rent within stipulated period of two months from the date of service of demand notice.

RELATIONSHIP OF LANDLORD AND TENANT In this regard, the petitioners have relied upon the Will Mark A. The same is dated 10/09/1991. The said Will was executed by Ms. Jamuna Devi (grand-mother of the petitioners). The respondent stated that he was inducted as a tenant in the year 1994 by Smt. Kiran Arora (mother of the petitioners). The respondent has denied that the petitioners are the landlords of the tenanted premises.

13. Section 2 (e) of the DRC Act defines the word "landlord", it includes a person, who is entitled to receive the rent of any premises on behalf of somebody else.

14. Smt. Kiran Arora, the admitted landlady has not questioned / denied the landlordship of the petitioners in this case. There is no reason to disbelieve the authenticity of the Will, by virtue of the Will Mark A, the petitioners become entitled to receive the rent of the tenanted premises.

15. Section 109 of the Transfer of Property Act does not prescribe any particular method / mode for intimating the lessee (the tenant) about the change of lesser (landlord).

E No. 87/11 (Old No.E No.101/09) Page 7 to 15 In view of the above, the petitioners are held to be the landlords of the tenanted premises in this case.

RATE OF RENT AND THE EXISTENCE OF ARREARS OF RENT LEGALLY RECOVERABLE AS ON DATE OF SERVICE OF DEMAND NOTICE

16. The rate of rent as claimed by the petitioners is Rs. 3,200/- per month. No written agreement / document has been produced by the petitioners to prove the rate of rent. The respondent has asserted that the rate of rent was fixed at Rs. 2,000/- per month.

17. It was argued on behalf of the respondent that RW 2 is a tenant in the tenanted premises having two rooms in the tenancy @ Rs.2,500/- per month. RW 2 deposed (in 2014) that he is the tenant for the last ten years that is to say since 2004. It was further argued that the respondent was inducted as a tenant in the year 1994, as such, the rate of rent could not have been as high as Rs.3,200/- per month.

18. The petitioners examined two witnesses i.e., PW 1 and PW 2. Both these two witnesses stated that RW 2 is not a tenant, he is only a licensee and he is treated as a brother by PW 2 Smt. Kiran Arora. No license fee is charged from RW 2. Whereas, RW 2 appeared as a witness and deposed that he is a tenant on the first floor of the tenanted premises. No specific reason has been cited by the petitioners as to why RW 2 has deposed against their case. Both PW 1 and 2 stated that RW 2 is a licensee and no license fee is being charged from him. It is admitted that RW 2 is having two rooms on the first floor of the tenanted premises. No explanation has been given by the petitioners as to why they have given two rooms to the RW 2, E No. 87/11 (Old No.E No.101/09) Page 8 to 15 free of cost / rent / license fee, that too, for a long period of ten years. It dents the credibility of PW 1 and 2.

19. In the petition, the petitioners stated that the respondent was inducted as a tenant many years ago. Whereas, PW 2 stated that the respondent was inducted as a tenant in the year 1994. PW 1 during his cross-examination, also stated that the respondent was inducted as a tenant in the year 1994. It is not clarified, why in the petition the year of induction of the tenant was not specifically mentioned and was concealed. It further dents the credibility of PW 1 / petitioner.

20. RW 2 is an independent witness, no suggestion was given to him that he is an interested witness. Even otherwise, in the facts and circumstances of this case and in view of the testimony of RW 2, he does not appear to be an interested witness, since has no interest in the outcome of petition.

In view of the above, it emerges that RW 2 was inducted as a tenant in the year 2004 @ Rs.2,500/- per month in respect of two rooms on the first floor in the tenanted premises. Although, the portion under the tenancy of the respondent is bigger than the portion under the tenancy of the RW 2, still it is highly improbable that in the year 1994, the respondent was inducted as a tenant @ Rs.3,200/- per month. In such circumstances, the RWs appears to be more reliable with regard to the rate of rent. Accordingly, believing the respondent / RW 1, it is held that the rate of rent of the tenanted premises under the tenancy of the respondent was fixed at Rs.2,000/- per month. Further, it is not the case of the petitioners that the rate of rent was enhanced at any point of time.

               REGARDING ARREARS OF RENT

21. It is settled law that when the petitioner alleges non- payment of rent, the onus lies upon the respondent / tenant to prove that he has paid the rent. It has been held in Sukhanand Vs. IVth Additional District Judge, Bulendshahar & ors. 1994 (2) RCR (Rent) 408 that the onus to show payment of rent lies on the tenant and oral testimony of tenant in regard to the payment of rent claiming discharge of liability in this regard cannot be admitted to be worth reliance at all.

22. Similar view has been reiterated in the case titled as Raghubir Prasad Vs. Rajendra Kumar Gurudev, 1993(2) R.C.R. (Rent) 234, wherein it has been held that on default in payment of rent, the onus to show payment of rent lies on tenant. Further, in the case of Satya Prakash Vs. District Judge Ghaziabad, 1982(1) R.C.R. (Rent) 295, it was observed that if in a petition, tenant alleges that rent is paid, then as per Evidence Act, burden to prove payment lies on the tenant, as he alleges that payment is made.

23. Reference can also be made to the judgment titled as Karamchand Deojee Sanghavi Vs. Tulshiram Kalu Kumawat, 1992(1) RCR 118 wherein the Hon'ble Bombay High Court observed that in a case of eviction on arrears of rent, the onus would always be on tenant to prove that he has paid the rent.

In view of the above, the burden was upon the respondent to show that he has deposited the rent up-to-date.

24. The respondent in his Written Statement in Preliminary Objection No.4, admitted that he has not paid the rent since March 2008. In his affidavit of evidence Ex. RW 1 / A, the respondent stated that he has not paid the arrears of rent for E No. 87/11 (Old No.E No.101/09) Page 10 to 15 5-6 months since March 2008. This is an improvement made by the respondent in his evidence. The respondent in his cross- examination, admitted that he is a practicising lawyer. It is presumed that he is aware of the law and his legal rights. The respondent has not produced any rent receipts, whereas, the law gives a right to the tenant to ask for the rent receipts and the tenant has further legal remedies, in case, the landlord does not issue rent receipts despite requests. The burden is upon the tenant to show that he has deposited the rent. In the present case, the respondent has not produced any material to show that he has deposited the entire arrears of rent. The respondent has only claimed a set off with regard to the amount of Rs.15,000/- allegedly spent on the maintenance of the tenanted premises and Rs.12,000/- towards the value the furniture allegedly supplied to the son of the PW 2. This aspect shall be dealt with while deciding, whether the respondent has tendered or paid the whole of arrears of rent within the stipulated period after the service of legal notice. Accordingly, the respondent is held to be in arrears of rent since March 2008 @ Rs.2,000/- per month.

SERVICE OF NOTICE OF DEMAND

25. The Demand Notice dated 23/05/2008 is exhibited as Ex. PW 1 / 2. Reply to the legal notice is dated 23/07/2008. The respondent has not denied the service of notice of demand. FAILURE OF THE TENANT TO PAY / TENDER THE ENTIRE LEGALLY RECOVERABLE ARREARS OF RENT WITHIN STIPULATED PERIOD OF TWO MONTHS FROM THE DATE OF SERVICE OF DEMAND NOTICE

26. The respondent has not claimed that he has paid or tendered the whole of arrears of rent within the stipulated period E No. 87/11 (Old No.E No.101/09) Page 11 to 15 from the date of service of demand notice. The respondent has claimed that he paid Rs.15,000/- towards the maintenance of the tenanted premises. He further claimed that he supplied furniture worth Rs.12,000/- to the son of the RW 2. This amount was agreed to be settled towards arrears of rent. During cross- examination, the respondent / RW 1 admitted that he had not filed any bills in respect of the maintenance expenses incurred by him. It is observed that the respondent has not given any specific details of the time period / dates when the alleged maintenance expenses were made. These are only bald averments. The PW 2 denied that any permission was granted to the respondent to carry out repairs and the repair expenses shall be adjusted towards rent.

27. Section 44 of the DRC Act casts a duty on the landlord to keep the tenanted premises in good repair. Where the landlord neglects his duty, the tenant can get the necessary repairs done, after giving a notice in writing. The expenses incurred upon such repair can be adjusted towards the rent payable. However, such adjustment cannot exceed 1/12th of the rent payable for every year. It further provides that if with permission of the Controller, the tenant gets the repairs done. In such case, the amount adjustable shall not exceed one half of the rent payable for that particular year.

28. It is not the case of the respondent that he gave any notice in writing to the petitioner to carry out necessary repairs. It is also not the case of the respondent that he got permission from the Controller for carrying out necessary repairs. In such circumstances, the claim of the respondent about having adjusted the arrears of rent towards the amount spent on the E No. 87/11 (Old No.E No.101/09) Page 12 to 15 repair of the tenanted premises, is not legally tenable.

29. The respondent further claimed that he supplied furniture worth Rs.12,000/- to the son of the RW 2 (the admitted landlady). In this regard, the respondent could not produce any bills. The respondent also did not explain anything about the source of the furniture. It is not clear whether the respondent claims to have purchased the said furniture and supplied it to the son of the RW 2 or it was procured otherwise. There is no credible evidence on record to show that the respondent provided furniture worth Rs.12,000/- to the son of RW 2.

30. Even if it is presumed, that such furniture was supplied. The DRC Act does not provide for adjusting such amount towards arrears of rent.

In view of the above, it is held that the respondent failed to pay or tender the entire arrears of rent within the stipulated period from the date of service of notice.

It is observed that the present petition was filed on 20/01/2009. The respondent is in arrears of rent since October 2005. The legally recoverable arrears of rent will be only for the period of three years immediately preceding the date of filing of the present petition. The legally recoverable arrears of rent in the present case shall be w.e.f., 20/01/2006.

SECTION 14 (1) (f) OF THE DRC ACT

31. In a petition for eviction under Clause (f), the landlord must establish the following ingredients :

(a) the premises are unsafe or unfit for human habitation.
(b) the same are required bonafide for carrying on repairs.
(c) which cannot be carried out unless premises are vacated by the tenant.

E No. 87/11 (Old No.E No.101/09) Page 13 to 15

32. It is not any damage to the premises, which give rise to the right of eviction in favour of the landlord but the damage shall be one, which render the premises unsafe or unfit for human habitation. Whether the building is unsafe or unfit is a question of fact.

33. The petitioners have not led any cogent evidence to show that any part of the building is in dilapidated condition so as to make it unsafe and unfit for human habitation. The petitioners have not specifically pleaded as to what are the specific repairs which they intend to carry out at the tenanted premises. In the absence of disclosure of the specific repairs which are intended to be carried out at the tenanted premises, it cannot be inferred whether the repairs are so extensive or fundamental in character and are of such nature that the premises need to be necessarily vacated to carry them out.

34. The petitioners have not specifically pleaded that they will get the tenanted premises repaired after getting the respondent evicted. The petitioners have not got the plan for repairing sanctioned from the competent authority. Hence, their need of tenanted premises cannot be termed to be bonafide.

35. It was argued on behalf of the respondent that the petitioners as well as RW 2 are residing in the same premises, comprising of the tenanted premises. The respondent is occupying the ground floor portion. In my considered opinion, if the condition of the ground floor is dilapidated, as a necessary corollary the upper floors would also be unsafe, since the place is not in good condition. It is no-where stated by the petitioners that they need / want to get the entire premises vacated for repairs.

E No. 87/11 (Old No.E No.101/09) Page 14 to 15 In view of the above, I am of the opinion that none of the essential ingredients have been established so as to justify eviction under Clause (f) of Section 14 (1) DRC Act.

36. Therefore, as per the provisions of Section 15 (1) DRC Act, the respondent is directed to pay arrears of rent @ Rs. 2,000/- per month w.e.f., October 2005 till date along with interest @ 15% p.a. as per section 26 DRC Act within a period of one month from today after adjusting the rent which has already been deposited / paid in the Court.

Put up on 07/08/2014 for consideration of benefit U/s 14 (2) DRC Act. Copy of this judgment be also kept in the separate miscellaneous file.
