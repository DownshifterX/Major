                                     ORDER

1. This is a petition for eviction of tenant under Sections 14­D and E - 40/14 Smt. Poonam Chadha vs. Suresh Chand Jain Page 1 of 22 14 (1) (e) of the Delhi Rent Control Act, 1958 (herein after referred to as the 'DRC Act').

2. The petitioner Smt. Poonam Chadha filed a petition under Section 14­D as well as 14 (1) (e) of the DRC Act. Summons under prescribed format were served upon the respondent on 20.05.2014. An application seeking leave to defend the petition was filed on 04.06.2014. Petitioner filed reply to the same.

3. (a) The petitioner has sought an eviction against the respondent Suresh Chand Jain stating that the petitioner is a widow and owner/landlady of the property bearing no. 6/81, Gali No.1, Vishwas Nagar, Shahdara, Delhi­110032 measuring 100 sq. yds. and she had let out a shop/office on the ground floor to the respondent in the property as shown with red colour in the site plan attached.

(b) It is submitted that the petitioner is a victim of circumstances as her husband and elder son namely Deepak have expired on 17.03.2004 and 15.04.2008 respectively and now the petitioner solely depends upon her only son Tanu who is married and these days has not fixed source of income and is compelled to do temporary work for day to day care and medical expenses for the petitioner and rest of the family member and he is also dependent upon the petitioner for residence.

(c) It is submitted that the petitioner herself has been residing on rent @ Rs.11,000/­ per month. The petitioner has been suffering from various ailments and diseases including obesity, high B.P., heard, knee and joint problem etc. and she is unable to move to one place to other and to climb stairs and her medical papers copy are enclosed. It is submitted that the respondent and other tenants are taking undue advantage of poor health and financial conditions and two deaths in her family and always intend to grab the property of the petitioner.

(d) It is submitted that the tenanted premises is required bonafidely by the petitioner for her and her family's residence as she has no other suitable alternative accommodation available to live with her family. The petitioner is a religious and pious lady and wants to spend the remaining years of her life in her own house and to live with dignity and status.

(e) It is submitted that the petitioner requires one drawing room, one dining, three bed rooms, one room for guests, one room for Pooja, one for maid/nurse and besides this, the petitioner also requires a store room, space for kitchen and bath etc. But she has only one room in the suit property at first floor in her possession which is being used for storage of belongings of the petitioner and her family. It is submitted that relatives of petitioner use to visit frequently and E - 40/14 Smt. Poonam Chadha vs. Suresh Chand Jain Page 3 of 22 regularly for her care and well being but she is unable to accommodate them as per status due to shortage of space in rental property. Hence due to growing family and needs, the petitioner requires the premises for residence.

(f) It is submitted that few other rooms in the same building in residential/mixed land use area are under occupation of other tenants at ground and first floor, which have been used by them for office purposes or godown/shops and the petitioner is also willing to file petitions against them as per law for want of more space. It is submitted that the petitioner has no much resources and after demise of her husband, she is not in a position to afford huge rent for paying to her landlord and is compelled to surrender present rental accommodation shortly and hence this petition for speedy and immediate possession of tenanted premises for self occupation.

(g) It is submitted that the petitioner has not filed any other petition against the respondent. The need of the petitioner and her family is bonafide and genuine and she undertakes to use the same for the purpose as stated above and will not re­let or transfer to any third persona and will abide the provision of law. It is prayed that an eviction order be passed in favour of the petitioner and against the respondent.

4. Ld. counsel for the respondent has argued through his leave to E - 40/14 Smt. Poonam Chadha vs. Suresh Chand Jain Page 4 of 22 defend application that the present petition is false and frivolous and the petitioner has concealed material facts before this Court.

(a) Ld. Counsel for the respondent has argued that despite the suit property i.e 6/81, Gali No.1, Vishwas Nagar, Shahdara, Delhi­32, the petitioner has following properties (i) 32/19, Gali No.9, Bhikam Singh colony, Shahdara, Delhi­32 and (ii) 7/76, Arjun Gali Vishwas Nagar, Shahdara, Delhi­32 and the petitioner has sufficient accommodation with herself in the said properties.

(b) It is further argued that the suit property is a three storey building and the petitioner with ulterior motives got demolished the entire second floor of the suit property to create a false paucity of accommodation and with a view to harass the tenants in the suit property by making holes in the roof at the first floor and had been pouring water from the said holes to destroy the articles of the tenants lying in their respective shops. It is submitted that had the petitioner been in need of accommodation, she would not have got demolished the entire second floor of the suit property. The petitioner has four big rooms available in the suit property which are lying vacant and she has converted the three rooms in one big hall by way of removing walls between them and has affixed two shutters on the same. The accommodation lying vacant with the petitioner is shown in red colour E - 40/14 Smt. Poonam Chadha vs. Suresh Chand Jain Page 5 of 22 in the site plan filed by the respondent.

(c) It is further argued that the petitioner filed a false complaint with the MCD with regard to the unauthorized construction in the tenanted shops and herself received a notice from the MCD with a view to get the property sealed but the petitioner did not reply the said notice and as a result of which, shop in question remained sealed by the MCD for the period from 30.12.2010 to 15.10.2011 which was got de­ sealed by the respondent on their filing appeals before the Appellate Tribunal wherein the petitioner was also impleaded as a party. As the sealing of the said tenanted shop was at the behest of the petitioner so the respondent was not liable to pay the rent for the said period however, in the month of January, 2012 with the intervention of the respectable persons of the market, all the tenants including respondent paid the rent for the said period and thereafter, the tenants and the respondent have been regularly paying the rent to the petitioner and the rent stood paid till 28.02.2010. That on 07.02.2010, all the tenants had gone to the petitioner and requested her for carrying out the repairs of the roof of the suit property which she had also agreed subject to increasing the rent by 10% to which all the tenants had agreed but instead of carrying out the repairs, she has got sent a notice claiming arrears of rent since 01.08.2010.

(d) It is argued that the suit property is a commercial property and is occupied by different tenants who are running their respective business in the suit property and the alleged need of the petitioner for residence is misconceived and the present petition is not maintainable for evicting the tenants from the shop as the petitioner is residing in her own house.

Thus, it is argued on behalf of the respondent that he has raised triable issues and the respondent is entitled to leave to defend.

5. The plaintiff filed reply to the application seeking leave to defend and denied the contentions of the defendant and reaffirming the averments made in the petition.

6. Before adverting to the submissions of the parties, it would be apposite to encapsulate the legal provisions under Section 14­D as well as 14 (1) (e) of the Delhi Rent Control Act, 1958, which governs the current litigation which read as under:­ 14­D. Right to recover immediate possession of premises to accrue to a widow.-(1) Where the landlord is a widow and the premises let out by her, or by her husband, are required by her for her own residence, she may apply to the Controller for recovering the immediate possession of such premises.

(2) Where the landlord referred to in sub­section (1) has let out more than one premises, it shall be open to her to make an application under that sub­section in respect of E - 40/14 Smt. Poonam Chadha vs. Suresh Chand Jain Page 7 of 22 any one of the premises chose by her.

14. Protection of tenant against eviction.-(1) Notwithstanding anything to the contrary contained in any other law or contract, no order or decree for the recovery of possession of any premises shall be made by any court or Controller in favour of landlord against a tenant: ­ Provided that the Controller may, on an application made to him in the prescribed manner, make an order for the recovery of possession of the premises on one ore more of the following grounds only, namely: ­

(e) that the premises are required bona fide by the landlord for himself or for any member of his family dependent on him, if he is the owner thereof, or for any person for whose benefit the premises are held and that the landlord or such person has no other reasonably suitable accommodation.

7. From the provisions of Section 25­B and 14­D the legislative intent is clear that if a widow to recover the premises in question for her self­occupation files an application for eviction of a tenant, the Controller shall pass an order for eviction of the tenant from the premises with utmost expedition. The provision is intended to serve the social need to help a widow in getting possession of the premises required for her personal occupation. To subserve that purpose she has been included in the special class of landlord's who are entitled to E - 40/14 Smt. Poonam Chadha vs. Suresh Chand Jain Page 8 of 22 recover possession of the premises let out by them when they require the same for self­occupation and special provision has been made in Section 25­B providing for an enquiry by the Controller following a summary procedure to satisfy himself that the plea of self­occupation taken by the widow/landlady as a genuine and bona fide one and not a mere pretence to get the tenant evicted from the premises. For that reason, heavy burden is placed on the tenant to satisfy the Controller that the plea of need of personal occupation by the petitioner widow is nothing but pretence.

8. The scope of granting leave is very narrow in case of eviction petition under Section 14­B, 14­C or 14­D. The Hon'ble Supreme Court in Busching Schmifz Private Ltd. vs. P.T. Menghani, AIR 1977 SC 1569 observed that "The social setting demanding summary proceeding, the nature of the subject matter and, above all, the legislative diction which has been deliberately designed, differ in two provisions. The Controller's power to give leave to contest the application filed under Section 14 (1) (e) or Section 14­A is cribbed by the condition that the affidavit filed by the tenant discloses such facts as would disentitle the landlord from obtaining an order for the recovery of possession of the premises on the ground specified in the respective sections. Needless to state, therefore, if an application is filed under E - 40/14 Smt. Poonam Chadha vs. Suresh Chand Jain Page 9 of 22 14­B or 14­C or 14­D, the tenant's right to contest the application is narrowed down and is restricted to the parameters of the respective sections. Tenant cannot widen the scope of his defence by relying upon Section 14 (1) (e)."

9. Whether leave to defend will be granted to the tenant in a case depends on the facts and circumstances of the case. No hard and fast rule or straitjacket formula can be laid down for judging the question. It is to be kept in mind by the Controller and the Courts that the petition for eviction filed by a widow under Section 14­D should not be frustrated by granting leave to the tenant to raise any plea denying/refuting the case pleaded in eviction petition. Unless a real and a substantial case is made out on the averments made in the affidavit filed by the tenant in support of the petition filed under Section 25­B of the Act, the proceedings should not be dragged on unnecessarily and should be disposed of with due expedition. A balance has to be maintained between the general object of the stature, which is to prove protection to the tenants against arbitrary action of the landlord's for their eviction and the assistance to be referred to the special class of landlord's including a widow to recover possession of premises let out by her for self­occupation.

10. In the present case the petitioner Smt. Poonam Chadha has filed E - 40/14 Smt. Poonam Chadha vs. Suresh Chand Jain Page 10 of 22 the petition stating that she is the landlord of the tenant/respondent Suresh Chand Jain in respect of a tenanted premises one shop on the ground floor in the property bearing no. 6/81, Gali No.1, Vishwas Nagar, Shahdara, Delhi­32. The petitioner has also claimed that the reason for eviction is a bonafide claim for residential purpose for herself as well as for her family members. She has no other property except the present property in the vicinity of Delhi.

11. What has to be seen now is whether the leave to defend application moved by the respondent has raised any triable issues or not for which evidence is to be led? I shall deal with all the defences raised in the leave to defend application of the respondent in a sequential manner: ­

12. The first ground on which leave to defend is sought is that in addition to the suit property, the petitioner has following properties (i) 32/19, Gali No.9, Bhikam Singh Colony, Shahdara, Delhi­32 and (ii) 7/76, Arjun Gali Vishwas Nagar, Shahdara, Delhi­32 and the petitioner has sufficient accommodation with herself in the said properties.

13. Perusal of the case file reflects that no documentary proof in support of the contention of petitioner having other sufficient alternative accommodation, filed by the respondent. The petitioner has E - 40/14 Smt. Poonam Chadha vs. Suresh Chand Jain Page 11 of 22 specifically denied of having any right, title or interest in properties bearing no. 32/19, Gali No.9, Bhikam Singh Colony, Shahdara, Delhi­32 and 7/76, Arjun Gali Vishwas Nagar, Shahdara, Delhi­32. Admittedly, she is stated to be residing on rent since long. In absence of any documentary proof, the averment remains a bald statement raising no triable issue for the purpose of proceeding with the trial with the present petition. In Naval Kishore Khandelwal vs. Kunti Devi 2013 (1) CLJ 295 Delhi while following case of Rajender Kumar Sharma vs. Leelawati, 155 (2008) DLT 383, it was held that mere assertion made by a tenant in respect of landlords ownership of other buildings and in respect of alternate accommodation are not to be considered sufficient for the grand of leave to defend. If this is allowed, the whole purpose of Section 25­B of the Act shall stand defeated and any tenant can file a false affidavit and drag a case for years together in evidence, defeating the very purpose of statute. The Rent Controller is, thus, precluded from considering the material placed before it by the landlord in response to leave to defend to show that the tenant's assertions and averments were totally false. In Prithpal Singh vs. Satpal Singh (D) through LRs (2010) 2 SCC 15, it was held that mere bald assertion made without any basis cannot create a triable issue. As regards property bearing no.6/81, Gali No.1, Vishwas Nagar, E - 40/14 Smt. Poonam Chadha vs. Suresh Chand Jain Page 12 of 22 Shahdara, the present petition has been filed. In Praveen Jain & Ors. vs. Dr. Vimla, 174(2010) DLT 689, it has been held that genuine requirement of landlady for premises in question made out for her and her family's residence - landlady's requirement of premises of her own use is not confined to actual physical use by landlady herself - requirements includes requirement of other family members of the landlady who are living with her and dependent on landlady for residence or on whom the landlady is a dependent for her care, security and wellbeing and who are living with landlady because of love and affection - court while considering requirement under Section 14­D was to Act independent under Section 14 (1) (e) of the Act as requirement under Section 14 (1) (e) was more rigorous under Section 14­D of the Act. The test is to be applied whether the requirement pleaded may properly be regarded as landlady's own requirement and the requirement of the family members i.e. her son and daughters living with her. In Kishan Lal vs. Devki 85 (2000) DLT 326 it was held that the test under Section 14­D of DRC Act is not as rigorous as under

Section 14 (1) (e) of the Act.

14. In K. Puta Raju vs. A Hanuman Gowda, Civil Appeal No. 5648/08, it was held by Hon'ble Apex Court that the special E - 40/14 Smt. Poonam Chadha vs. Suresh Chand Jain Page 13 of 22 provisions are introduced by the legislature to reflect the policy of the legislature for speedy justice to landladies belonging to special categories of citizens namely widow, handicapped persons and a person who is citizen of above 65 years. The legislature has introduced these provisions for the purpose of giving immediate possession of the premises to such landladies. These provisions are independent of each other and they can be set in motion in individual fields.

15. The next ground taken by the respondent is that the suit property is a three storey building and the petitioner with ulterior motives got demolished the entire second floor of the suit property to create a false paucity of accommodation and with a view to harass the tenants in the suit property by making holes in the roof at the first floor and had been pouring water from the said holes to destroy the articles of the tenants lying in their respective shops. It is submitted that had the petitioner been in need of accommodation, she would not have got demolished the entire second floor of the suit property. The petitioner has four big rooms available in the suit property which are lying vacant and she has converted the three rooms in one big hall by way of removing walls between them and has affixed two shutters on the same. The accommodation lying vacant with the petitioner is shown in red colour in the site plan filed by the respondent. However, the same is strongly E - 40/14 Smt. Poonam Chadha vs. Suresh Chand Jain Page 14 of 22 denied by the petitioner stating that she is unable to move due to her long illness and obesity and other various ailments.

16. In this regard, it is relevant to mention the dilapidated condition of the premises so as to make it habitable for residence is itself a ground of eviction. The arguments as advanced is not tenable in the light of the fact that she is herself in need of the accommodation and that she herself is the best judge of her own requirements. In Sudesh Kumar Soni & Anr. vs. Prabha Khanna & Anr., 153 (2008) Delhi Law Times 652, the court observed that "it is not for tenant to dictate terms to landlord as to who else he can adjust himself without getting possession of tenanted premises­suitability has to be seen for convenience of landlord and his family members and on the basis of circumstances including their profession, vocation, style of living, habit and background."

17. In Sanjay Mehra & Ors. vs. Sunil Malhotra & Anr. 170 (2010) DLT 797 while dealing with scope of Section 25­B of DRC Act, it was held that the Section 25­B is a code in itself providing for summary procedure with object to facilitate expeditious disposal and unless a tenant is able to bring forth a challengeable credit to the assertion of bonafide need of the landlord, readily granted the tenant leave to defend has danger of reducing every summary proceedings intended to E - 40/14 Smt. Poonam Chadha vs. Suresh Chand Jain Page 15 of 22 help speedy disposal into a regular trial thus defeating the very object of the provision.

18. Perusal of case file reflects that no site plan so as to counter the existing site plan annexed with the petition of the petitioner, filed by the respondent. In the absence of any site plan (alleged to be correct), the argument of the respondent does not hold water and is not tenable. It was held in Harivansh Lal vs. Madan Lal, 1997 RLR 383 that "if landlord produces site plan and tenant does not produce counter site plan then landlord plan has to be accepted as correct".

19. The other ground taken by the respondent is that the petitioner filed a false complaint with the MCD with regard to the unauthorized construction in the tenanted shops and herself received a notice from the MCD with a view to get the property sealed but the petitioner did not reply the said notice and as a result of which, shop in question remained sealed by the MCD for the period from 30.12.2010 to 15.10.2011 which was got desealed by the respondent on their filing appeals before the Appellate Tribunal wherein the petitioner was also impleaded as a party. As the sealing of the said tenanted shop was at the behest of the petitioner so the respondent was not liable to pay the rent for the said period however, in the month of January, 2012 with the intervention of the respectable persons of the market, all the tenants E - 40/14 Smt. Poonam Chadha vs. Suresh Chand Jain Page 16 of 22 including respondent paid the rent for the said period and thereafter, the tenants and the respondent have been regularly paying the rent to the petitioner and the rent stood paid till 28.02.2010. That on 07.02.2010, all the tenants had gone to the petitioner and requested her for carrying out the repairs of the roof of the suit property which she had also agreed subject to increasing the rent by 10% to which all the tenants had agreed but instead of carrying out the repairs, she has got sent a notice claiming arrears of rent since 01.08.2010.

20. The pendency of any proceeding before another forum with respect to the unauthorized construction on the suit property, is not tenable for the purpose of construing the triable issue as the premises sought to be evicted are for residence of the petitioner as well as for her family members.

21. The another ground is taken that the suit property is a commercial property and is occupied by different tenants who are running their respective business in the suit property and the alleged need of the petitioner for residence is misconceived and the present petition is not maintainable for evicting the tenants from the shop as the petitioner is residing in her own house.

22. The arguments as advanced on behalf of the respondent to contend the bonafide requirement of the petitioner by stating that E - 40/14 Smt. Poonam Chadha vs. Suresh Chand Jain Page 17 of 22 respondent is running a commercial activity in the suit property do not raise any triable issue for the purpose of adjudication of the present petition. Firstly, the status of the respondent who is running commercial activity is not relevant for the purpose of determining whether a triable issue raised or not. Secondly, it is not disputed that the petitioner requires the suit premises for the bonafide requirement of herself for residence. Thirdly, the bonafide requirement of the residence for herself as well as for her family members has been generally challenged without filing any documentary proof in support of the contention.

23. In J. Chatterjee vs. Mohinder Kaur 2000 Rajdhani Law Reporter 561 (SC), it was held that the legislative intention of giving immediate relief to landlady cannot be defeated by granting leave to tenant on mere denial or refuting landlady's case. Unless tenant makes out a substantial case, proceedings should not be dragged on but should be disposed off expeditiously.

24. It is argued on behalf of the respondent that he has raised triable issues and the respondent is entitled to leave to defend. In this regard reference can also be had to the judgment of Hon'ble High Court of Delhi in Shafiqqudin vs. Mohd. Ibrahim, in R.C.Rev 502/2012, E - 40/14 Smt. Poonam Chadha vs. Suresh Chand Jain Page 18 of 22 wherein it was held that: "It is important for the tenant, in any leave to defend application to submit relevant documents on record to support any triable issue raised by him. In absence of any material, the ARC cannot come to a conclusion as to the weight of the issue raised by the tenant. Thus impairing his power to decide whether an issue raised is triable or not."

25. It is also a settled law that it is for the landlord to decide how and what accommodation to use for meeting his bonafide requirement and tenant cannot dictate the same to the landlord.

26. In Gulab Chand Pukhraj vs. R.B. Jinender Raj & Anr., 2009 (4) Civil Court Cases 748 (SC), it was held that once it is not disputed that landlord is in bonafide need of premises, court cannot dictate to landlord which floor he should use for his business. Moreover, it is well settled law that it is the landlord's prerogative as to in which location he prefers to run business and law should not and cannot prevent such preference by a landlord in order to meet his bonafide requirement.

27. In Joginder Pal vs. Naval Kishore Behal MANU/SC/0453/2002 (2002) 3SCR 1078 the court after noticing several judicial precedents on the subject observed that "the rent E - 40/14 Smt. Poonam Chadha vs. Suresh Chand Jain Page 19 of 22 control legislations are heavily loaded in favour of the tenants treating them as weaker sections of the society requiring legislative protection against exploitation and unscrupulous devices of greedy landlords. The legislative intent has to be respected by the courts while interpreting the laws. But it is being uncharitable to legislatures if they are attributed with an intention that they lean only in favour of the tenants and while being fair to the tenants, go to the extent of being unfair to the landlords. The legislature is fair to the tenants and to the landlords - both. The courts have to adopt a reasonable and balanced approach while interpreting rent control legislations starting with an assumption that an equal treatment has been meted out to both the sections of the society. In spite of the overall balance tilting in favour of the tenants, while interpreting such of the provisions as take care of the interest of the landlord the court should not hesitate in leaning in favour of the landlords. Such provisions are engrafted in rent control legislations to take care of those situations where the landlords too are weak and feeble and feel humble."

28. As regards, the maintainability of the present petition for more than one premises given to the tenants, the needs of the petitioner for residence for herself as well as for her family members is taken into consideration and it is pertinent to mention as held in Surjeet Singh E - 40/14 Smt. Poonam Chadha vs. Suresh Chand Jain Page 20 of 22 Kalra vs. Union of India Spl. Leave Petition (C) No.7146 decided on 13.09.1991 that "landlord can seek eviction of tenant from more than one premises if he establishes his need. Specified landlord seeking relief under Section 14­B, 14­C, 14­D, tenant cannot contest the application on the ground specified in Section 14 (1) (e) of the Act [1977 (2) RCR 233(SC) relied]."

Conclusion: ­

29. It is well settled law that leave to defend can be granted to the tenant in case of any triable issue has been raised by him, which can be adjudicated by consideration of additional evidence. The mere existence or averment of any triable issue is not sufficient. The nature of the triable issue raised by the tenant must be such that it would disentitle the landlord from obtaining the eviction order. The whole purpose and import of summary procedure under Section 25B of the Act would otherwise be defeated. The prayer for leave to contest should be granted to the tenant only where a prima facie case has been disclosed by him. In the absence of the tenant having disclosed a prima facie case i.e. such facts which disentitles the landlord from obtaining an order of eviction, the Court should not mechanically & in routine manner grant leave to defend.

E - 40/14 Smt. Poonam Chadha vs. Suresh Chand Jain Page 21 of 22

30. In the light of the aforesaid legal ratio, all the pleas taken by the respondent seems to be sham and moonshine, which have failed to raise any requisite triable issues. The contents of the application for leave to defend have failed to rebut the presumption of bonafide qua the need of the petitioner. The application for leave to defend filed by the respondent is thus rejected.

31. Consequently, an eviction order is passed under Section 14 (1) (e) of the DRC Act against the respondent regarding the One shop/office at the ground floor in the property bearing no. 6/81, Gali No.1, Vishwas Nagar, Shahdara, Delhi­110032 as shown in red colour in the site plan filed by the petitioner in this case.

32. However in light of Section 14 (7) DRCA, the aforesaid eviction order shall not be executable for a period of six months from today.

33. The parties are left to bear their own costs.

34. File be consigned to Record Room after due compliance.
