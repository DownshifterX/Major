SUIT FOR DECLARATION AND RECOVERY OF ARREARS OF RENT

                              Case filed on ­ 21.09.2011
                           Arguments heard on ­ 22.02.2014
                         Judgment pronounced on ­ 13.03.2014

                                        JUDGMENT

1. On the strength of ratio decidendi in Raghunandan Saran Ashok Saran (HUF) vs. Union of India, 95(2002) DLT 508, declaring sections 4, 6 & 9 of Delhi Rent Control Act, 1958 (for short the 'DRC Act') as ultra vires CS No. 291/11 Raj Bhushan Gupta v. Nathu Ram Page 1 of 17 the Constitution of India and the observation of Apex Court in Mohammad Ahmad and Anr. vs. Atma Ram Chauhan & Ors., AIR 2011 SC 1940, the plaintiff­landlord has sued the defendant­tenant, who enjoys the protection of DRC Act, for declaring that the monthly rental of the tenanted premises under his occupation is Rs. 14,000/­ and the consequent recovery of arrears of such rental for the period during pendency of the suit and for the month of August, 2011 along with 18% per annum interest thereon. The Apex Court's observations in Mohammad Ahmad (supra), which the plaintiff­landlord has invoked in aid of his case are as under:

21. According to our considered view majority of these cases are filed because landlords do not get reasonable rent akin to market rent, then on one ground or the other litigation is initiated. So before saying omega, we deem it our duty and obligation to fix some guidelines and norms for such type of litigation, so as to minimise landlord­tenant litigation at all levels. These are as follows:
(i) The tenant must enhance the rent according to the terms of the agreement or at least by ten percent, after every three years and enhanced rent should then be made payable to the landlord. If the rent is too low (in comparison to market rent), having been fixed almost 20 to 25 years back then the present market rate should be worked out either on the basis of valuation report or reliable estimates of building rentals in the surrounding areas, let out on rent recently.
(ii) Apart from the rental, property tax, water tax, maintenance charges, electricity charges for the actual consumption of the tenanted premises and for common area shall be payable by the tenant only so that the landlord gets the actual rent out of which nothing would be deductible. In case there is enhancement in property tax, water tax or maintenance charges, electricity charges CS No. 291/11 Raj Bhushan Gupta v. Nathu Ram Page 2 of 17 then the same shall also be borne by the tenant only.
(iii) The usual maintenance of the premises, except major repairs would be carried out by the tenant only and the same would not be reimbursable by the landlord.
(iv) But if any major repairs are required to be carried out then in that case only after obtaining permission from the landlord in writing, the same shall be carried out and modalities with regard to adjustment of the amount spent thereon, would have to be worked out between the parties.
(v) If present and prevalent market rent assessed and fixed between the parties is paid by the tenant then landlord shall not be entitled to bring any action for his eviction against such a tenant at least for a period of 5 years. Thus for a period of 5 years the tenant shall enjoy immunity from being evicted from the premises.
(vi) The parties shall be at liberty to get the rental fixed by the official valuer or by any other agency, having expertise in the matter.
(vii) The rent so fixed should be just, proper and adequate, keeping in mind, location, type of construction, accessibility with the main road, parking space facilities available therein etc. Care ought to be taken that it does not end up being a bonanza for the landlord.

2. Facts, as set out in the plaint, may be recounted. The defendant is a tenant since year 1960 in suit shop/property no. 92, Gali Jain Mandir, Chhota Bazar, Shahdara, Delhi­32 admeasuring 5'x16' (80 sq. ft.). He was originally inducted by one Kanhiya Lal at monthly rental of Rs. 20/­ only. Kanhiya Lal sold out the property in 1974 to late Gain Wati, plaintiff's mother. On the demise of late Gain Wati in 2001 and on execution of a duly registered relinquishment deed dt. 19.04.2009 in his favour by the other legal heirs of CS No. 291/11 Raj Bhushan Gupta v. Nathu Ram Page 3 of 17 late Gain Wati, plaintiff became the sole owner/landlord of the suit property.

3. The monthly rental, as per the plaintiff, is pegged at a meagre sum of Rs. 68.33/­ only (excluding electricity, water and other charges), including house tax. Out of this amount, the rental portion is stated to be Rs. 26.62/­ only and the house tax Rs. 41.71/­. It is averred that the defendant, a regular defaulter in payment of rental, is not diligent in paying even this meagre sum. The plaintiff, along with other legal heirs of his late mother, it is stated, perforce instituted a suit in the Court of Small Causes for recovery of rent against the defendant which stood decreed with costs. It is stated that a petition for deposit for rent under section 27 of DRC Act filed by the defendant­tenant, whereby and whereunder he tendered only the deficient rent and not the actual rent due to the plaintiff, is pending disposal.

4. This amount of monthly rental, as per the plaintiff, is a minuscule fraction of the actual prevailing market rental and the market rate of land and construction cost in the backdrop of inflationary effect on the money value. Plaintiff goes on to state that the monthly rental at no point of time was mutually agreed/consented/contracted between him or his predecessor­in­ interest and the defendant­tenant and the latter kept on tendering the old frozen rent, in view of the statutory protection, which was not in consonance with the prevailing market rental. Raghunandan Saran Ashok Saran (supra) and Mohammad Ahmad (supra) are pressed into service by the plaintiff to urge that it has now become incumbent upon the defendant­tenant CS No. 291/11 Raj Bhushan Gupta v. Nathu Ram Page 4 of 17 to not only pay the market rental but also his share of property tax, water tax, maintenance charges, electricity charges and other charges. Plaintiff, therefore, served a legal notice dt. 30.07.2011 upon the defendant thereby calling upon him to start paying monthly rental, with effect from 01.08.2011, at the market rate of Rs. 14,000/­ and in the alternative, he gave the latter an option to get the rental fixed by an official valuer or by any other agency having expertise in the matter with joint consent. Along with the legal notice, plaintiff annexed a rent deed dt. 21.10.2010 of a nearby tenanted premises (this lease deed of a property in the same locality is between the plaintiff and a company for five years on stamp paper of Rs. 100/­ reflecting monthly rental of Rs. 14,000/­). This legal notice was preceded by a legal notice dt. 06.04.2010 to the similar effect, which was replied to by the defendant vide his reply dt. 20.05.2010. To the legal notice dt. 30.07.2011 the defendant responded vide his reply dt. 26.08.2011 wherein he questioned the plaintiff's right to enhance the rental in tune with the market rental notwithstanding the aforesaid decisions.

5. Plaintiff avers that the defendant is in the arrears of the rent for the period from 01.04.2011 to 31.07.2011 at the old monthly rent, i.e. Rs. 68.33/­ including house tax. It is claimed that from August, 2011 onwards, defendant is liable to pay market rental of Rs. 14,000/­. On these plaintiff seeks reliefs of the following nature:

(a) A decree of declaration to the effect that plaintiff is entitled to revise CS No. 291/11 Raj Bhushan Gupta v. Nathu Ram Page 5 of 17 the monthly rental with effect from 01.08.2011 to Rs. 14,000/­ or to any other rate found by the court to be in consonance with the market rent, excluding electricity/water charges, property or any other tax/charge, in view of the law laid down in the aforesaid two decisions,
(b) A decree of recovery of arrears of rent for the period during the pendency of the suit and for the month of August, 2011 with 18% interest thereon, and
(c) Entire cost of the suit with pendente lite interest on arrears of rent.

6. Defendant entered the contest and filed his written statement. He has taken few preliminary objections which are as follows. The lease deed dt. 21.10.2010 (which plaintiff presses into service to show that the monthly market rental of a nearby tenanted premises is about Rs. 14,000/­) is stated to be unregistered in contravention of section 17, Registration Act and deficiently stamped and thus inadmissible in evidence. The said deed is stated to be false and having been got prepared by the plaintiff in collusion and connivance with the alleged tenant for making its use as a piece of evidence in the present suit. Plaintiff is accused of concealing the material fact that his father, for himself and on behalf of other legal heirs, has been increasing monthly rent by 10% after every three years ever since the demise of plaintiff's mother in 2001. It is submitted that the last time the monthly rental was enhanced was with effect from 01.10.2008 from Rs. 24.20/­ to Rs. 26.62/­. The instant suit is stated to be barred under section 50, DRC Act. Defendant asserts that the plaintiff is not entitled to claim rent from him more than the contractual rent under the provisions of DRC Act. The suit is also CS No. 291/11 Raj Bhushan Gupta v. Nathu Ram Page 6 of 17 stated to be without cause of action.

7. On merits, the defendant denies plaintiff's ownership over the tenanted premises. He, however, states that he is a tenant under the plaintiff. He denies having defaulted in payment of monthly rent and states that in the proceedings in DR no. 3/10 titled as 'Nathu Ram vs. Ram Kishore Gupta', disposed of on 18.02.2011, plaintiff's father had received a sum of Rs. 1532/­ in cash from him plus Rs. 518/­ which he had deposited on account of arrears of rent for the period from 01.10.2008 to 31.03.2011 (the said amount, as per defendant, also includes the house tax). Defendant goes on to aver that thereafter the rent for the period from 01.04.2011 onwards was also deposited under section 27, DRC Act. He avers that along with the agreed rent he has also been paying the house tax. He states that the aforesaid two decisions are not inapplicable. Defendant seeks dismissal of the suit.

8. In the replication, plaintiff reiterated and reaffirmed his stand as set out in the plaint and refuted those made by the defendant in his written statement.

9. The issues, framed on 07.12.2011, are as follows:

1) Whether the plaintiff is entitled to decree for declaration revising the rate of rent @ Rs. 14,000/­ per month w.e.f. 01.08.2011 or at any other rate in consonance with market rate of rent excluding electricity, water charges and any other tax/charge whatsoever as prayed for by plaintiff?

OPP CS No. 291/11 Raj Bhushan Gupta v. Nathu Ram Page 7 of 17

2) Whether plaintiff is entitled to decree of arrears of rent at the above­ said rate of Rs. 14,000/­ per month w.e.f. 01.08.2011 as prayed for? OPP

3) Whether suit of the plaintiff is barred under section 50 of the DRC Act? OPD

4) Relief.

10. The plaintiff entered the witness box as PW1. His examination­in­ chief is entirely along the same lines as averred by him in the body of the plaint. The site plan is Ex. PW1/A, the relinquishment deed dated 19.04.2009 is Ex. PW1/B, legal notice dated 30.07.2011 is Ex. PW1/C and defendant's reply thereto is Ex. PW1/E, lease deed dated 21.10.2010 between the plaintiff and a company in respect of a nearby premises is Ex. PW1/D. In his cross­examination he stated that his father collects the rent. He further deposed that at the time of demise of his mother, the rent was Rs. 25/­ apart from the house tax. To a question whether from October, 2008 onwards, the rent was Rs. 26.62/­ he feigned ignorance. One Sh. V. K. Arora from Punjab National Bank was examined as PW2. He exhibited the bank statement of account of the plaintiff as Ex. PW2/A. This statement of account was exhibited to show that the plaintiff is receiving rent of 14,000/­ per month from a tenant in respect of a nearby tenanted premises in terms of the aforesaid rent deed dt. 21.10.2010. One S. L. Dheer, a valuer of properties, was examined as PW3 and he exhibited his valuation report as Ex. PW3/A. In CS No. 291/11 Raj Bhushan Gupta v. Nathu Ram Page 8 of 17 his evidence, he stated that the market rental of the tenanted premises under defendant's occupation was Rs. 16,000/­ per month. When cross­examined, he stated that there is no reference to any property dealer or the shopkeepers whom he had contacted during his field visit in his report Ex. PW3/A.

11. Defendant entered the witness box as DW1. His examination­in­chief is along the same lines as averred by him in his written statement. He exhibited on record counter­foils of two rent receipts as Ex. DW1/2 and Ex. DW1/3 to show that the prevailing rent of nearby tenanted premises is only Rs. 350/­ per month. In his cross­examination, he stated that his predecessor was a tenant in the tenanted premises from pre­independence era. He further stated that at the time of inception of tenancy, the rent was Rs. 7/­ per month. He also stated that he presently tenders Rs. 68.83/­ every month to the landlord which includes Rs. 26.62/­ being the monthly rental. He also admitted that the plaintiff is the landlord/owner of the tenanted premises. He conceded that he has not tendered the rent with effect from 01.04.2011 till date to the plaintiff.

12. I have heard arguments at Bar and perused the record.

13. The issue­wise findings are as follows:

14. Issue no. 1 - Plaintiff seeks a declaration to the effect that he is entitled to revise the monthly rental with effect from 01.08.2011 to Rs. 14,000/­ or to any other rate found by the court to be in consonance with the market rent, excluding electricity/water charges, property or any other CS No. 291/11 Raj Bhushan Gupta v. Nathu Ram Page 9 of 17 tax/charge, in view of the law laid down in the aforesaid two decisions.

15. The Apex Court's decision in Mohammad Ahmad (supra) which was very strongly pressed into service by the plaintiff already stands distinguished by a Division Bench of the Hon'ble Delhi High Court in the case reported as Santosh Vaid & Anr. vs. Uttam Chand 188 (2012) DLT 293. Ld. Counsel for the plaintiff in the course of his arguments had fairly conceded this point. It would be apt to take a note of the observations of the Division Bench in Santosh Vaid on this aspect, which reads as under:

"Mohd. Ahmad (supra) was also a case where the Supreme Court gave certain suggestions/laid guidelines to minimize landlord­tenant litigation. The same were again in the context of UP Rent Act. The same also have no application to the position as prevailing in Delhi."

16. Therefore, inasmuch as the aforesaid decision of the Apex Court stands distinguished by holding that the same may not have application to the situation prevailing in Delhi, the same may not come to plaintiff Raj Bhushan Gupta's aid.

17. Ld. Counsel for the plaintiff, however, argued that the decision in Santosh Vaid (supra) has been challenged in the Supreme Court and therefore the observations made by the Division Bench therein should not adversely influence the outcome of the instant case. A query was put to plaintiff's counsel as to whether there is any stay by the Apex Court on the Santosh Vaid judgment. In all fairness, he conceded that there was no such stay. Therefore, inasmuch as there is no stay from the Supreme Court on the CS No. 291/11 Raj Bhushan Gupta v. Nathu Ram Page 10 of 17 decision in Santosh Vaid, the observations made therein would, of course, be taken into consideration by this court. The mere fact that a judicial decision has been challenged in a higher forum, without there being any stay, does not ipso facto mean that the same ceases to have any effect.

18. The other decision which plaintiff's counsel forcefully relied upon is Raghunandan Saran Ashok Saran (supra). This decision too cannot assist the plaintiff. This decision has merely struck down sections 4, 6 & 9 of DRC Act relating to fixation of standard rent as ultra vires the Constitution of India. It did not strike down the entire DRC Act. The only effect, therefore, of this judgment is that a tenant could not approach the Court for determination of standard rent and thus could not avoid paying the agreed rent. In Santosh Vaid the Division Bench had an occasion to make certain observations regarding the extent of applicability of Raghunandan Saran Ashok Saran, which are as under:

"15. A Division Bench of this Court in Raghunandan Saran Ashok Saran held that Sections 4, 6 & 9 of the Delhi Rent Control Act relating to standard rent has not taken into account the huge difference between the cost of living in the past and the present time and did not pass the test of reasonableness and had become obsolete and archaic and accordingly struck down the same. However, the only effect of the said judgment is that a tenant could not apply to have the standard rent thereof determined and thus could not avoid paying agreed rent, as he was able to before this judgment. Undoubtedly the Division Bench, while so striking down the said provisions, did observe that the said provisions dealing with the standard rent did not take into account the rise in the consumer price index and the huge costs required for CS No. 291/11 Raj Bhushan Gupta v. Nathu Ram Page 11 of 17 maintaining the tenanted premises and there was no justification for not updating the frozen rents but all this was in the context of striking down Sections 4, 6 & 9 only. Thus the said judgment cannot be said to be a judgment on the proposition that landlords are entitled to have the rent increased as per the consumer price index or rate of inflation."

19. Therefore, even the decision of Raghunandan Saran Ashok Saran cannot assist the plaintiff in contending that the rent should be revised to the prevailing market rent thereby taking the defendant­tenant out of the purview of DRC Act.

20. Rentals of properties, which are covered within the ambit of DRC Act, can be enhanced only by taking recourse to sections 6A & 8 thereof. These two provisions of the DRC Act have not been struck down as ultra vires the Constitution of India. Rather, a perusal of Santosh Vaid judgment reflects that Hon'ble Delhi High Court in Kamlesh Bagga vs. Mahinder Kaur, CM (M) 948/2004 in the order dt. 5th December, 2005 clearly held that contractual rent within the ambit of DRC Act attracts provisions of DRC Act and any increase of such rent is permissible only by taking recourse to section 6A thereof. An Appeal against this order {SLP (civil) no. 11536/2006} has been dismissed in limine on 14th July, 2006. It would be pertinent to take note of certain observation made in Santosh Vaid with reference to section 6A, DRC Act. The same are as under:

"28. Even though the 10% increase in the rent every three years provided for under the Delhi Rent Control Act may be perceived by some as inadequate but that is no reason for this Court to provide for a CS No. 291/11 Raj Bhushan Gupta v. Nathu Ram Page 12 of 17 higher or more frequent increase. The same falls in legislative domain. This Court cannot step into the shoes of Legislature (see Union of India V. Deoki Nandan Aggarwal, 1992 Supp (1) SCC 323). It may be noted that Section 6A (supra) was inserted in the Delhi Rent Control Act with effect from 1st December, 1988 to quell the criticism thereof of being unevenly balanced against the landlord. The legislature in its wisdom having considered increase in rent as provided in Section 6A as appropriate to balance the rights of the landlord and the tenant governed by the provisions of the Delhi Rent Control Act, it is not for this Court to delve into the validity thereof particularly in exercise of appellate/revisionary jurisdiction."

21. Thus, in the absence of a provision in the statute it cannot be held that a landlord is entitled to market rent from a tenant having the protection of DRC Act. It is pertinent to mention that in Santosh Vaid, the plaintiff­ landlord therein had sought a similar relief of enhancement of rental in tune with the prevailing rent. The suit was held to be not maintainable. On similar footing, plaintiff Raj Bhushan Gupta's plea to revise the rent in tune with the prevailing market rental is also liable to be turned down.

22. It is also pertinent to mention that a similar situation as in the present one had also arisen in the case reported as Tilak Raj Narula vs. M. L. Sethi 164 (2009) DLT 39. The plaintiff­landlord had sought to revise the rent, qua tenanted premises within the ambit of DRC Act, in tune with the market rentals. His plea was turned down by holding that there is no provision of increase of contractual rent in the DRC Act except under the provisions of section 6A thereof.

23. In view thereof, the plea of the plaintiff­landlord Raj Bhushan Gupta CS No. 291/11 Raj Bhushan Gupta v. Nathu Ram Page 13 of 17 to revise the monthly rental in tune with the prevailing market rent so as to bring the tenanted premises in question out of the purview of DRC Act is legally unsustainable. Such a declaration, as sought for, cannot be granted.

24. Plaintiff's counsel during the course of arguments submitted that in the event of this issue being decided against the plaintiff, liberty be given to him to approach the Court again in the event of Supreme Court reversing the decision in Santosh Vaid. The plaintiff may, subject to all just exceptions, try his luck for fixation of rent in tune with the prevailing market rental in the event of the Supreme Court reversing the decision in Santosh Vaid. However, to avoid any controversy, I hereby make it crystal clear that the observations of this Court should not be construed as giving a right to institute a suit or proceedings. A suit or a proceedings can be instituted only if and if the law of the land permits the same (see Shiv Kumar Sharma vs. Santosh Kuamri, AIR 2008 SC 171).

25. This issue is decided against the plaintiff and favour of the defendant.

26. Issue no. 2 - The issue is whether the plaintiff is entitled to a decree of arrears for rent at the rate of Rs. 14,000/­ per month with effect from 01 st August, 2011 as prayed for. The necessary consequence of the decision on issue no. 1 is that the plaintiff is not entitled to claim arrears of rent from the defendant at the rate of Rs. 14,000/­ per month.

27. It was, however, submitted by plaintiff's counsel that the plaintiff, in any event, is entitled to the arrears of rental with effect from 01.08.2011 at CS No. 291/11 Raj Bhushan Gupta v. Nathu Ram Page 14 of 17 the contractual rate. Certainly no fault can be found with this line of argument. However, the plaintiff, legally speaking, can only claim those arrears of rentals which had fallen due at the time of institution of the suit. A payment towards rent which became due after the institution of the suit cannot possibly be covered within the ambit of the suit. This is for the reason that cause of action for filing the suit for the arrears of rent would cover only such arrears which had fallen due at the time of filing of the suit. It is to be noted that this Court at this juncture is not taking recourse to Order XV­A CPC or Order XXXIX Rule 10 CPC. Furthermore, payment of rental which accrue after filing of the case is not akin to payment of mesne profits on account of unauthorized possession. In the case at hand, the defendant has the protection of DRC Act, therefore, his possession cannot be said to be unauthorized.

28. The parties are ad idem on the point that the monthly rental is Rs. 26.62/­ apart from house tax of Rs. 41.71/­, the total being Rs. 68.33/­. From the evidence on record, particularly the admission of the defendant, it appears that he has been paying the house tax dues also to the plaintiff. The defendant in his cross­examination very much conceded that he had not paid the rent since 01.04.2011. This suit was filed on 21.09.2011. Perusal of the record of this Court reveals that for the period from 01.04.2011 to 30.06.2011, the tenant has on 05.08.2011, already deposited rental @ Rs. 26.62/­ along with the statutory interest thereon under section 27, DRC Act. CS No. 291/11 Raj Bhushan Gupta v. Nathu Ram Page 15 of 17 Plaintiff's prayer is limited to the arrears of rent from 01.08.2011 onwards. Therefore, he can only recover the arrears of rent for the month of August, 2011. This suit was filed even before the completion of the tenancy for the month of September, 2011. Therefore, taking into account the fact that the relief of plaintiff is limited to arrears of rental starting from 01.08.2011 onwards and the fact that the tenancy for the month of September, 2011 had not come to an end when the suit was filed, the plaintiff is entitled to the amount of Rs. 68.33/­ only. On this amount of Rs. 68.33/­ the plaintiff is entitled to interest @ 15% per annum from 01.09.2011 till the date of full and final payment of this amount. This issue is accordingly decided in favour of the plaintiff in the aforesaid terms.

29. Issue no. 3 - The issue is whether the suit is barred by section 50, DRC Act. The plaintiff's prayer that the monthly rental be revised to Rs. 14,000/­ per month or to some other rate as found by the court to be in tune with the prevailing rent is certainly barred by section 50, DRC Act. This is for the reason that a Civil Court cannot venture into such a domain of fixing rental of any premises in tune with the prevailing rent in violation of the provisions of sections 6A and 8 of DRC Act. In this context, the observations in Tilak Raj Narula (supra) can be referred to which are as under, "Section 50 of the Act clearly bars the present suit in relation to fixation of standard rent as no civil Court can entertain any suit or proceedings in relation thereto. In the present case, it is agreed rent between the parties by virtue of agreement and the defendant who is tenant is not CS No. 291/11 Raj Bhushan Gupta v. Nathu Ram Page 16 of 17 agreeable to increase in the rent as suggested by the plaintiff except under the provisions of section 6A of the Act."

30. However, if a landlord seeks a recovery of the arrears of contractual rent, the same may not be barred by section 50, DRC Act. This issue is accordingly answered.

31. Relief ­ In view of the findings upon the foregoing issues, the suit of the plaintiff stands partly decreed in the following terms. Suit is decreed in the sum of Rs. 68.33/­ only along with interest @ 15% per annum thereon from 01.09.2011 till the date of full and final payment of this amount. Costs follow the event, unless the court directs otherwise. The general rule is that the successful party is entitled to costs unless he is guilty of misconduct, negligence or omission or unless there is some other cause for not allowing costs. In the case at hand, plaintiff is not guilty of any misconduct, negligence or omission. And neither does any other cause exist which may be a justification for not awarding the costs of the suit to him. And as such, the plaintiff is very much entitled to the costs of the instant suit. Costs of the suit is therefore also awarded to the plaintiff and against the defendant. Decree sheet be prepared accordingly. File be consigned to record room.
