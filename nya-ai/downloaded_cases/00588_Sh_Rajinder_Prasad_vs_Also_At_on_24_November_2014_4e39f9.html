Delhi District Court

Sh. Rajinder Prasad vs Sh. Gian Dhar Singh on 24 November, 2014

O R D E R:

1. Vide this order, an application for leave to defend, filed on behalf of the respondent under Section 25­B (4) of The Delhi Rent Control Act, E­392/2013 Page 1/22 1958 (hereinafter referred to as DRC Act, 1958) seeking leave to contest the present eviction petition is disposed of.

2. The present eviction petition was filed by the petitioner namely Sh. Rajinder Prasad in respect of the tenanted portion in property no. 33/3154, Beadonpura, Karol Bagh, Delhi. The petitioner is the absolute and exclusive owner of the premises in question.

3. The respondent is tenant from April, 1993 on execution of an agreement in respect of one shop measuring 7ft. X 15 ft., situated in property no. 33/3154 (Ground Floor), Beadonpura, Karol Bagh, Delhi as shown in red colour in the site plan. The tenanted premises was let out for non­residential purposes. The rate of rent is Rs. 550/­ per month exclusive of electricity charges.

4. The suit premises consists of four floors as reflected in the site plan, that of the above portions the second floor of the premises has already been sold by the petitioner in the year, 2001 to one Sh. Jugal Sharma in order E­392/2013 Page 2/22 to meet the regular expenses of the family, later the said portion was purchased by the respondent from Sh. Jugal Sharma on the name of daughter­in­law of respondent namely, Ms. Neelam, 2011. Now, they have rented out the said portion. The ground floor of the premises consists of two rooms/shops one of which is under the tenancy of the respondent while the other is under the use and occupation of son of petitioner.

5. The first floor of the premises consists of two rooms, a toilet and a bathroom and a kitchen. The first floor, occupation of petitioner, his wife and mentally challenged daughter. The third floor of the premises consists of two rooms, a latrine and a bathroom and a kitchen which are used as guest room for the three daughters of the petitioner, who frequently used to visit for certain time period to look after their ageing parents and mentally challenged daughter.

6. The fourth floor of the premises consists of two rooms, toilet and kitchen which is being occupied by the family of the son of the petitioner, who has separate kitchen from his parents and has no relationship with his E­392/2013 Page 3/22 parents.

7. The petitioner is retired from department of education, Delhi government where he was working in the capacity of Lab Assistant; in the month of January, 2013. The pension payment order of the pension of petitioner is only Rs. 5,490/­, is not sufficient to maintain his family and it is primarily the income/pension of the petitioner and the meager rent received from the tenanted portions of the premises, the day to day needs of the petitioner's family are being met besides the expensive medical treatment of the petitioner's wife who is suffering from Arthritis and mentally challenged daughter.

8. The petitioner requires the shop/premises in question to run a shop sale of jewelery box. There are numbers of shops of jewelers in the locality in which the shop is situated, therefore, there is very good scope of jewelery box business. The wife of the petitioner having developed medical problem of gout/arthritis and the petitioner's daughter, Ms. Kiran Bala who is mentally challenged had also grown up, the petitioner asked the respondent E­392/2013 Page 4/22 to vacate the suit premises as he needed the same for his bonafide use. The petitioner accordingly requested the respondent to vacate the suit premises as the respondent now also has two other shops, one situated in same building, which is let out by the respondent to one tenant, and other in Shastri Nagar, Delhi, where he is capable of carrying his business and as the petitioner wants the suit premises for his own bonafide use.

9. The petitioner is in bonafide requirement of shop which is under tenancy of the respondent and no other alternative premises is available with the petitioner to start his business of jewelery box. Due to high rates of prevalent rent the petitioner has not been able to start any proper working setup and has to do with his limited resources. In view of the medical illness of the wife and daughter of the petitioner and constrained by the limited income the petitioner desires to use the tenanted premises for his personal bonafide use. The petitioner has no other place or shops in their possession or ownership where he can start his business to and for the said reason also the petitioner bonafide requires the premises in question. It is prayed that an eviction order may be passed in respect of the tenanted premises as shown in E­392/2013 Page 5/22 red colour in the site plan.

10. The respondent has filed an application for leave to defend U/s 25­B of DRC Act to defend and contest the eviction petition. The respondent has contended that the present petition is not maintainable under law and the petitioner is guilty of suppressing and concealing the material facts from the court and misrepresenting the facts in order to mislead the court. The petition U/s 14 (1) (e) is barred by Section 14 (6) of the Act which provide that where a landlord has acquired any premises by transfer, no application for recovery of possession of such premises shall lie under sub section (1) on the ground specified in clause (e) of the proviso thereto, unless a period of five years has elapsed from the date of acquisition. The petitioner had suppressed and concealed the material fact that he had acquired the possession of a shop measuring 7 ft. X 15 ft. situated at property no. 33/3154, Ground Floor, Beadonpura, Karol Bagh, New Delhi­110005 just adjacent to the shop of the respondent under an order dated 15.05.2012 in case bearing no. E­10/2012, titled as 'Rajender Prasad Vs. Tulsi Ram' passed by the court of Sh. Ajay Goel, ASCJ­cum­RC, Central, Delhi. E­392/2013 Page 6/22

11. In para 18 (a) (ii) the petitioner has made false statements and declarations to the effect that the second floor of the premises already sold by the petitioner in the year 2001 to one Sh. Jugal Sharma and purchased by the respondent from Sh. Jugal Sharma in the name of the daughter­in­law of the respondent, Ms. Neelam in 2011 and now they have rented out the said portion. Ms. Neelam Singh, W/o Sh. Dinesh Kumar, son of the respondent had purchased the second floor of the said property and they were in possession of the said premises after its purchase and the same has been rented out by her for commercial purposes to a tenant who is in possession of the said premises and the respondent has no concern with the said property purchased by Ms. Neelam Singh, wife of his elder son, Sh. Dinesh Kumar who are living separately from the respondent and running their independent business as a separate unit and family from the respondent.

12. The petitioner has also made false statements and declarations in the present petition in para no.18 (a) (vi) malafidely and illegally to procure eviction order against the respondent by committing fraud before this court by such false statements and declarations to the effect that, "the Respondent E­392/2013 Page 7/22 now also has two other shop one situated in the same building which is let out by the Respondent to one tenant and other in Shastri Nagar, Delhi where he is capable of carrying his business."

13. Shop at L­52, Ground Floor, Shastri Nagar, Delhi is in the possession of Smt. Neelam, W/o Sh. Dinesh Kumar, elder son of the respondent who has separated himself from the family of the respondent and they are running their independent business in the said shop in the name and style of 'Patel Jewelers' as proprietor thereof and Neelam has been paying the income tax for the income form the business running in the said shop at ground floor as a separate entity and after their separation under the family settlement. The only shop in possession of the respondent is under the tenancy of the petitioner for which the present petition is illegally filed against him and no other shop is in occupation or possession of the respondent and in that shop the respondent is running business of goldsmith with his younger son of the Avdesh.

14. The petitioner has also concealed the material facts that as a E­392/2013 Page 8/22 retired Government servant he is entitled to the medical facilities for the treatment of his family members i.e. wife, his dependent daughter and himself for which the entire expenditure incurred is reimbursed by the Government under the Delhi Government Employees Medical Scheme and at the same time he is having suitable income from the two shops which are already rented out at the ground floor in the same building and he is earning income through the business at the shop at ground floor of the same building after the same has been acquired on transfer on or after 15.11.2012 under the orders of the court.

15. The petitioner has now three shops out of which two are at the ground floor in the same building adjacent to the shop of the respondent and one is at the first floor of the same building and the petitioner is earning handsome amount of income either by rent or by his own business he is undertaking business in a shop at ground floor adjacent to the shop of the respondent. The tenancy created by the petitioner in favour of the respondent by rent agreement dated 30.04.1993 is not for any specific period, but for unending period as he had specifically agreed in that rent E­392/2013 Page 9/22 agreement that the tenancy shall continue to be inherited in the name of his legal heirs under the term and condition no.9. The petitioner had received from the respondent an amount of Rs. 2,25,000/­ at the time of creation of the tenancy of the said shop, but the petitioner has not specified this material fact under any paragraph of his petition despite the fact that he has filed some receipt signed by him regarding payment of Rs. 45,000/­ and balance payment of Rs. 1,80,000/­ made on 07.05.1993.

16. The respondent is otherwise entitled to leave to contest the eviction petition since the petitioner has altogether taken false and frivolous stands in the eviction petition. The petitioner wants to get the suit premises vacated from the respondent by concocting false story. The existence of the business of the respondent at this shop for more than 20 years has created a lot of goodwill and the same is in the name of respondent who is registered with the excise and income tax department and removal of his business from the shop shall ruin the respondent as the respondent has no other place in his possession to run his business and earn his livelihood in any manner after his elder son Dinesh had separated from the family of the respondent and has E­392/2013 Page 10/22 been undertaking separate independent business as a separate unit with his wife Neelam with whom the respondent has no concern. The alleged bonafide requirement purported for the requirement of the petitioner after retirement is absolutely fanciful and imaginary. The petitioner infact, is not in need of the suit premises in question and has only made a false and frivolous ground.

17. The petitioner filed his reply alongwith counter affidavit to the application for leave to defend, filed by the respondent and has denied the allegations leveled by the respondent. He has denied the averments made by the respondent in his application for leave to defend and reaffirmed his stance, as averred in the petition.

18. Respondent filed rejoinder and allegations leveled by the petitioner were controverted and rebutted. The averments made in application for leave to defend have been reiterated and reaffirmed.

19. The court has heard submissions advanced on behalf of both the E­392/2013 Page 11/22 parties and have perused the material on record.

20. While deciding the question as to whether leave to defend should be granted or refused, the Court is required to examine the case on the following aspects :­

(a) That the petitioner is owner of the tenanted premises;

(b) Purpose of letting;

(c) That the premises is required bonafide by the petitioner; and

(d) That the petitioner has no other alternative suitable accommodation in Delhi.

21. In Precision Metal & Engg. Works Vs. Prema Deva, Niranjan Deva Tayal, AIR 1982 SC 1518, it has been held that "while deciding the application for leave to contest, the controller has to confine himself to the affidavit filed by the tenant under Sub Section (4) and the reply if any. On perusing the affidavit filed by the tenant and the reply if any filed by the landlord, the controller has to pose to himself the only question, "Does the affidavit disclose, not prove, facts as would disentitle the landlord from E­392/2013 Page 12/22 obtaining an order for the recovery of possession on the ground specified in clause of the proviso to Section 14 (1)?". The controller is not to record a finding on disputed questions of facts or his preference of one set of affidavits against the other set of affidavits."

The gist of the various decisions is that if any triable issue is raised in the application which can not be decided unless the parties lead evidence, leave to contest should be granted. In all other cases, the leave has to be refused.

22. In Rita Lal Vs. Raj Kumar Singh (2002) 7 SCC 614, Hon'ble Supreme Court has observed as under :­ "If the Court is satisfied that though in the pleadings an issue is raised but that is not a triable issue then the Court is justified in refusing the leave to defend. A defence, which is practically moonshine, sham or illusory can not be held to be raising a triable issue. Else the whole purpose behind enacting a provision for granting leave to defend, and not permitting a contest unless leave was granted, would stand defeated." E­392/2013 Page 13/22 Ownership

23. The respondent has admitted that he is a tenant of the petitioner in respect of the tenanted premises. He has not disputed the ownership of the petitioner in respect of the tenanted premises. Therefore, the court holds that the petitioner is the owner of the tenanted premises and he is landlord in respect of the tenanted premises, qua the respondent. Purpose of Letting

24. Purpose of letting has become redundant as in Satyawati Sharma Vs. Union of India 148 (2008) DLT 705 Supreme Court, it has been held that the premises let out either for residential or for commercial purposes can be got vacated by the landlord for bonafide requirements. Moreover, admittedly, the tenanted premises is a residential one. Availability or Non­Availability of alternative suitable accommodation in Delhi

25. It has been averred on behalf of the respondent that the petitioner E­392/2013 Page 14/22 has concealed the material fact that he had acquired possession of a shop measuring 7ft. X 15ft., situated at property no. 33/3154, Ground Floor, Beadonpura, Karol Bagh, New Delhi which is adjoining to shop in possession of the respondent, pursuant to eviction order dated 15.05.2012 passed in eviction petition no. E­10/12 titled as "Rajinder Prasad Vs. Tulsi Ram". The petitioner is having three shops, out of which two shops are situated on the ground floor in the same building, adjoining to the shop in possession of the respondent. Third shop is situated at first floor of the same building and petitioner is earning handsomely either by way of rent or by his own business.

26. Per contra, it is contended on behalf of the petitioner that the petitioner had categorically mentioned in para no. 18 (a) (ii) of the petition that there are two shops situated on ground floor. One shop is in possession and tenancy of the respondent and the other shop is under use and occupation of son of the petitioner. Further, there are differences between the petitioner and his son. Both are not on talking terms with each other and are living separately. It is denied that the petitioner is having three shops, out of which E­392/2013 Page 15/22 two shops are situated on the ground floor in the same building, adjoining to the shop in possession of the respondent. It is also denied that one shop is situated on first floor of the same building. As a matter of fact, there are two shops on the ground floor. One shop is in possession and tenancy of the respondent and the other shop is under use and occupation of estranged son of the petitioner. The first floor of the property is in occupation of the petitioner, his wife and mentally challenged daughter. The complete details of all the floors of the property and their occupation were mentioned in detail in the petition itself.

27. The court is of the considered view that no material fact has been concealed by the petitioner. It has been specifically averred by the petitioner that there are two shops situated on the ground floor. One shop is in possession and tenancy of the respondent and the other shop is under use and occupation of his son. The respondent has failed to file any document to establish his contention that there are three shops, out of which two shops are situated on the ground floor in the same building, adjoining to the shop in possession of the respondent and third shop is situated at first floor of the E­392/2013 Page 16/22 same building from which petitioner is earning handsomely either by way of rent or by his own business. Therefore, the court holds that the petitioner has been successful in establishing that he is not having any other reasonably suitable accommodation for his commercial use and the respondent has not been able to raise any triable issue on this aspect.

Bonafide requirement

28. It has been contended on behalf of the respondent that the petitioner had got possession of shop measuring 7ft. X 15ft., situated on ground floor of property bearing no.33/3154, Ground Floor, Beadonpura, Karol Bagh, New Delhi, adjoining to shop in possession of the respondent, pursuant to order dated 15.05.2012 passed in eviction petition bearing no. E­10/12. Therefore, the petition is barred U/s 14 (6) of the DRC Act as the petitioner cannot file a petition unless a period of five years has elapsed from the date of acquisition of the property. The purported requirement of petitioner is not bonafide as admittedly, second floor of the property was let out in the year, 2011. Had the petitioner required the tenanted premises for his own commercial use, the second floor would not have been let out in the E­392/2013 Page 17/22 year, 2011.

29. The petitioner has falsely averred that the respondent has two other shops, one situated in the same building and the other in Shastri Nagar, Delhi. The shop situated at Shastri Nagar, Delhi is in possession of daughter­in­law of the petitioner, where she is running independent business under the name and style of Patel Jewelers. The petitioner is entitled to reimbursement of medical expenses incurred by him on his own treatment and the treatment of his family members dependent upon him under the DGEHS. Moreover, he is having suitable income from two shops let out by him. He is also earning from the business run in shop situated at the ground floor of the same building, the possession of which was received under eviction order dated 15.05.2012.

30. The tenancy created in favour of the respondent by virtue of rent agreement dated 30.04.1993 was not for any specific period, but perpetual. It was specifically mentioned in the rent agreement that tenancy rights will be inherited by legal heirs of the respondent. The petitioner had received Rs. E­392/2013 Page 18/22 2.25 lacs from the respondent at the time of creation of the tenancy. He had also signed receipt in respect of payment received by him.

31. Per contra, it is averred on behalf of the petitioner that the other shop situated on ground floor of the same building, the possession of which was received under eviction order 15.05.2012 is in use and occupation of his estranged son. The bar created U/s 14 (6) of the DRC Act is not applicable in respect of the tenanted shop in question, which was acquired by petitioner long ago. Admittedly, the tenanted shop was let out to the respondent on 30.04.1993. Therefore, the petition has not been filed within five years from the date of acquisition of the tenanted shop. It is immaterial and irrelevant that the respondent is not having any other alternative shop for running his business.

32. The petitioner is getting his own treatment and the treatment of his family members dependent upon him, done from Private Hospitals to which the DGEHS is not applicable. He is getting a meager pension of Rs. 5,490/­ per month. It is denied that he is having suitable income from two E­392/2013 Page 19/22 shops let out by him or that he is also earning from the business run in shop situated at the ground floor of the same building. It is also denied that the petitioner had received Rs. 2.25 lacs from the respondent at the time of creation of the tenancy. No document has been filed by the respondent to establish his contentions. It is immaterial and irrelevant that the tenancy was not confined to any particular period.

33. The court is of the considered view that the bar created U/s 14 (6) of the DRC Act is not applicable in respect of the tenanted shop, which was admittedly let out to the respondent on 30.04.1993. The respondent has failed to establish that the petitioner had acquired the tenanted shop within five years of filing the present petition. Receipt of possession of adjoining shop on the ground floor of the same building does not create a bar U/s 14 (6) of the DRC Act. It is not disputed by the respondent that the adjoining shop, the possession of which was received under eviction order dated 15.05.2012, is in use and occupation of the estranged son of the petitioner. Letting out second floor of the building in 2011 is irrelevant for deciding the current personal need of the petitioner. Non­availability of suitable E­392/2013 Page 20/22 alternative accommodation with the respondent is immaterial and irrelevant for deciding the present petition. Similarly, availability of medical benefits under the DGEHS and income of petitioner from other sources; tenancy being perpetual is also immaterial and irrelevant for deciding the petition. The respondent has failed to file the alleged receipts regarding payment of Rs. 2.25 lacs to the petitioner at the time of creation of the tenancy in his favour. Therefore, the court holds that the petitioner has been successful in establishing that he require the tenanted premises for his own bonafide use.

34. In view of the above discussion, the court is of the view that the respondent has failed to set up any triable issue which requires inquiry or trial. In other words, prima facie there is nothing on record which would dis­entitle the petitioner of the right of immediate possession in respect of the tenanted premises.

35. Accordingly, the application under Section 25­B (4) of Delhi Rent Control Act, 1958, filed by the respondent is dismissed. The petition is allowed and eviction order is passed in favour of the petitioner and against E­392/2013 Page 21/22 the respondent in respect of one shop measuring 7ft. X 15 ft., situated in property no. 33/3154 (Ground Floor), Beadonpura, Karol Bagh, Delhi, as shown in red colour in site­plan attached alongwith the petition. This order shall not be executable before expiry of six months from the date of this order. Parties to bear their own costs.

File be consigned to Record Room.
