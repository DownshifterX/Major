JUDGMENT:

1. The plaintiffs have filed the suit for partition against the defendants, on the averments that Shri Ram Krishan Garg owned and possessed the suit property bearing Municipal no. 205(Old) measuring 81 sq. yards situated in Azad Nagar,Village Ghondli, Shahdara, Delhi, bearing Khasra no. 691/152/2 and new number of the property is 124/6, West Azad Nagar, Delhi - 110 051 and this property was his self acquired property.

2. The plaintiffs have further averred that the suit property is two storeyed building comprising of ground floor and first floor. The ground floor of the suit property consists of two rooms, kitchen, WC, latrine and lobby, whereas, the first floor of the property consists of three rooms, kitchen, bathroom, WC and a balcony as shown red in the site plan. The plaintiffs have further averred that the defendant no.1 alongwith his family is residing on the ground floor, whereas, the plaintiff no.1 and defendant no.2 are residing on the first floor. The plaintiffs have further averred that the plaintiff no.2 to 4 are in constructive possession of the suit property through their mother i.e. the plaintiff no.1.

3. The plaintiffs have also averred that Shri Ram Krishan Garg died S.no: 771/2009 Santosh Garg and another vs Ajay Garg and another 2 of 22 intestate on 29.8.2001 leaving behind the plaintiffs and the defendants, as his legal heirs and the plaintiffs no. 2 to 4 are his daughters,whereas, the defendants no.1 and 2 are his sons. The plaintiffs have further averred that as per the law of inheritance, the parties to the present suit have inherited the suit property in equal share to the extent of 1/6th share each.

4. The plaintiffs have further stated that the plaintiffs have approached to the defendants several times in the past 7­8 years to partition the suit property and give their shares, but the defendants have paid no heed to the demand of the plaintiffs and again on 3.3.2009 the plaintiff no.1 and 3 approached to the defendants for partition of the suit property or in alternative to dispose of the suit property and demanded for their legitimate share, but the defendants have flatly refused to do so and threatened that they will dispose of the suit property or create third party interest in the suit property, in the absence of the plaintiffs. They have also stated that a notice dated 16.3.2009 was served upon the defendants, vide which, they had demanded for the partition of the suit property, but he defendants have failed to comply with the same.

5. It is further averred that the defendants have become dishonest and they want to grab the shares of the plaintiffs in the suit property and the plaintiffs have come to know that the defendants have been approaching to the property dealers to sell the suit property and they are representing themselves as the exclusive owners of the suit property. It is further averred that the plaintiffs are the co­owners S.no: 771/2009 Santosh Garg and another vs Ajay Garg and another 3 of 22 and they have the 1/6th share each in the suit property and they are legally entitled to have the property partitioned by metes and bounds and the defendants have no exclusive right, title or interest in the suit property and as such they have no right to dispose of, transfer or create any third party interest in the suit property and further averred that in case the defendants succeed in their evils designs or motives, the plaintiffs will suffer an irreparable loss.

6. Feeling aggrieved with the acts and conducts of the defendants, the plaintiffs have filed the suit for partition and prayed for passing a preliminary decree for partition in favour of the plaintiffs and against the defendants by fixing 1/6th share each of the plaintiffs in respect of the suit property bearing no. 124/6, West Azad Nagar, Delhi­51, measuring 81 square yards, as per the site plan and prayed for passing a decree of partition by dividing the suit property by metes and bounds between the parties.

7. On completion of the service, the defendants have put their appearance.

8. The defendants no.1 and 2 have filed the separate Written Statements.

9. The defendant no.1 has contested the case on the ground interalia that the suit of the plaintiffs is without any cause of action against him, hence the same is liable to be dismissed. The defendant no.1 has further stated that the plaintiffs are not entitled to file the present suit for partition and their suit is barred by section 23 of the Hindu Succession Act,1956 and further stated that cause of action arose on dated 29.8.2001, when Shri Ram Krishan Garg expired. The S.no: 771/2009 Santosh Garg and another vs Ajay Garg and another 4 of 22 defendant no.1 has also stated that the suit has not been propely signed and verified, as the plaintiff no.2 and 4 were not residing at Delhi, when the present suit was filed and they are living at Lucknow and Chennai with their husbands and their signatures were obtained through courier. Replying to the suit on merit, the defendant no.1 has not denied that Shri Ram Krishan Garg was the owner of the suit property. He has also not denied that Shri Ram Krishan Garg died intestate leaving behind the parties to the present lis, as his legal heirs. He has admitted that plaintiff no.1 and the defendant no.2 are residing on the 1st floor of the suit property, but he has denied that the plaintiff no. 2 to 4 are in constructive possession of the suit property and also denied that the plaintiffs have inherited 1/6th share each in the suit property. It is stated that the plaintiffs are not entitled to file the present suit for partition. The defendant no.1 has also denied that the plaintiffs have approached to the defendant no.1 several times in the past 7/8 years to partition the suit property. It is stated that the defendant no.1 has no other property in Delhi, where he may reside with his family members. The defendant no.1 has also denied that on 3.3.2009, the plaintiff no.1 and 3 had approached to the defendant no.1 or that the defendant no.1 had picked up a quarrel with the plaintiffs or that he had threatened them with dire consequences, if they demand their shares in future or that the defendant no.1 had threatened to dispose off the suit property or that he will create third party interest in the suit property or that the defendant no.1 became dishonest or that the defendant no.1 wants to S.no: 771/2009 Santosh Garg and another vs Ajay Garg and another 5 of 22 grab the shares of the plaintiffs or that he had approached to the property dealers for the sale of the suit property or that the plaintiffs are entitled to1/6th share each in the suit property and after denying the other averments mentioned in the plaint, prayed for the dismissal of the suit.

10. Whereas, the defendant no.2 has filed the written statement and denied the allegation leveled against him and stated that he has no objection, if the suit property is partitioned by metes and bounds.

11. The plaintiffs have filed the replication to the written statement of the defendant no.1 and reiterated the contents of the plaint and denied the averments made in the written statement and stated that section 23 of the Hindu Succession Act has already been omitted way back in the year 2005 and denied that the plaint is not properly signed or verified or that signatures of the plaintiff no.2 and 4 were obtained through courier.

Issue no.1 was treated as Preliminary issue and decided in favour of the plaintiffs vide order dated 29.8.2011.

13.In order to prove their case, the plaintiff no.3 has examined herself as PW1 vide her affidavit Ex.PW1/A and relied upon certain documents and the plaintiffs have not examined any other witness and closed their evidence.

14.Whereas, the defendants have examined defendant no.2 as DW1 vide his affidavit Ex.DW1/A. He has also deposed on the line of his written statement and they were cross examined by the ld.counsel for the plaintiffs and the defendants have not examined any other witness and closed their evidence.

15.I have heard the ld.counsel for the parties and perused the record.

16.The Ld. Counsel for the plaintiffs has submitted that suit property was owned by Sh. Ram Krishan Garg i.e. husband of plaintiff no.1 and father of plaintiffs no.2 to 4 and both the defendants, who had purchased the suit property vide sale deed Ex.PW1/1. He has further submitted that similarly in para no.3 of the plaint, the plaintiffs have stated that Sh. He has also submitted that defendant no.1 is the only contesting defendant in the case in hand and also submitted that defendant no.1 has taken the plea in the Written statement that suit of the plaintiffs is barred U/s 23 of the Hindu Succession Act and also submitted that the predecessor of this court had framed the issues in the case in hand on dated 26.10.2009 and issue no.1 "Whether the present suit is barred by section 23 of Hindu Succession Act" was treated as preliminary issue and this court vide order dated 29.08.2011 had decided the same issue in favour of the plaintiffs and burden of proving issues no.3&4 was on the plaintiffs and also submitted that since plaintiff no.1 is admittedly in physical possession of the suit property and plaintiffs no.2 to 4 are in constructive possession of the suit property, as whenever they come to meet to their mother i.e. the plaintiff no.1, they reside in the suit property and they have never been ousted from the suit property and the plaintiffs have valued the suit in para no.12 of S.no: 771/2009 Santosh Garg and another vs Ajay Garg and another 8 of 22 the plaint, wherein, plaintiffs have valued the suit for the purpose of jurisdiction as Rs.16 lacs and for the purpose of court fee Rs. 200/­ and affixed the fixed court fees of Rs.20/­ and further submitted that defendant no.1 in corresponding para no.12 of his Written statement has merely denied that plaintiffs have properly valued the suit, but, defendant no.1 has failed to categorically mention as to what should be the value of the suit for the purpose of court fee and jurisdiction, so, in the absence of any categorical averments regarding valuation of the suit, the same cannot be looked into and the valuation of the suit done by the plaintiffs may be deemed to be correct. He has also submitted that since plaintiffs have filed the present suit for partition and defendant no.1 has taken the plea in the Written statement that plaintiffs no.2 to 4 were not present in Delhi, when the present suit was filed and they are residing with their husbands at Lucknow and Chennai respectively and the signatures of them were obtained through courier, but, defendant no.1 has not denied that plaintiffs no.1&3 have signed and verified the plaint and signatures of the plaintiff no.2 and 4 on the plaint have not been denied by him. He has also submitted that during cross examination of defendant no.1, he has stated that there is no basis of such assertion in his affidavit that suit has not been signed and verified in accordance with law and thus,it is clear that allegations levelled by the defendant no.1 in S.no: 771/2009 Santosh Garg and another vs Ajay Garg and another 9 of 22 his Written statement and affidavit Ex.DW2/A are bald and baseless, so, the same cannot be looked into. He has also submitted that since Sh. Ram Krishan Garg was owner of the suit property and parties to the present lis are his legal heirs and he died intestate, so, the plaintiffs and defendants are entitled to get 1/6th share each in the suit property and submitted that preliminary decree of partition is liable to be passed in favour of the plaintiffs and against the defendants.

17.On the other hand, the Ld. counsel for the defendant no.1 has submitted that Sh. Ram Krishan Garg expired on dated 29.08.2001 and further submitted that cause of action arose to the plaintiffs to file the present suit way back on dated 29.08.2001 and then the section 23 of the Hindu Succession Act was not omitted. He has further submitted that since cause of action arose to the plaintiffs way back in the year 2001, on the death of Shri Ram Krishan Garg, so, the suit of the plaintiffs is barred in accordance with section 23 of Hindu Succession Act, as it is a dwelling house and female members of the family, who S.no: 771/2009 Santosh Garg and another vs Ajay Garg and another 10 of 22 are the plaintiffs in the case in hand, cannot file the suit for partition of the suit property, which is a dwelling house. He has further submitted that plaintiffs have filed the suit for partition, but, only one plaintiff no.3 has chosen to appear in the witness box and examined herself as PW1 and plaintiffs no.1,2&4 did not come in the witness box and also submitted that in view of non examination and non coming to the court of the plaintiffs no. 1,2&4, the suit of the plaintiffs is liable to be rejected and an adverse inference is liable to be drawn against the plaintiffs. He has further submitted that plaintiff no.3 has admitted during her cross examination that plaintiffs no.2,3&4 are not residing in the property and also submitted that plaintiffs no.2&4 did not come in Delhi to sign the plaint nor they have come in the witness box to prove their case, so, the suit of the plaintiffs is liable to be dismissed. He has relied upon the judgment passed by their lordship of Supreme Court of India in case G. Sikar v. Githa & Ors., III(2009) SLT 349.

18.In rebuttal thereof, the ld. counsel for the plaintiffs has submitted that since admittedly, Sh. Ram Krishan Garg expired intestate, so, as per the provisions of Hindu Succession Act, the parties to the present lis are entitled to succeed him and further submitted that judgment relied upon by the ld. Githa & Ors., III(2009) SLT 349 is pleased to hold that ''Even otherwise, it is not a fit case where we should exercise our discretionary jurisdiction under Article 136 of the Constitution of India as the fact remains that Section 23 of the Hindu Succession Act as it stood was to be applicable on the date of the institution of the suit. Respondents may file a new suit and obtain a decree for partition.'' The ld.counsel for the plaintiff has further submitted that so the section 23 of the Hindu Succession Act cannot be invoked to deprive the plaintiffs from their rights to succeed the estate(suit property) of Sh. Ram Krishan Garg and also submitted that since the plaintiff no.3 has been examined as PW1, so, there was no need for the plaintiffs no.1,2&4 to come in the witness box and to depose on the similar line, as that of the plaintiff no.3, as the quality of the evidence is always relevant and not the quantity and in order to avoid repetition of the testimony of plaintiff no.3, only plaintiff no.3 has come in the witness box and examined herself as PW1 vide her affidavit Ex.PW1/A and it is sufficient for decreetal of the suit and prayed for the decreetal of the suit.

19.I have given thoughtful consideration to the submissions made by the ld. counsels for the parties and perused the record.

20.Perusal of the record shows that vide order dated 26.10.2009, the predecessor of this court had framed four issues and issue no.1 S.no: 771/2009 Santosh Garg and another vs Ajay Garg and another 12 of 22 was as under :­ "whether the present suit is barred by section 23 of Hindu Succession Act, 1956 ? OPD" and said issue was treated as preliminary issue and this court vide its order dated 29.08.2011 had decided the said issue in favour of the plaintiffs and held that on the date of institution of the present suit, the provisions of section 23 of the Hindu Succession Act, was not in existence and in the considered opinion of this court, the provisions of section 23 of Hindu Succession Act, cannot be invoked to dismiss the suit of the plaintiffs and defendant no.1, who is the only contesting defendant has not chosen to challenge the findings on the said issue before any higher court, so, the findings of this court on the said issue, have achieved finality. Thus, I am inclined to hold that the counsel for the defendant no. 1 who is only contesting defendant has re­agitated the same contentions, which were raised by him at the time of arguments on the preliminary issue, which are not permissible under the law, so the same contentions are not considerable.

21.In order to avoid any repetition of the discussions, all the issues have been taken up together and my findings on issues no.2 to 4 are as under :­

22. The burden of proving issues no.3&4 was on the plaintiffs and in order to discharge their burden of proving these issues, the plaintiff no.3 namely Ms. Vibha Goyal has examined herself, as S.no: 771/2009 Santosh Garg and another vs Ajay Garg and another 13 of 22 PW1, vide her affidavit Ex.PW1/A and in one way or other, she has reiterated the contents of plaint in her affidavit Ex.PW1/A. She has relied upon the documents i.e. certified copy of the Sale deed dated 23.1.1976 of the suit property Ex.PW1/1, site plan of the suit property Ex.PW1/2, office copy of legal notice dated 16.3.2009 Ex.PW1/3,, postal receipt Ex.PW1/4, two couriers receipts Ex.PW1/5 and Ex.PW1/6, AD Card Ex.PW1/7. She was subjected to cross examine by the ld. Counsel for the defendant no.1 and the ld. Counsel for the defendant no.1. The ld. Counsel for the defendant no.1 has submitted that plaintiff no. 3 has admitted during her cross examination that plaintiffs no. 2,3&4 are not residing in the suit property and he has also submitted that plaintiffs no.2&4 did not come in Delhi to sign the plaint and their signatures were obtained through courier, nor they have come in the witness box to prove their case, so, the suit of the plaintiffs is liable to be dismissed. Whereas, the ld. Counsel for the plaintiffs has controverted the same contentions raised by the ld. Counsel for the defendant no.1 and submitted that the plaintiffs have claimed that the plaintiff no.1 is in the actual physical possession of the suit property and plaintiffs no.2 to 4 are in constructive possession of the suit property and admittedly plaintiffs no.2 to 4 are married daughters of the deceased Ram Krishan Garg and they are residing in their S.no: 771/2009 Santosh Garg and another vs Ajay Garg and another 14 of 22 matrimonial homes and whenever they come to meet to the plaintiff no.1, they live in the suit property, which is their parental house and it is not the case of the defendant no.1 that the plaintiff no.2 to 4 were ever ousted from the suit property and since the plaintiff no.2 to 4 are never ousted from the suit property,so, the plaintiffs no.2 to 4 are in constructive possession of the suit property.

23.Since, this is an admitted fact that plaintiff no.1 is in actual physical possession of the suit property and admittedly the plaintiffs no.2 to 4 are the married daughters of Sh. Ram Krishan Garg and they are permanently residing in their matrimonial homes. But, as the defendant no.1 has admitted that the plaintiff no.1 is continuously residing in the suit property and being in possession of the suit property, the plaintiffs no.2 to 4 being in constructive possession of the suit property, they have valued the suit for the purpose of jurisdiction as Rs.16 lacs and for the purpose of court fee Rs.200/­ and affixed the fixed court fee of Rs.20/­ and the perusal of the corresponding para no.12 of the Written statement shows that defendant no.1 has merely denied the same averments made in para no.12 of the plaint, but, defendant no.1 has not categorically mentioned as to what should be the value of the suit for the purpose of jurisdiction and court fee, so, in the absence of any categorical averments and any S.no: 771/2009 Santosh Garg and another vs Ajay Garg and another 15 of 22 cogent evidence, regarding the valuation of the suit, contrary to the valuation done in the para no.13 of the plaint, the valuation of the suit done by the plaintiffs in para no.13 of the plaint are deemed to be correct. Reliance is placed upon the judgment passed by their lordship of Supreme Court of India in case Ram Narain Prasad and another v. Atul Chander Mitra and others, (1994) 4 S.C.C. 349 and in case Sathappa Chettiar vs Ramanathan Chettiar MANU/SC/0003/1957 :

[1958]1SCR1021.

24. Since, the plaintiff no.1 is in actual physical possession of the portion of the suit property, whereas, the plaintiff no.2 to 4 are in constructive possession. The defendant no.1 has denied the possession of the plaintiffs no.2 to 4 over the suit property. But, the mere denial by the defendant no.1, does not tantamount ouster of the plaintiff no.2 to 4 from the suit property, so the suit is governed by Article 17(iv) of the Schedule II of the Court Fee Act, so the plaintiffs have rightly affixed the court fee of Rs.20/­ on the plaint. Reliance is placed on the judgment passed by their lordship of Hon'ble High Court of Delhi in Jagdish Prasad vs Jyoti Prasad 1975 I.L.R.(Delhi) 841. So, in view of the above discussion, the issue no.3 is decided in favour of the plaintiffs and against the defendants.

25.The defendant no.1 has taken the plea in the Written statement S.no: 771/2009 Santosh Garg and another vs Ajay Garg and another 16 of 22 that the plaint has not been properly signed and verified and the ld. Counsel for the defendant no.1 has submitted that plaintiffs no.2 and 4 were not present in Delhi when the present suit was filed and they were residing with their husbands at Lucknow and Chennai respectively and their signatures were obtained through courier, but, as the defendant no.1 has not denied the signatures of the plaintiffs no.2&4 on the plaint. He has also not denied that signatures of the plaintiffs no.1&3 on the plaint and it is pertinent to mention here that at the time of cross examination of the defendant no.1, the defendant no.1 has admitted that there is no basis of such assertion in his affidavit that suit has not been signed and verified in accordance with law and thus, it is clear that allegations levelled by the defendant no.1 in his Written statement and affidavit Ex.DW2/A are held to be absolutely bald and baseless and I am inclined to hold that defendant no.1 has miserably failed to prove on record any infirmity or illegality in the signing or verification of the plaint, so, in the absence of any cogent evidence, the issue no.2 is hereby decided against the defendants.

26.Since this is the suit for partition filed by the plaintiffs and the plaintiffs have claimed that suit property was owned by Sh. Ram Krishan Garg who was the husband of plaintiff no.1 and father of plaintiffs no.2 to 4 and both the defendants, who had S.no: 771/2009 Santosh Garg and another vs Ajay Garg and another 17 of 22 purchased the suit property vide sale deed Ex.PW1/1. Since the perusal of the record shows that plaintiffs have pleaded in the para no.1 of the plaint that Sh. Ram Krishan Garg was the owner in possession of the suit property and it was his self acquired property and replying to this para, the defendant no.1 has not denied the same and stated in the corresponding para of his written statement that it needs no reply and similarly perusal of the para no.3 of the plaint shows that, the plaintiffs have pleaded therein that Sh. Ram Krishan Garg expired intestate on dated 29.08.2001 and in the corresponding para no.3 of the Written statement of the defendant no.1, he has not denied the same that Sh. Ram Krishan Garg died intestate on 29.08.2001 and the defendant has stated that it needs no reply, so, in the absence of any denial thereof, the same paras no.1&3 of the plaint, are deemed to have been admitted by the defendant no.1 in accordance with the provisions of order 8 rule 5 of CPC.

27.The ld. Counsel for the defendant no.1 has submitted that since Sh. Ram Krishan Garg expired on 29.08.2001 and at the time of his death, section 23 of Hindu Succession Act was not omitted, so, the cause of action arose to the plaintiffs to file the present suit on dated 29.08.2001 i.e. at the time death of Sh. Ram Krishan Garg and in view of section 23 of the Hindu Succession Act, the suit of the plaintiff is barred. He has relied upon the S.no: 771/2009 Santosh Garg and another vs Ajay Garg and another 18 of 22 judgment passed by their lordship of Supreme Court of India in case G. Sikar v. Githa & Ors., III(2009) SLT 349. I have perused the said judgment and this court finds that the said case for partition of a dwelling house was filed prior to the omission of section 23 of the Hindu Succession Act and during the pendency of the Appeal, in the said case the section 23 of the Hindu Succession Act was omitted, so, hon'ble Supreme Court of India in case G. Sikar v. Githa & Ors., III(2009) SLT 349 is pleased to hold that ''Even otherwise, it is not a fit case where we should exercise our discretionary jurisdiction under Article 136 of the Constitution of India as the fact remains that Section 23 of the Hindu Succession Act as it stood was to be applicable on the date of the institution of the suit. Respondents may file a new suit and obtain a decree for partition.''

28.So, from the judgment of the hon'ble Supreme Court of India,it is clear that since on the date of filing of the said suit by a female, the section 23 of the Hindu Succession Act was therein existence, so the hon'ble Supreme Court of India, was pleased to hold that the respondents may file new suit and obtain the decree for partition and since in the case in hand, on the date of institution of the present suit, section 23 of the Hindu Succession Act was not in existence, so, in view of the above discussion, this S.no: 771/2009 Santosh Garg and another vs Ajay Garg and another 19 of 22 court does not find any force in the submissions made by the ld. Counsel for the defendant no.1. In the considered opinion of this court, the suit of the plaintiffs, which is filed on dated 16.04.2009 can not be dismissed by way of invoking section 23 of Hindu Succession Act, which has already been omitted on dated 09.09.2005 and I am of the firmed view that the plaintiffs cannot be deprived from their right to seek partition even of the dwelling house after the omission of the section 23 of the Hindu Succession Act, so the judgment relied upon by the counsel for the defendant no.1 is of no help for the defendant no.1.

29.Since, it is proved on the record that Sh. Ram Krishan Garg i.e. husband of the plaintiff no.1 and father of the plaintiffs no.2 to 4 and defendants was the registered owner of the suit property who had purchased the same vide sale deed Ex.PW1/1 and Sh. Ram Krishan Garg has already been expired on dated 29.08.2001 intestate and the parties to the present lis are admittedly the legal heirs of Sh. Ram Krishan Garg, so, as per section 8 of the Hindu Succession Act, all the legal heirs are entitled to succeed the estate / suit property of deceased Ram Krishan Garg and the plaintiff no.3 has been examined as PW1 and in one way or other she has reiterated the contents of plaint in her affidavit and her testimony has gone unimpeached and the ld. Counsel for the defendant no.1 has submitted that since the plaintiffs no.1,2,4 S.no: 771/2009 Santosh Garg and another vs Ajay Garg and another 20 of 22 failed to come in the witness box to prove their case, so, an adverse inference is liable to be drawn against them and suit of the plaintiffs is liable to be dismissed. But, I do not find any force in the submissions made by the ld. Counsel for the defendant no.1, because this is suit for partition and the plaintiff no.3 has examined herself as PW1, vide her affidavit Ex.PW1/A and relied upon the documents Ex.PW1/1 to Ex.PW1/7 and she has successfully proved on record that the plaintiffs are entitled to the preliminary decree of partition, as in a suit for partition, the plaintiffs and defendants sail in the same boat and this court is of the firmed view that for the determination of the issues and disposal of the case, quality of the evidence is relevant and not the quantity and had the plaintiffs no.1,2&4 appeared in the court, they could only repeat the evidence, which has been brought on record by the plaintiff no.3 in her testimony and in the considered opinion of this court, it was no more necessary for the plaintiffs no.1,2&4 to come in the witness box and to repeat the testimony of the plaintiff no.3. In view of above discussion, I am inclined to hold that the testimony of the plaintiff no.3 is sufficient, reliable and unimpeached, so, from her unimpeached testimony, she has successfully proved on record that the plaintiffs and defendants are entitled to 1/6th share each in the suit property. Accordingly, issue no.4 is hereby decided in favour of the plaintiffs and against the defendants. S.no: 771/2009 Santosh Garg and another vs Ajay Garg and another 21 of 22 Relief : In view of my findings on all the issues in favour of the plaintiffs and against the defendants, as discussed above, I am inclined to hold that that the plaintiffs and defendants are entitled to get 1/6th share each in the suit property.
