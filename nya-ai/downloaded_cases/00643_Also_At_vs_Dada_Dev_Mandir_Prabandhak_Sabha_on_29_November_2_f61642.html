J U D G M E N T:

All these appeals are against the judgment/decree dated 29.09.2014, passed by the Ld.Trial Court, whereby the suit for possession and mesne profits filed by the respondent against the appellant has been decreed. It is relevant to note here that this is second round of litigation between the parties in respect of the same suit property, i.e the shop in question. Earlier, the decree for possession in respect of suit property was passed by the Ld.Trial Court U/o 12 Rule 6 CPC. The appeal against the said decree was dismissed by this court by way of common judgment dated 23.07.2013, rendered in a batch of appeals, the leading appeal case being this very case itself. The matter was carried forward to the second appeal by the appellant herein, being RSA No.201/2013, titled as, "Anil Kumar V/s Dada Dev Mandir Prabhandak Sabha (Barah Gaon), Palam", which was disposed off by the Hon'ble High Court vide order dated 06.09.2013, thereby setting aside the order of the Ld.Trial Court as also of this court and the matter was remanded back to the trial court with the direction to frame issues with regard to jurisdiction of the civil court as well as the competence of the President of respondent to institute the suit. The said two issues were specifically framed in all the cases, out of which the present appeals have arisen. The issues with regard to competence of the President to institute the suit is issue No.(i), whereas the issue with regard jurisdiction is issue No.(ii) in all the cases. The said issues are re­produced herein below: All RCAs U/s 96 CPC r/w Order 41 CPC: "All RCAs Dismissed" Page 10 of 23

2. For the sake of convenience, the parties in this judgment are being referred to as per their respective status before the Ld.Trial Court. The appeal being RCA No.146/2014, titled as, "Anil Kumar V/s Dada Dev Mandir Prabhandak Sabha (Barah Gaon), Palam" is treated as the lead case and the facts of this case would be referred to in this judgment and this judgment would be applicable in the facts and circumstances of all the other appeals.

3. The facts of the case, relevant for the present are that plaintiff Dada Dev Mandir Prabhandak Sabha (Barah Gaon), Palam is a society registered under Delhi Societies Act (hereinafter referred to as the "society"). The society has a huge Mandir and a lot of devotees who come from nearby different villages and villages outside Delhi, to worship the deity. The front portion of Mandir, managed by the society has 54 shops, which have been rented out to different tenants. The defendants in these appeals are some of the tenants in respect of shops given on rent to them by the plaintiff. The suits for eviction against the defendants were filed by the society through its Pradhan namely Shri Chander Bhan, pursuant to a Resolution dated 06.05.2012, passed by the society in his favour. Thereafter, a further Resolution dated 20.05.2012 was passed by the society, authorising Shri Chander Bhan to institute the suit for eviction against the defendants and after signing and All RCAs U/s 96 CPC r/w Order 41 CPC: "All RCAs Dismissed" Page 11 of 23 RCAs No.146/2014; 140/2014; 142/2014; 143/2014; DOD: 29.11.2014 144/2014; 147/2014; 145/2014; 148/2014 & 141/2014 verifying the same. It was stated in all the suits that the possession of all the shops was required for the purpose of construction of a Dharamshala and parking lot. It was further stated that because of the commercial activity carried out by the defendants in their shops, a lot of crowding takes place at or around the area, which causes hindrance in the free ingress and egress of the devotees, who come to pay their respect to the Mandir. It was also averred that the defendants were in the arrears of rent w.e.f January' 2012.

4. Notices u/s 106 of Transfer of Property Act, 1882 were served upon the defendants by the plaintiff society, whereby their tenancies were terminated and they were asked to handover the vacant and peaceful possession of their respective shops to the society, so that the society could demolish the shops and start the work of construction of Dharmshala and parking lot therein.

5. Rather than vacating their respective shops, the tenants of all the shops formed a union in the name of "Dada Dev Mandir Welfare Association" and filed a suit for injunction against the "society", which is pending disposal in the court of Ld.ACJ (South­West), wherein the defendants have admitted themselves to be the tenants of plaintiff society.

6. In the written statement, the defendants have questioned the ownership of the shops by the society; it has been contended that the provisions of Delhi Rent Control Act are applicable to the shops in question and that the society has approached the court with unclean hands and its motive is to evict the defendants All RCAs U/s 96 CPC r/w Order 41 CPC: "All RCAs Dismissed" Page 12 of 23 RCAs No.146/2014; 140/2014; 142/2014; 143/2014; DOD: 29.11.2014 144/2014; 147/2014; 145/2014; 148/2014 & 141/2014 from the shops to let out the same to other proposed tenants at a higher rent.

10. The " impugned judgment " has been questioned before this court by the defendant with regard to the findings on first two issues only. I have heard arguments advanced at bar by Shri Rakesh Kumar, advocate, learned counsel for the defendant and Shri B.S Kharb & Shri Vijender Singh Kharb, advocates, learned counsels for the plaintiff.

11. In challenge to the findings of Ld.Trial Court on issue No.(i), it is contended by the learned counsel for the defendant that the Ld.Trial Court failed to All RCAs U/s 96 CPC r/w Order 41 CPC: "All RCAs Dismissed" Page 14 of 23 RCAs No.146/2014; 140/2014; 142/2014; 143/2014; DOD: 29.11.2014 144/2014; 147/2014; 145/2014; 148/2014 & 141/2014 appreciate that the rules and regulations of the plaintiff framed on 13.02.2003 changed the constitution of plaintiff and as such, the defendant is not presumed to be the tenant of new society which came into existence after framing of the rules in the year 2003. 12. In order to appreciate the aforesaid contentions of the learned counsel for the defendant, I have myself gone through the original minute book containing resolutions, which has been produced in court by the learned counsel for the plaintiff. I have also meticulously gone through the evidence recorded in the matter as well as the documents proved on record. After considering the entire material on record in totality, I do not find substance in any of the arguments of the learned counsel for the defendant in challenge to the findings on issue No.(i) rendered by the Ld.Trial Court. The reasoning given by the Ld.Trial Court while deciding issue No.(i) in favour of plaintiff is not only self­sufficient, but also based upon sound All RCAs U/s 96 CPC r/w Order 41 CPC: "All RCAs Dismissed" Page 15 of 23 RCAs No.146/2014; 140/2014; 142/2014; 143/2014; DOD: 29.11.2014 144/2014; 147/2014; 145/2014; 148/2014 & 141/2014 appreciation of the evidence and the law applicable thereto.

13. The resolution dated 16.08.2009 (Ex.PW1/4) has been questioned by the learned counsel for the defendant on the ground that the same stands signed by about 110 persons with their mobile numbers also affixed thereupon and as per the list of members of plaintiff furnished to the Registrar of Societies, there were only 61 members of the plaintiff. Rule 8 of the plaintiff explains the constitution of governing body and Rule 15 deals with the election process to be conducted by secret ballot or by show of hands as the Election Officer may decide. The elections are supposed to be held after every three years. Admittedly, election dated 16.08.2009 was conducted under the guidance of Rao Surat Singh, as admitted by the witnesses of defendant, i.e DW­5 to DW­7. Besides the members of governing body, persons from the general public, who are the devotees coming to the plaintiff society which primarily runs the Mandir were also present. The elections for the governing body were probably held in the Mandir itself, where all the persons present at that time affixed their signatures in the original proceeding book maintained in the plaintiff's office and as such, no fault could be found with about 100 persons signing the proceeding sheet. This court cannot loose sight of the fact that plaintiff society was formed to cater to the religious needs of the people of named twelve villages. The primary object of the plaintiff society is religious and not commercial. Therefore, they are not supposed to maintain the records meticulously, as is done by commercial associations. Even otherwise, there is no procedures prescribed either under the Societies Registration Act, 1860 or under any other law as to how the minute books of a religious society is to be maintained. 14. The challenge to other two resolutions, being Ex.PW1/2 and Ex.PW1/3 is on the ground that the same does not bear the signatures of atleast 2/3rd members of the governing body. The argument is factually incorrect. The learned counsel for the plaintiff has demonstrated on record from the original resolutions that both the resolutions bear the signatures of more than six members of the governing body as admittedly in the year 2012 there were only nine members of the governing body. Therefore, by now it is clearly apparent that PW­1 was elected as President on 16.08.2009 and under ordinary circumstances his tenure as President would have gone on till 15.08.2012, but on account of some reasons the election of the plaintiff society could not take place till 24.02.2013 and as such, there is presumption that the governing body continued to function till the next election. There is clear evidence on trial court record, produced by DW­3, i.e Ex.DW3/A with regard to framing of rules, it has details of members of governing body as also the correspondence with the Registrar of Societies regarding misplacement of the file of plaintiff in the O/o Registrar of Societies and re­constitution of the rules of plaintiff society. Therefore, the Ld.Trial Court was right in coming to the conclusion that PW­1 was the duly authorized person to verify, institute and maintain a suit on behalf of plaintiff. There are certain facts which are required to be noticed in this case and the same are very relevant to the findings on issue No.(i). Firstly, in the cross­examination of PW­1, defendant did not put any suggestion to him about the resolutions Ex.PW1/2 to Ex.PW1/4 being either in violation of the rules or not being executed properly. No question with regard to the authority of PW­1 in signing, verifying and instituting the suit was put. The alleged irregularities in preparation of the aforesaid resolutions being argued before this court was neither argued before the Ld.Trial Court nor any suggestion to this effect was given to PW­1.

V/s Ranganatha Enterprises", the Hon'ble High Court of Delhi has been pleased to hold that when a witness deposes a particular fact and no suggestion to the contrary is given to him during his cross­examination, party against whom deposition is made is deemed to have admitted that part of the deposition.

Secondly, it is worth noting that DW­5 to DW­7, who are independent persons were duly cross­examined by the plaintiff and they categorically stated that PW­1 was appointed President of the plaintiff society in their presence and they had All RCAs U/s 96 CPC r/w Order 41 CPC: "All RCAs Dismissed" Page 18 of 23 RCAs No.146/2014; 140/2014; 142/2014; 143/2014; DOD: 29.11.2014 144/2014; 147/2014; 145/2014; 148/2014 & 141/2014 exercised their votes in the election process. In the teeth of the evidence of witnesses of defendant, the Ld.Trial Court is clearly right in holding that the plaintiff was duly elected President of plaintiff society and he continued as such till fresh elections were held on 24.02.2013 and he was even authorized to depose as PW­1 in the court on the strength of fresh resolution passed in his favour by the new governing body, ratifying the earlier resolutions. The learned counsel for the plaintiff has relied upon the law laid down by the Hon'ble Supreme Court in case titled as, "Union Bank of India V/s Naresh Kumar & Ors." (decided on 18.09.1996 by Hon'ble Supreme Court of India) and has contended that the ratification of the authority to sign, verify and institute the suit in favour of PW­1 by the subsequent governing body is enough to loath PW­1 with the authority to represent the plaintiff in the suit. I have gone through the aforesaid judgment. The law laid down in the said judgment is applicable on all fours in this case.

In "Goa State Co­ op Bank Ltd.'s" case (supra), the issue was with regard to interpretation of Section 52 of Multi­State Co­operative Societies Act, 2002 and the authorization to file a complaint U/s 138 NI Act in that case was not in accordance with the statutory requirement. In "Deepak R. Mehtra's" and "Gorakh Hilal Patil's" case (supra), the issue was entirely different and totally unrelated to the controversy in hand.

19. In the end, the learned counsel for the defendant has very bleakly argued that the suit filed by the plaintiff was barred U/s 50 of the Delhi Rent Control Act, as the suit property falls in the area of Palam, being part of Najafgarh estate where the provisions of Delhi Rent Control Act are applicable. The aforesaid argument is factually and legally incorrect, as this aspect is no more res­integra and we have authoritative pronouncements of the Hon'ble Supreme Court of India as well as our own Hon'ble High Court in this regard. The Ld. Trial Court while returning findings on issue No. 2 has already taken note of one such judgment in the case of "Mittar Sen Jain V/s Shakuntala Devi" 2000 (9) SCC 720.

20. In my considered opinion, this argument made by the Ld. Counsel for the defendant is incorrect and the same has been taken by suppressing the relevant notification of the government, applicable to the land forming part of village Palam. Presently, there are 11 Districts/Divisions existing in Delhi. The suit property falls in South­West revenue district and village Palam is part of Najafgarh Sub­Division. South­West District has three Sub­Divisions, i.e Delhi Cantonment, Vasant Vihar and Najafgarh. Village Palam forms part of Najafgarh Sub­Division.
