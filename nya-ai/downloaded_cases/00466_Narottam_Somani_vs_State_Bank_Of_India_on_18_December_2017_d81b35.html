Madhya Pradesh High Court

Narottam Somani vs State Bank Of India on 18 December, 2017

                                          1

HIGH COURT OF MADHYA PRADESH BENCH AT INDORE
  (S.B.: HON. SHRI JUSTICE PRAKASH SHRIVASTAVA)
                       Writ Petition No.6213/2017

Narottam Somani, Chief Managing Director & Guarantor,
M/s. Midex Global Pvt. Ltd and others ...    Petitioners
                                        Vs.
State Bank of India and others                       ...       Respondents

Whether approved for reporting :
                                   ORDER

(Passed on 18th December, 2017 ) By this writ petition the petitioners have challenged the order dated 11/6/2013 Annexure P/2 passed by the Additional Magistrate, Indore u/S.14 of the Securitisation Act as also the notice dated 18/9/2017 Annexure P/1 directing the petitioners to remain present on the specified date for delivery of possession of the secured assets to the bank as also seeking a direction to the bank for redemption of the properties No.1,2, 4 and 5 mentioned in Para J-15 of the petition.

2- The case of the petitioners is that the petitioner No.3 which is a registered private limited company, in course of its business had taken financial assistance from the respondent No.1 bank and the petitioner No.1 and 2 had stood as 2 guarantor. On 19/1/2006 the bank had sanctioned various credit facilities of 23.25 crores to the petitioner No.3 in respect of which security interest was created. On 19/9/2007 modification in the existing credit facilities was sanctioned by the bank. On 9/10/2007, mortgage credit facilities of 16 crore was created along with credit exposure limit of 4.40 crores and thereafter on 10/4/2008 ISDA (International Swap and Derivative Agreement) was executed and on 12/1/2009 the petitioner No.3 sought working capital loan of 18 crores to make the crash credit limit account regular and thereafter on 9/2/2009 respondent No.1 had sanctioned enhanced credit facilities of 27.39 crores and on 14/5/2009 the security documents were executed for all the credit facilities aggregating to Rs.30.55 crores. Subsequently the account of the petitioner No.3 was declared NPA, therefore, OA No.149/2011 was filed by the respondent bank before the DRT under the Recovery of Debts Due to Financial Institutions Act, 1993 for recovery of a sum of Rs.41,42,47,514/-. In the mean while on 21/5/2011 the notice u/S.13(2) of the Securitisation Act was issued to the petitioners raising a demand of Rs.40,52,30,116/-. The objections filed by the petitioner No.3 u/S.13(3)(A) was rejected and the measures u/S.13(4) read with Rule 8(1) and (2) (possession notice) was initiated. The 3 petitioner had filed SA No.199/2011 u/S.17 of the Act and pending the said appeal, auction notice dated 28/2/2012 was issued. The prayer for stay was rejected by the DRT vide order dated 27/2/2012 and the auction was conducted by the bank on 28/2/2012. In respect of the property No.3 in Para J-15 of the petition being House No.14/2, Block No.658 and 15/2 Block No.657, New Palasia Road one K.K. Goyal was the successful bidder and the other four properties namely properties at Sl.No.1,2,4 and 5 in Para J-15 were auctioned for Rs.9.56 crores (approximately). The details of the properties as given in Para J-15 are as under:-

No. Property Details Owned by Reserve Price Auction Price 1 Flat No.401-A and 0.60 crores 0.65 crores 401-B Appolo Trade Centre Indore 2 Flat No.403 & 404 .00 crores 1.01 crores Appolo Trade Centre 2-B, Rajgarh Kothi, Manorama Ganj, Indore 3 House property on Meenakshi 5.25 crores 5.31 crores plot No.14/2, Block Somani No.658 and 15/2, Block No.657 (total 8000 Sq.Ft) New Palasia, Indore 4 Plot No.16/2, Block Narottam 2.30 crores 2.60 crores No.656, New Palasia, somani Indore HUF 5 Property at Vijaydurg 2.34 Crores 2.35 crores and Sindhudurg Maharashtra TOTAL 10.49 crores 11.92 crores 3- The petitioner had filed appeal before the DRAT in which 4 initially the DRAT had passed the order dated 28/2/2012 that the tenders may be opened, but the auction may not be finalised without the leave of the appellate tribunal, but on 27/9/2012 the DRAT had directed the DRT to consider the preliminary objection of the petitioner regarding applicability of the SARFAESI Act. The WP No.17203/2012 and 17372/2012 filed against the order of DRAT by the bank and successful bidder were dismissed by the Principal seat by order dated 11/10/2012. The DRT vide order dated 9/4/2013 had rejected the preliminary objection of the bank, but the said order of the DRT was set aside by DRAT vide order dated 30th July, 2013 while allowing the appeal of the petitioner holding that the provisions of SARFAESI Act do not apply. Meanwhile on 11/6/2013 the Additional District Magistrate had passed the impugned order u/S.14 of the Securitisation Act. WP No.11720/2013 was filed by the bank and WP No.9918/2013 was filed by the successful bidder against the order of the DRAT dated 30th July, 2013 and pending the said writ petitions on 25/10/2014 OTS was arrived at between the borrower and the bank for a sum of Rs.15 crores, therefore, on 5/1/2015 the respondent No.1 bank had filed an application before the ADM, Indore for withdrawing and closing the proceedings u/S.14 of the Act. On 27/1/2015 the WP No.9918/2013 and 5 11720/2013 were allowed by the division bench and the order of the DRAT dated 30th July, 2013 was set aside. Against this order of the division bench, the bank as well as the other affected parties had preferred SLP(C) No.9478-9479/2015 and SLP(C)9828-9829/2015 and the supreme court by order dated 22/8/2017 had dismissed the SLP with certain directions.

Thereafter the impugned notice dated 18/9/2017 was issued by Tehsildar for taking the possession of the secured assets, hence the present writ petition has been filed. 4- The first argument advanced by the learned counsel for petitioner is that the respondent bank had filed the application dated 5/1/2015 before the District Magistrate for closing the proceedings u/S.14 of the Securitisation Act as the bank did not want to prosecute it in view of OTS, therefore, on the basis of the impugned order dated 11/6/2013 now the possession notice Annexure P/1 could not have been issued.

5- Having examined the above argument in the light of the material available on record, it is found that before filing of the application dated 5/1/2015 by the bank for closing the proceedings u/S.14, the Additional District Magistrate had already acted upon the application of the respondent bank and had passed the order dated 11/6/2013 Annexure P/2 u/S.14 of the Securitisation Act., therefore, such a subsequent application 6 by the respondent bank for closing the proceedings was of no consequence. That apart, the Additional District Magistrate had not passed any order on the said application of the bank for closing the proceedings, therefore, the order of the Additional District Magistrate dated 11/6/2013 had remained in force and the impugned possession notice dated 18/9/2017 has rightly been issued in pursuant thereto. Even otherwise it is also noticed that the application dated 5/1/2015 was filed by the bank in view of OTS arrived at with the petitioners, but the division bench of this court vide order dated 27/1/2015 in WP No.9918/2013 and WP No.11720/2013 has held that the OTS was against the norms and guidelines framed by the bank and the Hon.Supreme Court also vide order dated 22nd August, 2017 while dismissing SLP No.9478-9479/2015 has held that the settlement arrived at between the bank and the borrower would stand nullified, hence the very basis of filing the application dated 5/1/2015 by the bank does not survive. Hence, the above argument of the counsel for the petitioner is found to be devoid of any merit.

6- The learned counsel for petitioner has also raised challenge to the order of the Additional District Magistrate dated 11/6/2013 passed u/S.14 of the Securitisation Act on merit, but undisputedly in pursuance to the said order, the 7 possession of the properties has already been taken from the petitioner on 20th September, 2017, therefore, in view of the judgment of the supreme court in the matter of Standard Chartered Bank Vs. V.Noble Kumar and others (2013) 9 SCC 620, the petitioner has remedy of approaching the DRT against the said order u/S.17 of the Securitisation Act. Hence, in view of the availability of alternative efficacious remedy of appeal, the petitioner is not entitled to directly approach this court challenging the order of the Additional District Magistrate. 7- At this stage it would not be out of place to mention the observations of the division bench in the order dated 27/1/2015 in WP No.9918/2013 (supra) which is as under:-

"37- It is further submitted that on 03.08.2012, respondent No.5/Bank filed an application under Section 14 of the Securitization Act before the Additional District Magistrate for taking possession of the property and the same was allowed by the Additional District Magistrate, Indore vide order dated 11.06.2013 (Annexure P/17) in Case No.04/A- 74/2012-13 by exercising the powers conferred under Section 14 of the Securitization Act and the possession of the property was ordered to be delivered. This order was never challenged by respondents No.1 to 4 before any competent court of law and thus, the aforesaid order has attained finality."

8- Learned counsel for petitioner has submitted that inspite of the interim order of this court, the respondents have taken possession of the assets. The record reflects that on 20th 8 September, 2017 while issuing notice in the present writ petition, the respondents were restrained from taking coercive measure against the petitioner in pursuance to the impugned order. The reply filed by the respondents reveals that the writ petition was filed on 20th September, 2017 when the respondent No.2 had taken possession of the subject properties in the presence of the petitioner No.1, but this fact was not disclosed to the court. The reply further reveals that the interim order passed by this court was conveyed to the respondent No.1 bank by the petitioner No.1 through e-mail at 5.55 pm on 20th September, 2017, but before that the possession of the subject properties was already taken by the Tehsildar and handed over to the respective auction purchaser. It has been disclosed that the physical possession of the last property (property No.2) was handed over by the respondent bank to the auction purchaser at 4.50 pm on 20th September, 2017. No authentic material has been shown by the counsel for the petitioner to contradict the same, hence it cannot be held that the respondents have deliberately acted in contravention of the interim order of this court while obtaining possession of the subject properties in pursuance to the impugned order Annexure P.1 and P.2.

9- Counsel for petitioner has also referred to Annexure R/9 9 to the amended reply which is the settlement of account and has also raised an issue that the amount of Rs.9.57 crores which is the auction money was created on 28/9/2017, therefore, the plea that the sale was confirmed on 20th September, 2017 is wrong and ante dated, but such a plea is also found to be devoid of any merit because learned counsel for respondent bank has rightly pointed out that after confirmation of sale it took some time to credit the amount.

10- The next issue raised by the counsel for petitioner is that the sale deed has not been registered till now in favour of the auction purchaser, therefore, in terms of the amended Section 13(8) of the Securitisation Act and the judgment of the supreme court in the matter of Mathew Varghese Vs. M.Amritha Kumar and others (2014) 5 SCC 650 the petitioners have right of redemption of mortgage. He further submits that the petitioner is willing to redeem the mortgage in respect of the property No.1,2,4 and 5 contained in Para J-15 of the amended writ petition. He further submits that the DRT had passed the interim order dated 28/2/2012, therefore, sale was not confirmed and on 30th July, 2013 DRAT had quashed the proceedings and in the writ petition the division bench by order dated 27/1/2015 had set aside the order of DRAT against which 10 the SLP was preferred by the petitioner and the bank in which on 6/4/2015 interim order was passed by the Hon.Supreme Court and the SLP has been dismissed on 22nd August, 2017, hence the sale has been confirmed in 2017, therefore, confirmation of sale in 2017 in pursuance to the auction in 2012 would not fetch the maximum price.

11- So far as the petitioners plea in respect of the right to redeem the mortgage u/S.13(8) of the Securitisation Act is concerned, Sec.13(8) has been amended with effect from 1/9/2016 and the comparative chart of the amended and un- amended provision as disclosed in the rejoinder is as under:-

Before amendment Post amendment (w.e.f 01.09.2016) Section 13(8)- If the "dues" of Section 13(8)- where the amount of the secured creditor together dues of the secured creditor together with all costs, charges and with all costs, charges and expenses expenses incurred by him are incurred by him is tendered to the tendered to the secured creditor secured creditor at any time before at any time before the date the date of publication of notice for fixed for sale or transfer, the public auction or inviting quotations or secured asset shall not be sold tender from public or private treaty for or transferred by the secured transfer by way of lease, assignment or creditor, and no further step sale of the secured assets.- shall be taken by him for 1] The secured assets shall not be transfer or sale of that secured transferred by way of lease assignment asset or sale by the secured creditor; and 2] In case, any step has been taken by the secured creditor for transfer by way of lease or assignment or sale of the assets before tendering of such amount under this sub-section no further steps shall be taken by such secured creditor for transfer by way of lease or assignment or sale of such secured assets.
12- The petitioner is seeking redemption of mortgage by 11 offering a sum of Rs.6.61 crores vide cheque Annexure P/21 whereas Sec.13(8), pre and post amendment provides for tendering to the secured creditor all the dues together with all costs, charges and expenses incurred by him. The dues of the secured creditor is much more than that. As per the counsel for respondents the dues as on today are of more than 65 crores.
That apart, dues were required to be tendered by the petitioner under the un-amended provision at any time before the dates fixed for sale or transfer. The auction sale took place on 28/2/2012 and the auction has been confirmed, but before that the petitioner had not tendered the dues to the secured creditor.
Similarly under the amended provision the dues were required to be tendered to secured creditor at any time before the date of publication of notice for public auction or inviting quotations or tenders from public or private treaty. The said stage was over in 2012 itself. Hence the petitioner has failed to satisfy the conditions which are prescribed in Sec.13(8) of the Securitisation Act.
13- It is worth noting that the properties No.1,2, 4 and 5 which the petitioner is now seeking to redeem were auctioned along with the property No.3 which was subject matter of issue before the division bench in WP No.9918/2013 and connected writ petitions and the division bench by order dated 27/1/2015 12 has already held that the sale stood confirmed in favour of the auction purchaser. The division bench in the aforesaid writ petition has held as under:-
"57-Section 13(8) of Securitization Act clearly provides that borrower has right to pay the amount 44 WP No.9918/2013 only before the date fixed for sale or transfer and he cannot be permitted to exercise this right after a particular date i.e. the date fixed for sale. After making full payment within the stipulated time under Rule 9 (4), no further confirmation of sale is required. Respondent No.5 - bank received the entire amount before the due date and issued a communication to this effect to the petitioner as per Rule 9 of the Rules. The respondent No.5 - bank accepted the full amount, and therefore, the sale was deemed to have been confirmed, as per the Rules. On accepting the whole sale amount on 14.03.2012, the sale becomes absolute; a sale certificate shall be deemed to have been issued in favour of the petitioner.
58- As per Section 13 (8), if the borrower tenders dues of the secured creditor together with all costs, charges and expenses incurred at any time before the date fixed for sale, then the secured assets shall not be a sale in the auction. In the case in hands, the borrower failed to tender dues before the date fixed for sale, and therefore, the Authorized Officer proceeded with the matter in auctioning the property.
59- In the present case, it is not the case of the bank - secured creditor that he has not confirmed the sale. In spite of pendency of the matter before the DRT 45 WP No.9918/2013 and DRAT, the bank vide letter No.2875 dated 29.02.2012, directed the petitioner - auction purchaser to deposit the whole amount and after receiving the whole amount, a sale letter / certificate will be issued, as per the terms of the tender notice. The petitioner, on coming to know and after depositing 25% of the amount, requested the bank not to accept the whole amount, as the stay has been granted by the 13 DRAT, the bank forced the petitioner to deposit the amount, and therefore, the petitioner deposited the whole amount by 14.03.2012. Thus, it is not right on the part of the bank to raise an objection and say that the sale has not been confirmed by the secured creditor.
60- As per the terms and conditions of the auction, sale in favour of the highest bidder shall be confirmed on payment of the entire bid amount of rupees 5.31 crores. Therefore, it is clear that the moment, the total bid amount is paid by the auction purchaser, the sale becomes absolute and the title vests in the purchaser. Therefore, the contention that since balance amount of purchase price has been paid on or before the 15th day by the auction purchaser, it is deemed that the property vests in the petitioner / auction purchaser, has merit.
61- In the case of Sagar Maha vidyalaya, Sagar 46 WP No.9918/2013 v. Pandit Sadashiv Rao Harshe (supra) it was held that "It may be noted that once an order was made under Order XXI Rule 92 confirming the sale, the title of the auction purchaser related back to the date of sale as provided under Section 65 C.P.C. The title in the property thereafter vests in the auction purchaser and not in the judgment debtor. The issue of sale certificate under Order XXI Rule 94 C.P.C. in favour of the auction purchaser though mandatory but the granting of certificate is a ministerial act and not judicial."

62- In the present case, the borrower never approached the secured creditor or Authorized Officer before the date fixed for sale or after the sale or before the sale becomes absolute, therefore, the decision cited by the bank in the case of India Finlease Securities Limited v. Indian Overseas Bank (supra) would not be applicable to the facts of the present case." 14- The aforesaid reasoning of the division bench applies in full force in the present case. The Hon.Supreme Court vide order dated 22nd August, 2017 has dismissed SLP(C) No.9478- 14 9479/2015 and connected SLPs against the aforesaid order of the division bench finding no merit therein. 15- So far as the judgment of the supreme court in the case of Mathew Varghese (supra) is concerned, in that case also the Hon.Supreme Court has held as under:-

"39- When we apply the above principles stated with reference to Section 60 of the TP Act in respect of a secured interest in a secured asset in favour of the secured creditor under the provisions of the SARFAESI Act and the relevant Rules applicable, under Section 13(1), a free hand is given to a secured creditor to resort to a sale without the intervention of the court or tribunal. However, under Section 13(8), it is clearly stipulated that the mortgagor ie. the borrower, who is otherwise called as a debtor, retains his full right to redeem the property by tendering all the dues to the secured creditor at any time before the date fixed for sale or transfer. Under sub-section (8) of Section 13, as noted earlier, the secured asset should not be sold or transferred by the secured creditor when such tender is made by the borrower at the last moment before the sale or transfer. The said sub section also states that no further step should be taken by the secured creditor for transfer or sale of that secured asset. We find no reason to state that the principles laid down with reference to Section 60 of the TP Act, which is general in nature in respect of all mortgages, can have no application in respect of a secured interest in a secured asset created in favour of a secured creditor, as all the above stated principles apply on all fours in respect of a transaction as between the debtor and secured creditor under the provisions of the SARFAESI Act."

16- Hence, the petitioner does not derive any benefit from that judgment.

17- So far as the series of development which have taken place after the auction held on 28/1/2012 as pointed out by 15 counsel for petitioners is concerned, in view of the detailed examination of the issue and conclusion arrived at by the division bench in the order quoted above, the issue raised by the petitioner in this regard is concluded by the said judgment. 18- Having regard to the aforesaid, I do not find any reason to interfere in the impugned order nor any merit is found in the plea raised by the petitioners in respect of the redemption of mortgage u/S.13(8) of the Act. Hence, the writ petition is dismissed.
