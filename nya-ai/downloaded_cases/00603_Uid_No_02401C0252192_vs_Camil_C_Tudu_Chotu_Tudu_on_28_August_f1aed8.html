9) In order to succeed in a petition for eviction filed under section 14(1)(e) of the Delhi Rent Control Act the petitioner must establish that:

E. No. 36/2012 Pradeep Kumar Nayyar Vs Camil C. Tudu @ Chotu Tudu Page no. 6 of 14 i.He is the owner and landlord in respect of the tenanted premises. ii.That he requires the premises bonafide for himself or for any member of his family dependent upon him.
iii.That he has no other reasonably suitable accommodation
10) In the present case, at the very outset it is pertinent to observe that the contention of the petitioner that the application for leave to defend has not been filed in correct form is not tenable as it is settled law that a litigant having no knowledge of law may file an application in normal course supported by an affidavit so as to place the same on record within the given time. In such cases there is no bar in treating such an application as an application u/s 25B of the DRC Act. Further, the contention that the application has not been filed in time is also not tenable as the court cannot impute notice given in a separate petition between the same parties as service of notice in this petition also. For the said reasons, the court now proceeds to deliberate upon the defences sought to be raised in the application.

DEFENCES

11) Petitioner is not the owner of the premises; it was sold by the petitioner to the respondent:

11.1 In rent control legislation, the landlord can be said to be owner, if he is entitled in his own legal right, as distinguished from for and on behalf of someone else, to evict the tenant and then to retain, control, hold and use the premises for himself.
11.2 In M.M.Quasim Vs Manohar Lal Sharma: (1981) 3 SCC 36 it was observed by the Apex Court that an "owner-landlord" can seek eviction on the ground of his personal requirement is one who has a right against the whole world to occupy the building in his own right and exclude anyone holding a title lesser than his own.
11.3 It was observed in Shanti Sharma Vs Smt Ved Prabha: AIR 1987 SC 2028 that the term "owner" has to be understood in the context of the background of the law and what is contemplated in the scheme of the Act. The Act has been enacted for protection of the tenants. But, at the same time, it has provided that the landlord under certain circumstances will be entitled to E. No. 36/2012 Pradeep Kumar Nayyar Vs Camil C. Tudu @ Chotu Tudu Page no. 7 of 14 eviction and bona fide requirement is one of such grounds. Ordinarily, the concept of the ownership may be absolute ownership in the land as well as of the structure standing thereon. But in the modern context, where all lands belong to the State, the persons who hold properties will only be lessees or the persons holding the land on some term from the Government or the authorities constituted by the State. The legislature, when it used the term "owner" in s.

14(1)(e), did not think of ownership as absolute ownership. The meaning of the term "owner" is vis-a-vis the tenant i.e. the owner should be something more than the tenant. In cases where the plot of land is taken on lease, the structure is built by the landlord and he is the owner of the structure. 11.4 In the present case the defence raised by the respondent is that after transaction of Rs. 5,50,000/-, the petitioner had assured that he will execute necessary documents in favour of the respondent but never did so. That the respondent served the petitioner a legal notice dated 29.01.2011 calling upon the petitioner to execute the necessary documents for sale in favour of the respondent.

11.5 It is an admitted case of the respondent himself that the possession of the premises in question was handed over to him by the petitioner. In fact, it is the very case of the respondent that he purchased the premises in question from the petitioner who did not execute documents in favour of the respondent. 11.6 Firstly, there is nothing on record to show any such sale transaction or that the respondent took any steps in any court of law to get the petitioner to execute such documents. Secondly, the respondent has himself sought to bring the copies of petitioner's title documents for the premises in question on record which he claims were given to him at the time of the alleged transaction of sale. Thus, even if the respondent's version is assumed to be true then as no documents were executed in favour of the respondent by the petitioner, the respondent cannot challenge the ownership of the petitioner. 11.7 Thus, this defence raised by the respondent is a vague and sham defence and it does not raise any triable issue.

12) No relationship of landlord - tenant between the parties; no document/rent agreement, rent receipt filed by the petitioner:

12.1 It has been stated by the respondent that as the petitioner had sold the premises in question to him, there is no land lord tenant relationship between the parties. Although, a substantial portion of the said defence has been dealt with above it is important to mention here that it is not necessary that a writ- ten agreement has to be in existence for creation of tenancy under the DRC Act. Although, the petitioner has not placed on record any rent receipt, it is an admitted fact that the possession of the premises was handed over by the peti- tioner to the respondent. The petitioner has also alleged that Rs.10,000/- was paid by the respondent to him for rent, against which no specific denial has come forth from the respondent's side.

12.2 In this regard, reliance is also placed on the case of Sh. Ram Chander Vs. Smt. Ram Pyari: 2004 (72) DRJ 545 wherein it was held that:

"3. Learned counsel for the petitioner contends vehemently that once relationship of landlord and tenant is challenged, tenant is entitled to leave to contest the eviction petition. There is no dispute as to this proposition of law as it is the owner of the premises who has right to seek eviction of the tenant and none less on the ground of bonfire requirement of the premises. In this case petitioner claims himself to be the owner of the premises and not as a tenant. The authority of the Supreme Court relied upon by the learned counsel for the petitioner in Manoj Kumar Vs. Bihari Lal (Dead) by LRs 91(2001) Delhi Law Times 25 (SC) is not at all applicable in the given facts and circumstances of the case as any person who denies to be a tenant and claim to be the owner of the premises cannot claim to be granted leave to defend eviction petition as eviction proceedings initiated under the Delhi Rent Control Act cannot be converted into a title suit. The title of the party is always decided though civil proceedings. As such claim of the petitioner that he is the owner of the premises and not the tenant could have been vindicated by way of filing civil suit and not in the eviction proceedings.
4. The first and foremost requirement to become entitled to leave to defend is that there should exist relationship of landlord and tenant E. No. 36/2012 Pradeep Kumar Nayyar Vs Camil C. Tudu @ Chotu Tudu Page no. 9 of 14 between the parties. By denying the ownership or land lordship of the petitioner and claiming himself as owner, the petitioner cannot be granted leave to defend the eviction petition."

12.3 Moreover, it has been observed in judgment titled as Rajender Kumar Sharma & Ors. Vs. Leela Wati & Ors.: 155 (2008) DLT 383 by Hon'ble Del- hi High Court as under:

"11)....Thus, the affidavit filed by the tenant was shown to be false by the landlady on the basis of documents placed by it. No Rent Con-

troller is supposed to grant leave to the tenant on the basis of a false affidavit and false averments and assertions. Such affidavit should be outrightly rejected by the Rent Controller. Only those averments in the affidavit are to be considered by the Rent Controller which have same substance in it and are supported by some material. Mere as- sertions made by a tenant in respect of landlord's ownership of other buildings and in respect of alternate accommodation are not to be considered sufficient for grant of leave to defend." 12.4 In his application, the respondent has merely stated that the respondent is the owner of the property even though admitting that no documents were ever executed in his favour. It was for the respondent to show that he was not a tenant in the property which he has miserably failed to do. 12.5 Accordingly, this defence raised by the respondent is a sham defence and it does not raise any triable issue

13) The description of the premises in question is not correct:

13.1 In leave to defend, respondent has taken objection that petitioner has not given correct description of the premises in question, but, he has not filed any contrary site plan showing the proper details of premises in question, thus, the plea of the respondent as regards description of premises is incorrect, is vague and baseless. It may be noted that the respondents have also not mentioned as to how the site plan is incorrect.

13.2 In this regard, reliance can be placed on judgment titled as V. S. Sachdeva Vs. M. L. Grover: 67 (1997) DLT 737, wherein Hon'ble Delhi High E. No. 36/2012 Pradeep Kumar Nayyar Vs Camil C. Tudu @ Chotu Tudu Page no. 10 of 14 Court has observed as under:

"if, the tenant failed to file any site plan, then the site plan filed by the landlord should be accepted"

In the absence of any contrary site plan of the respondent, plea of the respondent is rejected being bald averment without any substance. Thus, this defence raised by the respondents does not raise any triable issue.

14) Dispute regarding identity of the respondent:

14.1 The respondent contended that the petitioner has concealed the order dated 05.11.2011 passed by another Court. The respondent had appeared before that Court and filed application U/s. 151 CPC to disclose that his real name was Chhotu Tudu and not Camil C.Tudu. Thereafter, the petitioner produced the name of the respondent as Camil C. Tudu @ Chhotu Tudu as an afterthought.

14.2 It is an admitted fact that the respondent received possession of the premises in question from the petitioner. It is also an admitted fact that it was the respondent who was served by way of notice at the premises in question in the present petition. Thus, this dispute regarding the identity of the respondent is not material in such eviction proceedings. Moreover, the petitioner has acted diligently in incorporating the correct name of the respondent in the memo of parties after he came to know of the same.

14.3 It is settled law that not just any dispute raised can be made a ground for granting leave to defend. In Nem Chand Daga Vs. Inder Mohan Singh Rana: 94 (2001) Delhi Law Times 683; it was held:

"That before leave to defend is granted, the respondent must show that some triable issues which disentitled the applicant from getting the order of eviction against the respondent and at the same time entitled the respondent to leave to defend existed. The onus is prima facie on the respondent and if he fails, the eviction follows."

14.4 In the present case, the dispute raised by the respondent is not such that would dis-entitle the petitioner from getting an order of eviction.

15) Discrepancies in the address of the petitioner:

15.1 The respondent has submitted that the petitioner in his affidavits dated 31.05.2011 and 18.10.2011 has referred to his address as 12, Urja Vihar, Sector-51, Noida, U.P. whereas in the petition he has claimed that he has shifted to Gurgaon.

15.2 In this regard the petitioner has come out clean and confessed that the said discrepancy in the address was a clerical error. Even otherwise, the respondent has not stated that the petitioner is having some other accommodation. Thus, it is immaterial whether at the time of filing the petition, the petitioner was staying on rent in Gurgaon or Noida. The dispute sought to be brought out is irrelevant to the ingredients of the Section 14(1)(e) of the DRC Act. In this regard it has been held by the Honorable High Court of Delhi in Inderjeet Singh vs Harish Chandra Bhutani: in R.C.REV. 98/2010 decided on 06.07.2012 that:

7. It is immaterial that who pays the rent for the tenanted shop being used by the petitioner's son. The fact of bonafide requirement and lack of any other accommodation for business purposes has been amply established by the petitioner. His son is in fact carrying out his business from a tenanted accommodation; he has no other alternate suitable accommodation. 15.3 Thus, the dispute sought to be raised is a minor discrepancy which does not raise any triable issue.
16) Second petition on same cause of action:
16.1 The respondent contended that the petitioner filed the same petition E. No. 36/2012 Pradeep Kumar Nayyar Vs Camil C. Tudu @ Chotu Tudu Page no. 12 of 14 besides the present petition before the Hon'ble Court of Sh. Rakesh Kumar, ld.

CONCLUSION

17) The special summary procedure provided for u/s 25B of the DRC Act is an exception to the general intent to the Act. The intention of the legislature is to provide an expeditious remedy to landlords who seek eviction on the ground of bonafide requirement under the stringent conditions imposed in the special procedure.

18) It is well settled that leave to defend is granted to the tenant in case any triable issue is raised by him, which can be adjudicated by consideration of additional evidence. The mere existence of any triable issue is not sufficient. The nature of the triable issue raised by the tenant must be such that it will disentitle the landlord from obtaining the eviction order.

19) In the judgment titled as Ragavendra Kumar v. Firm Prem Machinary: AIR 2000 SUPREME COURT 534 the Hon'ble Supreme Court held that it is settled position of law that the landlord is best judge of his requirement for residential or business purpose and he has got complete freedom in the matter. Moreover, It is held by the Hon'ble High Court of Delhi in a case tiled as Hari Shanker Vs. Madan Mohan Gupta: 111 (2004) DLT 534 that:

"Summary procedure in Section 25-B of Delhi Rent Control Act, 1958 cannot be defeated by merely making frivolous and vague allegations which can never be substantiated."

20) In the present case the respondent has not uttered even a whisper against the E. No. 36/2012 Pradeep Kumar Nayyar Vs Camil C. Tudu @ Chotu Tudu Page no. 13 of 14 requirement of the petitioner or its bonafide. In the absence of anything contrary to this effect, this court has no hesitation in holding that the petitioner's requirement of the premises in question for the purpose of his residence and that of his family is sufficient to order eviction in the present case. This is specially in view of the fact that the petitioner despite having his own house in Delhi as well as working here has to live in a rented accommodation in Gurgaon and incur added expenditure for rent and for commuting. Thus, the requirement of the petitioner is held to be bonafide.

21) In the judgment titled as Sarwan Dass Bange Vs. Ram Prakash: 167 (2010) DLT 80 the Hon'ble High Court of Delhi referring to the judgment of Baldev Singh Bajwa v. Monish Saini: (2005) 12 SCC 778 observed in para 17 as under:

"..It was held that the legislative intent is of expeditious disposal of the application for ejectment of tenant filed on the ground of requirement by the landlord of the premises for his own occupation; a special category of landlords requiring the premises for their own use has been created; if there is any breach by the landlord, the tenant is given a right of restoration of possession; the landlord who evicts a tenant on the ground of own requirement is not only prohibited from letting out the premises or disposing of the same but also required to use the same for his own residence only..."
"...the landlord who evicts a tenant on the ground of own requirement is not only prohibited from letting out the premises or disposing of the same but also required to use the same for his own residence only. It was held that these restrictions and conditions inculcate in built strong presumption that the need of the landlord is genuine; the conditions and restrictions imposed on the landlord make it virtually improbable for the landlord to approach the Court for ejectment of tenant unless his need is bona fide - no unscrupulous landlord in all probability, under this Section, would approach the Court for ejectment of the tenant considering the onerous conditions imposed on him. It was further held that this inbuilt protection in the Act for the tenants implies that whenever the landlord would approach the Court his requirement shall be presumed to be genuine and bona fide. It was further held that a heavy burden lies on the tenant to prove that the requirement is not genuine.." (emphasis supplied) E. No. 36/2012 Pradeep Kumar Nayyar Vs Camil C. Tudu @ Chotu Tudu Page no. 14 of 14
22) The whole purpose and import of summary procedure under Section 25B of the Act would otherwise be defeated. The prayer for leave to contest should be granted to the tenant only where a prima facie case has been disclosed by him. In the absence of the tenant having disclosed a prima facie case i.e. such facts which disentitles the landlord from obtaining an order of eviction, the Court cannot mechanically and in routine manner grant leave to defend. In the light of the aforesaid facts, circumstances and legal propositions, all the pleas taken by the respondent have failed to raise any triable issues regarding the ownership of the petitioner or the land lord-tenant relationship; the bonafide requirement of the landlord; or the availability of any alternative suitable accommodation. The contents of the application for leave to defend have failed to rebut the presumption of bonafide qua the requirement of the petitioner. The application for leave to defend filed by the respondents is thus rejected.
23) As a consequence thereof, an eviction order is passed U/s. 14 (1) (e), DRC Act against the respondents regarding the tenanted premises i.e premises comprising of two rooms attached with toilet and a kitchen on the second floor bearing No. 324, Second Floor, Hastal, DDA Flats (LIG) New Delhi, as shown in red colour in the site plan
24) However in light of Section 14 (7) DRCA, the aforesaid eviction order shall not be executable for a period of six months from today.
25)The parties are left to bear their own costs.
