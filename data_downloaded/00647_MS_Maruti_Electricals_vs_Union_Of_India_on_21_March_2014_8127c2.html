JUDGMENT

AWARD REFERENCE UNDER SECTION 18 OF THE LAND ACQUISITION ACT

1. Vide Notification bearing No F.7(19)/2000/L&B/LA/ MRTS/ PB/7262 dated 27.07.2004 under section 4 of LA Act and a declaration vide notification No F.7(19)/2000/L&B/LA/MRTS/PB/20154 dated 17.02.2005 alongwith section 17 of the L. A. Act also dated 17.02.2005, the property of the petitioner bearing No 8713, Pulbangash, Roshanara Road, out of khasra No. 388 min Delhi Patti, has been acquired by the Government and possession of the same was taken on 26.6.2006. The Land Acquisition Collector, after completing all the formalities as provided under the Act assessed the market value of the land of the petitioner @ Rs. 14,490/- per sq. meter duly holding that the property of the petitioner was a commercial property and the land use of the said property was also found to be commercial as per the joint survey report conducted by the beneficiary.

2. Vide this award, I shall dispose of the instant reference under section 18 of the L. A. Act preferred by the petitioner against the respondents being aggrieved with the quantum of compensation awarded by the LAC in lieu of the acquisition of the land of the petitioner.

3. It was pleaded by the petitioner in reference that petitioner was the owner and in possession of commercial land alongwith LAC-1/2013 Page:-2/28 structure bearing property No. 8713, Roshanara Road, Delhi out of khasra No. 388 Min, Delhi Patti. It was also pleaded that petitioner was in possession of land with structures measuring 427.44 Square meters whereas the present Award has been made only in respect of land measuring 335.8 sq. meters, although the rest of the area i.e. the land acquired vide this award and the remaining area has area has been taken by the respondents. It was also stated that petitioner has not accepted the award as the market value of the land and structures has been assessed by respondent No. 1 at very low rates. Certain grounds were also raised in the reference petition stating that market value of land as assessed by LAC is very much on lower side and does not represent the correct and real market value of land as on date of notification. It was also stated in grounds that land is a free hold land and the Collector has failed to convert the leasehold land rates granted by him into free-hold land. It was also stated that market value of the land, if sold in open market under the conditions of demand and supply, was in any case not less than Rs. 2,50,000/- per sq. meter. It was also pleaded that by taking over possession of land, the entire business of petitioner has ceased to exist as the petitioner has no other alternative place of business and petitioner claimed Rs. 75,00,000/- in Maruti Electricals and Rs. 60,00,000/- as loss of earnings in Moonlight Enterprises. Hence, it was prayed that petitioner be paid compensation for various items at the rates stated above besides statutory solatium, additional amount and interest as per the amended Act.

4. The respondents No. 1 i.e. Union of India filed separate written statements to the instant reference petition wherein the LAC-1/2013 Page:-3/28 respondent No. 1 raised preliminary objection to the extent that DLR Act is applicable to the land in dispute. It was also stated that land in question is not surrounded by any developed or undeveloped colony and can only be used for agricultural and there was no structure, treewell or tubewell on the land in question at the time of publication of notification U/s 4 of the L. A. Act. On merits, the other averments in the reference petition were denied and opposed. It was pleaded in reply that compensation has been legally and correctly assessed by the LAC and same is adequate and just, hence it was prayed that reference petition be dismissed and petitioner is not entitled to compensation for loss of earning severance charges etc.

5. In the written statement filed on behalf of the respondent No. 2 namely the DMRC, it was pleaded by the DMRC that the respondent No. 2 had acquired only 425 Sq. mtr. land and taken over possession. It was averred that contention of petitioner is totally wrong and misconceived that 335.8 Sq. M. land was acquired and rest of the area was taken by the respondent No. 2. It was also stated that as per records, respondent No. 2 acquired only 425. sq. mtr. of land and compensation thereof was granted vide award No. 3/LAC/N/2006-07. It was also pleaded in reply that land was acquired vide award No. 3/LAC/N/2006-07 by LAC on 27.07.2006 and it was challenged by respondent No. 2 before the Hon'ble High Court. It was also stated that petitioner challenged the above award on the ground that the Ld. Acquisition Officer awarded the amount at commercial rate in that award whereas in award No. 05/DC/N/2001-02 dated 08.06.2001 the LAC awarded amount at residential rate for the same khasra number land. The LAC-1/2013 Page:-4/28 other contents of reference petition were denied by DMRC in its written statement.

6. On the pleadings of the parties, the following issues were framed vide order dated 28.7.2008 by my Ld. Predecessor:-

(i) What was the market value of the land at the time issuance of notification under section 4 of the LA Act? Onus on parties.
(ii) Whether the Petitioner is entitled to enhancement in compensation, if so, to what amount?OPP
(iii) Relief.

7. The petitioner in support of his claim produced and examined 9 witnesses in witness box.

8. On the contrary, in evidence of respondent No. 1, Ld. counsel for the LAC tendered the award in question as Ex. R-1 and sale deed dated 24.12.2009, 10.11.2009, 18.11.2009, 29.05.2009 as Ex. R-2 to R-5. It is pertinent to mention that the Ld. Counsel for respondent No 1 had filed the same sale deeds in LAC No. 5/1/11 in the matter of "National Cold Storage and Refrigeration Pvt. Ltd. Vs UOI and Ors". The respondent No. 2 namely the DMRC, in spite of denying the market value awarded by the LAC in the present award, relied upon the award bearing Ex. No R-1 and other evidence filed by the respondent No. 1. The DMRC failed to lead any independent evidence in rebuttal in order to prove that the land use of the acquired property was residential and not commercial. Rather, by relying on the present award, the DMRC has accepted the land use of the acquired property to be commercial.

9. That the present petition was adjourned sine die by my Ld. Predecessor vide order dated 06.01.2009 and was later on LAC-1/2013 Page:-5/28 restored after the disposal of the writ petition vide order dated 21.10.2013.

10.That the Hon'ble High Court of Delhi, while disposing of the writ petition bearing No. 3097/2007 had stated that it would be open to this court to determine the land use of the acquired property and if the land use of the acquired property was found to be residential, then the amount of compensation could be reduced by this court accordingly.

11.Issue No. (i) What was the market value of the land at the time issuance of notification under section 4 of the LA Act? Onus on parties and Issue No. (ii) Whether the Petitioner is entitled to enhancement in compensation, if so, to what amount?OPP:- All these issues are taken up together as they are interconnected. The petitioner having been aggrieved by the compensation awarded by the LAC sought enhancement through the reference made and have put forth a number of grounds on which it claims to be entitled to the enhanced compensation. It has been argued that even slight disturbance or variation in routine unsettles a person and that too depends upon the degree of impact and vulnerability of the individual, the actual impact, thus, may even be devastating. In these circumstances, unsettling someone alongwith the entire family with habitat and hearth lock, stock and barrel can very well be imagined especially when establishment is stable and running for quite sometime. The proximity not only breeds contempt but also develops into fascination, bondings and different types of relations between the individual, and at times encompasses in itself, the relationship between the humans animals and even the stones and structures.

LAC-1/2013 Page:-6/28 One may, more often than not, feel the pain of leaving particular abode, locality, city or country.

12.Against this backdrop, un-settling someone from his abode is, without doubt, disturbing if not devastating but then it has to be coped up depending upon the circumstance especially when it is supported by the legislation (may be through a more than hundred years old 'colonial' legislation, not much amended, retaining its basic features) and when there is no escape or no way out and has limited rather no option to exercise but to toe the line or so to say that no option whatsoever except the one thrusted upon by the circumstances.

13.The requirement for the extension of Pulbangash Metro Station was the need of the hour and the creation of the same armed and empowered with the Will of Government to remove all possible obstacles with all the might of the state in order to have smooth passage/path/road in place at the earliest in the City of Delhi resulted into acquisition of land even in thickly populated urban landscape under the Land Acquisition Act, 1894 and persons whose land/ commercial properties/houses were the subject matter of this notification under section 4 of Land Acquisition Act for this purpose were not even given an option to file any objections as contemplated under Section 17-A of Land Acquisition Act thereby leaving them practically with no option but to concede to the Directives of Government. In such scenario, those who were displaced could have only consoled themselves that they got suitable compensation, which may compensate them to the extent possible though at times there cannot be a compensation for such situations. To fulfill the same, the Govt. acquired the land at LAC-1/2013 Page:-7/28 Roshanara Road/ Pulbangash, Rani Jhansi Road for the purpose of extension of Pulbangash Metro Station and in the process, some individuals were forced to surrender their houses/shops/ establishments/factory and land which of course was not to their liking or pleasure.

14.It was compulsory acquisition so much so that land was required on urgent basis and therefore the formalities with regard to the provisions of Land Acquisition Act which under normal circumstances, are routinely followed were dispensed with resorting to Section 17 of L. A. Act. After processing the matter, the compensation was fixed by the Land Acquisition Collector at the rate of Rs.14,490/- per sq. mtr. which was not acceptable to the claimants and therefore the reference was made under section 18 of Land Acquisition Act whereby the claimants have sought enhancement in the amount of compensation based upon various facts that it was an area located in heart of city, located very close to the prestigious Delhi University, Interstate Bus Terminal, Railway Station, Popular and big markets, on the legendary Grant Truck Road. Since the land was in possession of its owner for long and they had raised commercial structure over the same according to their own requirement, choice and taste and in the process invested handsome amount of money, were deprived of not only of the means of livelihood but they were also deprived off with the benefits which were available to the claimants on account of its situational advantages and the potential of property from commercial angle specially when metro is going near the property in question.

15.It is further argued that all the amenities of good life were LAC-1/2013 Page:-8/28 available to the claimant at a stones throw and that too of a very high standard and better quality. To elaborate, the claimants have asserted that the land/houses surrounded by popular residential and commercial areas like Kamla Nagar, Roop Nagar, Gulabi Bagh, Shakti Nagar, Malka Ganj, Barf Khana and reputed Delhi University in its toe besides having important commercial civic, educational hospitality and medical establishment such as Oberoi Maiden's Hotel, Tirath Ram Shah Hospital, St Stephen Hospital, District Courts and Railway Claims Tribunals etc., surrounded by lush green area located adjacent to the Northern Ridge which was become so important in the city life as it is a sort of big source of fresh air oxygen which is scarce if not exclusive and missing in his metropolis choking with various kinds of pollutions. The importance of such a place can very well be imagined. Whereas while awarding the compensation, it is asserted that the Land Acquisition Collector has not taken into account all these factors and has awarded the compensation which is not suitable to the kind of properties which have been acquired by the Govt. It has been argued by petitioner that he has been able to prove that the acquired property of the petitioner was a commercial property and the market value of the said property was not less than 2.5 lakhs per sq meter at the time of notification under section 4 of the L. A. Act.

16.The both parties have relied upon the judgment passed in LAC No. 05/1/11 in case titled "National Cold Storage and Refrigeration Pvt. Ltd. Vs UOI & Ors" and also the judgment titled "Methodist Churches in India Vs UOI & Anr. bearing LAC No. 3/1/11" in order to prove the actual market value of the LAC-1/2013 Page:-9/28 properties in the vicinity qua the transactions which took place in or around the same time when the notification under section 4 was published in order to drive home their case that the value of the acquired property of the petitioner should be the same rather much more than the properties No. 12, 14, ,16 and 18, Boulevard Road duly acquired vide awards No. 09 & 10/LAC/N/10-11 wherein the petitioner therein demanded that the compensation may be given @ Rs.2,38,474/- per sq. mtr. alongwith solatium, additional amount coupled with the interest etc. so far as the property at Boulevard Road, village Civil Lines belonging to petitioner is concerned. The petitioner stated that the value of the acquired property was much more than properties No. 12, 14, 16 and 18, Boulevard Road since his property was touching the Pulbangash Metro Station and was a free hold property.

17. In this regard, it will be appropriate to refer the broad parameters laid down by the Hon'ble Supreme Court of India. In case of "Chaman Lal Hargovind Dass Vs Special Land Acquisition Officer (1988) 2 SCC 751" wherein it was observed that the case of enhancement of compensation on account of acquisition of land are required to be considered on the following parameters:-

"4. The following factors must be etched on the mental screen:
(1) A reference under section 18 of the Land Acquisition Act is not an appeal against the award; and the court cannot take into account the material relied upon by the Land Acquisition Officer in his award unless the same material is produced and proved before the court.
     (2)     So also the award of the Land Acquisition Officer is not to be

LAC-1/2013                                                 Page:-10/28
treated as judgment of the trial court open or exposed to challenge before the court hearing the reference. It is merely an offer made by the Land Acquisition Officer and the material utilized by him for making his valuation cannot be utilised by the court unless produced and proved before it. It is not function of the court to sit in appeal against the award, approve or disprove its reasoning, or correct its error or affirm, modify or reverse the conclusion reached by the Land Acquisition Officer, as if it were an appellant court.
(3) The court has to treat the reference as an original proceedings before it and determine the market value afresh on the basis of the material produced before it.
(4) The claimant is in the position of a plaintiff who has to show that the price offered for his land in the award is inadequate on the basis of the material produced in the court. Of course, the material placed an proved by the other side can also betaken into account for this purpose.
(5) The market value of land under acquisition has to be determined as on the crucial date of publication of notification under section 4 of the Land Acquisition Act (dates of notifications under section 6 & 9 are irrelevant).
(6) The determination has to be made standing on the date line of valuation (date of publication of notification under Section4) as if the valuer is a hypothetical purchaser willing to purchase land from the open market and is prepared to pay a reasonable price as on that day. It has also to be assumed that the vendor is willing to sell the land at a reasonable price.
     (7)     In doing so by the instances method, the court has to

LAC-1/2013                                                Page:-11/28
correlate the market value reflected in the most comparable instance which provides the index of market value. (8) Only genuine instances have to be taken into account.

(Sometimes instances are rigged up in anticipation of acquisition of land).

(9) Even post-notification instances can be taken into account (1) if they are very proximate, (2) genuine and (3) the acquisition itself has not motivate the purchaser to pay a higher price on account of the resultant improvement in development prospects. (10) The most comparable instances out of the genuine instances have to be identified on the following considerations :

(i) proximity from time angle.

(ii) proximity from situation angle.

(11) Having identified the instances which provide the index of market value the price reflected therein may betaken as the norm and the market value of the land under acquisition may be deduced by making suitable adjustments for the plus and minus factors vis-a-vis land under acquisition by placing the two in juxtaposition.

(12) A balance-sheet of plus and minus factors may be drawn for this purpose and the relevant factors any beevaluated in terms of price variation as a prudent purchaser should do. (13) The market value of the land under acquisition has thereafter to be deduced by loading the price reflected in the instance taken as norm for plus factors and unloading it for minus factors.

(14) The exercise indicated in clause (11) to (13) has to be undertaken in a common sense manner as a prudent man of the LAC-1/2013 Page:-12/28 world of business would do. We may illustrate some such illustrative (not exhaustive) factors : 

             Plus factors                  Minus factors
     1. Smallness of size            1. Largeness of areas
     2. proximity to a road          2. situation in the interior at
                                        a Distance from the road.
     3. frontage on a road           3. narrow strip of land with
                                        Very small frontage
                                        Compared to depth

4. nearness to developed area 4. lower level requiring the depressed portion to be filled up

5. regular shape 5. remoteness from Developed Locality

6. level vis-a-vis land under 6. some special disadvantageous acquisition factor which would deter it purchaser

7. Special value for an owner of an adjoining property to whom it may have some very special advantage.

(15) The evaluation of these factors of course depends on the facts of each case. There cannot be any hard and fast or rigid rule. Common sense is the best and most reliable guide. For instance, take the factor regarding the size. A building plot of land say 500 to 1000 sq. yds. cannot be compared with a large tract or block of land of say 10000 sq. yds. or more. Firstly while a smaller LAC-1/2013 Page:-13/28 plot is within the reach of many, a large block of land will have to be developed by preparing a lay out, carving out roads, leavings open space, plotting out smaller plots, waiting for purchasers (meanwhile the invested money will be blocked up) and the hazards of an entrepreneur. The factor can be discounted b make a deduction by way of an allowance at an appropriate rate ranging approximately between 20 per cent to 50 per cent to account for land required to be set apart for carving out lands and plotting out small lots. The discounting will to some extent also depend on whether it is a rural area or when urban area, whether building activity is picking up and whether waiting period during which the capital of the entrepreneur would be locked up, will be longer or shorter and the attendant hazards.

(16) Every case must be dealt with on its own fact pattern bearing in mind all these factors as a prudent purchaser of land in which position the judge must place himself.

(17) These are general guidelines to be applied with understanding informed with common sense.

18. In the present case, it is not disputed that the acquisition was of compulsory in nature as enlisted in section 17 of the Land Acquisition Act, 1894 and the petitioner were not given an opportunity to file objections under section 5-A of the Land Acquisition Act which, in the present reference was the commercial property of the petitioner who was using the said property for commercial activities in order to earn his livelihood. All the civic amenities were available to the land in question and the acquired property was completely abutting to the Pulbangash Metro Station LAC-1/2013 Page:-14/28 and the shops leased out therein. The petitioner also filed evidence to prove that property No. 18, Boulevard Road was in the near vicinity of the acquired property and had also relied upon the entire evidence led in case bearing LAC No. 5/1/11 in case titled "National Cold Storage and Refrigeration Pvt. Ltd. Vs UOI & Anr". The petitioner further relied upon an auction having taken place in the area of Gulabi Bagh which was in the near vicinity of the acquired property. The petitioner contended that the said property though being a leasehold property was commercial property which was auctioned by the DDA in the year 1995 for a amount of Rs.81,10,000/-.

19.Against the back drop of the present scenario, the acquisition for the purpose of extension of Pulbangash Metro Station which undoubtedly is good for larger part of the public, by the Govt cannot be faulted or questioned but in these circumstances and even otherwise, the petitioners were entitled to suitable compensation which according to the Govt. has already been given to them, but being not satisfied, the present references under section 18 of the L. A. Act came into being. In the instant matter where the acquisition was of compulsory nature, one did not have even the opportunity to question it but to resign to one's fate and accept the monetary compensation. Thus, the compensation (consolation) in the form of money should be potent enough atleast to console if not compensate.

28.That the respondents, in their evidence, relied upon the award of the collector itself i.e. R-1 and filed certain sale deeds which were filed by the UOI in case bearing LAC No. 5/1/2011 as Ex. R-22 to R-5. R-1 to R-5 which included the award passed by the LAC itself. R-2 to R-5 are post notification sales and that too 4-5 years subsequent to the notification and are therefore of no consequence in the present case. R-2 to R-5 were mainly residential in nature and the acquired property was a free hold property purchased in open auction from the Govt of India as a commercial property. The properties which was a subject matter of R-2 and R-5 and the acquired property are in no manner similar in location, potentiality, land use and most importantly proximately and time. Hence the same are rejected.

29.The petitioner had tried to prove the market value by way of three types of evidences (i) the license agreements executed by the DMRC itself in the year 2004 situated at Pulbangash metro Station which was admittedly at zero distance wherein the market value of the acquired as on the date of notification under section comes to Rs.3,22,000/- per sq. meter after applying the method of 20 years capitalization as is usual practice for the purpose of charging of rent or license fee etc. The petitioner in support of the said method, has filed a copy of the award passed by the LAC District South West, pertaining to village Nangal Raya wherein the said method has been relied upon. If the said method is applied in the present case, the market value for the acquired property having an FAR of 350 would come to Rs.3,22,000/- per sq. meter on the basis of the license amount charged which varies between 1100/- to 1200/- per sq meter per quarter which comes to an average of 1150/- per sq. meter per quarter or Rs.4600/- p.a. Per sq. meter for rental or Rs.92,000/- per sq. meter for an area of 100 FAR and Rs. 3,22,000/- per sq. meter for an area of permitted FAR of 350 as per document Ex. PW-4/4 for 20 years capitalization. The petitioner has further stated in his arguments that the market value of the acquired property was much more than the above noted market LAC-1/2013 Page:-21/28 value since the license agreement have a clause that quarterly maintenance charges of Rs.50/- per sq. meter would also be charged and the amount of license fee would be increased by 20% after the completion of every three years. The petitioner further stated that an additional amount of 12% should be added on the above noted market value since the acquired property was a free hold property and there were no encumbrances of any sort on the acquired property. The above contentions raised by the petitioner is not acceptable since the RTI replies Ex. PW-4/1 to PW-4/3 as well as the present award admit that the acquired property as well as the licensed properties were similar in location, potentiality and market value. (ii) The petitioner has proved an auction in the area of Gulabi Bagh which held in the year 1995. The area of the auctioned property was 162 sq. meters and the said property was auctioned for a sum of Rs.81,10,000/- meaning thereby Rs. 50,061/- per sq. meter in the year 1995. That the petitioner has proved that the distance of the acquired property and the auctioned property is less than one Km. The said examination has not been rebutted and the respondent have not been able to show any discrepancy in the evidence led by the petitioner through their cross examination. The petitioner has contended that in case an appreciation of 12% p.a. compounded annually is awarded to the consideration of Rs.81,10,000/- for a period of 9 yeas approx. then the market value as on the date of notification under section 4 would arrived at Rs.1,40,000/- per sq. meter approx. The petitioner pointed out that the above noted property was an encumbered property since the said property was lease hold and not free hold. Therefore the market value of the acquired would LAC-1/2013 Page:-22/28 be much higher than the market value arrived at after due appreciation. (iii) The next way of proving the market value of the acquired property was the way of proving the distance between the property bearing No. 18, Boulvard Road which was acquired vide award No. 10/LAC/N/10-11 and this court has already enhanced the market value of the said property to Rs. 2,34,000/- per sq. meter. That the date of notification under section 4 in the said award was 15.04.2010 and if a depreciation at the same rate of 12% p.a. is discounted backward then the market value of the acquired property would come to Rs. 1,25,000/- (approx) per sq. meter. The petitioner pointed out that the above noted property was an encumbered property since the said property was a lease hold property and not free hold. Therefore the market value of the acquired would be much higher than the market value arrived at after due depreciation. As far as nature of property is concerned, it is clear that other approximate area has already been held to be commercial in the judgment "National Cold Storage (Supra). Even otherwise, the petitioner has produced numerous evidence on record which could not be refuted by the respondent. The case of the LAC itself is that nature of the property was commercial because in award it was mentioned so. DMRC though challenged this part which was referred to this Court and this question was left upon for decision by this court but they have failed to rebut the same by producing any evidence and in fact they have adopted the evidence of LAC and relied upon award so now they are estopped from challenging the same. So as per the evidence led by both the parties and the admissions made on behalf of respondents, I hold that the land use of the property is LAC-1/2013 Page:-23/28 rightly held to be commercial in the present award announced by the LAC.

30.For determining the market value in respect of the acquired property, there is no doubt that the evidence led by the petitioner pertaining to license agreements executed by the DMRC regarding the shops situated at Pulbangash Metro Station is the best evidence since the acquired property also situated in a well established commercial situation abutting to the Pulbangash Metro Station is the best evidence on account of the admissions made by the respondents. It has been argued by petitioner that the value of the acquired property ought to have been Rs.3,20,000/- approx. but I am not inclined to award the said amount of compensation since the other evidence led by the petitioner is far less than the amount arrived at Rs.3,20,000/-. Though this piece of evidence relates to the properties absolutely abutted to the acquired property as proved by them, they cannot be altogether accepted and cannot be taken solely for consideration for arriving at the market value of the acquired property. The counsel for the petitioner has also argued that this court has already determined the market value of the land in question/ adjoining land in awards No. 9 & 10/LAC/N/10-11, but the value of their land i.e. the acquired land is much higher than the market value assessed in the above stated awards as per evidence already led by them. As stated earlier, the date of notification under section 4 in the said awards was pertaining to year 2009 and 2010 and if a depreciation at the same rate of 12% p.a. is discounted backward then the market value of the acquired property would come to Rs. 1,25,000/- (approx) per sq. meter. The petitioner, though partly LAC-1/2013 Page:-24/28 relying on said evidence has strongly contended that the lease deeds situated at Pulbangash Metro Station are the best piece of evidence for arriving at the market value wherein the market value of the acquired property comes to Rs. 3,20,000/- per sq. meter. I have perused the entire evidence led by the both the parties and the lease deeds filed by the petitioner pertained to the lease agreements executed by the DMRC itself in favour of various persons. The said lease agreements have been duly proved and have not been rebutted by the respondents. Therefore, this court cannot determine the amount of compensation payable to the petitioner only on the basis of the previous judgments passed by this court. Further, I also do not find it fit and appropriate to rely only on the lease deeds proved by the petitioner since the market value as arrived at on the basis of lease deeds are on the higher side and there are some instances which throw light on the fact that the market value of the acquired property may be less than Rs. 3,20,000/- per sq. meter. If the average price of the Rs. 3,20,000/- per sq. meter and Rs. 1,40,000/- per sq. meter is taken then the market value of the acquired property comes to Rs. 2,30,000/- as on the date of notification U/s 4 of the L. A. Act in the present case i.e. 27.07.2004. This value can also not be granted since the market value of the year 2004 and of the year 2010 will be the same, therefore, the sole values of Rs.3,20,000/- per sq. meter and Rs.1,25,000/- per sq. meter are discarded and I, therefore, find it fit and appropriate to average the entire amounts i.e. Rs. 3,20,000 per sq. mtr., Rs.1,40,000/- per sq. mtr. and Rs. 1,25,000/- per sq. mtr. arrived at and fix the market value of the acquired property bearing No. 8713, Roshanara Road measuring LAC-1/2013 Page:-25/28 335.08 sq. meter though at Rs.1,95,000/- per sq. mtr. as on the date of notification under section 4 but I am assessing the market value of the land in question at Rs. 1,70,000/- per sq. meter.

31.The next point which requires the decision of this court is whether the petitioner is entitled to any business loss. If yes, to what extent?

32.The petitioner has proved the chief data of the present award wherein the LAC had granted business to the petitioner to the tune of Rs.50.45 lakhs. The said amount was granted to the petitioner by the LAC on the basis of report prepared by the CA and submission of various documents by the petitioner such as Balance Sheet, P&L Accounts, Trading A/c ITRs etc. The noting sheets of the award file proved by the petitioner also revealed the fact that the business loss was granted to the petitioner on the basis of a report prepared by the CA which was admittedly a vetted document. I see no reason to disallow the amount of business loss which was initially granted to the petitioner specially when the same has been provided in the LA Act. The respondents have also not given any evidence in rebuttal to prove that the said amount was declined to the petitioner on account of any justified clause/ reason. Since the said amount was initially allowed to the petitioner and latter on disallowed without any proper justification. The same is now allowed in favour of the petitioner. The petitioner is therefore entitled to amount of Rs.50 lakhs 45 thousand as business loss.

33.Relief:- In view of the findings qua the issues hereinabove, the reference is answered in favour of the petitioner and the market LAC-1/2013 Page:-26/28 value of the land of the petitioner in respect of property bearing No. 8713, Roshanara Road, Pulbangash, admeasuring 335.08 sq. meter which has been acquired vide the present award is fixed at Rs.1,70,000/- per sq. mtr. as on the date of notification under section 4 i.e. 27.7.2004. The said amount has been arrived at by averaging all the evidence led by the petitioner.

34.Besides, the said amount, the petitioner would also be entitled to business loss Rs.50.45 lakhs alongwith solatium @ 30% under section 23 (2) of the LA Act on the value of land as well as structures and the petitioner would also be entitled to additional amount @ 12% p.a. from the date of notification under section 4 till the date of award or date of dispossession whichever is earlier as per section 23(1-A) of the L. A. Act. Apart from this the petitioner would also be entitled to interest on the entire enhanced amount @ 9% p.a. for the first year from the date of dispossession and @ 15% p.a. thereafter till the enhanced amount is paid to the petitioner. The petitioner is further entitled to interest on solatium and additional in terms of the judgment of the Hon'ble Supreme Court of India in "Sunder Vs UOI" (DLT (2001) SC 569). The Ld. Counsel for petitioner also argued that the petitioner should also be entitled to all the additional benefits under the amended provisions of the LA Act but the said contention is rejected being premature at this stage.

35. The petitioner has failed to prove that he is entitled to any shifting/severance charges and the petitioner is therefore not entitled to any amount of compensation as shifting/severance charges.

36.The reference is answered accordingly. The amount already paid on the basis of compensation awarded by the LAC, if any, shall be taken care off in order to avoid any over payment. No order as to cost. Decree be drawn accordingly.
