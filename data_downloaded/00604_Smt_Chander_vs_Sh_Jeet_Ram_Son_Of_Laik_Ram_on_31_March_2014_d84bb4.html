1 This is the appeal filed by the appellants against the impugned order dated 31.3.2001 passed by the then Civil Judge, Delhi granting decree of possession in favour of the respondents herein on the following grounds:

2 The plaintiffs/respondents are the owners in possession of a piece of land situated within the red line/lal dora of the village bearing Khasra No. 189 of village Kakrola, Delhi. The portion in possession of the plaintiffs in this Khasra is of multiple dimension, a plan of which has also been filed. This piece of land has been in possession of the plaintiffs and their ancestors, which was being used as Gher/Gitwar/enclosure for teathering their cattle, stocking dung cakes, fodder and fuel wood and for purpose of connected with agriculture. At the time of the 4 coming into force of the Delhi Land Reforms Act 1954, this piece of land was having a building/enclosed by boundary walls on all the sides. The plaintiffs have been enjoying the peaceful possession of the whole of the land shown in the site plan since the time of their ancestors till August 1986, when the possession of a portion of the same measuring 218' - 6" in length and 45' - 6" in width was illegally and unauthorisedly given to S/Shri Ganga Ram, Ram Singh, Kanwar Singh, Ram Phal, Ram Kanwar, Attar Singh, Rattan Singh and Smt. Dhappa (now dead) of village by the Sub Divisional Magistrate/Revenue Assistant/Deputy Director of Panchayats, Delhi with the police aid. However, Smt. Dhappa, widow of late Sh. Badley who had been put into possession of the land in dispute by Sh.

J.K.Dadoo, the then Sub Divisional Magistrate/Revenue Assistant, Delhi in the manner mentioned in the following paragraphs has died about 6/7 months ago. She was a widow at the time of her death. She has left behind neither any male or female issue. However, late Sh. Badley husband of Smt. Dhappa had two wives namely Smt. Dhappa and Smt. Chander, the present defendant. On the death of Smt. Dhappa Smt. Chander is continuing with the possession hence the suit was filed against her. This portion measuring 218' - 6" X 45' - 6" had been allotted in the shape of residential plots (9 in numbers) to each one of the above mentioned persons illegally, forcibly and in the high handed manner with the police aid on the ground that the same had been allotted to the above mentioned persons by the Gaon Sabha of the village. As a matter of fact, there had never been any allotment of these plots by the Gaon Sabha as the land never vested in the Gaon Saba, although the Gaon Sabha had been litigating to take possession of the same from the plaintiffs in different courts both Civil and Revenue. The portion measuring 218 ' - 6" x 45' - 6" has been depicted in the site plan. The Red portion is the one, which is in possession of the defendant. The present suit relates to the red portion.

5

The Gaon Sabha of the village had been litigating with the plaintiffs regarding the subject matter of dispute. This litigation between the plaintiffs and the Gaon Sabha of the village took place both in the Revenue as well as in the Civil courts in respect of the whole of the land. It has been held by all the courts that the plaintiff had been in possession of the land in suit. Sh. Chander Singh, who was the pradhan of the Gaon Sabha and who has not died had instigated the 9 persons mentioned above and in collusion with the Dy. Director of Panchayats/Sub Divisional Magistrate, Delhi, the said 9 persons threatened to encroach upon the land of Khasra No. 189 alongwith their supporters and musslemen in the month of August, 1986. Consequently, the plaintiffs were compelled to file a suit for permanent injunction against them and the Gaon Sabha. The suit being suit No. 398 of 1986 and styled as Sh. Jeet Ram and others Vs. Gaon Sabha Kakrola and 8 others was tried by the court of Sh. R.S.Khanna, Sub Judge, Delhi but later on the same was dismissed in default. At the time of the institution of the suit the Ld. Sub Judge, Delhi was pleased to grant an Exparte injunction in favour of the plaintiffs. But later on, the same was vacated on 26.2.1987. An appeal against the vacation of the exparte stay order was filed in the court of the Senior Sub Judge, Delhi, which was entrusted to the Court of Sh. P.K.Dham, the then Addl.Senior Sub Judge, Delhi who appointed a Local Commissioner for visit on the spot. The Local Commissioner was directed to note down the construction existing on the site after the visit to the place. In pursuance of the orders of the learned Addl. Senior Sub Judge, Delhi Sh. Girish Aggarwal, the Local Commissioner, visited the site on 2.7.1987 and submitted his report to the Hon'ble court. The inspection of the site was made in the presence of the parties and their counsel. The local commissioner found on the spot on 2.3.1987 that only an area measuring 218' - 6" X 45' - 6" was under construction. The portion that was under construction at 6 the time of spot inspection by the Local Commissioner has been depicted int he plan and marked as 'A'. The defendant was also present at the spot at the time of the visit of the Local Commissioner and had shown the construction raised by him on the portion shown red in this plan. As a matter of fact, the persons mentioned in the earlier part of the plaint, had started raising construction on that portion with effect from Ist day of March, 1987 when Sh. J.K.Dadu the then Sub Divisional Magistrate invested with the powers of the Deputy Director of Panchayats wrote a letter to the station House Officer, Najafgarh, New Delhi on 1.3.1987 exhorting the above 9 persons to raise construction on the land. Hence, all the 9 persons mentioned in the earlier para of the plaint raised construction on the land measuring 218' - 6" x 45' - 6" on the instigation of Sh. J.K.Dadu, the then Sub divisional Magistrate, Delhi. The SHO of the local police sent an armed guard consisting of a Hd. Constable and constables etc. at the direction of the Sub Divisional Magistrate, Delhi. As such this construction was raised by the 9 persons (including the defendant) illegally, forcibly and under the protection of the Local Police, with the result that the plaintiffs were helpless spectators to this high handedness on the part of Sh. J.K.Dadu, the then Sub Divisional Magistrate, Delhi. The plaintiffs have no option but were advised in law to take proper steps in the court of law to regain possession. The plaintiffs have asked the defendant several times to remove the structures from the land in suit and handover the vacant peaceful possession of the same to the plaintiffs. But the defendant does not pay any heed to the repeated requests of the plaintiffs, hence this suit. The cause of action arose in favour of the plaintiffs and against the defendant in September, 1986, when the defendant occupied the land illegally, forcibly and in a highhanded manner with the police aid and further raised construction thereon. The cause of action is a recurring one and the suit is within time. The land in suit 7 is situated within the revenue estate of Kakrola, Delhi state, Delhi. The cause of action also arose there.

Thereafter written statement was filed on behalf of the defendants in which the plea was taken that the allotment of this land by the Gaon Sabha under 20 Point Programme was held in the year 1976, possession of which was given to them on the day. Plaintiff has no locus standi to file the present suit and it was specifically pleaded that the plaintiff are not the owners. Value of the suit is beyond the pecuniary jurisdiction of Rs. 1 lakhs of the Sub Judge. He should have valued the suit to the market value. Land is governed by the Delhi Land Reforms Act and suit for ejectment is governed by Section 84 of Delhi Land Reforms act such jurisdiction of civil court is barred. Suit is not within time. Suit is not properly prescribed. It is stated that the possession was handed over with the police aid because plaintiff being big Zamindar of the area and was not allowing the allotment in 1976 under the 20 Point Programme and even the revenue authority has to seek police aid. It is denied that the constructions raised are illegal. The replication was filed by the plaintiff to the written statement of the defendant.

On the pleadings of the parties, following issues were framed:

Issues:
1 Whether the plaintiff has no locus-standi or right or interest in the land in question ?
2 Whether the plaintiffs are owners of the suit property ? 3 Whether the suit is properly valued and stamped ?
4 Whether this court has no pecuniary jurisdiction ?
5 Whether this court has no jurisdiction in view of Section 84 of DLR Act ?
8
6 Whether the suit is bad for non-joinder ?
7 Whether the suit is time barred ?
8 Whether the plaintiff is entitled to the relief claimed ? 9 Relief.
3 Vide judgment of the trial court dt. 31.3.2001, the suit was decreed giving following findings on the evidence before it.
4 First finding on issue No. 3 & 4 was given which objection of the defendant was declined in the absence of any substantive evidence on the part of the defendant as to the market value of the property and both these issues were decided in favour of the plaintiff.
5 Findings on issue No.5 which was also decided against defendant on the ground that the provisions of Delhi Land Reforms Act qua possessions of agricultural lands are and such matters of land in dispute being land dora land which is not and as the plaintiff has claimed themselves to be the owner and not Bhumidar, so the question of applicability of the Act does not arise.
6 Finding on issue No. 6 was given on the ground that the defendant has not disclosed who was the necessary party and how it was bad for non joinder of necessary parties and the suit was held within limitation as the possession was given to them in the September 1986 and the suit for possession can be filed within 12 years bringing the relevant articles i.e article 67 of Limitation Act and the suit having been filed within 12 years being government by article 64 of Limitation Act. It was the contention of the defendant that the suit land being 9 having been vested with Gaon Sabha on coming into the portion of the Act it is covered U/s. 7 of the Gaon Sabha Act. Had it would have been a private property of the plaintiff then the common passage of the village which is of the width of the 25 ft. would have of the existence.
7 I have perused the remaining two findings on the issues relating to the ownership.
8 As far as the finding on issue No. 6 is concerned the Ld. Trial Judge while relying upon the judgment interse between the Gaon Sabha and the plaintiff which have been filed the suit since against the plaintiff since 1973 on the basis of the judgments dismissing the suit of the Gaon Sabha for possession and these suits were dismissed. The trial court came to the conclusion that on the basis of the evidence before the trial court they have been in the position to establish that they were in possession of the land in suit being having a boundary wall and using the same as gher and gitwar and has come to the conclusion that they were the owner in possession and according to the trial court the possession by the SDM with the police aid forcibly and illegally was handed over to defendant, hence plaintiff has locus standi and right to maintain the suit. While finding on issue No. 2 and 8, the trial court has cited the provision of Delhi Land Reforms Act of Section 7 and 8 and quoted the same and then finally decreed the suit.
9 The arguments as far as issue No. 3, 4 & 5 are concerned, I found the reasoning given by the trial court based on the evidence before it and there is no infirmity in the reasoning given by the Ld. Trial Court and hence there is no ground for interference.
10
10 As far as issue No. 6 is concerned, plaintiff has converted this suit by writing a para under Cause of action admittedly as mentioned in the plaint, the possession of the property in suit was handed over to the defendant by a government agency i.e. SDM after the allotment was given to the m in the year 1976 under 20 point programme of Hon'ble Prime Minister Indira Gandhi and possession of the same was handed over to them by the SDM in the year 1986 with police aid. The Ld. Trial court has dismissed the suit on issue relating to non joinder on the ground that defendant has not proved how the suit is bad for non joinder of parties. Perusal of the plaint and written statement clearly shows that it is pleaded by the plaintiff that possession of the land in suit was handed over to the defendant after allotment of the same was done in the year 1976 after 20 point programme by Prime Minister of India and handed over in the year 1986 by the then SDM, Revenue Assistant, Panchayat Secretary Mr. Dadu with the help of police aid. Though this facts are mentioned in the plaint but while writing cause of action clause plaintiff has specifically skipped the factom that this possession was handed over to the defendant through the SDM after the allotment of these plots were done in their names in 1976 under the 20 Point Programme of Prime Minister of India with the help of police aid so it is not the case of the plaintiff himself also that defendant unauthorizedly came into possession on their own.

Infact what plaintiff by filing of suit for possession against the defendant directly is challenging the act and the proceeding conducted by the SDM in handing over the possession of the plot to the defendant, infact, he is challenging the order of the official machinery without making them a party and without seeking the declaration to the effect that order of the then SDM was against the law and the order is liable to be declared illegal and defendant after that declaration given the 11 defendant consequently are liable to hand over the possession given to them through the agency of SDM concerned which act of the SDM which the plaintiff is challenging, infact, without either making Revenue Assistant, SDM concerned party and without seeking a declaration. The plaintiff has infact converted this suit into a suit for possession though mentioning the factom of possession of that plot in suit having been handed over to the defendant through SDM in 1986 after allotment was done in 1976 under 20 point programme of the Prime Minister of India. Hence the Revenue authorities under the authority and act of which the defendant has given possession were not only essential and necessary party to the suit and suit without challenging their order, act or proceeding directly against the defendant is bad for non joinder of necessary party. The reasoning of the trial court that the plaintiff has not disclosed or proved that their suit is bad for non joinder of parties is against the record because the objection was taken by the defendant and that objection was clear from the plaint itself even if defendant has not pleaded anything the plaint itself shows by making narration to the effect possession was handed over by the SDM with the help of police aid to the defendant. As far as finding on the above issue of the trial court is reversed is being against the record and have been given without applying mind and without appreciating the material available on record.

11 Finding on issue No. 1 & 2, observation of the trial court is based on the dismissal of the suit filed by the Gaon Sabha on the ground that the Gaon Sabha has filed a suit in the year 1973 which was dismissed being barred by time. The finding of the trial court that though the plaintiff have claimed that they are in the possession of the suit prior to 1973 the trial court has missed the point that it was the part of the land and not the entire land and it was dismissed on the ground 12 having not been filed within the limitation. Atleast it established the possession of the plaintiff qua the part of the rent for which the suit was filed by the Gaon Sabha against the plaintiff. In this case, they claimed that they were in possession since the time of their ancestors prior to coming into force of the Act finding of the trial court is based only on that allegation. The trial court has relied upon the oral testimony of PW2 and 3 stating that which, fact is not corroborated by material on record including the report of Local Commissioner to the effect that when a suit filed by the plaintiff against the defendant in the year in September 1986 both the Local Commissioners did not found any boundary wall at the spot and the Kothas claimed by the plaintiff. The oral testimony of PWs relied upon by the trial court is contradicted by the Local Commissioner report of two Local Commissioners appointed at the instance of the plaintiff which did not found any boundary wall or the kothas at the spot in suit and that report is not challenged. That report of Local Commissioner is based upon the physical inspection and not having been challenged at all. The oral testimony of the witness of the plaintiff that these were demolished 9-10 years ago is not corroborated by any evidence on record. Even the site plan filed by the plaintiff in suit which they were filed these boundary wall were not shown and depicted and the trial court without disposing of the argument raised by the defendant that these judgments were interse between plaintiff and Gaon Sabha where defendant was not a party. The finding on issue No.1 and 2 is based on no reasoning.

12 I quote para 37 of the judgment dt. 31.3.2001 where the trial court has jumped to the conclusion without giving any reason except basing their reasons on the oral testimony of the plaintiff himself. Whereas there is nothing on record to corroborate this. The judgment between Gaon Sabha and the plaintiff which 13 was for the part of the land and not for the entire land were dismissed on technical ground that the suit has been barred by time. There is nothing on record to arrive at the conclusion except the oral testimony of PW, the plaintiff himself that they are in possession since before coming into the prior to coming into force of the Delhi Land Reforms Act in 1954. In fact in the additional evidence produced before the court in appeal, defendant has specifically established on record that plaintiff ancestors of the plaintiff exchanged around three bighas of their lal dora land during consolidation proceedings for agricultural land which has been duly proved on record. There is nothing on record to show that boundary wall was in existence, gher gitwar was in existence inside the boundary wall. The oral testimony of PW was relied upon by the trial court that gher-girtwar and the boundary wall was demolished when the possession was handed over to the defendant is also belied by the testimony of Jeet Ram himself and the argument of the plaintiff counsel has been belied by the testimony of plaintiff witness himself who has stated that the private road constructed in between of 25 futa road and the installation of electric poles were prior to the handing over of the possession to the defendant. Moreover, the judgment cited at bar interse between the plaintiff and Gaon Sabha were also not for the entire land it was only for three bighas or some biswas. There is nothing on record for the Trial court to come to the conclusion that they were in possession prior to coming into force of Delhi Land Reforms act and that is why they are considered to be owner having these lands at private land U/s. 8 of the Delhi Land Reforms Act.

13 Furthermore in the absence of any evidence of record for the trial court to go to the conclusion that their possession was prior to that of coming into force of Delhi Land Reforms Act, the suit of the plaintiff is based on settled position. The 14 observation of the trial court that the possession was handed over to defendant forcibly and illegally is unjustified. There is nothing on record to show that this act of the SDM was illegal though use of force was necessitated since the allotment was done in the year 1976 as per the allotment letter duly proved on record but the possession could not be handed over till 1986. Furthermore the counsel for the defendant/appellant has argued that plaintiff has no locus standi to file the present suit as plaintiff has failed to prove the ownership of the entire land having failed to prove that they were in possession, prior to coming into force of the Act at best what has come on record with the possession qua the part of the land prior to 1973 though the plaintiff has claimed himself to be the owner and possession, he cannot be allowed to prejudice the case of the defendant by now saying that his possession was anterior to that of the defendant.

14 The case of the plaintiff is based on the ownership which he has failed to prove, there is nothing on record for the trial court to come to the conclusion that their possession was prior to the coming into force of the 1954 Delhi Land Reforms Act and by virtue of Section 8 of DLR Act, they have became owner and at this stage to that extent trial for the trial court are vitiated not based on anything, based on material on record except the oral testimony of the plaintiff not corroborated by any documentary evidence.

15 The case of the plaintiff was based on title and possession and now basing his arguments on his possession being anterior ink nature U/s. 5 of the Delhi Land Reforms Act. Now he cannot come to the court only on the basis of his possession being anterior to that of the defendants, should has come into being as per the allotment being done by the SDM as per the allotment being done by the 15 SDM concerned under 20 Point Programme under Prime Minister of India which act and proceedings of the SDM has not been challenged at all in the entire plaint. Possession of the defendant under the circumstances is not unauthorised, he was handed over the possession under the proceedings conducted by SDM, Revenue Assistant and Panchayat Secretary Incharge of the area and not directly taken by the defendant through the agency of the law Incharge of the affairs. Plaintiff having failed to challenge the order of the SDM, having failed to seek any declaration that the proceeding and acts of SDM in allotting the plots of the defendant amongst them under 20 Point Programme and finally handed over the possession in the year 1986 and directly filing proceedings against defendant who was put in possession by SDM. The plaintiff has no right. They have failed to prove the title and simply by basing their claim being anterior possession though first claimed their anterior possession passed on ownership and they have failed to prove in this proceedings without first challenging the order of SDM and seeking the declaration that the Act of the then SDM and proceeding conducted by him were unlawful and liable to be declared so.

16 Consequently the finding of the trial court on Issue No. 2 and 8 as to the ownership of the plaintiff having not been established on the basis of any material on record except the oral testimony of the Pws including the plaintiff on record which contrary to the report of the Local Commissioner and finding on Issue No.9 and 10 is based on no reason in para No.46, the trial court has recorded after giving due thought to the rival submission of the counsel for the parties, that "I have come to the conclusion that the plaintiff has successfully proved that they were owners and were in possession of the land in dispute till the time defendants were allotted/given the land by aforesaid SDM and police aid", 16 infact no reason has been mentioned by the trial court in para 46 for coming to that conclusion. Hence, on this issue, the finding of the trial court are without reasoning,what were the reasons for after recording the rival contention of the parties by the trial court to come to the conclusion that plaintiff has successfully proved that they were owner in possession having not been even discussed or mentioned. The Trial court has come to the royal conclusion not based on any reasoning in para No.46, though the rival contention of the counsel for the parties were noted. Hence, the finding of the trial court that the land inquestion was a private land, property of the plaintiff and it does not fall U/s. 7 of the Delhi Land Reforms Act rather it was covered U/s. 8 of the said act is based on the conclusion which has been arrived by the Trial Court without any reason but just after noting the rival contention of the counsel for the parties is not sustainable under law.

17 Finding on issue No. 1, 2 & 8 are in my considered opinion arbitrary, without reasoning, for the conclusion and royal finding only not based on any reasoned decision only and after plaintiff having filed the suit on the basis of ownership and possession and having failed to prove the ownership by bringing on record cogent and corroborative evidence on to the ownership and based on anterior possession only is not maintainable in view of AIR 1960 Allahabad 227. Defendant not being a trespasser, the suit of the plaintiff is based on mere anterior possession is not maintainable in view of AIR 1976 Rajasthan page 108. Hence, in view of my reasoning given above, findings of the trial court on Issue No. 1, 2, 7 & 8 for the reasons above are not based on reasoning, arbitrarily and perverse and set aside and it is held that plaintiff has failed to prove the existence of locus- standi and ownership and therefore, they are not entitled to the possession of the property in suit from the defendant for the reasoning mentioned above.

17

i) having failed to prove the ownership and their consequent possession,

ii) having failed to prove that they were in possession of the land in suit prior to the coming into force of the act i.e 1954 by leading cogent and corroborative evidence, infact the material on record is against the plaintiff, the oral testimony of PW infact the report of Local commissioner appointed at the instance of the plaintiff does not show any existence of boundary wall, gher or gitwar, infact the plaintiff in the plaint is claiming because the Gaon Sabha took the case against the plaintiff, their right has been extinguished and they have become owner and their predecessor having building, kothas and gitwar prior to the coming into force. Infact, the earlier suit filed by the plaintiff restraining the defendant taking the possession was dismissed in default and no restoration of same was sought. The suit of the plaintiff for possession filed subsequently, the contention of the defendant that defendant illegally and forcibly occupied the land of the plaintiff with the police aid in September '86 is contradiction to his own pleadings wherein he has specifically stated that this possession took place with the police aid at the instance of Sh. J.K.Dadu, the then Dy. Director of Panchayat Secy, SDM with the assistance of the local police and is illegal and is against his own pleading. Infact, the proceedings conducted by the Revenue authorities in allotting the land in suit under 20 Point Programme of the then Prime Minister of India and subsequently the proceeding resulting into the handing over the possession of the same duly proved on record having been recorded in the daily diary of the Patwari cannot be said to be defendant taking possession illegally and unauthorisedly. Hence for the reasons mentioned above, I set aside the finding of the trial court on Issue No. 1, 2, 6, & 8, findings on these issues are set aside, not being based on material on record, arbitrarily, perverse, not being based on any 18 sound reasoning infact finding on issue No. 2 & 8 are without any reasoning just after writing the rival contentions of the parties and not giving any reason for coming to the conclusion arrived at by the Ld. trial court set aside. In view of the my above findings, the appeal is allowed and the judgment of the trial court is set aside and suit of the plaintiff is decreed by the trial court is dismissed with no order as to costs. Decree sheet be prepared.
