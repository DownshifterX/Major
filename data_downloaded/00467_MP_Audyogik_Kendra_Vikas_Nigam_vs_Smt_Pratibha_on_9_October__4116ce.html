                           ORDER

(Passed on 09.10.2017) Per P.K. JAISWAL, J:-

Since the common question of law is involved in these writ appeal, therefore, they were heard together and are being disposed of by this common order. For the sake of convenience the facts are borrowed from W.A.No.215/2017.

2. The appellant - M.P. Audyogik Kendra Vikas Nigam (M.P.A.K.V.N) has called into question a judgment of a single Bench, dated 23 rd March, 2017, passed in W.P.No.1971/2008. The writ petition has been allowed holding that all the proceedings under the Land Acquisition Act, 1894, (for short "the 1894 Act") have come to an end and observed that the authorities are certainly free to proceed by passing an award under the provisions of the Right to Fair Compensation and Transparency in Land Acquisition, Rehabilitation and Resettlement Act, 2013, (for short "the 2013 Act") if they so desired.

3. The respondents No.1 to 14 in present writ appeal no.215/2017, 1 to 4 in writ appeal no.226/2017 and writ appeal no.227/2017, are landowners (22 in numbers) have filed three writ petitions (W.P.No.1971/2008, W.P.No.1980/2008 and W.P.No.6584/2008). Being aggrieved by the order dated 2.8.2007 (Annexure P/1) by which the Council of Ministers have resolved that the land in question meant for establishing "Diamond Park"

under the "Indore Development Scheme, 2021" is residential / commercial land. The landowners are also against the order dated 20.7.2007 (Annexure P/14) passed by the Principal Secretary of Commerce proposing a Cabinet decision and accepting the proposal of M.P.A.K.V.N officer that the entire land was required. The relief(s) claimed in the writ petition reads as under :-
"(a) By way of appropriate writ, order or direction, this Hon'ble Court may be pleased to quash the State Government Order dated 2.8.2007 (Annexure P/1) passed by the Chief Secretary of the Government of Madhya Pradesh and Report/Order dated 20.7.2007 of the Principal Secretary, of the Department of Commerce, Industry and Employment (Annexure P/14).
(b) By way of appropriate, writ, order or direction, this Hon'ble court may declare that the order of the State Government dated 2.8.2007 is illegal, unconstitutional and contrary to the judgment and orders dated 30.7.2003 passed by Hon'ble Supreme Court in the matter of Smt. Pratibha Nema and Ors.

V/s. State of M.P. & Ors in Civil Appeal Nos.7133-36 and 9138 of 1999; 2003 (10) SCC 626, (Annexure P/2).

(c) By way of appropriate writ, order or direction, this Hon'ble court may declare that the entire proceedings for acquisition of the lands in question have lapsed and the Award (Annexure P/3) dated 30.10.2004 is null and void under Section 9, 11-A and 12 of the Act;

(d) By way of appropriate writ, order or direction, this Hon'ble court may direct that the establishment of Diamond Park at Rangwasa, Indore is not envisaged in the New Industrial Policy of 2004 (Annexure P/9) and thereafter public purpose ceases and the acquisition lapses;

(e) The costs of the petition kindly be awarded in favour to the petitioners;

(f) Any other relief which this Hon'ble court may find fit be provided to the petitioners in the interest of justice."

4. The appellant M.P.A.K.V.N ltd., is a company registered under the Companies Act, 1956 and is a subsidiary of the M.P.T.R.I.F.A.C Ltd., which is a Government of Madhya Pradesh undertaking. On 6.5.1994, the State of Madhya Pradesh declared its industrial policy popularly known as the Industrial Policy and Action Plan, 1994. Item No.8.12 of the said policy envisaged establishment of the diamond park in the State of M.P. A resolution was passed on 23.11.1995 by the Board of M.P.A.K.V.N to acquire the land for establishment of diamond park for which the State Government was required to acquire the land under the Act of 1894. On 18.1.1996, Government of Madhya Pradesh accorded principal sanction to establish the Diamond Park at village Rangwasa near Rau Tehsil and Distt. Indore.

5. On 30.1.1996, Notification under Section 4(1) of the 1894 Act was issued for acquiring 73.304 hectares (181.06 acres) of land for establishing the Diamond Park. Enquiry under Section 5(A) of the 1894 Act was dispensed with by invoking the urgency Clause under Section 17 of the said Act. On 9.2.1996, a notification was issued under Section 6 of the 1894 Act.

6. Acquisition of land by the State Government was challenged by the respondents No.1 to 14 by way of writ petition bearing W.P.No.228/1996, which was dismissed. Thereafter, Letters Patent Appeal (LPA) No.70/1996, was preferred, which was also dismissed on 25.6.1996.

7. On 24.2.1996, notices under Section 9 of the 1894 Act, were issued to the land owners for furnishing details of their respective land and calling objections / claims.

8. The respondents No.1 to 14, preferred Special Leave Petition before the Apex court wherein, it was submitted by the State Government that the Notification issued under Section 6 of the 1894 Act, dated 9.2.1996 was being withdrawn and it was also submitted that the procedure prescribed under Section 5A of the 1894 Act would be followed before issuing fresh notification under Section 6 of the 1894 Act. It was also mentioned that the Government did not desire to apply the urgency clause as provided under Section 17 of the 1894 Act . The Hon'ble Supreme Court vide order dated 11.10.1996 in Civil Appeal No.12848/1996, recorded the statement of the counsel for the Government of M.P. that they will withdraw the notification issued under Section 6 dated 9.2.1996 will follow the procedure prescribed under Section 5A giving hearing to the landowners before issuing a fresh notification under Section 6 of the 1894 Act.

9. On 3.1.1997, a fresh notification under Section 6 of the 1894 Act, was issued, after completing the enquiry as provided under Section 5A of the 1894 Act. The landowners subsequently preferred writ petition no.403/1997, challenging the aforementioned fresh notification issued under Section 6 of the 1894 Act. Vide order dated 23.7.1999, writ petition no.403/1997, was dismissed against which special leave petition was preferred before Hon'ble Supreme Court bearing Civil Appeal No.7133/1999. The Hon'ble Supreme court framed four questions, to be determined by the High court and directed to record its finding on the aforesaid four questions and forward the report to the Supreme court. On 5.7.2002, High court gave finding on four questions in M.C.C.No.506/2001 and forwarded the report to the Apex court. On 30th July, 2003, the Hon'ble Supreme court gave final judgment, dismissing the challenge to the acquisition and particularly holding that "we would only like to observe that it is desirable that the State Government makes a fresh assessment in the light of the latest situation and shall exclude any part of the land which may be found excess"

10. On 30.10.2004, after complying with the requisite formalities and directions issued by the Court, the respondent No.21 passed the award for acquisition of 73.304 hectares of private land at village Rangwasa Tehsil and District Indore.

11. The appellant M.P.A.K.V.N deposited an amount of Rs.50 lacs, another amount of Rs.2.50 crores was deposited with the Land Acquisition Officer on 30.10.2004 vide Cheque No.289308.

12. On 2.8.2007 vide Annexure P/1, the Council of Ministers took a decision that the land use of the land acquired for Diamond Park shall be kept as residential / Industrial (Pollution Free) in the Indore Development Plan, 2021.

13. On 22.8.2007, the physical possession of the land ad-measuring 73.247 hectares situated at village Rangwasa, comprising of a total of 70 survey numbers of 54 landowners was taken after preparing spot memo (Mauka Panchnama) and was handed over to the Sub- Divisional Officer, District Industries Centre, Indore. Individual possession receipts were also prepared for each survey number. Thereafter, the Manager, District Trade and Industries Centre, handed over possession of the said land to the Manager, M.P.A.K.V.N (I) Ltd.

14. Private land ad-measuring 73.304 hectares owned by 54 land owners was acquired for the Diamond Park project. The compensation amount payable to the aforesaid 54 land owners was determined and award dated 30.10.2004 was passed. The landowners applied for reference under Section 18 of the 1894 Act, for enhancement of compensation. On 14.12.2007, notices were given to the owners to collect the amount of compensation. Out of 35 landowners 4 had received the compensation amount from the office of the Land Acquisition Officer. However, remaining 31 landowners failed to turn up to collect the compensation. The reference of remaining 31 landowners along with cheques of compensation amount were forwarded to the District Judge, Indore. Vide letter dated 7.1.2008, the Land Acquisition Officer forwarded the reference application of remaining 18 landowners to the District Judge along with the cheques of compensation amount in the names of respective landowners.

15. Out of a total 54 landowners only 36 have approached this court, the remaining 18 landowners have not challenged the award and have also not made prayer in the light of Section 24 of the 2013 Act. From 36 landowners only 22 persons approached this court through Writ petition Nos.1971/2008, 1980/2008 and 6584/2008.

22. The learned writ court after appreciating the material on record came to the conclusion that the respondents No.15 to 22 have taken simply paper possession as the landowners are still cultivating their land and depositing their electricity bill in respect of electricity connection of the house / other electrical equipment in respect of the land in question. It is also observed that no document has been produced before the writ court by the State or by the appellant M.P.A.K.V.N. that the amount in respect of award was deposited in the court and held that the respondents No.15 to 22 have taken only paper possession and nothing has been deposited in the court, therefore, the land acquisition proceedings have come to an end. The learned writ court relying on the decisions of the Apex court in the case of Pune Municipal Corporation and Anr. V/s. Harakchand Mishrimal Solanki and Ors. reported in (2014) 3 SCC 183, Sita Ram V/s. State of Haryana & Anr., (2015) 3 SCC 597 , Delhi Development Authority Vs. Sukhbir Singh and Ors. in Civil Appeal No. 5811/15 vide judgment dated 09.09.2016 and Banda Development Authority V/s. Motilal Agrawal, reported as 2011 (5) SCC 394 has held that the State and appellant have taken symbolic possession and the Government has failed to produce any evidence that possession was taken by preparing a Panchnama in the presence of the independent witnesses and that their signatures were obtained on the Panchnama, as held by the Hon'ble Supreme Court in the case of Prahlad Singh and Ors. Vs. Union of India & Ors., reported in (2011) 5 SCC 386.

23. The learned writ court also held that, no actual possession has been taken by the State and empty formalities have been done by the State Govt. and the amount has not been offered to the land owners, of which, no proof has been brought to the notice of this Court nor there is any order-sheet of the State Govt. establishing that the amount has been deposited with the Court, and held that the proceedings under the 1894 Act, shall be deemed to have been lapsed and the appropriate Govt. if so chooses shall be free to initiate proceedings afresh under the provisions of the Right to Fair Compensation Transparency in Land Acquisition, Re- rehabilitation and Re-settlement Act, 2013.

31. We have heard the learned counsel for the parties and perused the record of the case.

32. Section 24 of the 2013 Act reads as under :-

"24. Land acquisition process under Act No.1 of 1984 shall be deemed to have lapsed in certain cases.- (1) Notwithstanding anything contained in this Act, in any case of land acquisition proceedings initiated under the Land Acquisition of the Act 1894(1 of 1894).
(a) where no award under Section 11 of the said Land Acquisition Act has been made, then, all provisions of this Act relating to the determination of compensation shall apply; or
(b) where an award under said Section 11 has been made, then such proceedings shall continue under the provisions of the said Land Acquisition Act, as if the said Act has not been repealed.

2. Notwithstanding anything contained in sub section (1), in case of land acquisition proceedings initiated under the Land Acquisition Act, 1894, where an award under the said Section 11 has been made five years or more prior to the commencement of this Act but the physical possession of the land has not been taken or the compensation has not been paid the said proceedings shall be deemed to have lapsed and the appropriate Government, if it is so chooses, shall initiate the proceedings of such land acquisition afresh in accordance with the provisions of this Act :

Provided that where an award has been made and compensation in respect of a majority of land holdings has not been deposited in the account of the beneficiaries, then, all beneficiaries specified in the notification for acquisition under Section 4 of the said Land Acquisition Act, shall be entitled to compensation in accordance with the provisions of this Act.
33. Similarly, Section 31 of the 1894 Act, reads as under :-
"31.Payment of compensation or deposit of same in the court -
(i) On making an Award u/s 11, the Collector shall tender payment of compensation awarded by him to the person interested entitled thereto according to the Award and shall pay it to them unless prevented by someone or more of the contingencies mentioned in the next subsection.
(ii) If they shall not consent to receive it or if there be no person competent to alienate the land or if there be any dispute as to the title to receive compensation or to the apportionment of it, the Collector shall deposit the amount of the compensation in the Court to which a reference u/s 18 would be submitted."

34. A perusal of sub section (2) of Section 24 indicates that - Notwithstanding anything contained in sub section (1) of Section 24, in case where land acquisition proceedings are initiated under the 1894 Act and when an award under Section 11 has been made five years prior to commencement of the 2013 Act, but physical possession of the land has not been taken or compensation has not been paid, the said proceeding shall be deemed to have been lapsed. Further Section 31 of the 1894 Act, pertains to payment of compensation or deposit of the same in Court. This provision contemplates that on making of an award under Section 11, the Collector shall tender the payment of compensation awarded by him to the person interested, entitled thereto according to the award and shall pay to them unless prevented by someone or some incompetency. Sub section (2) contemplates that if for any reason, amount is not paid or there is no competent person to receive the compensation, Collector shall deposit the amount of compensation to the Court to which reference under Section 18 would be submitted. A conjoint reading of both these sections clearly indicates that if award under land acquisition proceeding held under the 1894 Act, is passed five years prior to coming into force of 2013 Act and if either physical possession of the land has not been taken over or compensation is not paid to the beneficiaries, then the land acquisition proceedings lapse. The manner of payment of compensation is contemplated under Section 31 of the 1894 Act and the eventualities or non receipt of compensation warrants the Collector to deposit the amount with the Court where the reference can be submitted. Both these provisions, particularly, the provisions of Section 24 has been interpreted by the Supreme Court in the case of Pune Municipal Corporation (supra) and after considering various aspects of the matter in para 11, 19 and 21 the following principles have been laid down :-

11. Section 24(2) also begins with non obstante clause. This provision has overriding effect over Section 24(1). Section 24(2) enacts that in relation to the land acquisition proceedings initiated under 1894 Act, where an award has been made five years or more prior to the commencement of the 2013 Act and either of the two contingencies is satisfied, viz; (i) physical possession of the land has not been taken or (ii) the compensation has not been paid, such acquisition proceedings shall be deemed to have lapsed. On the lapse of such acquisition proceedings, if the appropriate government still chooses to acquire the land which was the subject matter of acquisition under the 1894 Act then it has to initiate the proceedings afresh under the 2013 Act. The proviso appended to Section 24(2) deals with a situation where in respect of the acquisition initiated under the 1894 Act an award has been made and compensation in respect of a majority of land holdings has not been deposited in the account of the beneficiaries then all the beneficiaries specified in Section 4 notification become entitled to compensation under 2013 Act.
19. Now, this is admitted position that award was made on 31.01.2008. Notices were issued to the landowners to receive the compensation and since they did not receive the compensation, the amount (Rs.27 crores) was deposited in the government treasury. Can it be said that deposit of the amount of compensation in the government treasury is equivalent to the amount of compensation paid to the landowners/persons interested? We do not think so.

In a comparatively recent decision, this Court in Agnelo Santimano Fernandes[2], relying upon the earlier decision in Prem Nath Kapur[3], has held that the deposit of the amount of the compensation in the states revenue account is of no avail and the liability of the state to pay interest subsists till the amount has not been deposited in court.

21. The argument on behalf of the Corporation that the subject land acquisition proceedings have been concluded in all respects under the 1894 Act and that they are not affected at all in view of Section 114(2) of the 2013 Act, has no merit at all, and is noted to be rejected. Section 114(1) of the 2013 Act repeals 1894 Act. Sub-section (2) of Section 114, however, makes Section 6 of the General Clauses Act, 1897 applicable with regard to the effect of repeal but this is subject to the provisions in the 2013 Act. Under Section 24(2) land acquisition proceedings initiated under the 1894 Act, by legal fiction, are deemed to have lapsed where award has been made five years or more prior to the commencement of 2013 Act and possession of the land is not taken or compensation has not been paid. The legal fiction under Section 24(2) comes into operation as soon as conditions stated therein are satisfied. The applicability of Section 6 of the General Clauses Act being subject to Section 24(2), there is no merit in the contention of the Corporation."

35. It has been clearly laid down by the Supreme Court in the aforesaid case, that if compensation is not paid or if possession of the land is not taken over and if five years period or more is over, prior to commencement of the 2013 Act, the land acquisition proceedings lapse. Section 31 (1) of the 1894 Act is also taken note of and it has been clearly held that if compensation is neither paid to the beneficiaries nor deposited in the Court where reference would be met under Section 18 , land acquisition proceedings would lapse. It is also held that deposit of the amount as per the award with the treasury of the Government of State Revenue Department is not sufficient compliance. This judgment of the Supreme Court in the case of Pune Municipal Corporation (supra) has been subsequently considered in the case of Shiv Raj and others (supra) and after taking note of the said judgment certain other judgments in the case of Bharat Kumar (supra) and Bimla Devi and others (supra) have been taken note of and in para 26 and 27 the matter has been crystallized in the following manner :-

26. The objects and Reasons of the 2013 Act and particularly Clause 18 thereof fortify the view taken by this Court in the judgments referred to hereinabove. Clause 18 thereof reads as under :-
"18. The benefits under the new law would be available in all the cases of the land acquisition under the Land Acquisition Act, 1894 where award has not been made or possession of land has not been taken." (Emphasis added)
27. However, the aforesaid appeals have to be decided in the light of the above settled legal prepositions. The admitted facts of the case remain that the respondent tenure holders had filed objections under Section 5A of the 1894 Act as admitted in the affidavit filed by Smt. Usha Chaturvedi, Deputy Secretary (Land Acquisition), Land and Building Department, Vikas Bhawan, New Delhi, filed in January 2014 before this Court. Award No.15/87-

88 had been made on 5.6.1987 and possession has not been taken till date though compensation has been deposited with the Revenue Department, which cannot be termed as "deemed payment" as has been held in Harakchand case."

36. Similar is the view taken by the Supreme Court in the case of Sharma Agro Industries (supra) wherein also the principles laid down in the case of Pune Municipal Corporation (supra) etc., has been considered and reiterated. It is therefore, clear from these judgments and interpretation of Section 24 of the 2013 Act and implication of Section 31 of the 1894 Act that if after passing of the award and five years prior to coming into force of 2013 Act, amount is not paid in accordance to the requirement of law, the entire proceedings lapsed. If the aforesaid principle is applied in the present case, we find that award in question was passed on 30.10.2014. The amount of compensation has been deposited, after obtaining the loan from the State Bank of India. Out of total compensation of amount of Rs.3.00 Crores, Rs.2.50 Crores was deposited with the land acquisition officer on 30.10.2004 vide Banker Cheque No.289308 and Rs.50 lacs was deposited on the same date. Vide letter dated 7.11.2007, the amount of compensation was deposited with the reference court. Two lists were prepared for depositing the amount with the reference Court. One list of 18 landowners and the other was of 35 landowners. Both these lists along with the corresponding cheques in the name of the landowners were submitted to the reference court under the seal of the Court and duly acknowledged and signed by the District Judge. It is also not in dispute that after passing of award by the land acquisition officer, the notices were given to 31 landownrs to collect compensation, but they did not turned up. Thus, the deposit of the amount as per the award with the reference Court is sufficient compliance of Section 31 of the 1894 Act.

37. Total 73.304 hectares private land, situated at village Rangwasa, comprising of total 70 survey numbers of 54 landowners was acquired for the Diamond Park project. After depositing the amount on 14.12.2007, notices were issued to the owners to collect the amount of compensation, but they failed to turn up to collect the compensation deposit being offered. Only 22 landowners have approached this court by filing three writ petitions, the remaining landowners have not challenged the award and have also not made any prayer in the light of Section 24 of 2013 Act. The four landowners had received the compensation amount from the office of the Land Acquisition Officer. As per details given in the appeal, 49 landowners whose reference application were forwarded to the District Judge by the land acquisition officer and the amount has been deposited with the reference court.

38. In the case of Aligarh Devt. Auth. V/s. Megh Singh & Others, the Hon'ble Supreme Court while dealing with the provision of 1894 Act and 2013 Act, held that the stand of the authority that it had deposited 80% of the compensation with the land acquisition officer and hence it was for the owner to collect the money, cannot be appreciated. That is a matter between Requisitioning authority and Acquisitioning authority. There is no question of 'come and get', the compensation while compulsorily acquiring the land; the approach required under law is `go and give'.

39. As per law laid down from time to time, the Hon'ble Supreme court clearly laid down the principle that if either of the eventualities contemplated under sub section 2 of Section 24 are in existence, the land acquisition proceedings lapsed. The two eventualities are that possession is not taken over or compensation in accordance to law is not given to the beneficiaries. In the present case, as per affidavit filed by the Managing Director of M.P.A.K.V.N the possession was taken over by the State Government on 22.8.2007 and thereafter, the name of the Government was mutated in the revenue record. After taking possession of the land in question, the same is handed over to the Managing Director, Trade & Industries Centre, Indore, who in turn, handed over to the appellant - M.P.A.K.V.N. After obtaining the possession fencing of the land was done, the appellant has already proceeded towards implementation of the project and has also assigned the work for preparing DPR for the development of Diamond Gems and Jewellery Park spread over in 118.020 hectares of land near Rau, Dist. Indore. After complying with the requisite formalities as to tender, the aforementioned work has been assigned to M/s. Azad Jain and Associations, Indore, who have already started the work. The appellant in order to protect the actual possession of the appellant had issued tenders for fencing the entire area in the year 2010 itself. The appellant had filed a Mauka Panchanama of the entire land as Annexures R/1 to R/14. The appellant had issued work order on 25.10.2010 for fencing of chain link mask & RCC Fork and on 6.4.2010, for fixing the RCC pole end barbed wire at Diamond Park. The landowners in para 5 of their rejoinder very categorically admitted that the possession was taken by the appellant - M.P.A.K.V.N. From the aforesaid, it is clear that the Government had taken the possession of the land proposed to be acquired only after an award of compensation thereof has been made under Section 11 of the 1894 Act. Upon taking of possession the land vests in the Government, ie., to say the owner of the land losses to the Government the title to it. It remains to deal with one submission of Shri Mathur. According to Shri Mathur, learned Senior counsel for the respondents, physical possession has not been taken of the land in dispute. We afraid this may not be correct. The Mauka Panchnama Annexures R/1 to R/14, specifically records that the possession of the land above stated was recovered on 22.8.2007 and handed over to the District Trade and Industries Centre and the District Trade and Industries Centre handed over the same to the appellant - M.P.V.K.N. The one of the ground of the landowners was that crop is standing on the acquired land and they are paying the electricity charges. As per panchnama, the acquisition is of a large tract of land, it may not be possible for the acquiring/designated authority to take physical possession of each and every parcel of the land and it will be sufficient that symbolic possession is taken by preparing appropriate document in the presence of independent witnesses and getting their signatures on such document. The Mauka Panchanama, which is of 2007, was never been disputed by the landowners for a period till 2.8.2013. On 2.8.2013, for the first time, they stated that paper possession was taken. It is also to be noted that after taking physical possession of the land, the entire land was secured by fencing and security guard was also deployed through security agency to prevent unnecessary encroachment on the said land. On 25.2.2010, the appellant issued work order for fencing of chain link mask and RCC Fork and on 6.4.2010 for fixing the RCC pole and barbed wire at the Diamond Park. The appellant started preparing the location plan, survey plan, layout for the project and also hired consultant for preparing DPR for the project. These facts shows that the appellant in order to protect the actual possession had issued tender for fencing the entire land in the year 2010 itself. The learned writ court failed to consider the aforesaid fact and held that neither the landowners have been dispossessed nor the compensation was paid to the landowners nor the amount of compensation was deposited with the reference court and erred in law in allowing the writ petition by holding that the entire proceedings lapsed.

40. Having regard to the law declared in the case of Pune Municipal Corporation and Anr. V/s. Harakchand Mishrimal Solanki and Ors. (supra) as followed by other judgments of the Hon'ble Supreme court, we are of the opinion that the compensation is paid to the beneficiaries by depositing the amount in reference court and as physical possession of the land was also taken within 5 years from the date of the award, the land acquisition proceeding would not be lapsed.

41. For the above mentioned reasons, the impugned order dated 23.3.2017, passed in W.P.Nos.1971/2008, 1980/2008 & 6584/2008, is set aside and the writ appeals filed by the appellants are allowed as indicated herein above. No costs.
