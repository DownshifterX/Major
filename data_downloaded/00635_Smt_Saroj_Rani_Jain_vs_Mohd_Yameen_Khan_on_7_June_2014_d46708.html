It is also stated that even the attorney of the plaintiff failed to prove the area Sadh Nagar in which the suit property exist is not covered within the purview of DRC Act. Whereas, the plaintiff reiterated its stands and argued forcefully that the provision of DRC Act, 1958 are not applicable to Sadh Nagar Colony, which is though notified by the MCD to be urbanized area but no corresponding notification in the DRC Act has been notified till date. It would not be an abrastion to give a glance to the relevant provision at this stage.

DELHI MUNICIPAL CORPORATION ACT, 1957 Section 507 - Special provisions as to rural areas Notwithstanding anything contained in the foregoing provisions of this Act:­

(a) the Corporation with the previous approval of the 1 [***] Government, May, by notification in the Official Gazette, declare that any portion of the rural areas shall cease to be included therein and upon the issue of such notification that portion shall be included in and form part of the urban areas;

(b) the Corporation with the previous approval of that 1 [***] Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette:­

(i) exempt the rural areas or any portion thereof from such of the provisions of this Act as if deems fit,

(ii) levy taxes, rates, fees and other charges in the rural areas or any portion thereof at rates lower than those at which such taxes, rates, fees and other charges are levied in the urban areas or exempt such areas of portion from any such tax, rate, fee or other charge;

(c) the Corporation shall pay a Gaon Sabha ­­

(i) an amount equal to the proceeds of the tax on profession, trades, callings and employments, as and when that tax is levied in the Gaon Sabha area, and

(ii) an amount equal to such portion of the proceeds of the property taxes of buildings in that area as may from time to time to be Suit No. 146/14 Saroj Rani Jain Vs. Yameen Khan Page No.11 / 18 determined by the Corporation of deducting the cost of collection from such proceeds.

Explanation­­ In this section the expressions "Gaon Sabha" and "Gaon Sabha area" have the same meanings as in the Delhi Panchayat Raj Act, 1954 (Delhi Act 3 of 1955).

DELHI RENT CONTROL ACT, 1958 Section 1 - Short title, extent and commencement

1. This act may be called the Delhi Rent Control Act, 1958.

2. It extends to the areas included within the limits of the New Delhi Municipal Corporation the Delhi Cantonment Board and to such urban areas within the limits of the Municipal Corporation of Delhi as are specified in the First Schedule:

Provided that the Central Government; may, by notification in the Official Gazette, extend this Act or any provision thereof, to any other urban area included within the limits of the Municipal Corporation of Delhi of exclude any area from the operation of this Act or any provision thereof.

3. It shall come into force on such date 1 as the Central Government may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint.

9.3 The combine reading of the above two provisions postulates that only the notification of urbanization is not, under section 507 of the MCD Act to, attract the provisions of the DRC Act, a separate notification as per the mandate of Section1 of the DRC Act, 1958 is also required. In this regard RSA No. 129/201 & CH No.2 10223­24/13 decided on 23rd January 2014 by the Hon'ble Justice Mr. Valmiki J. Mehta, while deciding other related issues in ­alia, the Hon'ble High Court has dealt with the issues that Suit No. 146/14 Saroj Rani Jain Vs. Yameen Khan Page No.12 / 18 whether Delhi Rent Control Act has been extended to the area of village Dabri, where the property in question was located and the application under section 507 of the Delhi Municipal Corporation Act 1957 would alone not suffice in order to attract the provisions of DRC Act. It is observed by the Hon'ble Court that it is necessary that after urbanization a specific notification is issued U/s 1 sub Section (2) of DRC Act extending the operation of this Act to the area in question. This has so been held by the Supreme Court in case of Mitter Sen Jain Vs. Shakuntala Devi ( 2000) 9 SCC

720. 9.4 It is also observed in the same judgment by Hon'ble High Court that there is no notification by which the DRC Act has been extended to the area of Delhi which falls in the Sub Division South West Delhi, Village Najafgarh. By way of Notification dated 23.05.1983, the village Najafgarh was declared urbanized by way of Notification dated 27.03.1979, the provision of DRC Act were made applicable to village Najafgarh and not to the entire revenue sub Division of Najafgarh as at that time Najafgarh was not even a sub division. It is also observed in the same judgment in para 18 that however, as seen from the notification dated 24.10.1994 ( No. F 33/Engg/TP (DP) /11424/94 Delhi dated 24.10.1994) the area comprising of village Palam with in the revenue estate of rural Najafgarh came to urbanized only recently. No notification has been issued under the provision of the DRC Act, 1958. The Notification dated 23/5/63 & 27/03/79 have no application to the area covered by the notification dated 24.10.1994.

9.5 The judgment of Village Palam Area Tenant Association Vs. UOI ( Public Interest Litigation) being CW No. 4284/2000 is also quoted, where the petitioner were seeking a writ of Mandamus against Union of Suit No. 146/14 Saroj Rani Jain Vs. Yameen Khan Page No.13 / 18 India and Delhi Government to issue notification U/s 1 (2) of DRC Act extending the applicability of the provisions of DRC Act with regard to the villages urbanized vide notification of the year 1994. The Division Bench of the Hon'ble High Court vide its judgment dt. 29.07.2003 dismissed the writ petition.

9.6 Attention of the court has also drawn to the CM (M) No. 119/2007, where by the Hon'ble High Court of Delhi decided the question that whether the locality in question known as Palam Enclave (now known as Sadh Nagar Part I) and the provision of the DRC Act is not applicable to the area and the petitioner cannot deposit rent under the provision of Section 27 of Delhi Rent Control, Act. The Deputy Secretary to the Government of India Ministry of Urban Development categorically stating that the Central Government has not issued any notification extending the provision of the said Act to the Palam Area.

9.7 It is clear from the above deliberation that the property in question falls under the Sub Division, South West Najafgarh and the notification dated 24.10.1994 ( No. F33/Eng/TP/DP)/11424/94 Delhi Dated 24.10.1994 is not applicable to the area comprising of Palam with in the revenue estate of Najafgarh which was urbanized recently. The case of the plaintiff squarely covered by the judgment of Ram Niwas Gupta (CM) M No. 19/2007 and it is established that the provision of the Delhi Rent Control, Act does not extent to the Sadh Nagar ( Palam Area). For the reasons stated so the issue no.1 and no. 3 are decided in favour of plaintiff and against defendant.

10. ISSUE NO. 2

Whether suit of the plaintiff is properly valued for Suit No. 146/14 Saroj Rani Jain Vs. Yameen Khan Page No.14 / 18 the purposes of court fees? OPP.

10.1 Onus to prove the issue is cast upon the plaintiff. The suit for possession is valued at the last annual rental as per Section 7 (iv) (e) ( suit between the landlady and tenant ) as per the Court Fee Act and the suit is accordingly valued on the last paid rent of Rs. 800/­ per month and suit was valued as Rs. 9,800/­ i.e. annual rental of the property in dispute. The plaintiff has also valued the suit on the arrears of rent which is Rs. 27,800 and damages and mesne profit @ 300 from the date of institution of the suit and mesne profit @ 100 per day. The rate of rent @ 800/­ per month was not disputed by the defendant. The testimony of the PW 1 remain unrebutted on this aspect and no question were put forth to the witness to rebut the same neither any documentary evidence is bring forth by the defendant. In these circumstances, the issue is decided in favour of plaintiff and against defendant.

Hence the issue is decided in favour of plaintiff and against defendant.

12.1 The juror relationship of landlady and tenant has not been disputed, neither the rate of rent has been disputed by the defendant. However, in the absence of any rent agreement specifically the premises for which the premises was let out to the defendant, the tenancy would be deemed to month to month tenancy and the same could be terminated by giving a notice U/s 106 of Transfer of Property Act is the established law of the land.

The defendant failed to show that he is not in arrear of rent since September 2004, the rent of receipt filed on the record is of prior to the said date. The landlady terminated the tenancy of the defendant by giving a legal notice U/s 106 Transfer of Property Act dated 23.07.2007 Ex. PW 1/8 & Ex. PW 1/9, the same was even replied by the deft. Ex. PW 1/11.

12.3 The defendant has tried to dispute the ownership of the plaintiff which is not permissible as per the mandate of Section 116 of Indian Evidence Act. It is clear that after the lapse of notice period, the plaintiff has filed the suit. The defendant failed to establish the defence taken by him and the case squarely falls where the jurisdiction of the court was disputed. The defence of the defendant taken in WS are not proved. He failed to satisfy the court that suit is barred on the aspect of jurisdiction, cause of action or otherwise. Whereas the plaintiff proved his case in Suit No. 146/14 Saroj Rani Jain Vs. Yameen Khan Page No.16 / 18 affirmative and her case remained almost unrebutted. Hence, for the reason stated above this issue is decided in favour of plaintiff against defendant.

13.2 As far as the matter relates to the damages and mesne profit @ 100 per day is concerned, the PW 1 was not examined on this point at the time of cross examination and the rate of rent are liable to increase in the span of almost 14 years.

13.3 In my opinion as the last paid admitted rent in the year 2004 was 800/­ the same would be liable to increase as per market value. Hence the property in question may fetch around 3000­4000 per month rent in the locality. Keeping in view the socio­economic viability of area concern.
