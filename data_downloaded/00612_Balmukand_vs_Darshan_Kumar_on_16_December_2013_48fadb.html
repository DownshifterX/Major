9. With respect to the grounds of eviction u/s 14(1)(b), (d) and (h) of the DRC Act under which the present eviction petition has been filed, the primary E-417/09/02 Balmukand & Anr. Vs. 10. It was argued by the Ld. counsel for the respondents by way of written submissions that a defence was taken up in the written statement that it is not the respondent no. 1 who was a tenant in the tenancy premises rather late Sh. Jamuna Dass, father of the respondents was a tenant under Sh. Ram Dayal @ Ram Dev, grand father of the petitioners, since the year 1946 onwards. It was further argued that this fact as asserted by the respondents was admitted to be correct by the petitioner no. 1/PW-1 Balmukand in his cross examination wherein it was stated that the tenancy premises was let out to Jamuna Dass by his grand father and that respondent no. 1 is the son of Jamuna Dass. It was further stated that the property was let out to Jamuna Dass by his grand father in between 1950 and 1960 and that Jamuna Dass was paying rent to his grand father since the inception of the tenancy. It was further argued that PW-1 also admitted that in the counterfoils of the rent receipts relied upon by him, sometimes the payment of rent has been shown to have been made by Ravi Dutt (respondent no. 2, since deceased and represented through LRs) and his family members namely Sneh Lata, Vinita, Pooja and Rajni. It was further argued that once it has been admitted by the petitioners that the original tenant with respect to the tenancy premises was late father of the respondents, the necessary corollary thereof would be that after his death, respondents being the LRs became the tenants in the tenancy premises. Reference was also made to the authoritative pronouncement of the Hon'ble Supreme Court in Gyan Devi Anand Vs. Jeevan Kumar & Ors., (1985) 2 SCC 683 wherein it was held that irrespective of the purposes of letting, commercial or residential, tenancy rights are to devolve according to E-417/09/02 Balmukand & Anr. (ii) and (iii) of the DRC Act) which provides the devolution of the tenancy rights upon the heirs of the original tenant in respect of residential premises. It was thus argued by the Ld. counsel for the respondents that not only the present eviction petition deserves to be dismissed on account of non-joinder of necessary parties who are the other LRs of the original tenant, even otherwise once it is admitted by the petitioners that father of the respondent Late Sh. Jamuna Dass was the original tenant, the eviction petition also falls on all the three grounds of eviction under which it has been filed. It was further argued that insofar as the issue of subletting of the tenancy premises by the respondent no. 1 to respondent no. 2 is concerned, the same also otherwise stood disproved on account of the testimony of the petitioner/PW-1 Balmukand. It was submitted that PW-1/petitioner in his further cross examination has admitted that both respondent no. 1 Darshan Kumar and respondent no. 2 Ravi Dutt are the sons of Late Jamuna Dass who was inducted as a tenant by his late grand father and that both the respondents were residing in the tenancy premises. It was further argued that the respondents through the documents proved on record as Ex. RW-1/1 to RW-1/13 have succeeded in establishing that the possession of the respondents was a continued possession, firstly in the capacity of being the family members of the original tenant Late Sh. It was thus submitted that the present eviction petition as filed by the petitioners fails on all the three grounds of eviction and it be dismissed accordingly.

12. Having heard the rival contentions of the parties, I am of the considered view that petitioners have succeeded in establishing on record that it was the respondent no. 1 who was a tenant in the tenancy premises in his own individual capacity. The reasons therefor are firstly, that the petitioners took a categorical stand in their eviction petition and in their evidence that respondent no. 1 Darshan Kumar was inducted as a tenant vide rent receipts Ex. PW-1/4 (colly.). Merely because petitioner/PW-1 in his cross examination admitted that Late Sh. Jamuna Dass, father of the two respondents was originally a tenant in the tenancy premises, it in itself does not mean that individual tenancy in the name of his son Darshan Kumar/respondent no. 1 could not have been created subsequently after his death. Moreover, while relying upon the counterfoils of the rent receipts as a proof of the tenancy in favour of the respondent no. 1, petitioners also relied upon two documents identified and proved as Ex. PW-1/2 and Ex. PW-1/3. Ex. PW-1/2 is the E-417/09/02 Balmukand & Anr. Vs. Darshan Kumar & Ors. Page 10 of 17 //11// original reply dated 26.05.1965 got issued upon the counsel of Sh. Ram Dayal @ Ram Dev, late grand father of the petitioners herein by Sh. Vijay Kumar Kapila, advocate and counsel for Sh. Darshan Kumar s/o Jamuna Dass. In Ex. PW-1/3 which is again a reply dated 18.06.1969 of Sh. Darshan Kumar s/o Sh. Jamuna Dass through his counsel Sh. Rajinder Kumar Shukla to Sh. Prabhu Dayal, late father of the petitioners herein, there is an admission that respondent no. 1 Darshan Kumar is a tenant with respect to premises no. 7784, Ramnagar, New Delhi comprising of rooms with tin roofed verandah in front thereof on the first floor at a monthly rent of Rs. 30/-. With respect to these two replies, petitioner no. 1/PW-1 Balmukand in his cross examination stood his ground and denied the suggestions that these notices were never sent on behalf of the respondent no. 1 Darshan Kumar or by the advocate authorized by him. Once the petitioner no. 1/PW-1 stood his ground regarding the veracity of the two abovesaid replies Ex. PW-1/2 & 3, the onus was upon the respondents to prove the contrary. Admittedly, respondent no. 1 Darshan Kumar did not step into the witness box and instead got examined his attorney Sh. Bhagwan Singh as RW-1. The testimony of RW-1 which is by way of affidavit Ex. RW-1/A shows that there is no denial of the replies Ex. PW-1/2 and Ex. PW-1/3. Cross examination of RW-1 also demolishes the defence of the respondents as in his cross examination RW-1 stated that he does not have complete knowledge of the facts of the present case and that even Darshan Kumar/respondent no. 1 has also not informed him of the facts of the present case. Also, from the rent receipts Ex. PW-1/4 (colly., total 317 in number) and which are for the period 1965 to 2002, petitioners proved on record that it was the respondent no. 1 who was the tenant in his individual capacity in respect of the tenancy premises. The contention of the Ld. counsel E-417/09/02 Balmukand & Anr. Vs. Darshan Kumar & Ors. Page 11 of 17 //12// for the respondents that from a bare perusal of some of the rent receipts it is evident that it bears the signatures of respondent no. 2 or his family members, an inference can be drawn against the petitioners that notwithstanding the rent receipts issued in the name of respondent no.1 still the tenancy was of the respondents and their brothers, same cannot be accepted and has no merits. The petitioners through the testimony of petitioner no. 1/PW-1 proved that the rent receipts Ex. PW-1/4 (colly.) shows the name of the tenant Darshan Kumar and the signatures of the person making payment of the rent on behalf of the tenant. PW-1 while admitting in his cross examination that in some of the counterfoils of the rent receipts, the payment of rent has been shown to have been made by respondent no. 2 and his family members categorically stated that the rent was collected from them on behalf of respondent no. 1. Merely because, at times payment of rent has been made by the respondent no. 2 or his family members, it does not have any bearing on the relationship of landlord-tenant between the petitioners and the respondent no. 1. When the rent was received by the petitioners/landlord, their interest was in the payment of rent alone and not in the person who was making the payment for and on behalf of tenant/respondent no. 1. Even otherwise RW-1 Bhagwan Singh and RW-1 Inder Singh both have admitted that respondent no. 1 Darshan Kumar was a tenant in his individual capacity and further that he was residing alongwith his family members at Blk-189 (MIG) Flat, Rajouri Garden, New Delhi even prior to the year 1993-94. Thus, it stands concluded that respondent no. 1 was a tenant of the petitioners in respect of the tenancy premises.

13. Once the relationship of landlord-tenant between the petitioners and respondent no. 1 gets established on record, petitioners were then to prove E-417/09/02 Balmukand & Anr. Vs. Darshan Kumar & Ors. Page 12 of 17 //13// firstly, the ground of subletting of the tenancy premises by the respondent no. 1 to the respondent no.2, secondly, that the tenancy premises was not used as a residence either by the respondent no. 1 or his family members for a period of six months immediately before the date of filing of the eviction petition and thirdly, that the respondent no. 1 had acquired vacant possession of or has been allotted a residence. Both the respondent witnesses namely RW-1 Inder Singh and RW-1 Bhagwan Singh, as already noted, admitted in their respective cross examinations that respondent no. 1 Darshan Kumar is not residing in the tenancy premises since long and is residing at Blk-189 (MIG) Flat, Rajouri Garden, New Delhi and that the tenancy premises is occupied by respondent no. 2 Ravi Dutt and his family. RW-1 Bhagwan Singh went a step further by admitting that it was the respondent no. 1 Darshan Kumar who has given the tenancy premises to respondent no. 2 Ravi Dutt to reside therein. Owing to the categorical admissions of the two respondent witnesses, petitioners succeed in proving on record that the possession of the respondent no. 2 and his family members in the tenancy premises was that of a sub tenant. The contention of the Ld. counsel for the respondents that respondent no. 2 being a real brother of the respondent no. 1 cannot be said to be a sub-tenant has no merits as the law is well settled that parting with possession even to a relative may amount to sub-letting u/s 14(1)(b) of the DRC Act. Reference herein can be made to the judgment of Hon'ble High Court of Delhi in Hari Chand (deceased) through LRs Vs. Rajender Nath & Ors., RC SA No. 16/2002 decided on 22.11.2010. It was observed therein that once it is proved that a third person which may be a relation or even a son is in possession of the tenancy property not just to assist the father, it establishes parting with the possession of the tenancy property and unless E-417/09/02 Balmukand & Anr. Vs. Darshan Kumar & Ors. Page 13 of 17 //14// and until there is a written permission of the landlord to act in that manner, the mischief of section 14(1)(b) of the DRC Act is attracted. In the present case as well, as per the admissions of the two respondent witnesses, respondent no. 2 was in exclusive possession of the tenancy premises. There is also an admission of the respondent witnesses that respondent no. 1 is not residing in the tenancy premises for the last many years and even prior to the year 1993-94 and that he is residing at another property which is Blk-189 (MIG) Flat, Rajouri Garden, New Delhi. Petitioners need to prove nothing more having once established on record that it was the respondent no. 1 who was the tenant in the tenancy premises and that it is his brother/respondent no. 2 (since deceased, now through LRs) who is residing in the tenancy premises alongwith his family. Reference herein can be made to the judgment of Hon'ble Supreme Court in Celina Coelho Pereira (Ms) & Ors. Vs. Ulhas Mahabaleshwar Kholkar & Ors., (2010) 1 SCC 217 wherein while summarizing the law regarding sub-letting, it was observed that initial burden of proving sub-letting is on the landlord but once he is able to establish that a third party is in exclusive possession of the premises and that tenant has no legal possession of the tenanted premises, the onus shifts to the tenant to prove the nature of occupation of such third party and that he (tenant) continues to hold legal possession in tenancy premises. Admittedly, in the present case, respondent no. 1 did not step into the witness box so as to establish that despite the possession of the respondent no. 2 in the tenancy premises, the legal possession and control of the tenancy premises always remained with him. Also, petitioners by placing on record various notices identified and proved as Ex. PW-1/11, Ex. PW-1/14 and Ex. PW-1/17 respectively established that respondent no. 1 was not residing in the tenancy E-417/09/02 Balmukand & Anr. Vs. Darshan Kumar & Ors. Page 14 of 17 //15// premises prior to and after the filing of the present eviction petition and that he was residing at Blk-189 (MIG) Flat, Rajouri Garden, New Delhi. Petitioners also placed on record the original certified copy of the extract from electoral rolls of 1993, identified and proved as Ex. PW-1/10 which showed the respondent no. 1 as a registered voter of the polling station no. 56 with his address of Rajouri Garden as aforesaid. Thus, it stands established on record that respondent no. 1 being a tenant of the petitioners parted with the possession of the tenancy premises to his brother/respondent no. 2 without the written consent of the petitioners and it falls within the category of sub-letting as provided u/s 14(1)(b) of the DRC Act.

14. With respect to the ground of eviction u/s 14(1)(d) of the DRC Act, it is not disputed that the purpose of letting was for residence. Petitioners, as already noted hereinabove, have established on record through their oral and documentary evidence as well as through the cross examination of RW-1 Inder Singh and RW-1 Bhagwan Singh that respondent no. 1 alongwith his family was residing at Rajouri Garden address much before the filing of the present eviction petition. Necessary reference can be made to the legal notice Ex. PW-1/5 which was issued by the petitioners upon the respondent no. 1 on the tenancy premises address and it was returned vide returned envelope and AD card Ex. PW-1/7 with the report 'left without address'. There is nothing in the respondents' evidence or in the cross examination of the petitioner/PW-1 that the said report is a false or a manipulated report. Further, during the pendency of the present eviction proceedings, petitioners got issued upon the respondent no. 1 notices u/o 12 rule 8 CPC r/w section 66 of the Indian Evidence Act at his Blk-189 (MIG) Flat, Rajouri Garden, New Delhi address. These notices were identified and proved on record by the petitioners as E-417/09/02 Balmukand & Anr. Vs. Darshan Kumar & Ors. Page 15 of 17 //16// Ex. PW-1/11, Ex. PW-1/14 and Ex. PW-1/17 respectively. The returned AD cards of the said notices were identified and proved as Ex. PW-1/13, PW-1/16 and PW-1/19 respectively. These notices and the returned AD cards proved on record that respondent no. 1 is residing at the Rajouri Garden address. Besides this, as already noted, petitioners also proved the extract of the electoral record of the year 1993 (Ex. PW-1/10) thereby again establishing that even way back in the year 1993, respondent no. 1 was a resident of Rajouri Garden address as aforesaid. The electoral list record of the year 1994 and of 2002 which was with respect to the tenancy premises was also brought on record and proved by the petitioners as Ex. PW-1/8 and PW-1/9 respectively. The name of respondent no. 1 was not in the said voter list with respect to the tenancy premises. Thus, petitioners succeed in establishing their case even u/s 14(1)(d) of the DRC Act.

15. With respect to the ground of eviction u/s 14(1)(h) of the DRC Act, petitioners were to establish that respondent no. 1 has acquired vacant possession of or has been allotted a residence which is Blk-189 (MIG) Flat, Rajouri Garden, New Delhi. As already noted, petitioners succeeded in establishing that respondent no. 1 was residing at the said address even in the year 1993 (through electoral record Ex. PW-1/8, Ex. PW-1/9 and Ex. PW-1/10 respectively). Further, petitioners got served upon the respondent no. 1 various notices which are Ex. PW-1/11, PW-1/14 and PW-1/17 respectively u/o 12 rule 8 CPC r/w section 66 of the Indian Evidence Act. Through these notices, petitioners asked the respondent no. 1 to produce various documents concerning him and his family members with respect to the property at Blk-189 (MIG) Flat as aforesaid. Neither respondent no. 1 replied to any of the said notices despite being duly served nor produced any E-417/09/02 Balmukand & Anr. Vs. Darshan Kumar & Ors. Page 16 of 17 //17// document as required by the petitioners and concerning the residential property at Rajouri Garden. As rightly argued by the petitioners, an adverse inference is to be drawn against the respondents. In terms of section 114 illustration (g) of the Indian Evidence Act, 1972, the Court may presume that the evidence which could be and is not produced would, if produced, be unfavourable to the person who withholds it. If the respondent no. 1 was not having the possession of the Rajouri Garden address, nothing prevented him or the other respondent to produce on record the documents concerning the said address so as to counter the case of the petitioners that respondent no. 1 has acquired a residential property at Rajouri Garden. Admittedly, no such evidence has been brought on record and instead both RW-1 Inder Singh and RW-1 Bhagwan Singh during their cross examination have admitted that respondent no. 1 alongwith his family members was residing at Rajouri Garden address even prior to the year 1993-94. Thus, petitioners also succeed in establishing their case u/s 14(1)(h) of the DRC Act.

16. In view of the foregoing, the petition as filed by the petitioners u/s 14 (1)

(b), (d) and (h) of the DRC Act is allowed and an eviction order in respect of the tenancy premises which comprises of one big room, one small room, one store room, one kitchen and open courtyard at the first floor of property bearing no. 10350/7784, Gali No. 3, Ramnagar, Paharganj, New Delhi-55, more specifically shown in red in the site plan Ex. PW-1/1 is hereby passed in favour of the petitioners and against the respondents. No orders as to costs.

File be consigned to record room.
