1. Plaintiff has filed the present suit against defendant for possession, recovery of rent and damages for use and occupation of the suit premises shown in the site plain in property no. B­6, Daya Nand Colony, Lajpat Nagar­IV, New Delhi­24 on the averments that plaintiff is the owner/landlord of the demised premises by virtue of registered Page no. 1 2 will and mutation letter in his favour.

2. It is further alleged that defendant is month to month tenant under the plaintiff in respect of entire ground floor of the premises no. B­6, Daya Nand Colony, Lajpat Nagar­IV, New Delhi­24 (hereinafter referred as suit property) shown in the site plan, which was let out to defendant for commercial purposes w.e.f. 01.12.2010 on monthly rent Rs. 25,000/­ payable in advance every month on or before 5th day of the each English Calender month.

3. It is further alleged that tenancy was oral and commenced from 1st of every English Calender month. It is further averred that defendant undertook to pay the rent in advance by the 5th day of each English Calender month but failed to adhere his commitment and was most irregular in payment of rent and was in arrear of rent since May 2011.

4. It is also stated that defendant filed a Civil Suit No. 244/11 for permanent injunction against the plaintiff for restraining the defendant (plaintiff therein) from disposing the plaintiff (defendant therein) from the tenanted premises without due process of law, which was disposed off after plaintiff (defendant therein) made the statement that he will not dispossess the defendant (plaintiff therein) from the suit property without due process of law.

5. It is further alleged that as defendant was irregular in payment of rent, Plaintiff served a notice dated 03.06.2011 U/s 106 of Transfer of Property Act (hereinafter referred as T.P. Act) on the defendant terminating the month to month tenancy and calling upon the defendant to handover the vacant and peaceful possession of the tenanted premises. It was also alleged that as defendant failed to hand over the possession of tenanted premises to the plaintiff, he is liable to pay damages @ 50,000/­ per month for use an occupation of Page no. 2 3 suit premises w.e.f. 01.07.11 till possession is handed over, with interest @ 24% per annum, but, as defendant failed to do so, plaintiff was constrained to file the present suit.

6. Defendant filed written statement contesting the suit taking preliminary objections that the suit was bad for non­joinder of necessary parties as all the legal heirs of the owner Smt. Harbans Kaur were not impleaded. It was also alleged that plaintiff was not the absolute owner of the suit property. It was also averred that plaintiff had fabricated and forged Will dated 10.10.1998 in connivance with his sister Gurbachan Kaur. On merits it was alleged that Late Smt. Harbans Kaur was the owner of the suit property and defendant was inducted as a tenant by her at the rate of rent of Rs. 9,000/­ per month for 22 months commencing from 07.09.2006 vide written lease agreement dated 08.09.2006. 7. It was further alleged that after expiry of the second lease agreement Late Smt. Harbans Kaur agreed to renew the lease and execute a fresh lease agreement for five years w.e.f 01.06.09 to 31.05.2004 at Rs. 25,000 per month in respect of the entire ground floor. It was also alleged that in pursuance of the mutual lease agreement to be executed by Smt. Harbans Kaur took interest free refundable security amount of Rs. 50,000/­ and also obtained the signatures of defendant on blank stamp papers for execution of lease agreement. It was further submitted that in pursuance of the oral mutual Page no. 3 4 agreement, defendant regularly paid rent @ Rs. 25,000/­ per month with effect from 01.06.09. It was also alleged that Smt. Harbans Lal expired on 24.11.2010 and after her death, plaintiff demanded the rent, which was paid to him up to May 2011.

19.My findings on issues are as follows:­ ISSUE No.1 (1) Whether the suit filed by the plaintiff is bad for non­joinder of necessary parties i.e. LRs of deceased Harbans Kaur? OPD Page no. 8 9 The onus to prove this issue was upon the defendant. DW­1 /the defendant stated that suit property was HUF and the erstwhile owner Smt. Harbans Kaur died intestate. DW1 further stated that will dated 10.10.1998 was a forged document. He also testified that plaintiff had not obtained the probate and letter of administration but had got the property mutated in his name by fraudulent means. He also stated that daughters of Late Smt. Harbans Kaur were co­owners of suit property and were necessary parties but were not impleaded as such suit was bad for non­joinder of necessary parties. In his cross­ examination, he stated that after the death of Smt. Harbans Kaur he paid rent to the plaintiff @ Rs. 25,000/­ per month. He also stated that plaintiff had obtained his signatures on an agreement but copy of the same was not supplied to him. He further stated that apart from present suit no other suit has been filed by any legal heir of Late Smt. Harbans Kaur for recovery of possession and rent. He further stated that he does not have any document to show that suit property belonged to HUF. He further stated that neither the Will dated 10.10.1998 was executed in his presence nor registered in his presence. He also testified that he had received a notice from one of the legal heir of Smt. Harbans Kaur. But further stated that Smt. Satpal Kaur, had not given notice to pay rent, she came and asked for rent.

20.On the other hand PW­1 had stated that his mother Late Smt. Harbans Kaur executed a will dated 10.10.1998 in his favour which was duly registered with the Sub­Registrar vide which she bequeathed the entire property no. B­6, Daya Nand Colony, Lajpat Nagar­IV, New Delhi­24 in his favour. He further stated that his sisters namely Smt. Amarjit Kaur, Ms. Gurcharan Kaur and Ms. Daljit Page no. 9 10 Kaur had executed a relinquishment deed duly registered dated 27.12.2011 reliquinshing their share in his favour. He also deposed that he had got the property mutated in his name in the record of the concerned department, the mutation letter was proved as Ex.PW­1/4. He further stated that he had handed over the copy of Will to the defendant and defendant attorned him and became the month to month tenant. He also deposed that by virtue of the Will and relinquishment deed, he became absolute owner of the demised premises.

21. As regards the contention of the defendant that the Will was a forged and fabricated document, I am of the view that the defendant being a tenant has no locus standi to challenge the validity of Will made by late Smt. Harbans Kaur as he (defendant) is not an heir of the landlord, as such he cannot chellenge the genuineness of the Will or bequests made therein, as held in 95 (2002) DLT 459 "Hans Raj and another Vs. Jeet Kaur (deceased)". I accordingly, hold that defendant not being a legatee under the Will and therefore, not being a legal heir, had no right to challenge the Will.

22. The next contention of defendant that Smt. Satpal Kaur had sent him a letter for demanding rent stands falsified by his contradictory versions in his cross examination stating that he had received a notice from one of the legal heirs of Smt. Harbans Kaur and thereafter, stating that Smt. Satpal Kaur had not given him notice to pay rent. Moreover, DW1 stated in his cross examination that apart from present suit, no other legal heir of Smt. Harbans Kaur had filed a suit for recovery of rent or possession of suit property.

23. The Ld. counsel for plaintiff further contended that in the suit filed by defendant against plaintiff for permanent injunction, for Page no. 10 11 restraining the plaintiff (defendant therein) from forcibly dispossessing the defendant (plaintiff therein) from the tenanted premises, without due process of law, defendant (plaintiff therein) has pleaded that after the death of erstwhile owner Smt. Harbans Kaur defendant (plaintiff therein) paid rent to the plaintiff (defendant therein), thereby, admitting landlord tenant relationship between the parties. DW­1 also stated in his cross­examination stated that the cheques given by him towards rent were encashed as is evident from his statement of account Ex.DW1/1. It was further alleged that the defendant attorned to plaintiff and paid rent to him hence defendant cannot challenge the title of plaintiff/ his landlord in view of Section 116 of Indian Evidence Act which provides as follows:­ "No tenant of immovable property, or person claiming through such tenant, shall during the continuance of the tenancy, be permitted to deny that the landlord of such tenant had, at the beginning of the tenancy, a title to such immovable property; and no person who came upon any immovable property by the licence of the person in possession thereof, shall be permitted to deny that such person had a title to such possession at the time when such licence was given."

24. I accordingly, hold that the contentions raised by the defendant that plaintiff was not the exclusive owner, does not hold ground as defendant/ DW1 stated in his cross­examination that after the death of late Smt. Harbans Kaur, he paid rent to the plaintiff. Section 116 of the Indian Evidence Act creates estoppel against such a tenant who had attorned to the plaintiff, as such he cannot challenge the title of the plaintiff/landlord to whom he was paying the rent. Thus, there Page no. 11 12 was an admission of landlord , tenant relationship between the parties by the defendant. Moreover, plaintiff had been receiving rent for himself and not on behalf of any other person and he (defendant) attorned to plaintiff, the defendant thus, cannot raise a plea that title did not vest in the plaintiff and that too when defendant had been paying rent to the plaintiff.

25. Moreover, the present proceedings are for eviction of tenant and defendant does not have the locus standi to challenge the genuineness of Will or bequests made therein not being a heir of testator.

26.Thus, issue no.1 is decided in favour of plaintiff and against the defendant.

27.ISSUE NO. 2 & 3 being connected hence taken together:­ The onus to prove both these issues was upon the plaintiff. PW­1 deposed that his mother Smt. Harbans lal was the owner of the property No. B­6, Daya Nand Colony, Lajpat Nagar­IV, New Delhi­24 vide Conveyance deed executed in her favour by President of India through its officer duly registered which is Ex.PW­1/1. He further stated that defendant became month to month tenant. He further stated that Smt. He Page no. 12 13 also stated that his sisters namely Amarjit Kaur, Ms. Gurcharan Kaur and Ms. Daljit Kaur had relinquished their share in the property No. B­6, Dayanand Colony, New Delhi in faovur of plaintiff vide relinquishment deed Ex.PW1/9. After the death of Smt. Harbans Kaur the plaintiff got the property mutated in his name. The mutation letter was proved as Ex.PW­1/4. Plaintiff further stated that he handed over the copy of the Will to the defendant and defendant attorned to him and became the month to month tenant under him in respect of suit property at a monthly rent of Rs. 25,000/­ w.e.f. 01.12.10. In his cross­examination he stated that after the death of his mother no partition took place between the legal heirs. He also stated that he did not apply for letters of administration or probate. He denied that abovesaid property was ancestral. He also denied that Will was a forged and fabricated document and the signatures of his mother and witness Smt. Satpal Kaur on the same were forged. He also denied that relinquishment deed as Ex.PW­1/9 was a forged and fabricated document.

28.PW­2 Smt. Amarjit Kaur and PW­3 Daljit Kaur corroborated the testimony of plaintiff PW­1 as regards relinquishment deed executed in his favour. PW2 and 3 also stated that the Will was a genuine document. PW­2 deposed in her cross­examination that Will was signed by attesting witnesses in the office of Registrar. She further stated that her mother had also signed the Will in the office of Registrar. She denied that the signature of her mother on the Will and attesting witness Smt. Satpal Kaur on the Will were forged. PW­3 denied in her cross­examination that her mother was not aware of the contents of the Will. She also stated that her mother signed the Will in the office of Sub­Registrar.

29.On the other hand DW­2 stated that the Will was a forged document. He also testified that plaintiff had not obtained the probate or letter of administration of the Will. He also deposed that after the death of Smt. Harbans Kaur, plaintiff was collecting rent from him and he paid rent to plaintiff w.e.f December 2010 to May 2011 . He also stated that Smt. Harbans Kaur vide oral lease agreement extended his tenancy for 5 years w.e.f. 01.06.09 to 13.04.014 @ Rs. 25,000/­. He also stated that plaintiff had demanded enhancement of rent from Rs. 25,000/­ to Rs. 30,000/­ but as he refused, the plaintiff threatened to dispossess him. DW­2 also stated that Smt. Harbans kaur entered into an oral lease agreement with defendant w.e.f. 01.06.2009 to 31.05.2014.

30. As regards the contention of the defendant that the Will was forged and fabricated document I have held that tenant not being a legal heir of deceased landlord has no locus standi to challenge the Will. Thus the contention of the defendant that the Will was a forged document is rejected.

31. As regards the relationship of landlord and tenant between the parties, Ld. counsel for plaintiff contended that defendant had filed a suit for permanent injunction against the plaintiff where defendant (plaintiff therein) admitted the relationship of landlord and tenant between the parties and had stated that after the death of Smt. Harbans Kaur defendant (plaintiff therein) was regularly paying the rent to plaintiff (defendant therein).

32. It is also pertinent to note that DW­1 admitted in his cross­ examination that after the death of Smt. Harbans Kaur he used to pay the rent to the plaintiff in respect of tenanted premises. Section 116 of the Indian Evidence Act creates estoppel against such a tenant Page no. 14 15 who attorned to the plaintiff, thus, in view of Section 116 of Evidence Act, a tenant cannot raise a plea that title of the suit premises did not vest in the landlord/plaintiff since he was paying rent to the landlord/plaintiff.

33.Moreover, a notice terminating the tenancy was served on the defendant. DW1 stated in his cross examination that notice was served upon him which is DW1/13. It was the case of the plaintiff that initially the defendant was inducted as a tenant vide written lease agreement dated 08.09.2006 for 22 months and after the expiry of the same the tenancy was renewed for six months vide registered lease agreement which came to an end by efflux of time on 31.05.09. The defendant thereafter, became month to month tenant. On the other hand DW1 had stated in his cross examination that the lease was renewed in 2008 but it was not renewed thereafter, though he was allowed he was allowed to use the suit property on payment of monthly rent of Rs.25000/­ per month. Thus, it is an admitted case of parties that there was no written lease agreement w.e.f. 01.06.09 between Smt. Harbans Kaur and the defendant, but the suit premises was let out to the defendant under oral agreement w.e.f. 01.06.09. The defendant accordingly became a month to month tenant, whose tenancy was terminated by giving a notice under section 106 of the Transfer Property Act (hereinafter referred to as T P Act).

34. Section 107 of the Transfer of Property Act provides that a lease of immovable property from year to year or exceeding one year can only be by a registered instrument. Section 17 (d) of the Registration Act 1908 makes it mandatory for a lease from year to year or exceeding one year to be executed by registered document only. In the absence of the same, tenancy would be treated as month to month which can Page no. 15 16 be determined by notice under section 106 of Transfer of Property Act. In the present case there is no registered lease deed w.e.f. 01.06.09, as such the plaintiff in accordance with the provisions of Section 106 of Transfer of Property Act, gave a notice to the defendant terminating his tenancy. The defendant stated in his cross examination that he has received the notice PW1/13 thus, the lease of defendant stood validly terminated vide a notice Ex. PW1/13. The contention of the defendant was that Smt. Harbans Kaur had orally agreed to renew the lease w.e.f. 01.06.09 to 31.09.14 and had obtained his signature on blank paper for executing the written lease agreement, but in his cross examination DW1 has stated that his lease was not renewed after 31.05.2009. Thus, I am in the view that the contention of the defendant that the lease was orally renewed cannot be accepted as a fresh lease could not have been automatically created without a registered instrument, as there is no provision in the TP Act or registration Act for automatic renewal of tenancy. For foregoing reasons I hold that there exists no tenancy after the notice of termination was given by plaintiff/ landlord.

35. Thus, the issues No. 2 & 3 are accordingly decided in favour of the plaintiff and against the defendant.

36.ISSUE No.4

4. Whether the plaintiff is entitled to recover rent @ Rs. 25,000/­ per month from the defendant for the month of May and June, 2011? OPP

37. PW­1 stated that he handed over the copy of the Will of his mother to defendant who attorned to him and became a month to month tenant in the suit property under him @ Rs. 25,000/­ per month. He further stated that defendant paid rent w.e.f. 01.12.2010 to Page no. 16 17 31.12.2010 and 01.01.2011 to 31.12.2011 by cash for which vouchers were issued. Thereafter, defendant paid rent vide account payee cheques w.e.f. 01.02.2011 to 28.02.2011, 01.03.2011 to 31.03.2011 and 01.04.2011 to 30.04.2011 but failed to pay rent w.e.f. 01.05.2011. In his cross­examination PW­1 stated that after the death of his mother he used to receive rent from the defendant @ Rs. 25,000/­ per month. He denied that after the death of his mother he received rent @ Rs. 25,000/­ from the defendant till May, 2011.

38. On the other hand, DW­1 stated that he had paid rent by cheque till May 2011. In this regard he proved his statement of account Ex. DW­1/1. As regards proof of payment, it is crystal clear from the statement of account of defendant that he had paid rent for the month of February 2011 to April 2011 by cheques and rent for the month of May and June 2011 @ Rs. 25,000 was due and payable by him. As per Ex.DW­1/1 one cheque for Rs. 25,000 issued in favour of plaintiff was encashed on 21.02.2011, thereafter a cheque issued in favour of plaintiff for Rs. 25,000/­ was encashed on 08.04.2011. DW­1 also deposed that he gave rent for December 2010 and January 2011 by cash and thereafter, he gave three cheques to plaintiff, the encashment of same was shown in his statement of account Ex. DW1/1. In his cross examination he stated that he was running a proprietorship concern and used to maintain books of accounts wherein income and expenditure of firm was shown. He also stated that he had shown the rent paid in the expenditure accounts, but the accounts books were not produced. DW­1 further stated that he did not remember the period for which he gave three cheques to plaintiff. Thus, from the testimony of DW­1 and the statement of account of defendant it is apparent defendant had only paid rent till April, 2011 Page no. 17 18 and rent for the month of May and June, 2011 was due and payable by defendant, issue no.4 is accordingly decided in favour of plaintiff and against the defendant.

39.ISSUE No. 5

5. Whether the plaintiff is entitled to claim damages from the defendant from 01.07.2011 till recovery of the possession, if so, at what rate? OPP.

40.The onus to prove this issue was upon the plaintiff. PW­1 stated that after the expiry of 15 days from the date of service of legal notice, as defendant failed to vacate the suit premises, he is liable to pay damages for use and occupation @ Rs.50,000/­ per month w.e.f. 01.07.2011 till he hands over the vacant and peaceful possession of the suit premises to him. PW­1 denied in his cross­examination that the tenancy was renewed w.e.f. 01.06.09 to 31.05.2014 as such, the defendant was not liable to pay damages or interest thereon.

41.On the other hand, DW­1 stated that after the expiry of lease deed dated 22.12.08 an oral agreement was executed between Smt. Harbans Kaur and defendant w.e.f. 01.06.09 to 31.05.14 for a period of of 5 years @ 25,000 per month. He further stated that as period of tenancy had not expired at the time of filing of the suit, plaintiff had no right to terminate his tenancy or claim damages for use and occupation @ Rs. 50,000/­ per month or the interest thereon. In his cross­examination DW­1 stated that he had not filed any document to show that lease was renewed w.e.f 2009.

42. Admittedly the lease agreement dated 22.12.08 came to an end by efflux of time on 31.05.2009 and thereafter, the tenancy was orally renewed. The defendant thus, become month to month tenant.

Page no. 18 19

43. Thus, there is no registered lease agreement between Smt. Harbans Kaur and defendant regarding renewal of the lease after 31.05.2009. Section 17 (1) (d) and Section 49 of the Registration Act provide that "whenever tenancy is created for a term exceeding one year or reserving yearly rent, it can only be by a registered instrument". Thus, as it is an admitted case of parties that there was no written lease deed between parties after the expiry of the lease on 31.05.09. The defendant become month to month tenant and his tenancy was determined by giving 15 days notice under section 106 of Transfer of Property Act. The service of notice was not denied by defendant.

44. Section 106 of T P Act is as follows:­

106. Duration of certain leases in absence of written contract or local usage. ­(1) In the absence of a contract or local law or usage to the contrary, a lease of immovable property for agricultural or manufacturing purposes shall be deemed to be a lease from year toyer, terminable, on the part of either lessor or lessee, by six month's notice; and a lease of immovable property for any other purpose shall be deemed to be a lease from month to month, terminable, on the part of either lessor or lessee, by fifteen day's notice. (2) Notwithstanding anything contained in any other law for the time being in force, the period mentioned in sub­section (1) shall commence from the date of receipt of notice.

(3) A notice under sub­section (1) shall not be deemed to be invalid merely because the period mentioned therein falls short of the period specified under that sub­section, where a suit or proceedings is filed after the expiry of the period mentioned in that sub­section. (4) Every notice under sub­section (1) must be in writing, signed by or on behalf of the person giving it, and either be sent by post to the party Page no. 19 20 who is intended to be bound by it or be tendered or delivered personally to such party or to one of his family or servants at his residence, or (if such tender or delivery is not practicable) affixed to a conspicuous part of property.

(1) The following documents shall be registered, if the property to which they relate is situate in a district in which, and if they have been executed on or after the date on which, Act XVI of 1864, or the Indian Registration Act, 1866, or the Indian Registration Act 1871 or the Indian Registration Act, 1877, or this Act came or come into force, namely,­­

(d) Leases of immovable property from year to year, or for any term exceeding one year, or reserving a yearly rent;

Section 49 of Registration Act ­ Effect of non registration of documents required to be registered­ No document required by section 17 [or by any provision of the Transfer of property Act, 1882,] to be registered shall­

(a) affect any immovable property comprised therein, or

(b) confer any power to adopt, or

46. It is pertinent to note that Section 17 (1) (d) of the Registration Act makes it mandatory that lease deed is to be registered in case it is for for one year or more, and Section 49 of the Registration Act bars the receiving in evidence of a document which is required to be registered under section 17 (1) (d) of the Registration Act, but not Page no. 20 21 registered. In the present case there is no registered lease deed to show the renewal of lease between Smt. Harbans Kaur and defendant w.e.f. 01.06.09 to 31.05.2014 as such the defendant was a month to month tenant, whose tenancy was determined by giving a notice U/s 106 of T P Act. Thus, the contention of defendant that Smt. Harbans Kaur orally agreed to execute a fresh lease agreement w.e.f. 01.06.09 to 31.05.2014 is without merits as a fresh lease cannot be automatically created without a written instrument duly registered.

47. Plaintiff has claimed damages @ Rs. 50,000/­ per month, however, no evidence has been led by the plaintiff in this regard.

48. As regards assessment of mesne profits, it has been held in M/s Roshaan Lal Vegetable Vs. M/s Param International & Ors, CS (OS) No. 59/2006 decided on 20th January 2011, that " for assessing mesne profits judicial notice can be taken on prevailing rents and that an element of guess work is always involved". It was further held that that "the court must assess mesne profits at a figure which closely resembles the rate at which the premises in question could have been let or the rate at which the lessee could have hired similar premises, i.e., prevalent market rate of rent."

49. Thus, after plaintiff adduces is evidence regarding the prevailing rate, judicial notice can be taken of the same and the burden thereof, shifts on the defendant to establish his version as to the prevailing rent to the case.

50. However, in the present case plaintiff did not file any sample lease deed of similar premises of his area. As regards assessment of mesne profits, it has also been held in RFA No. 441/2000 decided on 31st May 2005, titled M/s The National Radio & Electronic Co. Ltd. Vs. Motion Pictures Association as follows:­ Page no. 21 22 " Judicial notice is taken of only such facts of which there can be only one view. In the light of the aforesaid position in law, there can be no manner of doubt that so far as the increase of rent is concerned, judicial notice can be taken of a fact that over a certain period rents generally have arisen. However, so far as quantification of the rate at which the increase has actually taken place, a finding can be arrived at only on the basis of legal evidence and material establishing the actual rates at which properties have been let over the period and comparison of such properties with the property which is the subject matter of lis. Rents may vary based on location of properties, nature of construction, period of construction, purpose/user for which the premises are let, variation between demand for tenanted premises and availability of premises and even factors relating to the position of the economy. Therefore, while a learned trial judge may be justified in taking judicial notice of the fact that rents have risen over a certain period of time in the area in question, it would be incumbent upon a person laying a claim of entitlement to mesne profits to prove the same by cogent and reliable evidence in accordance with law.

51. As has been noticed in the judicial pronouncements noticed herein above, the evidence may include documentary evidence relating to tenancies of properties in the area which can be summoned from the office of the sub­Registrar or by examination of property dealers, oral testimony of the parties to the litigation and persons in the trade of real estate, other property owners.

52. The defendant/ tenant is entitled to know not only the landlord's claim but also the extent to which the same stands established. The defendant has a legal right to an opportunity to prove the contrary.

53. Plaintiff failed to lead any evidence. No list of witnesses even was filed Page no. 22 23 and the only witness who was sought to be produced as a witness, was being examined to prove the valuation of the property. The respondent­plaintiff miserably failed to discharge the onus and burden of proof cast on it in accordance with law.

54. In this background of the factual position and the law, despite taking judicial notice of the fact that rentals have generally increased in Delhi there was no material at all on which the claim of the plaintiff could have been granted by the learned trial judge.

55. We find that the learned trial judge has observed that it could take judicial notice of the prevalent market rats of rent of different areas in Delhi as the "court has routinely being deciding" similar suits of mesne profits and damages in respect of different areas.

56. We have no manner of doubt that these observations are totally contrary to the settled position in law. The extent to which the rents are actually rising in specific areas has to be proved as a statement of fact in every case. A party would need to lead evidence in respect of the period over which the rental has increased and also the percentage for which it has increased in order to enable a court to arrive at a finding of the actual prevalent market rate of rent at the given point of time with regard to a particular property. A finding of fact in a litigation binds only the parties to the lis.

57. It is also necessary that a defendant/ tenant is put to notice with regard to the basis and claim on which a relief is being ascertained. The defendant must know the rate which a plaintiff will be able to prove in its evidence so that it can lead its evidence in defence to the claim.

58. In the instant case the plaintiff did not lead any evidence and the defendant was thereby deprived of an opportunity to prove that the Page no. 23 24 prevalent market rate of rent was not Rs. 30/­ per sq. ft. per month which is the rate arrived at by the learned trial judge in its conclusions. We hold that this finding of the learned trial judge is based on no material whatsoever, is speculative and presumptuous and has no basis in fact.

59. Thus, as plaintiff has not lead any evidence to show the rate at which similar premises in the area were let out or that there was increase of rental. Thus, in the absence of cogent evidence and material on record, the claim of plaintiff for damages @ Rs. 50,000/­ per month cannot be accepted. I accordingly, grant the mesne profit at the same rate at which the premises had been let out to the defendants i.e. Rs. 25,000/­ per month w.e.f 01.07.2011 till the defendant hands over the vacant and peaceful possession of the suit premises to the plaintiff. The security amount of Rs. 18,000/­ paid by defendant, is liable to be adjusted. The contentions of defendants that he paid Rs. 50,000/­ as security at the time of renewal of the lease, does not stand proved as PW­1 denied in his cross examination that defendant paid Rs. 50,000/­ as security for execution of fresh lease. In this regard DW­1 stated in his cross examination that he had not shown the payment of Rs. 50,000/­ in books of accounts, thus, payment of Rs. 50,000/­ does not stand proved. Plaintiff is entitled to interest @ 9 % per annum on mesne profit from the date of accrual of the same till the date of payment.
