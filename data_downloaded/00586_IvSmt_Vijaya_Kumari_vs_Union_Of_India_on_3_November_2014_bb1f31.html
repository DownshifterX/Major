JUDGMENT

AWARD REFERENCE UNDER SECTION 18 OF THE LAND ACQUISITION ACT

1. Vide this award, I shall dispose of the instant reference under section 18 of the L. A. Act preferred by the petitioners against the respondents being aggrieved with the quantum of compensation awarded by the LAC in lieu of the acquisition of the land of the petitioners. 

LAC-07/13                                                Page:-2/14
   2. That   vide   Notification   No.   F.11(23)/98/L&B/LA/6085      dated

24.07.1998 U/s 4 of the Land Acquisition Act followed by the declaration vide notification No. F. 11(23)/98/L&B/LA/ 6749 dated 04.08.1998 Under section 6 of the LA Act, the land of the petitioner bearing Khasra No. 327, 12/1, 12/2, 12/3, 328, 12/1 and 12/2 situated in the revenue estate of village Wazirabad, Delhi was acquired for setting up of Dairy Colony Yojna by the Government and possession of the same was taken by Govt. The Land Acquisition Collector, after completing all the formalities as provided under the Act announced the award bearing No. 06/LAC/CL/2000 on 03.08.2000 and awarded the compensation @ Rs. 11.20 Lacs per acre apart from granting statutory benefits.

3. The reference petition U/s 18 of LAC Act was filed by petitioner wherein it was stated that petitioner is the owner/ bhumidhar in possession of the land bearing khasra No. 327, 12/1, 12/2, 12/3, 328, 12/1 and 12/2 total land measuring 48 bighas 12 biswas (1/8th share) situated in the revenue estate of village Wazirabad, Delhi. It was also averred that nobody else has any right, title or interest to receive the compensation of the land mentioned above except the petitioner. It was also stated that the setting up a dairy is not a public purpose in the eyes of law and setting up a dairy in the vicinity of village Wazirabad, Delhi which is near to river Yamuna will cause more pollution to the river. It was pleaded in reference petition that the LAC has assessed the market value of the land at too low and inadequate rates and while assessing the market value of the land, the LAC has not taken into consideration that the land of the petitioner was fully developed which can easily be converted into residential-cum-commercial colony as the entire LAC-07/13 Page:-3/14 area adjacent to the land of the petitioner is fully developed area having residential-cum-commercial complexes. It was also stated in petition that the LAC has assessed the market value of the land as an agricultural land, altogether ignoring the fact that the entire area had acquired commercial and residential, potentiality as the land of the petitioner is located in one of the posh colonies of Delhi. It was also stated that the land of the petitioner is located on main road and are surrounded by posh colonies and have acquired commercial/ residential potentiality and should be assessed as residential-cum-commercial land for the purpose of assessing the market value. It was further averred in petition that the land of the petitioner enjoy all amenities and facilities of life viz. availability of electricity, metaled road, petrol pump, telephone, historical places, etc. and nearness to a schools and colleges and hospitals, the petitioner's land should have been assessed accordingly and the LAC should have assessed the market value of the land on the basis of the commercial value of the land. Hence, present reference petition was filed by petitioner.

4. Written statement was filed by Union of India wherein preliminary objections were raised stating that petition of the claimant is hopelessly barred by time. It was also stated that despite service of all statutory notices on the petitioner, he failed to file the reference petition in the prescribed period. It was also stated that petition is liable to be dismissed for non-joinder of necessary party. On merits, it was denied that notification and the award is either illegal or malafide or bad in law. It was also denied that compensation assessed by LAC in respect of the land acquired is either inadequate or that it does not represent the true market LAC-07/13 Page:-4/14 value of the land as on the date of notification U/s 4 (1) of the L. A. Act. It was stated that compensation amount of Rs. 19,96,307/- has already been paid to the petitioner on 17.08.2001 by LAC (N) Delhi as per the statement U/s 19 of the L A Act. It was also stated that LAC while assessing the market value of the land and awarding the compensation has considered all the relevant factors and the compensation assessed and awarded by the LAC is just, sufficient, reasonable, legal and proper and does not call for any enhancement or interference. The other contents of reference petition were denied and it was prayed that reference petition under reply be dismissed with costs.

5. Written statement was also filed on behalf of MCD in which preliminary objections were raised stating that petition is barred by period of limitation and is barred by the provisions of Section 477/478 of the DMC Act, 1957. It was also stated that petition is bad for mis-joinder and non-joinder of the necessary parties. On merits, it was stated that petition pertains to DDA and not the answering respondent / MCD as the acquired land was handed over to the DDA, hence MCD does not play any role in the present matter. It was also stated that compensation awarded by the LAC is legal, adequate, just and correct. The other contents of petition were denied and it was stated that reference petition of the petitioner is liable to be dismissed.

6. Vide order dated 17.12.2008, Ld. Predecessor of this Court in view of the submissions as made in the Written Statement filed by MCD impleaded the DDA as necessary and proper party and being beneficiary of the acquired land and MCD was deleted from the array of parties.

7. Thereafter, none appeared on behalf of DDA, hence right to file written statement by DDA was closed vide order dated 22.12.2008. Similarly, none appeared for DDA for leading evidence, hence evidence on behalf of DDA was also closed vide order dated 23.12.2008.

8. On the pleadings of the parties, the following issues have been framed by this Court on 16.12.2008:-

1). What was the market value of the land in question at the time of issuance of notification U/s 4 of the LA Act? Onus on parties.
2). Whether the petitioner is entitled to enhancement in compensation, if so, to what amount? OPP
3). Relief

9. In petitioner's evidence, the counsel for petitioner appeared as witness and tendered the copy of judgment dated 30.07.2007 passed by Hon'ble Court in LAC No. 342/1/06 titled as Ram Pal Singh & Ors. Vs UOI and same was marked as Mark-A. Thereafter, evidence of the petitioner was closed.

10.In defence, the counsel for the respondent /Union of India tendered the copy of the award No. 06/LAC/CL/2000 pertaining to village Wazirabad, Delhi and same was Ex. R-1 and the statement Under Section 19 of the LA Act was Ex. R-2.

11.Vide judgment dated 24.12.2008, the Ld. Predecessor of this Court held that reference petition filed by the petitioner is time barred and petitioner is not entitled to any enhancement in compensation of the land in question.

12.Thereafter, being aggrieved with the judgment dated 24.12.2008, the petitioner herein preferred the appeal before Hon'ble High Court of Delhi which was registered as LA APP No. 383/09 and said LAC-07/13 Page:-6/14 appeal was decided by Hon'ble High Court of Delhi vide judgment dated 16.05.2013 and the order dated 24.12.2008 was set aside and the matter was remanded back to this court for framing an issue on the point of limitation, to give opportunity to the parties to lead evidence on said issue and to pass an appropriate order in accordance with law.

13.In terms of the order and directions of Hon'ble High Court, the matter was assigned to this court being successor court by Ld. District and Session Judge, Delhi and in view of the directions of Hon'ble High Court, the following issue was framed vide order dated 02.08.2013:-

1). Whether the reference U/s 18 LAC filed by the petitioner is time barred?OPP

14.Hence, issues framed earlier vide order dated 16.12.2008 and thereafter, vide order dated 02.08.2013 are hereby merged and are given below:-

1) Whether the reference U/s 18 LAC filed by the petitioner is time barred?OPP
2) What was the market value of the land in question at the time of issuance of notification U/s 4 of the LA Act? Onus on parties.
3) Whether the petitioner is entitled to enhancement in compensation, if so, to what amount? OPP
4) Relief

15.Thereafter, matter was directed to be listed for evidence.

16.In petitioner evidence, Kartar Singh was examined as PW-1 and also relied upon documents. Opportunity was granted for his cross-examination which was not availed, hence PW-1 was discharged and opportunity to cross-examine the witness was LAC-07/13 Page:-7/14 closed vide order dated 12.09.2013.

17.Thereafter, counsel for DDA stated that he wishes to adopt the DE led by Union of India on 23.12.2008 before the case was decided earlier and does not wish to lead any other evidence after remanding back of case by Hon'ble High Court. Hence, statement of counsel for DDA was recorded to that effect and DE of DDA was closed, however none appeared for Union of India and accordingly, Union of India was proceeded ex-parte vide order dated 12.12.2013. An application was also moved by DDA for review the order dated 12.09.2013 and for recalling the PW-1 and said application was also allowed subject to cost.

18.Thereafter, petitioner PW-1 passed away. The Lrs of petitioner were directed to be brought on record.

19.The matter was posted for RE, however no RW was produced despite opportunity, hence RE was closed vide order dated 27.05.2014 and matter was posted for final arguments.

20.Thereafter application was moved by respondent No. 2/ DDA U/s 114 and order 47 Rule 1 and 2 CPC for review and recalling of order dated 27.05.2014 and said application was allowed subject to cost vide order dated 30.07.2014 and Sh. T. Minz, Kanoongo from LAC was examined as R2W1 and was cross-examined by counsel for petitioner and thereafter RE was closed and matter was directed to listed for final arguments.

21.I have gone through the record and heard the argument of parties.

22.At the very outset, in terms of directions of Hon'ble High Court of Delhi, the following issue is required to be decided as framed vide order dated 02.08.2013:-

23.Issue No. 1. Whether the reference U/s 18 LAC filed by the LAC-07/13 Page:-8/14 petitioner is time barred?OPP:- The onus to prove this issue was on the petitioner. The petitioner in order to prove this issue has led his evidence and has produced himself in witness box. The PW-1 in para No. 9 of his affidavit has stated that LAC in his reference report has stated that notice of the said award dated 30.05.2001 U/s 12 (2) of the L.A. Act was sent to the deponent/PW-1 and same was served on 02.06.2001 but the said notice was never served upon him. In para No. 10 of his affidavit he further stated that no process server alongwith said notice came to him and no other person informed him about the notice of award. PW-1 in his affidavit further stated at the time of alleged service of notice of award he was living separately from his sons and had relied upon copy of Ration Card Mark X. On the other hand, R2W1, Sh. T. Minz, Kanoongo, from LAC has appeared in witness box and has relied upon notice U/s 12 (2) of LAC Act. 1984 dated 30.05.2001. In his cross-examination, he has stated that there is no other document on recod regarding the service of notice dated 30.05.2001. He further stated that there is no report of the Server of said notice on record with regard to service of said notice upon petitioner. He further stated that there is nothing on record which may bring out the fact of alternative service upon Mr. Ajit in place of petitioner namely Kartar Singh. He also stated that there is no noting on record about the presence or absence of the petitioner Kartar Singh from /at his residence at the time of effecting the service of notice. The PW-1 was not cross-examined and was discharged accordingly. Later on application for recalling of PW-1 was moved by respondent and said application was also allowed. However, unfortunately, the PW-1/petitioner expired and LAC-07/13 Page:-9/14 his testimony remained uncross-examined and unchallenged. The respondent has mainly relied upon service of notice U/s 12 (2) of LA Act, however the petitioner in his affidavit has denied the service of same. No other document has been proved by respondent except the abovesaid notice. The Hon'ble High Cout in the order dated 16.05.2013, while remanding the present reference, has stated that as per Section 45 of the L. A. Act, a notice must ordinary be served on the person concerned and it is only when the person cannot be found that the service can be made on an adult male member of his family residing with him. In the present case, the petitioner has relied upon the copy of ration card in which it is clearly shown that the petitioner was living separately from his sons. No contrary evidence has been produced on record by respondent. Hence, in view of the evidence of parties and documents available on record, it is held that the notice U/s 12 (2) of L. A. Act was not properly served on the petitioner. It is not proved that petitioner was present at the time of award and secondly notice was not served. In these circumstances, when it is clear that neither notice was served nor he was present then I find support from judgments i.e. 1988 Rajdhani Law Report 224 titled Bharat Chand Dilwali Vs. UOI, AIR 1989 Gujarat 56 titled Gopalbhai Becharbhai Vs. State of Gujarat & Anr. and 2005 VII AD (SC) 665 Parsottambhai Maganbhai Patel Vs. State of Gujarat through Deputy Collector Modasa & Anr. It is crystal clear from these judgments that six months is time limit to file reference from the date of award and the time for calculation starts from date of knowledge. Rather superior courts have also stated that delay can also be condoned. Hence LAC-07/13 Page:-10/14 reference petition is held to be within limitation, if counted from date of knowledge of petitioner qua acquisition in present proceedings. This issue is decided in favour of petitioners and against the respondents.

24.Now when it has been proved that reference petition was filed within limitation as observed above, hence issue-wise finding is also required to be given on the issues framed vide order dated 16.12.2008 for deciding market value of the land in question at the time of issuance of Notification U/s 4 of LA Act and enhancement of the compensation which are given below:-

25.Issue No. 2. What was the market value of the land in question at the time of issuance of notification U/s 4 of the LA Act? Onus on parties and Issue No. 3. Whether the petitioner is entitled to enhancement in compensation, if so, to what amount? OPP:- Both these issues are taken up together as they are inter-connected. The onus to prove both these issues was on the petitioner. The petitioner having been aggrieved by the compensation awarded by the LAC sought enhancement through the reference made and have put forth a number of grounds on which it claims to be entitled to the enhanced compensation. Even slight disturbance or variation in routine unsettles a person and that too depends upon the degree of impact and vulnerability of the individual, the actual impact, thus, may even be devastating. In these circumstances, unsettling someone alongwith the entire family with habitat and hearth lock, stock and barrel can very well be imagined especially when establishment is stable and running for quite sometime. The proximity not only breeds contempt but also develops into LAC-07/13 Page:-11/14 fascination, bondings and different types of relations between the individual, and at times encompasses in itself, the relationship between the humans animals and even the stones and structures.

One may, more often than not, feel the pain of leaving particular abode, locality, city or country. Against this backdrop, unsettling someone from his abode is, without doubt, disturbing if not devastating but then it has to be coped up depending upon the circumstance especially when it is supported by the Legislation (may be through a more than hundred years old 'colonial' legislation, not much amended, retaining its basic features) and when there is no escape or no way out and has limited rather no option to exercise but to the line or so to say that no option whatsoever except the one thrusted upon by circumstances. The requirement of setting up of Dairy Colony Yojna was the need of the hour and the creation of the same, armed and empowered with the Will of Government to remove all possible obstacles with all the might of the state in order to have smooth passage/path/road in place at the earliest in the City of Delhi resulted into acquisition of land even in thickly populated urban landscape under the Land Acquisition Act, 1894. In such scenario those who were displaced could have only consoled themselves that they got suitable compensation, which may compensate them to the extent possible though at times there cannot be a compensation for such situations.

In the present case, the petitioners have themselves only relied upon photocopy of certified copy of the judgment dated 30.07.2007 passed by Ld. Predecessor of this Court in LAC No. 342/1/06 titled Ram Pal Singh & Ors. Vs. UOI and same LAC-07/13 Page:-12/14 was marked as Mark A. The above said judgment was pertaining to same village which was acquired vide the same notification and award. Since this is a covered matter, so any further adjudication is not required and resjudicata applies. Issue No. 2 is already covered under the judgment supra which findings of Hon'ble Court are binding on this court. This court cannot differ from the findings of its Ld. Predecessor as Ld. Predecessor of this Court has already dealt with the issue pertaining to same notification under same award and the enhancement of compensation has already been decided pertaining to same village and under same award and notification. Furthermore, it is settled law that where a particular compensation is given in respect of a particular land then the same amount has to be given in respect of other land acquired under the same award. There exists no ground to decline the enhancement of compensation in the present case also. The petitioner is entitled to the same benefit of enhancement which is given in another matter as the property in question is situated just adjacent to the property involved in above case decided by Ld. Predecessor of this Court as both the properties were acquired under the same notification and award. Hence, both these issues are decided in favour of petitioners and against the respondent.

26.Relief:- In view of the above findings herein above, the reference is answered. The petitioners are entitled to increase @ 11.5% p.a. i.e. Rs. 40,580.82 Paise per acre (Rs. 8,454.33 Paise per bigha) on the compensation amount i.e. Rs. 11,20,000/- per acre assessed by the LAC from the date of financial year i.e. 01.04.1998 till the date of acquisition of the land i.e. 24.07.1998 as per their shares mentioned against the khasra numbers mentioned in the LAC-07/13 Page:-13/14 statement U/s 19 of the LA Act and award. It is made clear that petitioners are only entitled to 1/8th share in property bearing khasra No. 327, 12/1, 12/2, 12/3, 328, 12/1 and 12/2 total land measuring 48 bighas 12 biswas situated in the revenue estate of village Wazirabad, Delhi. The petitioners shall also be entitled to get additional amount U/s 23 (1A) of L. A. Act @ 12% per annum on the said increase from the date of notification U/s 4 of L. A. Act till the date of award or dispossession, whichever is earlier. The petitioners shall also get solatium U/s 23 (2) of L. A. Act @ 30% on the said increase in compensation and interest U/s 28 of L. A. Act @ 9% per annum for the first year from the date of dispossession and @ 15% per annum on the difference between the enhanced compensation i.e. increase awarded by this court and the compensation awarded by LAC for the subsequent period till the payment. The petitioners are further entitled to interest on solatium and additional amount in terms of judgment of Hon'ble Apex Court titled Sunder Vs. UOI reported in DLT 2001 (SC)

569. This reference is answered accordingly. Copy of this judgment be sent to concerned LAC & DDA to make the payment of the enhanced amount of compensation to the petitioners within three months from today. While making calculation due regard shall be made to deduct the amount initially arrived at by LAC to avoid any duplication. There shall be no order as to costs. Decree sheet be prepared accordingly. File be consigned to record room.
