     APPLICATION FOR EVICTION OF TENANT UNDER SECTION 
                     14(1) (d) of DRC ACT


                                        JUDGMENT:

1. In short the facts are as under :

The petitioner is the landlord of the property bearing no. D­35, Rana Pratap Bagh, Delhi­110 007 wherein the respondent is the tenant on rent at the rate of Rs. 500/­ per month exclusive of other charges in the said property. The respondent is residing in US and the premises is lying locked. That originally one room was let out to the father of respondent namely Sh. Khem Raj Bhagat at the rate of Rs. 25/­ per month and another room at monthly rent of Rs. 30/­. After his death, the rent receipt was transferred in the name of his wife namely Smt. Raj Bhagat. That, after the death of Smt. Raj Bhagat the rent receipt was transferred in the name of her son namely Sh. Gursharan Raj Bhagat in the year 1997. After his death it was transferred in the name of the respondent Ms. Usha Dave. The rent is being remitted to the petitioner through ECS/SI scheme. That the premises was let out to the respondent for residential purpose but neither the respondent nor any of family members have been residing therein for period of more than E. No. 22/10 Ram Ratan Singh Namdhari vs. Usha Dave 2 3 six months immediately before filing of the present petition. The respondent has been residing at US and is permanently settled there with her family. That the respondent holds the passport of US. Further as per the bills raised by NDPL, the consumption charges for last six months is on average basis and there is zero calls made through MTNL connection. Hence, the present petition.

2. After service of the summons respondent appeared and filed the WS submitting therein that she is the tenant under the petitioner. The premises was let out for residential­cum­commercial purpose and the same is being used for the both purposes since inception of the tenancy. The respondent is aged about 76 years and is residing in the aforesaid property for the last many years. The respondent is also running the office of Suarashtra Foundation Trust wherein the petitioner was also the member initially. It has been denied that the premises is lying locked. She is living in US but keeps on visiting the tenanted premises specially in the month of January to celebrate the Republic Day. Her entire goods are lying in the tenanted premises. She has never abandoned the said tenanted premises. She E. No. 22/10 Ram Ratan Singh Namdhari vs. Usha Dave 3 4 is also doing various social service activities in India. She went to US on 28.08.2009. She was supposed to return on 29.11.2009. But, due to her medical condition and VISA problem she was not able to return till July 2010. Ultimately, she came back on 28.07.2010 and since then she is residing at the tenanted premises. The MTNL landline connection is still running but mostly remains out of order. Thus, it has been denied that she has abandoned the tenanted premises. Subsequently, certain new facts were incorporated by the respondent in her amended WS as discussed above which was allowed by the Ld. Predecessor vide order dt. 05.07.2012.

3. Thereafter, replication to the written statement of the respondent was filed on behalf of the petitioner denying the contents of WS and reiterating the contents of petition.

4. Thereafter, petitioner examined himself as PW1 who deposed on the lines of his petition & tendered his evidence by way of affidavit Ex. PW1/A. He further relied upon documents Ex.PW1/1 to Ex.PW1/12. He was thereafter cross­examined by the counsel for the respondent. PE was E. No. 22/10 Ram Ratan Singh Namdhari vs. Usha Dave 4 5 thereafter closed.

5. Thereafter, the respondent led her evidence by examining herself as RW1 by relying upon her affidavit Ex.RW1/A. She also relied upon documents Ex.RW1/1 to Ex.RW1/14. The documents Ex.RW1/6 and Ex.RW1/12 were marked as Mark­X1 and Mark­X2. She also relied upon documents marked as Mark­A to Mark­M and Mark­X. The respondent also examined RW2/the husband of respondent who supported the version of the respondent through his affidavit Ex.RW2/A.

6. I have heard learned counsel for the petitioner Ms. Savita Malhotra and Sh. Rajeev Chauhan, counsel for respondent and perused the record very carefully.

LANDLORD & TENANT RELATIONSHIP

7. The petitioner in the present case was firstly required to prove the relationship of landlord and tenant between him and the respondent. The respondent in her WS has not disputed the version of petitioner regarding E. No. 22/10 Ram Ratan Singh Namdhari vs. Usha Dave 5 6 the relationship. Though she denied that the petitioner is owner of tenanted premises. The respondent stated that the adoption deed of previous owner Late Sh. Atma Ram Singh has not been filed on record and thus, he cannot claim the ownership.

The issue of ownership of the tenanted premises is not relevant for the decision in the present case as the respondent has admitted that the petitioner is her landlord. The PW1 in his cross­examination was confronted with the rent receipt Ex.PW1/D1 to Ex.PW1/D9 produced by the respondent which he admitted to have his signatures. Thus the respondent who produced the said rent receipts herself admits that the petitioner is the landlord of the tenanted premises. Even otherwise, Section 116 of The Indian Evidence Act prohibits the tenant from questioning the title of the landlord. The landlord only has to show that he is more than a tenant, which is the case in hand. In these circumstances, the relationship of landlord and tenant is proved between the parties.

NON­OCCUPATION OF PREMISES

8. The only ground on which the petition has been filed by the E. No. 22/10 Ram Ratan Singh Namdhari vs. Usha Dave 6 7 petitioner is non­occupation of the tenanted premises by the respondent and her family members for the period of more than six months prior to the filing of the present petition. Thus, the petitioner was required to prove the following two ingredients of the said ground of eviction under Section 14 (1) (d) of DRC Act :

a. the tenanted premises being let out for use as residence b. the premises being not used for residence for period of six months immediately prior to the filing of the petition by the tenant or family members

9. The burden of proving the said two ingredients of ground of eviction was initially upon the petitioner who has examined himself as PW1 and has deposed on the lines of his version as stated in the petition. The PW1 in his testimony has specifically claimed that the tenanted premises was let out to the father of the respondent initially in the year 1968 for the residential purpose only. The rate of rent is Rs. 500/­ per month as of today. He further deposed that the respondent is resident of US and has not been keeping permanent abode in India. The respondent came to India on E. No. 22/10 Ram Ratan Singh Namdhari vs. Usha Dave 7 8 07.07.2009 and left the country shortly thereafter. She returned back only on 29.07.2010. The NDPL bills as well of MTNL both shows no consumption during the said period i.e. w.e.f. July 2009 to July 2010.

10. On the contrary, the respondent entered into witness box as RW1 by stating on oath that the premises was let out for residential­cum­ commercial purpose since the inception of tenancy. She is running the office of Saurashtra Foundation Trust from the tenanted premises. She further admitted that she is residing in US but frequently visits India specially in the month of January. She is doing various social activities in India. Further in April 2009 she had an accident and sustained injuries on nasal portion, neck and skull. Inspite of that she returned to India in July. On 28.08.2009 she again left for US and was supposed to return on 29.11.2009. But, she was not able to return due to medical problems detected during check­up in September 2009. She again booked her ticket for India on 10.01.2010. Again she was not allowed to board the flight due to missing OCI Certification on the passport. The said OCI Certificate was applied through Indian Embassy but the same was received in March 2010. Thereafter, she E. No. 22/10 Ram Ratan Singh Namdhari vs. Usha Dave 8 9 booked the ticket for 27.06.2010 but again she was not able to board the flight due to major dental surgery conducted in the first week of July. Ultimately, she returned back on 28.07.2010 and her absence was due to the reasons beyond her control.

11. Thus, the second ingredient of the ground of eviction under Section 14 (1) (d) of DRC Act of period of absence has been admitted by the respondent herself in both the pleadings and her evidence also. But, it was argued on behalf of the respondent that the case is not covered under Section 14 (1) (d) of DRC Act as the tenanted premises was not being exclusively used for residential purposes.

On the contrary, the Ld. counsel for the petitioner argued that the purpose of tenancy right from the inception was residential only.

The pleadings of the petitioner show the initially one room with bathroom and latrine was let out to the father of the respondent in the year 1968. Subsequently, another room was let out to the father of respondent. PW1 in his cross­examination denied the suggestion that Sh. Khem Raj was running his office of advocate from the tenanted premises. He stated that he E. No. 22/10 Ram Ratan Singh Namdhari vs. Usha Dave 9 10 was having his office at Karol Bagh. The electricity bill Ex.PW1/10 of the tenanted premises which is in the name of the father of the respondent shows it to be a domestic connection. Similarly, document Ex.RW1/P1 which is the legal notice issued by father of the respondent shows his office address to be different. The said legal notice was admitted by RW1 in her cross­examination to have been issued by her father. Though she stated that during that period her father was in the process of shifting. She further stated that her father shifted the tenanted premises in April 1968. On the contrary, the said notice Ex.RW1/P1 has been issued on 10.09.1968 after period of more than five months of his shifting. Thus, the version of the respondent that her father was using the tenanted premises for his office appears to be doubtful.

One another document which has been produced by the petitioner Ex.PW1/3 dt. 01.03.1993 which is the assessment of the entire property for purposes of house tax is also perused. This is the document prepared by the officials of MCD which shows the portion in possession of respondent as 'residential' only. Qua the said document, no question was put to PW1 in the entire cross­examination nor the authenticity of the said E. No. 22/10 Ram Ratan Singh Namdhari vs. Usha Dave 10 11 document was challenged. The document Ex.PW1/3 has been prepared by the official of MCD in discharge of his official duty recording the purpose of accommodation as residential with the respondent. It has to be taken as true keeping in view the presumption attached to it under Section 114 of the Indian Evidence Act.

12. The respondent, on the other hand, has relied upon the diary notings of her father Mark­B and one notice Ex.RW1/1 to show that her father was running his office from the tenanted premises. The said diary notings Mark­A and Mark­B are the copies which cannot be read into the evidence. Similarly, the RW1 in her cross­examination admitted that the notings are not in handwriting of her father. The originals of the same were never produced as they were stated to have been destroyed. No explanation has been given as to how the photocopy of the same remained with the respondent. Thus, the only admissible document produced by respondent to show the use of tenanted premises as advocate's office is Ex.RW1/1 which is the legal notice issued by father of respondent. The said legal notice is dt. 29.11.1981 which records the office as well as residence address that of E. No. 22/10 Ram Ratan Singh Namdhari vs. Usha Dave 11 12 tenanted premises. Apart from the said document, no material has been produced by the respondent to show that it was being used for commercial purposes by her father. The said document also appears to be doubtful as RW1 herself in her cross­examination admitted that she had taken her father to US in the year 1981. Thus, it cannot be presumed that the father of the respondent was using the premises as his office in the year 1981.

13. The other piece of evidence adduced by the respondent to show that the tenanted premises is being used for commercial purposes is the trust deed is Ex.RW1/2. As per RW1, the said trust is functioning from the tenanted premises. But, the RW1, herself in her cross­examination admitted the fact that the Saurashtra Foundation Trust does not carry out any commercial activity. The documents Mark­C, D and E produced by the respondent shows the address of the trust as that of tenanted premises only. Supposedly, the claim of the respondent is taken to be true, it is her admitted case that no commercial activity is being conducted by the trust constituted vide document Ex.RW1/2. Even for the sake of argument, if it is believed that the premises was being used collaterally for commercial purposes, it E. No. 22/10 Ram Ratan Singh Namdhari vs. Usha Dave 12 13 does not means the tenancy has become non­residential. Admittedly, the tenanted premises is the residential accommodation which is being used for residence and incidentally for trust activities.

14. Thus, it has been shown that the tenanted premises is being used only incidentally for running the trust and the dominant object is the residence only. In this regard judgment of Hon'ble Supreme Court in case titled as M/s. Precision Steel & Engg. Works vs. Prem Deva Niranjan Deva Tayal cited Appeal (Civil) 2227 of 2000 is relevant and the relevant para is reproduced herein :

"Premises are capable of being classified into residential and non­residential depending on the purpose of letting. This is the broad classification. Question of construction and determining the purpose of letting may pose difficulty when the premises are let for mixed, composite or dual purposes, i.e., where the entire premises are allowed to be used for an overlapping purpose or the premises forming subject­matter of one tenancy are allowed to be used for purposes more than one. In such a E. No. 22/10 Ram Ratan Singh Namdhari vs. Usha Dave 13 14 case it cannot be said that the premises would cease to be of either category,i.e., they would be neither residential nor non­ residential. Rather it would be necessary to find out what is the "main and dominant purpose" of letting as distinguished from "subsidiary, ancillary or incidental purpose". The theory of determining the purpose of letting by reference to finding out the main and dominant purpose of letting has ample judicial authority to derive support from. In Dr. Sewa Singh Vs. Smt. Revinder Kaur and Anr. 1970 RCJ 615 SC it was held that residential building will remain so even if it is used by a person engaged in one or more of the professions partly for his business or partly for his residence. The building in the occupation of the tenant was undoubtedly residential and on the evidence it was found that part of it was being used by the tenant, a medical practitioner for examining patients and prescribing medicines. "

In the present case in hand, it is admitted by both the parties the main and dominant purpose of letting was residential only though it might E. No. 22/10 Ram Ratan Singh Namdhari vs. Usha Dave 14 15 have been used incidentally, firstly, by the father of the respondent as his office and now by the respondent as office of her trust. The said incidental use does not change the character of the tenancy and it continued to remain residential only. In these circumstances, the first condition for invocation of ground under Section 14 (1) (d) of DRC Act is made out.

15. The second condition for proving which the burden was upon the petitioner itself to show that the premises was not being used for six months prior to the filing of the present petition. The petition in hand has been filed by the petitioner on 05.06.2010 wherein he has alleged that it was lying locked for the period more than six months prior to the filing of the petition. The PW1 also deposed to this effect and also relied upon document Ex. PW1/10 and Ex.PW1/11 to show the non­occupation of the premises.

In the cross­examination of PW1 it was not denied that respondent remained absent from the tenanted premises w.e.f. 28.07.2009 to 28.07.2010. Thus, the petitioner has been able to discharge the initial burden to show the non­occupation of the premises for period of more than six months prior to the filing of the petition.

E. No. 22/10 Ram Ratan Singh Namdhari vs. Usha Dave 15 16

16. The Section 14 (1) (d) of the DRC Act does not provide for any explanation for the said absence period to the tenant. However, the word "residing" has been interpreted by the superior courts in a expansive manner. In this regard the judgment of Hon'ble High Court of Delhi relied upon by the respondent in case titled as Ranjit Singh & Anr. vs. Inez Richards cited as 93(2001) DLT 299 explained the purport behind the said ground of eviction. The relevant paragraph is reproduced herein :

"......It has, however, been recognised in the impugned judgment itself that in cases of the present genere, it is the tenant who can best discharge the burden of proving that he/she had not resided in the demised premises for a period of over six months immediately preceding the filing of the petition. However, residency inherently implies permanency. Before a finding can be returned to the effect that a tenant has not resided in the demised premises for the said period of six months, the court must be fully satisfied that there was an intention to shift the residence to an alternative place. For instance, a tenant may be transferred to another city E. No. 22/10 Ram Ratan Singh Namdhari vs. Usha Dave 16 17 and as a consequence it may become necessary for him to be away from the demised premises for even six months. The ground of eviction would be made out. Could the tenant defeat the purpose of the legislation by making a short stay, in between this period, I would think that it would be a futile exercise, and he would not succeed in sewing himself from eviction. For the same reason, if there is no intention to reside at another place for this period, if this incidentally transpires for circumstances beyond the control of the tenant, in my view he would not be liable to eviction. In the present instance, it appears to be the common case that the respondent had left India with the purpose of visiting her children. There is no evidence that she had the intention of shifting residence for a period of over six months. As it happened, she appears to have returned to within three months. The Court must be alive fact that it is conceivable that the tenant leaves the residence with the intention of staying out for a couple of months, and for reasons beyond their E. No. 22/10 Ram Ratan Singh Namdhari vs. Usha Dave 17 18 control, staying out for a period of over six months. The burden of giving an acceptable explanation would rest with the tenant. In such case, there is no intention not to reside in the said premises within this period. In my view, therefore, the initial burden would be on the petitioner not only to state that there has been an absence of the tenant from the demised premises for a period of six months but also that this absence was fully intended. Only once the initial burden of proof is discharged by the Landlord does it shift to the tenant. "

17. In the present case in hand the petitioner through his testimony and documents has been able to discharge the initial burden of proof by showing the absence of respondent for the period more than six months. It was thus argued by the petitioner that the respondent was intentionally absent from the tenanted premises as she being a citizen of US and she for all practical purposes has permanently shifted. But, on the contrary, the respondent has given the explanation for her absence for the said period by stating that due to medical reasons and VISA problem she was not able to E. No. 22/10 Ram Ratan Singh Namdhari vs. Usha Dave 18 19 come back. It was thus argued on behalf of the respondent that there was no intention of the respondent to not to reside in the tenanted premises. The Ex.RW1/9 and Ex.RW1/10 show her intention to return to India.

18. The intend behind the enactment of Rent Control Legislations was the outcome of paucity of accommodation to a common man. The tenant was protected from arbitrary evictions at the whims and fancies of the landlord. The tenant was given the protection of statutory tenant under the Rent Control Legislations. The statutory tenant can be evicted from the tenanted premises only on the grounds provided in the Rent Control Legislations. The mere wish of the landlord in getting the tenanted premises vacated was not sufficient as was the case under the Transfer of Property Act. The burden of proof is upon the landlord to show the existence of the various grounds of eviction of the tenant. The said purpose of the Rent Control Legislations is defeated if the tenant abandons the tenanted premises for long duration. Therefore, the eviction under Section 14 (1) (d) has to be interpreted keeping in view the said intent of the legislation. E. No. 22/10 Ram Ratan Singh Namdhari vs. Usha Dave 19 20

19. In the present case in hand, it has to be seen whether there was or not the intent on the part of the respondent to return for the continuous occupation of the tenanted premises for residence. The period of absence in the present case which has been proved on record through testimony of PW1 as well as through the documents of the respondent herself is 28.08.2009 to 28.07.2010. The respondent has tried to explain her absence by relying upon document Ex.RW1/10 and the air tickets Ex.RW1/9. All the said tickets show that the return ticket was booked by the respondent from US. Her intended stay in India as per the tickets relied upon by the respondent herself was not more than one and half month or two months. Thus, the said tickets itself show the intention of the respondent to use the tenanted premises only for short duration in India and it cannot be said that she intends to use the tenanted premises for her permanent residence as discussed by Hon'ble High Court in Ranjit Singh & Anr. (Supra).

20. Similarly, her medical record Ex.RW1/10 which has been relied upon by her to show that she was not able to return twice firstly in November 2009 and secondly in June 2010 due to medical illness, itself E. No. 22/10 Ram Ratan Singh Namdhari vs. Usha Dave 20 21 shows her intention of her permanent abode at US.

Her first scheduled return was for 29.11.2009 and the first visit to the doctor was 11th September 2009. She was diagnosed with essential hypertension, hoarseness, nail fungal infection and pulmonary nodule. None of the said disease was connected with her earlier accident as stated by her in April 2009. In the follow up instructions of said visit, she was merely advised for return if symptoms worsen or failed to improve. Thus, admittedly, she was firstly diagnosed only with minor illness for which even no medication was advised. The second visit was on 20.10.2009 where again she was diagnosed only with nail fungal infection. Third visit was on 3rd November 2009 wherein she only consulted with opthalmology. Thus, there was no occasion for the respondent to have delayed her return on 29.11.2009 due to medical reasons. None of her three visits during said period show her to be having any major illness for which any major treatment was required. The said medical record on the contrary shows her intention to permanently reside in US only by getting treatment over there for the minor illnesses. She delayed her return due to said minor illness which fortifies her absence from the tenanted premises with the intention to E. No. 22/10 Ram Ratan Singh Namdhari vs. Usha Dave 21 22 shift.

21. After 29.11.2009 she booked her return ticket for 10.01.2010. But, she was not allowed to embark the flight due to OCI Certificate (Overseas Citizen of India Certification). She applied for said certification on her new passport but he same has got delayed and hence, her subsequent travel plan for 26.02.2010 also got derailed. She has relied upon documents Ex.RW1/7, Ex.RW1/8 and Mark­H. But, no evidence has come on record to show that the respondent was refused to board the flight on 26.02.2010 due to OCI Certification. The Ex.RW1/8 is the copy of the passport which shows her to be American citizen and the passport has been issued way back on 02.07.2006. Further the Ex.RW1/7 the OCI Certificate issued on 19.02.2010 also shows her address as that of US. The VISA stamps on Ex.RW1/8 also reveals that the respondent came to India from the year 2007 to 2011 once in every year only that too for short durations. Thus, the said documents of respondent herself shows her intention to have shifted her residence to US only.

After 26.02.2010 she again booked her ticket for return for E. No. 22/10 Ram Ratan Singh Namdhari vs. Usha Dave 22 23 26.06.2010 i.e. gap of more than three months of receipt of OCI Certification. But, she was again not able to board the flight due to dental surgery conducted in the first week of July and lastly she came back on 29.07.2010. She has again relied upon her medical document Ex.RW1/10 and Mark­X for giving the justification for the said period between March 2010 to 28th of July 2010.

The medical record is perused. The first visit to the hospital in the March was on 10th day wherein a fracture was detected in the nasal bone, contusion of face and nose congestion. The next visit was after around two months on 11.05.2010 for opthalmology. The Mark­X pertains to period 05.11.2009. Apart from said medical record, there is no other medical document to show the performance of dental surgery in the first week of July 2010 as alleged by the respondent. The burden of proving the said fact was upon the respondent itself to show the reasonable cause of her absence. Thus, the period starting from March 2010 till July 2010, the time when the respondent was in US, is completely unexplained. She was not under any major treatment which could have prevented her return to India. E. No. 22/10 Ram Ratan Singh Namdhari vs. Usha Dave 23 24

22. It was also argued on behalf of the respondent that she is doing various social activities in India and hence, she had never any intention to permanently settle down in US. For proving the said fact she has relied upon the document Ex.RW1/3, Ex.RW1/4, Ex.RW1/5, Ex.RW1/11, Mark­K, Mark­L, Mark­M, Mark­F and Mark­G. The document Ex.RW1/3 is the recognition of the trust for the donation made in the year 2011. Thus, the said document is not relevant being beyond the relevant period. Further the other document Ex. RW1/4 dt. 12.02.2004 is the request letter to the respondent to follow up the social work on her return from US. Thus, the said document itself shows that she was residing in US itself and was coming to India occasionally. The Mark­X & Mark­L are also the relevant documents for deciphering the intention of respondent to return to India. The document dt. 15.01.2007 is addressed by respondent to Chief Commissioner, MCD. She states in the letter that she retired in 2003 from US Govt. Secondary Education after teaching there for 25 years. She then started the process of moving back for living in India. Similarly, the Mark­L is the representation addressed to the SHO Model Town dt. 23.02.2007. In the said representation she again reiterated her intention to permanently E. No. 22/10 Ram Ratan Singh Namdhari vs. Usha Dave 24 25 move to India along with her husband. Thus, it is the admitted case of respondent that she is residing in US and intended to move to India after her retirement in September 2003. But, till date after lapse of more than ten years neither she nor her husband was examined as RW2 have given up their US citizenship. Both of them hold the US passport. On the contrary, RW2 in his cross­examination stated that he gave up his Indian citizenship somewhere in 2003. Thus, the intention of the respondent and her husband to permanently reside in USA is reflected from their conduct by not giving up their US passport till date.

23. The other document Mark­F and Mark­G are the appreciation documents for the social work done by the trust run by the respondent. Even both the said documents do not pertain to the relevant period of absence of the respondent from the tenanted premises. Thus, the intention of the respondent during all these years was only to use the tenanted premises for short durations of her stay in India. Her entire family is settled in US and she holds the US passport as of today. The entire object and intend behind the enactment of Rent Control Legislations is defeated if a tenant who only E. No. 22/10 Ram Ratan Singh Namdhari vs. Usha Dave 25 26 resides at the tenanted premises for one or two months in a year is protected. The permanent abode of the respondent is in US only. In view of abovesaid reasons, I am of the considered opinion that respondent has failed to discharge onus upon her through any positive material on record which could show her intention to permanently settle down in India.

24. In view of above said reasons, I am of considered opinion that the petitioner has been successfully prove his case against the respondent under Section 14 (1) (d) of the DRC Act. The respondent is accordingly directed to vacate the tenanted premises D­35, Rana Pratap Bagh, Delhi­110 007 as shown in site plan.

File be consigned to Record Room.
