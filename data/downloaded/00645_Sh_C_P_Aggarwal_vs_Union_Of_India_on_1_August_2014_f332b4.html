 JUDGMENT

AWARD REFERENCE UNDER SECTION 18 OF THE LAND ACQUISITION ACT

1. Vide this award, I shall dispose of the instant reference under section 18 of the L. A. Act preferred by the petitioner against the respondents being aggrieved with the quantum of compensation awarded by the LAC in lieu of the acquisition of the land of the petitioners.

2. That vide instant Award No. 2/2003-04, notification for land measuring 2416.11 Sq. meter was issued by the Land & Building Department on 05.03.2003 under section 4 vide notification No. F. 7(60)/2001/L&B/MRTS-II/19421 of the L. A. Act. The declaration U/s 6 of the L. A. Act was issued on 17.04.2003 and since the land was also notified U/s 17 (1) of the L. A. Act too vide notification dated 17.04.2003, the provisions of Section 5 (A) of the L. A. Act were dispensed off. The land of the petitioners bearing No. 4-B, Pusa Road, Rajendra Park, Delhi having total area measuring 43.2 sq. meters situated in the revenue estate of village Pusa Road, Rajendra Park, Delhi was acquired by the Government for the public purpose namely "Mass Rapid Transit System" and possession of the same was taken by Govt. The Land Acquisition Collector, after completing all the formalities as provided under the Act assessed the market value of the land of the petitioner @ Rs.12,127/- per sq. meter deeming it residential.

LAC-03/13/2004 Page:-2/19

3. In the petition, it was stated that petitioners are the owners in possession of the land beneath property bearing Municipal No. 4-B, Rajendra Park, Pusa Road, New Delhi, the part of which was notified for acquisition under the L. A. Act on 05.03.2003 and declaration u/s 6 of the Act was made on 17.04.2003 and the land was finally acquired under said Award under Section 11 dated 11.11.2003 of the L. A. Act. It was averred in the petition that compensation assessed by the LAC does not represent the true and correct market value of the land in question as on the date of notification U/s 4 of the L. A. Act and the compensation assessed by the LAC is on the lower side and the same is assailed on the grounds as mentioned in the petition. In the grounds, it was stated that LAC has failed to consider that acquired land is from the developed inhabited area being situated in the posh and prime locality of the capital of India. It was also averred that LAC has failed to consider that the land in the locality and of the petitioners has the commercial value and the same had commercial prospectus in future and no discrimination could be made between the residential and commercial. It was also pleaded that LAC has failed to consider the value of sale deeds of relevant period available for assessing the market value for land and cost and expenses for demolishing the existed permanent structures and raising the new construction. It was also averred that land of the petitioners was the free hold land and was not the leased land from the competent authority. It was also stated that LAC has not considered the claim filed by the petitioners U/s 9 (1) and 10 (1) of L. A. Act. Hence, present reference petition was filed by petitioner.

4. In reply to reference petition, the Respondent No. 2 DMRC filed LAC-03/13/2004 Page:-3/19 written statement wherein it was averred that amount of compensation awarded by the LAC is adequate and just and as such the objections to the Award are not sustainable. It was also submitted that compensation awarded is rational and as per the fair market value. It was also pleaded that compensation for the land in question are assessed by LAC considering situation of land in the vicinity and thus arriving at the "Fair Market Value". It was also stated that LAC has taken into consideration sale deed of similar lands in the adjacent areas of Pusa Road, Shankar Road, Karol Bagh to estimate the fair market value and the evaluation of structure was based on the valuation report duly vetted by PWD and as such the demand for enhancement of compensation is arbitrary and without any basis. Hence, it was prayed that present reference petition under reply is liable to be dismissed with costs.

5. Despite grant of several opportunities and imposition of cost, the written statement was not filed by respondent No. 1 Union of India, hence right of filing the written statement on behalf of respondent No. 1 was closed and on the pleadings of parties, the following issues were framed vide order dated 21.04.2006:-

1) What was the market value of the property in question at the time of issuance of notification U/s 4 of the LA Act?Onus on parties
2) Whether the petitioner is entitled to enhancement in compensation, if so, to what amount?OPP
3) Relief.

6. In petitioner's evidence, the petitioner had produced four witnesses in witness box. Petitioner namely C. P. Agarwal produced himself in witness box as PW-1, Sh. Sunil Srivastava, LDC, Sub-Registrar-VII was examined as PW-2, Sh. O. P. Sharma, LAC-03/13/2004 Page:-4/19 Assistant, OSB Branch, Vikas Sadan, INA, DDA was examined as PW-3 and Sh. Satpal Singh, Bailiff, from office of SR-III was examined as PW-4 and thereafter, PE was closed.

7. In defence, counsel for Union of India had appeared and tendered copy of Award No. 2/2003-04 pertaining to village Pusa Road, Karol Bagh, Delhi. The counsel for DMRC had also appeared in witness box and adopted the evidence tendered by counsel for Union of India and further tendered the copy of letter dated 16.04.1999 to show the Schedule of Market Rate and thereafter R.E. was closed and matter was posted for final arguments.

8. During the course of arguments, the counsel for petitioner has tendered and placed on record the certified copy of judgment dated 09.10.2013 passed by the Court of Sh. Rajesh Kumar Singh, Ld. Addl. District Judge-05, Central, Delhi, bearing LAC No. 132/08 titled M/s Jaypee Hotels Limited Vs. Union of India & Ors. and has argued that Award in above referred case and in the present case is same and pertains to same area and this case is also covered under the above said judgment.

9. I have gone through the record and have heard the arguments of parties. My findings are as under:-

10.Issue No. 1. What was the market value of the property in question at the time of issuance of notification U/s 4 of the LA Act?Onus on parties:- The petitioner having been aggrieved by the compensation awarded by the LAC sought enhancement through the reference made and have put forth a number of grounds on which it claims to be entitled to the enhanced compensation. Even slight disturbance or variation in routine unsettles a person and that too depends upon the degree of LAC-03/13/2004 Page:-5/19 impact and vulnerability of the individual, the actual impact, thus, may even be devastating. In these circumstances, unsettling someone alongwith the entire family with habitat and hearth lock, stock and barrel can very well be imagined especially when establishment is stable and running for quite sometime. The proximity not only breeds contempt but also develops into fascination, bondings and different types of relations between the individual, and at times encompasses in itself, the relationship between the humans animals and even the stones and structures. One may, more often than not, feel the pain of leaving particular abode, locality, city or country. Against this backdrop, unsettling someone from his abode is, without doubt, disturbing if not devastating but then it has to be coped up depending upon the circumstance especially when it is supported by the Legislation (may be through a more than hundred years old 'colonial' legislation, not much amended, retaining its basic features) and when there is no escape or no way out and has limited rather no option to exercise but to to the line or so to say that no option whatsoever except the one thrusted upon by the circumstances. The requirement for constructing metro was the need of the hour and the creation of the same, armed and empowered with the Will of Government to remove all possible obstacles with all the might of the state in order to have smooth passage/path/road in place at the earliest in the City of Delhi resulted into acquisition of land even in thickly populated urban landscape under the Land Acquisition Act, 1894. In such scenario those who were displaced could have only consoled themselves that they got suitable compensation, which may compensate them to the extent LAC-03/13/2004 Page:-6/19 possible though at times there cannot be a compensation for such situations.

In this regard, it will be appropriate to refer the broad parameters laid down by the Hon'ble Supreme Court of India in case of Chaman Lal Hargovind Dass Vs. Special Land Acquisition Officer (1988) 3 SCC 751 wherein it was observed that the case of enhancement of compensation on account of acquisition of land are required to be considered on the following parameters:-

"4.-The following factors must be etched on the mental screen:
(1) A reference under Section 18 of the Land Acquisition Act is not an appeal against the award and the court cannot take into account the material relied upon by the Land Acquisition Officer in his award unless the same material is produced and proved before the court.
(2) So also the award of the Land Acquisition Officer is not to be treated as judgment of the trial court open or exposed to challenge before the court hearing the reference. It is merely an offer made by the Land Acquisition Officer and the material utilised by him for making his valuation cannot be utilised by the court unless produced and proved before it. It is not the function of the court to sit in appeal against the award, approve or disprove its reasoning, or correct its error or affirm, modify or reverse the conclusion reached by the Land Acquisition Officer, as if it were an appellate court.
                 (3)       The court has to treat the reference as an
                 original proceedings before it         and determine the

market value afresh on the basis of the material produced before it.
(4) The claimant is in the position of a plaintiff who has to show that the price offered for his land in the award is inadequate on the basis of the material produced in the court. Of course, the material placed and proved by the other side can also be taken into account for this purpose.
(5) The market value of land under acquisition has to be determined as on the crucial date of publication of notification under Section 4 of the Land Acquisition Act (dates of notifications under Section 6 & 9 are irrelevant).
(6) The determination has to be made standing on the date line of valuation (date of publication of notification under Section 4) as if the valuer is a hypothetical purchaser willing to purchase land from the open market and is prepared to pay a reasonable price as on that day. It has also to be assumed that the vendor is willing to sell the land at a reasonable price. (7) In doing so by the instances method, the court has to correlate the market value reflected in the most comparable instance which provides the index of market value.
(8) Only genuine instances have to be taken into account. (Sometimes instances are rigged up in anticipation of acquisition of land).
(9) Even post-notification instances can be taken into account (1) if they are very proximate, (2) genuine and (3) the acquisition itself has not motivated the LAC-03/13/2004 Page:-8/19 purchaser to pay a higher price on account of the resultant improvement in development prospects. (10) The most comparable instances out of the genuine instances have to be identified on the following considerations:
(i) proximity from time angle.
(ii) proximity from situation angle. (11) Having identified the instances which provide the index of market value the price reflected therein may be taken as the norm and the market value of the land under acquisition may be deduced by making suitable adjustments for the plus and minus factors vis-

a-vis land under acquisition by placing the two in juxtaposition.

(12) A balance-sheet of plus and minus factors may be drawn for this purpose and the relevant factors may be evaluated in terms of price variation as a prudent purchaser would do.

(13) The market value of the land under acquisition has thereafter to be deduced by loading the price reflected in the instance taken as norm for plus factors and unloading it for minus factors.

(14) The exercise indicated in clause (11) to (13) has to be undertaken in a common sense manner as a prudent man of the world of business would do. We may illustrate some such illustrative (not exhaustive) factors:

(16) Every case must be dealt with on its own fact pattern bearing in mind all these factors as a prudent purchaser of land in which position the judge must place himself.

(17) These are general guidelines to be applied with understanding informed with common sense"

23. In P. Rama Reddi & others Vs. Land Acquit ion Officer, HUDA Hyderabad (1995) 2 SCC 305, parameters was enumerated as follows :
"(i) the situation of the acquired land vis-a-vis the city or the town or village which had been growing in size because of its commercial, industrial, educational, religious or any other kind of importance or because of its explosive population:
(ii) the suitability of the acquired land for putting up the buildings, be they residential, commercial or industrial, as the case may be;
(iii) possibility of obtaining water and electric supply for occupants of buildings to be put up on that land;
(iv) absence of statutory impediments or the like for using the acquired land for building purposes;
LAC-03/13/2004 Page:-11/19
(v) existence of highways, public roads, layouts of building plots or developed residential extensions in the vicinity or close proximity of the acquired land;
(vi) benefits or advantageous or educational institution, health care centers, or the like in the surrounding areas of the acquired land which may become available to the occupiers of buildings, if built on the acquired land;
(vii) and lands around the acquired land or the acquired land itself being in demand for building purposes, to specify a few."

In the present case, the petitioners have relied upon three transactions Ex. PW-1/1, PW-1/2 and PW-1/3 pertaining to the year 1995 and 1997 for auction of residential plots. I have gone through the same. Firstly, those transactions are not of contemporary period. Secondly, out of these transactions, two transactions i.e. Ex. PW-1/2 and PW-1/3 belong to either Punchkuian Road or Barakhamba Road which is a commercial area and far away from the acquired land and the said transactions are apparently of different area and location. Furthermore, from Ex. PW-1/1, it is not made out that what were the circumstances for paying such huge amount and this single instance cannot be made criteria. I have also gone through Ex. PW-3/1 which are extract of register of auction and perusal of same reveals that figure pertains to Shankar Road which is different area and location and this record of auction cannot prove the exact value of acquired land in the present matter. It cannot be stated as to what was the actual price paid. Hence, all the instances are discarded. Moreover, in LAC-03/13/2004 Page:-12/19 connected matter titled Jasmeet Singh & Ors. Vs. UOI & Ors bearing LAC No. 05/13/04, valuation has been arrived by this Court fixing the valuation at the rate of Rs. 22,762/- per sq. meter and in these circumstances, it cannot be more than that amount.

In claim itself, petitioner has admitted that his property is residential property. The petitioner has not led any evidence regarding compensation of structure. I have to scrutinize the method of calculation adopted by LAC. The LAC tried to adopt two methods. By first method, the schedule of rates circulated by Department of Urban Development vide letter dated 19.04.99 were referred whereby indicative price in nearby area was given at Rs. 11,550/- per sq. meter for residential use. These rates were applicable till March 2000 and the present land was acquired in 2003 and as there was no indication available regarding the rate of 2003 as the same was not revised, so, the LAC had not taken these figure into consideration and ignored and discarded the same. Still for the sake of arguments, even if this criteria is adopted by court and 12% periodical enhancement is given on this amount of Rs. 11,550/- for 3 years i.e. upto March 2003 then it comes to Rs. 16,226/- per sq. meter.

Second method adopted by LAC was after taking into consideration the various sale deeds produced on record before LAC and average sale price of these transactions which comes to Rs. 17,991/- per sq. meter as per LAC. It has come on record that the properties relied upon in these transactions constituted land and structure appurtenant thereon. The LAC has separately given an amount of Rs. 22,295/- for structure and so if this amount of Rs. 17,991/- is calculated against the acquired area of 43.2 sq. meter LAC-03/13/2004 Page:-13/19 then it roughly comes to Rs. 7,77,211/- which includes cost of land and structure and thereafter, if awarded amount of structure to the tune of Rs. 22,295/- is deducted from the total amount and again divided by 43.2 then figure somewhere around comes to Rs. 17,475/- which will be cost of land only. There was no reason to once include the cost of old structure in the calculation and thereafter, reduce the whole of the amount so arrived by 30 to 35% and bringing it to near about Rs.12,600/- per sq. meter because value of land will remain as such and the cost of old structure should have been deducted and taken into account separately while determining the compensation for land. Rather, the LAC did not stick to the value so arrived but rather decreased it from Rs. 12,600/- per sq. meter to Rs. 12,127/- per sq. meter. So on both the counts, valuation fixed by LAC is wrong and has been given on lower side and is not accepted. Even as per criteria of LAC, it should have been more than Rs. 17,000/- per sq. meter.

The next most important criteria to be relied upon is judgment pronounced by the Court of Sh. Rajesh Kumar Singh, Ld. Addl. District Judge-05, Central, Delhi. Those ground as mentioned therein are applicable in the present facts also as this judgment pertains to adjacent building covered under same award by different court and I am in agreement with the observations given by the Court of Sh. Rajesh Kumar Singh, Ld. Addl. District Judge-05, Central, Delhi. Though judgment is not binding on this court and judgment has no persuasive value on this court but this court has also reached around the same figure far or less which has been arrived by that court regarding adjacent piece of land on the different mode/ criteria / approach / calculation. (It is made LAC-03/13/2004 Page:-14/19 clear that judgment of my Ld. Colleague is with respect to commercial property and the present judgment pertains to residential property). It is clear that the LAC has almost fixed the residential property value at Rs. 12,127/- and commercial property @ Rs. 25,357/- i.e. rate of residential property is deemed to be half of commercial property. Hence, if the valuation arrived for commercial property by my Ld. Colleague is Rs. 45,424/- per sq. yards then value of residential property will come around Rs. 22,750/- per sq. meter i.e. half of the commercial property, as per criteria adopted by LAC. The next criteria before this court is the rate calculated as per the sale deeds produced in connected case titled Mr. Jasmeet Singh & Ors. Vs. UOI & Ors. bearing LAC No. 05/13/04 wherein the rate of land was arrived @ Rs. 11,000/- per sq. meter and the acquired property in the present case is property No. 3-B, Pusa Road, Rajendra Park, Delhi and in other connected case titled Mr. Jasmeet Singh & Ors. Vs. UOI & Ors. the property No. is 10-B, Pusa Road, Rajendra Park, Delhi, both in same vicinity and locality. The counsel for petitioner in connected case titled Mr. Jasmeet Singh & Ors. Vs. UOI & Ors. bearing LAC No. 05/13/04 has argued that he has proved six sale deeds in his favour i.e. Ex. PW-1/A to PW-1/E and the acquired property was part of properties purchased for Rs. 51 Lacs. Area was 1800 Sq. yards so rate per square yards comes to Rs. 2833/- in the year 1993 and same be converted into sq. meter and 12% enhancement per year be given. The sale deeds relied upon by petitioner are of six properties and of the year 1993. The part of it was acquired in the year 2003 and there is a gap of 10 years so for such long period 12% periodical rise cannot be made criteria and LAC-03/13/2004 Page:-15/19 sale deed of the other property for the contemporary period should have been proves so the evidence is of no use. Even otherwise, if the arguments is accepted then the value of total land purchased through these six sale deeds for the total area of 1794 sq. yards is Rs. 51 Lacs i.e. 299 sq. yards for Rs. 8,50,000/- and if the square yards are converted into square meter then area purchased for Rs. 8,50,000/- is 250 sq. meter so per square meter rate comes to Rs. 3408/- in the year 1993-94 i.e. date of the sale deed placed on record and on that if 12% periodical rise is given then notional value on the basis of these sale deeds in the year 2003 comes around less than Rs. 11,000/- per square meter which is even less than rate give by LAC. So arguments of petitioner is self-destructive. This court is now having three figures of valuation which is summed up as under:

i) As per calculation of LAC and which is now recalculated as per method adopted by LAC which comes around Rs. 17,000/- per sq. meter;
ii) As per other petitioner in connected case titled Mr. Jasmeet Singh & Ors. Vs. UOI & Ors. bearing LAC No. 05/13/04 on the basis of sale deed relied upon by him which comes to around Rs. 11,000/- per sq. meter;
iii) On the basis of calculation made by my Ld. Colleague which comes around Rs. 22,750/- per sq. meter. Hence, in view the calculation made from different methods and in the totality of facts and circumstances, when valuation has been carried out for the similarly situated property at the same place in one of the judgment in award by my Ld. Colleague and myself in connected case titled Mr. Jasmeet Singh & Ors. Vs. UOI & Ors. bearing LAC-03/13/2004 Page:-16/19 LAC No. 05/13/04, the anamoly should be avoided and the value of the property due to parity should be kept on same value as given for adjacent land. As the value of adjacent properties is determined to the tune of Rs. 45,524/- for commercial properties and the same is made the basic criteria in the present case also and petitioner is granted Rs. 22,762/- per sq. meter rate for residential property. Accordingly, this issue is decided in favour of petitioner and against the respondents.

13. Issue No. 2. Whether the petitioner is entitled to enhancement of compensation, if so, to what amount? OPP:- The onus to prove this issue was on the petitioner. In view of the decision on issue No. 1, the claimant is entitled to enhancement of the compensation towards the acquired land from Rs. 12,127/- per sq. meter to Rs. 22,762/- per sq. meter. It is admitted fact that notice was not issued to the respondents by the claimants at the time of preparation of the report regarding the cost of removal and reconstruction of the boundary wall and therefore the same cannot be relied upon in view of the judgment by the Hon'ble Punjab and Haryana High Court in the matter of Kalawati Devi Vs. Haryana State Vol. CXIII - (1996-2) the Punjab Law Reporter 471. During arguments, it was submitted that when something is reconstructed, it is done in a better manner. The respondents are not liable to pay for the improvement and no reasons has been assigned by the claimant as to why the CPWD rates should not apply.

Even otherwise, except bald statement, no other evidence has been led to determine the compensation of construction etc. so in the absence of any other evidence, the assessment of Ld. LAC LAC-03/13/2004 Page:-17/19 towards the compensation of structure is not to be altered. This issue is decided accordingly and the assessment of the Ld. LAC towards the compensation for structure is not to be disturbed.

14. Relief:- In view of the above findings herein above, the reference is answered and market value of the land of the petitioners in respect of the the land bearing property bearing No. 4-B, measuring 43.2 sq. meters, Pusa Road, Karol Bagh, New Delhi which was acquired through the notification No. F. 7(60)/2001/L&B/MRTS-II/19421 U/s 4 of the LA Act, is fixed at Rs. 22,762/- per sq. meter, which the petitioner shall be entitled to claim compensation from all the respondents as mentioned in the Statement under Section 19 of LA Act on which 30% Solatium shall be admissible to the petitioners in terms of Section 23 of L.A. Act. The interest @ 12 % p.a. from the date of notification u/s 4 of LA Act till the date of award or possession which ever is earlier as per Section 23(1-A) of LA Act is also granted. As the acquisition was compulsory in nature, therefore, the petitioners are entitled to the interest at the rate of 9% for the first year from the date of dispossession and at the rate of 15% on the difference between the enhanced compensation awarded by this Court and the compensation awarded by the LAC for the subsequent period till the payment is made to the petitioners. The petitioners are further entitled to the interest on the Solatium/ additional amount in terms of the judgment of Hon'ble Supreme Court of India in Sunder Versus UOI DLT (2001) SC 569. Reference is answered accordingly. It is also made clear that since acquired property was free hold property as per record, hence the entire compensation will be given to petitioners. Copy of the Award be sent to the LAC-03/13/2004 Page:-18/19 respondents to make payment of enhanced amount of compensation to the petitioner within three months from today. The amount already paid on the basis of compensation awarded by the respondent, if any, shall be taken care of in order to avoid any over payment. No order as to cost. Decree be drawn accordingly. File be consigned to record room.
