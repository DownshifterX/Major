J U D G M E N T

1. This is a reference sent by Land Acquisition Collector (hereinafter referred as "LAC"), District South West, New Delhi u/s 31 of The Land Acquisition Act (in short, "The Act") vide his letter no. F./LAC/SW/S/5441 dated 25.08.11 describing the particulars of dispute between the parties as under :­ "IP no. 2 Shri Suresh @ Suresh Bahadur has filed objection against the release of compensation to IP no. 1."

2. Vide Notification No. F.9 (88)/2004/L&B/LA/176 Dated 07.04.2006 and F.9 (88)/2004/L&B/LA/77­91 Dated 04.04.2007 issued under Sections 4 & 6 of the Act and Award no. 09/2008­2009/SW of Village Dhoolsiras, 62 bighas and 8 biswas of land situated in Village Dhoolsiras including the subject matter under Reference, was acquired by the government for construction of 100 meter road under Planned Development of Delhi. IP no. 2 raised objections for release of compensation to IP no. 1, therefore, the LAC sent the Reference for adjudication with amount of compensation i.e. Rs. 14,00,480/­.

3. IP no. 1 Gaon Sabha claims to be the bhumidhar of the land in question i.e. land falling in Khasra no. 22/24, measuring 4 bighas and 12 biswas. According to IP no. 1, the land in question was never legally alloted to any person and nobody was admitted as Asami of the land by Gaon Sabha.

2 of 18 LAC No. 125/14 Union of India v Gaon Sabha Dhoolsiras & Others DOD : 11.11.2014 The Revenue Record shows that Gaon Sabha is the bhumidhar and is entitled to the entire compensation for the acquired land in question.

4. IP no. 2 Shri Suresh @ Suresh Bahadur has also filed his claim. He has alleged that the land in question was alloted to him by Gaon Sabha in the year 1986 as Military Personnel after obtaining approval from the Lt. Governor under 20 Points Programme on lease for five years vide Resolution no. 1. Gaon Sabha issued LR 37 on 7.5.1986 and Certificate of Allotment was issued in the name of IP on 16.5.1986 by the then Pradhan of the village. The IP deposited lease money with the Gaon Sabha on 7.5.1986. According to IP, land was bunjar and uncultivable at the time of its allotment to him and he with hard labour, made the same cultivable. IP claims to be in continuous cultivatory possession of land in question since the date of its allotment.

5. IP has contended that he was declared Asami of the alloted land under Rule 47 of The Delhi Land Reforms Rules 1954 vide order dated 3.11.1986 in case no. 235 of 1986. He states that he has reclaimed the land in question and therefore, he is entitled to be declared as bhumidhar in view of Section 74 (4) of The Delhi Land Reforms Act. The IP has stated that he filed a petition u/s 74 (4) of The Delhi Land Reforms Act for declaration of bhumidhari rights in respect of land in question, which is pending before the court of Revenue Assistant (Vasant Vihar) New Delhi.

6. IP stated that the lease period has already expired and Gaon Sabha has not taken any step as per the provisions of The Delhi Land 3 of 18 LAC No. 125/14 Union of India v Gaon Sabha Dhoolsiras & Others DOD : 11.11.2014 Reforms Act and no report has been made to the Revenue Assistant that reclamation of the land in question has been made from the IP.

7. The IP has stated that he has been in cultivatory possession of the land in question since 1986­1987 till the date of its acquisition. In the Khasra Girdawari, the name of IP is shown as an Asami. He has contended that the Gaon Sabha has not taken any step for his ejectment from the land in question from the date of its allotment till its acquisition, therefore, he is entitled to receive the entire compensation of the acquired land as assessed by the Collector.

8. In reply to the claim petition of IP no. 2, the Gaon Sabha has asserted that the alleged allotment of land in question in favour of IP, is not an allotment in the eyes of law as the proper procedure prescribed under Delhi Land Reforms Act, Delhi Panchayat Act and Rules framed thereunder, was not followed. According to Gaon Sabha, the land in question was never legally handed over to IP no. 2. The Gaon Sabha alleged that the land in question was neither legally alloted nor the said IP reclaimed the land as per the requirement of Section 74 (4) of Delhi Land Reforms Act. It further alleges that the Gaon Sabha, Dhoolsiras never admitted the said Asami of the land in question. Gaon Sabha contended that IP no.2 has no right, title or interest in compensation of the acquired land and he is not entitled to receive any compensation.

9. On 21.11.2012, it was noticed by learned predecessor of the 4 of 18 LAC No. 125/14 Union of India v Gaon Sabha Dhoolsiras & Others DOD : 11.11.2014 court that since village Dhoolsiras was urbanized U/s 507 of the DMC Act, therefore, notice was directed to be issued to the Ministry of Urban Development through its Secretary as well as Vice Chairman, DDA. DDA as well as Ministry of Urban Development, did not file any claim or replied to the claim of claimants despite due service of notice upon them.

10. On the basis of pleadings of parties, on 2.4.2012, the following issues were framed :­

(i) Which of the IPs are entitled to receive the amount of compensation in this case and to what extent ? OP on IPs.

(ii) Relief.

11. IP no. 2 Shri Suresh @ Suresh Bahadur examined himself as IP 2W1. He filed his evidence by way of affidavit, wherein, he has re­iterated the same facts as stated in the claim petition. In the course of his deposition, he relied upon and proved the following documents :­ (I)Photocopy of Resolution dated 5.5.1986 Ex. IP2W1/1. (II)Allotment Certificate dated 16.5.1986 Ex. IP2W1/2. (III)Copy of Receipt (lease money) dated 7.5.1986 Ex. IP2W1/3. (IV)Copy of Khatoni for the year 2002­2003 Ex. IP2W1/4. (V)Petition u/s 74 (4) of DLR Act Ex. IP2W1/5 and (VI)Khasra Girdawari since 1986­1987 to 2011­2012 Ex. IP2W1/6.

12. IP no. 2 also got examined IP2W2 Shri Brahm Pal Singh, Village Level Worker, BDO Office, Najafgarh, New Delhi. He proved the 5 of 18 LAC No. 125/14 Union of India v Gaon Sabha Dhoolsiras & Others DOD : 11.11.2014 Resolution passed by the Pradhan of the Gaon Sabha dated 5.5.1986, which is already Ex. IP2W1/1. IP2W3 Shri Naresh Kumar and IP2W4 Shri Ashok Kumar filed their affidavit in evidence. They have corroborated and supported the testimony of IP no. 2. IP2W5 Balbir Singh, PA to SDM, Dwarka, New Delhi brought the summoned record i.e. petition u/s 74 (4) of DLR Act filed by IP no. 2 in respect of Khasra no. 22/24 (4­12) of Village Dhoolsiras. He proved the copy of the petition, which is already Ex.IP2W1/5. IP2W6 Shri Mahender Prasad, Halka Patwari of village Dhoolsiras, Tehsil Dwarka, New Delhi proved Khatoni in respect of Khasra no. 22/24 (4­12) situated within Revenue Estate of village Dhoolsiras. Copy of Khatoni is Ex. IP2W1/4. He also proved Khasra Girdawari pertaining to Khasra no. 22/24 for the year 2011­2012 which is Ex. IP2W6/1.

13. Counsel for Gaon Sabha tendered certified copy of Khatoni for 2002­2003 (Form P­6) of village Dhoolsiras, which is Ex. R­1. No witness has been examined by the respondent/Gaon Sabha in support of its contentions.

14 I have heard and considered the submissions advanced by Shri S.S. Dalal, learned counsel for IP no. 1 Gaon Sabha, Shri Satbir Singh, learned counsel for IP no. 2 and perused the record of the case. My finding on the issues are as under:­ ISSUE No.1:

6 of 18 LAC No. 125/14 Union of India v Gaon Sabha Dhoolsiras & Others DOD : 11.11.2014 Which of the IPs are entitled to receive the amount of compensation in this case and to what extent ? OP on IPs.

In support of its claim, Gaon Sabha has filed certified copy of Khatoni for the year 2002­2003 Ex.R­1 and it is its contention that IP no. 2 was not alloted the land under reference and allotment made to him, was not as per rules. Learned counsel argued that the Gaon Sabha could not have alloted the land under reference to the petitioner except with the approval of the Competent Authority and that if the land is transferred in contravention of the provisions of law, it will have no legal effect. He further argued that though the petitioner came into possession of the land by virtue of the Resolution passed by the Gaon Sabha, he can not be stated to have acquired the status of an Asami.

15. IP2W2 Shri Brahm Pal Singh has duly proved on record the Resolution dated 5.5.1986 Ex.IP2W1/1 passed by Shri Sat Narain, the then Pradhan of the Gaon Sabha. The name of IP no. 2 is mentioned in the Resolution at serial no. 2. IP2W6 Shri Mahender Prasad proved Khatoni Ex. IP2W1/4 in respect of Khasra no. 22/24 (4­12) situated within revenue estate of Village Dhoolsiras. In the Khatoni, the name of Khatedar has been shown as Shri Suresh i.e. IP no. 2 (Asami). This witness also proved the Khasra Girdawari of Khasra no. 22/14 Ex. IP2W6/1 which shows that IP no. 2 has 7 of 18 LAC No. 125/14 Union of India v Gaon Sabha Dhoolsiras & Others DOD : 11.11.2014 been shown in possession of the land in question for the year 2011­2012.

16. The documents proved on record by the IP no. 2 clearly reveal that he has been shown as the cultivator of the land in question after the year 1986. They further show that this IP was in cultivatory possession of the land under reference and had been growing wheat and paddy etc. on the said land till the year 2011­2012. The document Ex. R­1 i.e. Khatoni relied upon by Gaon Sabha itself shows that it is the IP no. 2, who has been in continuous possession of the land under reference till its acquisition.

17. If the contention of Gaon Sabha is that the petitioner was never alloted the land in question and he was in illegal occupation of the land under reference without any right or title therein, then as a natural corollary, the IP no. 2 should be deemed to have become a bhumidhar of the land under reference in view of provisions of Section 85 of The Delhi Land Reforms Act. This Section clearly lays down that if a person is in illegal occupation of the land of Gaon Sabha and no suit is brought for his ejectment for a period of three years, then the illegal occupant becomes the bhumidhar thereof.

18. In order to properly appreciate the rival contentions of both the parties, it is relevant to refer to certain provisions of The Delhi Land Reforms Act 1954, The Delhi Land Reforms Rules 1954 and The Delhi Panchayat Rajya Rules 1959 and The Delhi Land Revenue Act :­ 8 of 18 LAC No. 125/14 Union of India v Gaon Sabha Dhoolsiras & Others DOD : 11.11.2014 Section 74 of the Delhi Land Reforms Act, 1954:­ Admission to land mentioned in sub clause (iii) of clause (a) of Section 6 or to waste land for reclamation :­

1)The Gaon Sabha shall have the right to admit any person as Asami to any land falling in any of the classes mentioned in sub­clause (iii) of clause (a) of Section 6 where­

(a) the land is vacant land.

(b) the land is vested in the Gaon Sabha, or

(c) the land has come into the possession of the Gaon Sabha under Section 72 or under any other provision of this Act.

Rule 47 of The Delhi Land Reforms Rules, 1954 :­ Admission to land :

1) Whenever the Gaon Sabha intends to admit any person to land under section 73 or 74, the Gaon Panchayat shall announce by beat of drum in Gaon Sabha area in which the land in situated, the number of plots, the areas and the date on which admission thereto is to be made.

9 of 18 LAC No. 125/14 Union of India v Gaon Sabha Dhoolsiras & Others DOD : 11.11.2014

2) On the said date, a meeting of the Gaon Panchayat shall be held to prepare a list of all such persons who are present and who express their desire to be admitted to the land. The Gaon Panchayat shall in the same meeting announce revenue or the rent to be fixed for the land. The rent shall be in accordance with the provisions of Section 88 of The Delhi Land Reforms Act, 1954 and the revenue shall not be less than half of the rent. The list so prepared containing the names of the persons to be admitted to the land, shall be examined and sorted classwise in accordance with the provisions of Section 75 of the Act. The order of preference in admitting the persons shall be observed as given in sub­section (1) of section 75. If more than one person belonging to the same order of preference mentioned in Section 75 appears and express their desire to be admitted to the land at the rent or land revenue fixed by the Panchayat, the Panchayat shall draw lots to determine the persons to whom the land would be given and the persons so determined shall before being admitted to the land pay to the Gaon Sabha an amount equal to 10 times the rent of the land 10 of 18 LAC No. 125/14 Union of India v Gaon Sabha Dhoolsiras & Others DOD : 11.11.2014 which shall be credited to the Gram Sabha fund.

Section 22 of The Delhi Land Revenue Act:­ Section 22: Report of Succession or transfer of possession :­

1) Every person obtaining possession on admission by the Gaon Sabha as bhumidhar or Asami or by 11 of 18 LAC No. 125/14 Union of India v Gaon Sabha Dhoolsiras & Others DOD : 11.11.2014 succession or by transfer other than a lease permitted under The Delhi Land Reforms Act, 1954, of any land in a village which is required to be recorded in the register specified in Section 20 shall report such admission, succession or transfer to the Tehsildar.

2) In the case of an admission by the Gaon Sabha as bhumidhar or Asami or of a succession or transfer, other than a lease permitted under Delhi Land Reforms Act, 1954, the report shall be made immediately after it has taken place.

3) In the case of lease permitted under The Delhi Land Reforms Act, 1954, the report shall be made by lessor immediately after the lessee has been delivered possession thereunder.

4) If the person so succeeding, or otherwise obtaining possession, or, in the case of a lease, the lessor is a minor or otherwise disqualified, the guardian or other persons who has charge of his property shall make the report required by this section.

5) No revenue court shall entertain a suit or application by the person so succeeding or otherwise obtaining possession or, in the case of a lease, by the 12 of 18 LAC No. 125/14 Union of India v Gaon Sabha Dhoolsiras & Others DOD : 11.11.2014 lessor until such person has made the report required by this section.

19. The aforementioned legal provisions make it abundantly clear that it is the Gaon Sabha and not the other authority, who has the statutory right to admit any person as an Asami to any land which stands vested in the Gaon Sabha. So far as the contention of Gaon Sabha that no approval was obtained by the Gaon Sabha for the allotment of land under reference to petitioner, therefore, the said allotment is void is concerned, in my considered view, once the Gaon Sabha allots land to a person then it is its duty to seek approval from the competent authority and then to get his name entered in the revenue records.

20. Section 22 (3) of The Delhi Land Revenue Act enjoins a duty upon the lessor i.e. the Gaon Sabha to report to the Tehsildar of the allotment made by it. There is no role to be played by the Lessee or Asami as per the provisions of Section 22 (3) of The Delhi Land Revenue Act. Once the Gaon Sabha gives the possession of the land to a person, who then cultivates it and reclaims it, it does not lie in the mouth of the Gaon Sabha to challenge the allotment on the ground that it itself has not followed the procedure and the rules in allotting the land to him. Infact, in taking such a contention, the Gaon Sabha is acting as a Zamindar, with the aim to exploit the landless labourers. This contention is therefore, is rejected, for any other interpretation would 13 of 18 LAC No. 125/14 Union of India v Gaon Sabha Dhoolsiras & Others DOD : 11.11.2014 only defeat the very purpose, for which The Delhi Land Reforms Act was enacted, namely upliftment of the landless labourers and the abolition of the Zamindari system.

21. It is to be noted that after advent of The Delhi Land Reforms Act 1954, only three categories of persons were recognized as having interest in the land of a village, namely the bhumidhar, Asami and the Gaon Sabha. There is no fourth category of tenure holder recognized by the Act. The Khatoni Ex. R­1 relied upon the Gaon Sabha itself shows that on the directions of SDM in case no. 235/86 dated 3.11.1986, IP no. 2 was declared as Asami on the land of Gaon Sabha. Further, on the directions of Tehsildar, Dwarka, dated 4.3.2014, Award no. 9/2008­2009, the name of this IP was entered as an Asami in the Gaon Sabha land. The aforesaid orders of the Tehsildar and the SDM, have not been challenged by the Gaon Sabha before any competent authority till date. It is an admitted fact that IP no.2 is an Asami in respect of the land in question and he remained in possession of the land till its acquisition.

22. In view of the above discussion, I am of the considered opinion that the contention of the Gaon Sabha, that allotment of the land under reference to IP no. 2 was invalid, can not be upheld at all and it is therefore to be held that the IP was admitted as an Asami to the land under reference.

23. Now coming to the question as to whether IP no. 2 is entitled to be declared as a bhumidhar of the land under reference, taking into 14 of 18 LAC No. 125/14 Union of India v Gaon Sabha Dhoolsiras & Others DOD : 11.11.2014 consideration that he had reclaimed the land, it would be expedient herein to refer to the provisions of Sub­Section 4 of Section 74 of the Delhi Land Reforms Act:­ Section 74 : Admission to land mentioned in Sub Clause (iii) of Clause (a) of Section 6 or to waste land for reclamation:­ (4) At the end of five years, the Gaon Sabha shall report to the Revenue Assistant the extent to which reclamation has been made. The Revenue Assistant, shall after necessary enquiry and after hearing the Asami, either order the termination of lease and his ejectment if there has been no reclamation or extend his lease for another period of two years. If, however, the land has been duly reclaimed during the period of five years or the extended period, the Revenue Assistant shall direct the Gaon Sabha to admit the Asami as bhumidhar under Section 73. The Asami on his admission as bhumidhar shall be liable to pay such land revenue as shall be equal to 50 percent, of the rent together with cesses and local rates, but he shall not be liable to pay any compensation.

24. The aforesaid provision of law makes it amply clear that where an Asami is found to have reclaimed the land alloted to him during the 15 of 18 LAC No. 125/14 Union of India v Gaon Sabha Dhoolsiras & Others DOD : 11.11.2014 period of 5 years of his lease, the Revenue Assistant shall direct the Gaon Sabha to admit the Asami as bhumidhar.

25. Admittedly, the IP no. 2 has been cultivating the land alloted to him since 1986 till its acquisition. No ejectment proceedings were initiated against the IP, either by the Gaon Sabha or by the Central Government or any other Revenue Authority. In such view of the matter, the mere delay on the part of the Revenue Authorities in declaring the petitioner as a bhumidhar, can not be a ground to deprive him of the said right. Hon'ble Apex Court, in Fateh Singh's case(MANU/SC/0357/1983) has held that a tenant, who claims bhumidhari rights under Delhi Land Reforms Act on the date of its commencement, can not be deprived of such rights because there was some delay by the Revenue Authorities in declaring him a bhumidhar. Hon'ble High Court of Delhi, in the case reported as Dhanpati V Govt. of NCT of Delhi, W.P. No. 12961/2005, relying upon the judgment of Hon'ble Apex Court, in Fateh Singh's case (Supra) held that merely because there was delay on the part of the Revenue Authorities in declaring a tenant as a bhumidhar, the tenant can not be deprived of acquiring the said rights. In this case, it was held that the petitioner had acquired the bhumidhari rights as per law before acquisition of her land.

26. In the present case, the revenue records, duly proved on record by IP no.2, show that IP no. 2 had reclaimed the land in question completely 16 of 18 LAC No. 125/14 Union of India v Gaon Sabha Dhoolsiras & Others DOD : 11.11.2014 and was duly cultivating the land till its acquisition. The land was alloted to the IP no. 2 in the year 1986 under Twenty Point Programme and thereafter, he had reclaimed it within the period of five years. It is an admitted fact that the Gaon Sabha never initiated any proceedings for ejectment of IP no. 2 or for the cancellation of his lease. In such view of the matter, it is to be held that the IP no. 2 would have been declared a bhumidhar, had the land not been acquired by the Government. Further, in the Dhanpati's case (Supra), Hon'ble High Court of Delhi has clearly held that once a person is stated to have acquired the rights of a bhumidhar, the said declaration is to necessarily relate back to the date, of which such rights of declaration as a bhumidhar had accrued and it can not be said that the applicant would become bhumidhar only on the date of declaration. In view of the aforesaid judicial dicta, the IP no. 2 is deemed to have acquired the rights of a bhumidhar before the acquisition of the land. Consequently, the question remains to be decided, is with respect to the apportionment of the compensation.

27. Learned counsel for Gaon Sabha contended that IP no. 2 should only be granted compensation either in the ratio of 60:40 or 80:20 since the IP no. 2 remained a tenant in the land under reference. The contention of Gaon Sabha can not be upheld once this court has held that the IP no. 2 had acquired the status of bhumidhar before the acquisition of the land. The IP Suresh, who had acquired the status of bhumidhar of the land in question 17 of 18 LAC No. 125/14 Union of India v Gaon Sabha Dhoolsiras & Others DOD : 11.11.2014 before its acquisition, in my view, is entitled to 100% compensation of the acquired land and the Gaon Sabha can not be stated to have been left with any interest in the land under reference before its acquisition. As such, I hereby hold that the IP no. 2 Suresh is entitled to the 100% compensation in the present case.

28. The Reference Petition is answered accordingly. A copy of this Award be sent to the Land Acquisition Collector for his information. File be consigned to record room.

Announced in open Court on 11th November, 2014.
