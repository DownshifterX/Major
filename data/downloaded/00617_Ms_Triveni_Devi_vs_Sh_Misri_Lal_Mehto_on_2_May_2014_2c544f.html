Ms. Triveni Devi vs Sh. Misri Lal Mehto on 2 May, 2014

        IN THE COURT OF SHRI  KAPIL KUMAR, CIVIL 
        JUDGE­01 (WEST) TIS HAZARI COURTS, DELHI..

  SUIT FOR  POSSESSION, RECOVERY OF ARREARS OF 
             RENT AND MESNE PROFITS


JUDGMENT

Case of plaintiff

1. Plaintiff states that she inducted defendant as a tenant Suit No. 136/12 2 with respect to one room at the first floor with common toilet at the ground floor of the property bearing no. 146, Baba Faridpuri, West Patel Nagar, New Delhi ( hereinafter referred to as suit property) at the monthly rent of Rs.4,200/­ which includes Rs. 500/­ p.m as electricity charges and Rs.100/­ as water charges. Further states that defendant is habitual defaulter in payment of rent and he has not paid the rent w.e.f January 2012. Further states that the tenancy of defendant was terminated vide notice dated 05.5.2012 U/s 106 of Transfer of Property Act which was sent by way of registered post by which the defendant was called upon to vacate the suit property. Despite service of notice defendant not vacated the suit property and hence the present suit.

2. By virtue of present suit plaintiff is praying for the decree of possession of the tenanted premises alongwith recovery of arrears of rent and mesne profits @ Rs.300/­ per day till the recovery of possession of tenanted premises from defendant. Case of defendant

3. The suit is resisted by the defendant by filing a written statement wherein it is stated that the suit is bad for non­ joinder of necessary parties as after the death of original landlord/owner Sh. Kishan Lal all his legal heirs became the joint owners of the suit property and as such they are necessary parties Suit No. 136/12 3 to the present suit. Further states that suit is barred U/s 50 of Delhi Rent Control Act as the rate of rent is Rs.500/­ p.m.

4. Defendant further states that plaintiff concealed material facts from the court as the defendant was the tenant qua two rooms but during the life time of Sh. Kishan Lal, possession of one room was handed over to Late Sh. Kishan Lal and one room remained with the defendant of which he is paying rent @ Rs.500/­ p.m. Further states that husband of plaintiff was ill and defendant paid Rs.40,000/­ to the deceased husband of plaintiff for his treatment. Further states that plaintiff is pressurizing him to enhance the rent to Rs.2,000/­ p.m otherwise she will dispossess him from suit property without due process of law. Further states that on 07.4.2012 plaintiff alongwith her associates started throwing the goods,of the defendant, but the possession was saved due to intervention of other persons of the locality, for which a suit for permanent injunction was filed by the defendant which was disposed off on the basis of statement of the plaintiff herein that she will not dispossess the defendant herein without due process of law. Further states that suit is frivolous and is liable to be dismissed with cost.

5. Replication filed by the plaintiff wherein all the averments of the defendant were denied and stand of plaint was Suit No. 136/12 4 reiterated.

6. Vide Order dated 28.8.2012 following issues were framed :

1. Whether the plaintiff is entitled to the decree of possession as prayed for ?

OPP.

2. Whether the plaintiff is entitled to decree of arrears of rent as prayed for ?

OPP.

3 Whether the plaintiff is entitled to decree of damages as prayed for. If yes, at what rate ? OPP.

4. Whether the suit of the plaintiff is bad for mis­joinder of parties ? OPD.

5. Whether the suit of plaintiff is barred U/s 50 of DRC Act? OPD

6. Relief.

7. Plaintiff examined herself as PW­1, Sh. Roop Kishore as PW­2 and Sh. Sandeep Kumar as PW­3. They relied upon the following documents :

" Copy of legal notice as Ex PW 1/1;
Suit No. 136/12 5 postal receipts as Ex. PW1 /2 , receipt of courier as Ex. PW 1/3 ( colly. ); site plan as Ex. PW 1/ 4; the original rent agreement dated 05.11.2012 as Ex. PW 2/1 ".

8. In defence evidence Sh. Misri Lal Mehto was examined as DW­1, Sh. Kuldeep Kumar, Junior Judicial Assistant, Record Room as DW­2 and Sh Swatantra Yadav , Assistant Ahlmad of the court of Sh. S.K. Sethi, Ld. ACJ­Cum­ARC, Delhi as DW­3. They relied upon following documents :

" True copy of plaint of the suit bearing no. 787/12 as Ex. DW 2/1 , true copy of petition U/s 45 of DRC Act bearing no. U­14/12 as Ex. DW 3/1. "

9. I have heard arguments advanced by Ld. Counsels for parties and carefully perused the record.

10. My issuewise findings are as under :

ISSUE No. 4

Whether the suit of the plaintiff is bad for mis­joinder of parties ? OPD.

11. The onus to prove this issue was upon the defendant Suit No. 136/12 6 This issue was not pressed during the final arguments but the finding on this issue has to be given as per the mandate of Order XIV Rule 2 CPC.

12. It is stated by the defendant that the original landlord/owner of the suit property was Sh. Kishan Lal and after his death all the legal heirs of Sh. Kishan Lal became the joint owners of the suit property and thus are necessary parties to the present suit.

13. Present suit is a suit for eviction of a tenant. Defendant admits his capacity of being a tenant in the suit property. In the cross­examination defendant admitted that he tendered rent to the plaintiff. The relationship of landlord and tenant between plaintiff and defendant stands proved. It is not a suit for recovery of possession on the strength of title of plaintiff. The other legal heirs of Sh. Kishan Lal are not necessary parties to the present suit.

Issue is decided in favour of plaintiff and against the defendant.

14. ISSUE No.1 Whether the plaintiff is entitled to the decree of possession as prayed for ? OPP.

Suit No. 136/12 7 The onus to prove this issue was upon the plaintiff.

15. The capacity of defendant as tenant in the suit property is an admitted fact. In a suit for eviction of tenant three things has to be proved by the plaintiff : 

           (a)         relationship of landlord and tenant

           (b)         rent above Rs.3,500/­

            (c)        service of notice of termination of tenancy U/s 106 of 

                       Transfer of Property Act.

16. Defendant admits that he is a tenant qua one room at the first floor of the suit property. It is also admitted by the defendant/DW­1 during cross­examination that he tendered rent to the plaintiff after January 2012 also but she refused to accept the same. Relationship of landlord and tenant stands proved.

17. Defendant denied of receiving the legal notice dated 05.5.2012 U/s 106 of Transfer of Property Act Ex. PW 1/1. The postal receipt Ex. PW 1/ 2 and courier receipt Ex. PW 1/3 are placed on record as to the prove of delivery of Ex. PW 1/1. Ex. PW 1/ 2 and Ex. PW 1/ 3 bears the correct address of defendant. I found no reason to believe that Ex.PW 1/1 was not delivered to defendant. Even otherwise it is well settled law that service of summons of the suit for eviction of a tenant amounts to the notice U/s 106 of Transfer of Property Act.

Suit No. 136/12 8

Here reliance could be placed upon a judgment of Hon'ble Supreme court titled as Nopany Investments (P) Ltd. Vs. Santokh Singh ( HUF), I 2008 SLT 195 wherein the Hon'ble Supreme Court held that filing of suit is itself a notice to quit on the tenant and therefore, no notice to quit under Section 106 of the Transfer of Property Act is necessary to enable the landlord to get the decree of possession. The observations of the Hon'ble Supreme Court are reproduced hereunder :

" In any view of the matter, it is well settled that filing of an eviction suit under the general law itself is a notice to quit on the tenant. Therefore, we have no hesitation to hold that no notice to quit was necessary under Section 106 of the Transfer of Property Act in order to enable the respondent to get a decree of eviction against the appellant. "

Reliance could also be placed upon the well established judgment of Hon'ble High Court of Delhi titled as M/s Jeevan Diesels & Electricals Ltd. versus M/s Jasbir Singh Chadha (HUF) & Anr. RFA No.179/2011of 25th March, 2011 , wherein the court observed:­ Suit No. 136/12 9 " The Supreme Court in the case of Nopany Investments (P)Ltd. Vs.Santokh Singh (HUF) 2008 (2) SCC 728 has held that the tenancy would stand terminated under general law on filing of a suit for eviction. Accordingly, in view of the decision in the case of Nopany (supra) I hold that even assuming the notice terminating tenancy was not served upon the appellant (though it has been served and as held by me above) the tenancy would stand terminated on filing of the subject suit against the appellant/defendant.

The court further observed that:

"(iii) In the suits for rendition of accounts of a dissolved partnership at will and partition of HUF property, ordinarily it is required that a notice be given of dissolving the partnership at will or for severing the joint status before the filing of such suits because such suits proceed on the basis that the partnership is already dissolved or the joint status of an HUF stands severed by service of notices prior to the filing of such suits. However, it has been held in various judicial pronouncements that the service of summons in the suit will be taken as the receipt of notice of the dissolution of the partnership or severing of the joint status in case of non service of appropriate notices and therefore the suits for dissolution of partnership and partition of HUF property Suit No. 136/12 10 cannot be dismissed on the technical ground that the partnership was not dissolved before filing of the suit or the joint status was not severed before filing a suit for partition of the HUF property by serving of appropriate notices. In my opinion, similar logic can be applied in suits for possession filed by landlords against the tenants where the tenancy is a monthly tenancy and which tenancy can be terminated by means of a notice under Section 106 of the Transfer of Property Act. Once we take the service of plaint in the suit to the appellant/defendant as a notice terminating tenancy, the provision of Order 7 Rule 7 CPC can then be applied to take notice of subsequent facts and hold that the tenancy will stand terminated after 15 days of receipt of service of summons and the suit plaint. This rationale ought to apply because after all the only object of giving a notice under Section 106 is to give 15 days to the tenant to make alternative arrangements. In my opinion, therefore, the argument that the tenancy has not been validly terminated, and the suit could not have been filed, fails for this reason also. In this regard, I am keeping in view the amendment brought about to Section 106 of the Transfer of Property Act by Act 3 of 2003 and as per which Amendment no objection with regard to termination of tenancy is permitted on the ground that the legal notice did not validly terminate the Suit No. 136/12 11 tenancy by a notice ending with the expiry of the tenancy month, as long as a period of 15 days was otherwise given to the tenant to vacate the property. The intention of Legislature is therefore clear that technical objections should not be permitted to defeat substantial justice and the suit for possession of tenanted premises once the tenant has a period of 15 days for vacating the tenanted premises."

Reliance could also be placed upon Rabindernath Saha Vs. Sushma Jain, 182 (2011) DLT 456; Sh. Radha Krishnan Temple Trust Maitha, Agra Vs. M/s Hindco Rotaron Pvt. Ltd. (2012) DLT 548. The same view is of the Hon'ble Delhi High Court in the recent judgment of Skyland International Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Kavita P. Lalwani 191 (2012) DLT 594.

18. Now the issue remains as to the rate of rent of tenanted premises under the possession of defendant. Plaintiff averred that the rate of rent is Rs.4,200/­ p.m including electricity and water charges amounting to Rs.600/­. Per contra, defendant states that the rate of rent is Rs.500/­ p.m excluding electricity and water charges.

19. Admittedly, there is no document on record as to the documentary evidence of rate of rent of the tenanted premises. This issue requires appreciation of oral evidence keeping in mind Suit No. 136/12 12 Section 101 of Indian Evidence Act, 1872 as per which it is the burden of the plaintiff to prove that the rate of rent is more than Rs.3,500/­ p.m.

20. To prove her case plaintiff stepped into the witness box as PW­1 and reiterated the stand of plaint in examination in chief by way of affidavit Ex. PW 1/A. She was cross­examined by Ld. counsel for defendant wherein she denied the suggestion of Ld. counsel for defendant that the rent is Rs.500/­ p.m. She reiterated the averment of plaint that rent is Rs.3,600/­ excluding other charges.

21. PW­2 is Sh. Roop Kishore . In examination­in­chief he deposed that he is a tenant qua one room at the second floor of the suit property. He deposed that he is paying rent @ Rs. 3,600/­ p.m excluding electricity and water charges. He further deposed in examination in chief that he is a tenant in the suit property since 2010. He was cross­examined at length by Ld. counsel for defendant. In the cross­examination PW­2 deposed that he is paying rent at the rate of Rs.4,200/­ to the plaintiff. In examination in chief he deposed that his rent is Rs.3,600/­ p.m but in cross­examination he deposed that the rent is Rs. 4,200/­ p.m. He then deposed in cross­examination that he does not have rent agreement executed between him and plaintiff and the rent Suit No. 136/12 13 agreement is with the counsel for plaintiff. Cross­examination was deferred on that day for the want of rent agreement and on the next date of hearing PW­2 brought the rent agreement dated 05.11.2012 which was exhibited as Ex. PW 2/1. Now the poser is whether Ex. PW 2/1 can be relied upon or not ? The rent agreement does not came on record from the custody of a person in whose custody that agreement must have been in the ordinary course of events. PW­2 deposed that rent agreement is with the counsel for plaintiff. It is pertinent to mention here that he did not rely on rent agreement Ex. PW 2/1 in examination­in­chief, the same was brought on record during cross­examination. Why the rent agreement Ex. PW 2/1 was withheld during the examination in chief if that was the best evidence for plaintiff to prove the rate of rent of other tenants residing in the same property is more than Rs.3,500/­? Why the rent agreement was with the counsel for plaintiff on the day when the affidavit of PW­2 was tendered and why on that day rent agreement Ex PW 2/1 was not tendered in evidence? No explanation has been given as to why Ex.PW 2/1 was withheld from being produced in evidence at the first instance when admittedly, the rent agreement was with the counsel of plaintiff. Unnatural custody of Ex. PW 2/1 with the counsel for plaintiff and withholding the same during the evidence leads to Suit No. 136/12 14 serious doubts upon the authenticity of Ex.PW 2/1. 21.1. Ex. PW 2/1 is perused carefully. Rent agreement was prepared on 05.11.2012. It is mentioned in the rent agreement that the tenancy of PW­2 has commenced w.e.f. 01.10.2012. If the tenancy of PW­2 was already commenced from 01.10.2012 then why the rent agreement was prepared on 05.11.2012. What was the occasion for executing Ex. PW 2/1 after one month of induction of PW­2 as tenant. In examination in chief PW­2 deposed that he is tenant in the suit property since 2010. As per rent agreement Ex. PW 2 /1 he was inducted as tenant on 01.10.2012 it means that the term as to the induction of PW­2 as tenant on 01.10.2012 is wrongly mentioned in Ex.PW 2/1 or PW­2 is deposing falsely that he is the tenant in the suit property since 2010. PW­2 does not have any document to show that he is residing in the suit property since 2010. He did not even know the name of the other tenants residing in the suit property. If a person is residing for around three years in the same house then the person must have known the names of other tenants. In view of the above discussion, I am of the considered opinion, PW ­2 is not a reliable witness and Ex.PW 2/1 cannot be read as evidence as there are serious clouds upon the authenticity of Ex.PW 2/1.

22. The other witness of the plaintiff is Sh. Sandeep Suit No. 136/12 15 Kumar who deposed as PW­3. In examination in chief he deposed that he is the neighbour of plaintiff and defendant was inducted as a tenant by the plaintiff at the monthly rent of Rs.3,600/­ excluding other charges. He further deposed that since January, 2012 defendant stopped making any payment to the plaintiff. From the examination in chief of PW­3 it could be deduced that PW­3 , who is neighbour of plaintiff, was present with plaintiff in almost every occasion. During cross­examination he deposed that he does not know when the defendant was inducted as a tenant. He further deposed that defendant never paid rent in his presence and plaintiff told him that defendant is the tenant at the monthly rent of Rs.3,600/­. From this, it is clear that evidence of PW­3 qua the rate of rent is a hearsay evidence only because he admitted that plaintiff told him that defendant is tenant at the monthly rent of Rs.3,600/­. PW­3 admittedly that he never seen defendant paying rent to the plaintiff. It is clear that PW­3 does not have first hand knowledge of the facts on which he is deposing . The testimony of PW­3 as to the rate of rent is not admissible in evidence being hearsay evidence.

23. Plaintiff also placed on record the affidavit of one Sh. Deepak Kumar Dixit who mentioned in his affidavit that he is the tenant of plaintiff qua one room at the first floor of suit Suit No. 136/12 16 property at the monthly rent of Rs.3,600/­ since November, 2011. Surprisingly, plaintiff not called Sh. Deepak Kumar Dixit as her witness. This person deposed on behalf of plaintiff herein in petition U/s 45 of Delhi Rent Control Act filed by defendant herein before the court of Ld. ARC, Delhi wherein he deposed that he is tenant under the plaintiff since February, 2013. Before Ld. ARC, Delhi he mentioned his address as village Samogar, P.O Bamroli Katra, Agra , U.P. On the other hand, on the affidavit on record he mentioned his address as Faridpuri , West Patel Nagar, Delhi. It is clear from the affidavit of Sh. Deepak Dixit , filed on record that this person is deposing contrary facts after taking oath in front of oath commissioner. Here he averred that he is tenant under plaintiff since November ,2011 and before Ld. ARC,Delhi he deposed that he is tenant since February, 2013. Though the affidavit of Sh. Deepak Dixit was not tendered in evidence yet the conduct of plaintiff's witnesses could be commented upon who dares to depose contrary facts on oath and thereafter filed those affidavits before the courts.

24. On the other hand, defendant examined himself as DW­1 wherein he reiterated his stand of written statement in examination in chief by way of affidavit Ex DW 1/A. In Ex. DW 1/A defendant categorically deposed that the rent is Rs.500/­p.m. Suit No. 136/12 17 It is pertinent to mention here that DW­1 was not cross­examined as to his averment of rate of rent being Rs.500/­ p.m. There is not even suggestion from Ld. counsel for plaintiff that rent is Rs. 3,600/­ p.m. Not cross­examining DW­1 qua rate of rent amounts to admission of plaintiff as to the rate of rent being Rs.500/­ pm. It is well settled law that if a witness is not cross­examined upon a vital aspect that amounts to admission of the opposite party of that aspect. The whole defence of defendant is that the rent is Rs. 500/­p.m and on this fact DW­1 was not cross­examined which is the implied admission of plaintiff that rent is Rs.500/­p.m.

25. In the light of testimony of plaintiff's witnesses and the admission of the plaintiff as to the rent being Rs.500/­ by not cross­examining DW­1 on this aspect, it is proved on record that the rate of rent of the tenanted premises under the possession of plaintiff is Rs.500/­ p.m. Accordingly, the third condition i.e the rate of rent more than Rs.3,500/­ p.m not fulfilled and accordingly plaintiff is not entitled to recovery of possession from defendant because the defendant is protected tenant under Delhi Rent Control Act.

Issue is decided in favour of defendant and against the plaintiff.

Suit No. 136/12 18

26. ISSUE NO.2 Whether the plaintiff is entitled to decree of arrears of rent as prayed for ? OPP.

The onus to prove this issue was upon the plaintiff. Defendant admitted in the cross­examination that he has not paid the rent w.e.f. January, 2012. He deposed that plaintiff not accepted the rent from him. By virtue of admission of defendant it is clear that he is arrears of rent w.e.f January, 2012. Plaintiff is entitled to recovery of arrears of rent @ Rs.500/­ p.m from the defendant w.e.f. January 2012. Issue is decided in favour of plaintiff accordingly.

27. ISSUE No.3 Whether the plaintiff is entitled to decree of damages as prayed for. If yes, at what rate ? OPP.

The onus to prove this issue was upon the plaintiff. In view of findings on Issue No.1 plaintiff is not entitled to any damages from defendant.

Issue is decided in favour of defendant and against the plaintiff.

Suit No. 136/12 19 28. ISSUE NO. 5

Whether the suit of plaintiff is barred U/s 50 of DRC Act? OPD The onus to prove this issue was upon the defendant. In view of finding on Issue No.1 defendant is protected tenant under Delhi Rent Control Act.

Issue is decided in favour of defendant and against the plaintiff.

29. RELIEF In view of findings on above mentioned issues the suit of the plaintiff is partly decreed as to the recovery of arrears of rent from the defendant. Plaintiff is entitled to the recovery of arrears of rent from the defendant w.e.f January, 2012 at the rate of Rs.500/­ p.m alongwith future interest @ 9 % p.a. Parties to bear their own costs.

Decree Sheet be prepared accordingly.

File be consigned to record room after due compliance. 

Announced in the open court                                        ( Kapil Kumar)

today on 02.5.2014                                               Civil Judge­01 (West)/Delhi
