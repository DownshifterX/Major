Issue No. 5:
Whether the rate of rent is Rs. 3650/­ or Rs. 2000/­ as alleged by the parties? Onus of proof on parties

28. This issue is the most material issue in the present suit. The onus to prove the present issue lies upon the parties. It was upon the plaintiffs to prove that the rate of rent per month of the demised premises is Rs. 3,650/­ (Three Thousand Six Hundred and Fifty Rupees). Likewise, it was for the defendant to establish that the rate of rent was Rs. 2,000/­ (Two Thousand Rupees) per month. The issue is decided prior to the other issues as this issue will determine the subject matter jurisdiction of this Court.

Suit No.464/14 Sheela Devi Vs. Imran Qureshi Page 18 of 25

29. The plaintiffs have not adduced any documentary evidence in support of the fact that the rate of rent as mutually agreed between the parties is Rs. 3,650/­. In absence of any documentary evidence, the oral testimony needs to be scrutinized. PW­1 namely Sh. Harish Malik had deposed in his testimony for himself and on behalf of his mother/plaintiff no.1 that:

"...during the period of his tenancy 1998 the monthly rent was Rs.2,000/­(Two Thousand Rupees only) excluding water and electricity charges.""...the plaintiff no.1 and defendant mutually started the tenancy at the rate of Rs.3,000/­ per month..."."...the rent was mutually increased to the tune of Rs.3,300/­ in January 2005 and the defendant started paying Rs 3,300/­ per month...". "...in January 2008 defendant and plaintiffs mutually agreed the rent to be at the rate of Rs.3,650/­ per month...". "the defendant had paid the rent of Rs.3,650/­ per month in front of Sh.

Ravinder Malik and Surender Malik...".

30. During the cross examination PW­1 maintained that he did not know the initial rate of rent. He stated:

"In the year 2000, we used to take Rs. 3,000/­ from defendant as rent. In the year 2005, the rent was increased to Rs. 3,300/­ and subsequently in the year 2008 to Rs. 3,650/­."

31. Despite the oral testimony regarding the rate of rent being Rs. 3,650/­ there is no other evidence on record to prove that the rate of rent. The plaintiff took efforts to examine PW­2 and PW­3, but both the witnesses are relatives of the plaintiffs and the have seen the defendant tendering the rent only once. Though, they say that the rent Suit No.464/14 Sheela Devi Vs. Imran Qureshi Page 19 of 25 tendered was Rs. 3,650/­ per month but besides that both could not state any other details about the tenancy. The most important element in their testimonies is that they had seen the rent being tendered only once. PW­2 states that he had gone with the plaintiff no.2 to collect the rent from the defendant for the month of January 2008 but when was it paid, the exact rate of rent and such details are not known to the witness.

32. Similarly, PW­3 says that the rent was tendered in his presence at the plaintiffs's residence on plaintiff no.2's birthday. He has also like PW­2, stated that it was just once in his presence.

33. Though, both the witnesses have stated that the amount tendered was Rs.3,650/­ but still they did not know the rate of rent and other such details. It is also on record that both these witnesses are close relatives of the plaintiffs. Hence, only limited credibility can be given to their testimonies. Therefore, the testimonies of these witnesses do not fortify the oral testimony of the plaintiff.

34. Besides these oral testimonies, the plaintiffs have not led any other evidence to throw some light on the rate of rent.

35. Now, weighing the evidence adduced by the defendant, it can be seen that the defendant has placed reliance over Ex. DW1/A (OSR), dated 16.03.2005. The document recites that the plaintiff no. 1 has leased one of the shop at F­243, Katwaria Sarai to the defendant. It is also Suit No.464/14 Sheela Devi Vs. Imran Qureshi Page 20 of 25 stated in the document that the defendant has received the rent till 16.03.2005. The rent was agreed as Rs. 1,600/­ per month. The document has been witnessed by two witnesses­ Mr. I.A. Khan and Mr. Salim. Purportedly, the document also has the thumb impression of Smt. Sheela Devi on the stamp. The plaintiff no. 2 has denied this document stating that his mother/plaintiff no.1 is illiterate and does not know how to read. However, plaintiff no.1 despite good health and availability did not step into the witness box to rebut the document.
