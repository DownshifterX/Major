                          JUDGMENT

(Delivered on this 13th day of October, 2008) This is the defendants/tenants' second appeal against the reversing judgment and decree dated 06- 02-1997 passed in Civil Appeal No.106-A/1995 by the District Judge, Raipur, whereby the lower appellate Court has allowed the appeal by the landlord against the judgment and decree dated 24-07-1990 passed by the IVth Civil Judge Class-I, Raipur in Civil Suit No.75-A/1989 and granted a decree for eviction of the appellants/defendants from the suit shop.

(2) In this appeal, the location and dimensions of the suit shop and the shops adjacent thereto as shown in the map annexed to the plaint are not in dispute.

(3) On grounds identical to Civil Suit No.75- A/1989, Baldeo Prasad Gupta had instituted suit for eviction of one Iqbal Hassan from the shop adjacent to the suit shop on one side titled "Dressco Tailors" and had got vacant possession thereof on 14-04-1987. Another Civil Suit No.74-A/1989 for eviction of Ram Chandra Ballaya, the tenant in the other shop also adjacent on the other side to the suit shop was dismissed by the IVth Civil Judge Class-I, Raipur. The District Judge, Raipur vide judgment and decree dated 06-02-1997 allowed Civil Appeal No.101-A/1995 and ordered eviction of the tenant Ram Chandra Ballaya. In second appeal (Second Appeal No.266/1997) by the legal representatives of the tenant Ram Chandra Ballaya, this Court has, by judgment pronounced earlier during the day, allowed the appeal and remanded the matter to the lower appellate Court to proceed from the stage of consideration of the applications filed under Order 22 Rule 4 and under Order 32 Rule 3 of the Code of Civil Procedure on behalf of the legal representatives of the tenant Ram Chandra Ballaya.

(4) Relevant facts in brief in this appeal are as under. Baldeo Prasad Gupta instituted Civil Suit No.75-A/1989 in the Court of IVth Civil Judge Class- I, Raipur for eviction from the shop shown as bounded by red line in the map annexed to the plaint as a part of Maharani Bhawan situated at Chhotapara, Ward Baijnathpara bearing Municipal No.7/270, (henceforth `the suit shop), of one Ravindranath Bose who was carrying on business of dry cleaning and laundry under the name of "Calcutta Dry Cleaners" in the suit shop, on the ground of bona fide requirement for starting business of provisions and groceries and general goods of high and selected quality in retail and wholesale for himself and his major son Ramshankar Gupta. It was pleaded that the landlord had no other reasonably suitable accommodation of his own for the said purpose in the city.

(5) Ravindranath Bose resisted the suit on the ground that the need projected by the landlord was a mere pretext to let out the accommodation on exorbitant rent. Alternatively it was also pleaded that one of the shops adjacent to the suit shop in which Iqbal Hassan was carrying on business as "Dressco Tailors" had fallen vacant during pendency of the suit which was sufficient to meet bona fide requirement projected by the landlord. (6) The respondent/plaintiff Baldeo Prasad Gupta examined himself as P.W.-1 and his son Ramshankar Gupta as P.W.-2. Ravindranath Bose, the tenant deposed before the trial Court and examined one Vinod Bihari Ghosh, D.W.-2.

(7) The trial Court, on appreciation of evidence led by the parties dismissed Civil Suit No.75- A/1989 vide judgment and decree dated 24-07-1990 on the ground that the plaintiff Baldeo Prasad Gupta had failed to prove bona fide requirement as pleaded in the plaint. It was further held that the shop titled "Dressco Tailors" adjacent to the suit shop had fallen vacant and the plaintiff had not led any evidence to show why it did not satisfy the bona fide requirement projected by him in the plaint.

(8) Being aggrieved, Baldeo Prasad Gupta preferred Civil Appeal No.106-A/1995 before the District Judge, Raipur. During pendency of the appeal, Baldeo Prasad Gupta died and his three sons Vijay Shankar Gupta, Kailash Chandra Gupta and Ramshankar Gupta were substituted as legal representatives. By judgment and decree dated 06-02-1997, the learned District Judge allowed the appeal on the ground that the landlord had established bona fide requirement of the suit shop for himself and his son Ramshankar Gupta for carrying on business of provisions and general stores. The learned District Judge further held that the shop "Dressco Tailors" which had fallen vacant during the pendency of the suit was very small in area and was not sufficient to satisfy the bona fide requirement projected by the landlord.

(9) After the lower appellate Court's judgment, the tenant Ravindranath Bose died on 22- 03-1997. His legal representatives, i.e., the present appellants were permitted by this Court to prefer second appeal. During pendency of second appeal, Ramshankar Gupta also died in July, 2007. His widow Mrs. Shyama Gupta and daughters Ku. Ambika and Ku. Rani were substituted as legal representatives/respondents.

(10) During pendency of the second appeal, the appellants/defendants filed an application (I.A.No.1/07) under Order 6 Rule 17 read with Section 151 of the Code of Civil Procedure to amend the written statement by adding the following as paragraph 3A:

"3A.: The suit was brought by Baldev Prasad Gupta Under section 23A(b) of the Accommodation Control Act, 1961 requiring the suit shop under bona fide for himself and his major son Shri Ram Shankar Gupta for starting business in dealing in Kirana and grocery in general goods of high retail and wholesale. During the pendency of proceeding Baldev Prasad Gupta has breathed his last. The suit was brought on the cause of action based on bona fide need for Baldev Prasad and his major son Ram Shankar Gupta. Presently as has been revealed from the Municipal record, after death of Baldev Prasad all the 3 sons have mutually partitioned the suit house bearing 270 from 1 to 3 as shown in the plaint map and the suit house has devolved on Kailash Chandra Gupta. The appellant therefore reiterates that the cause of action on which the suit for ejectment was brought have been taken/washed away with the subsequent happening. The respondent therefore is liable to be non suited since the cause of action on which the suit was brought have been washed out and suit is liable to be dismissed."

In support of the application, the appellants/defendants filed certain documents evidencing that in a family partition the suit shop had fallen to the share of Kailash Chandra Gupta, respondent No.1(b) herein and was also mutated in his name in municipal records. This fact was admitted by Kailash Chandra Gupta, respondent No.1(b) herein by filing an affidavit before this Court.

(11) Respondents No.1(c-i), (c-ii) and (c-iii) herein also filed an application under Order 6 Rule 17 read with Section 151 of the Code of Civil Procedure on 03-10-2007 to amend the plaint by substituting paragraph 3 of the plaint as under:

"3. During the pendency of the matter before the courts of law, as luck would have it, not only the original plaintiff namely Baldeo Prasad Gupta died but also the person for whose need i.e. said Late Ram Shankar Gupta has also expired on 23.7.2007. Ironically, when the suit has been filed the said Late Ram Shankar Gupta was about 28 years of age and wanted to start business of dealing in provisions and groceries of high & selected quality in retail and wholesale packaged spices i.e. Garam Masala etc. in the said shop and further to install grinding machine etc., as narrated on oath before the court below. The original plaintiff has also therefore, instituted a eviction suit against the other tenants namely Iqbal Hussain & Ramchandra for recovery of the vacant possession of the respective shops adjacent to the suit accommodation. As luck would have it the original plaintiff Baldeo Prasad Gupta and said Late Ram Shankar Gupta have expired and now the applicants wish to fulfill the said desire of Late Ram Shankar Gupta but also to manage the family left behind by said Late Ram Shankar Gupta, bonafidely required the suit shop to start the said business in the suit shop and other adjacent shops and thus, the plaintiffs are entitled for decree of ejectment under Section 12(1)(f) of the C.G. Accommodation Control Act, 1961. For starting the said business the applicants' i.e. legal representatives of the said Late Ram Shankar Gupta have no reasonable and suitable accommodation in the city of Raipur. At this juncture the applicants say and submit that right to sue have been crystallized on the date of institution of the suit by the original plaintiff and though during pendency of the matter the aforesaid incidents of death of original plaintiff and also the death of said Ram Shankar Gupta has taken place, but the death of said persons do not extinguish the need of the plaintiffs particularly need of the applicants being the legal heirs of the said Late Ram Shankar Gupta to start the said business in the suit shop. The applicants say and submit that applicant Nos.2 & 3 have become major and after the death of said Ram Shankar Gupta, the applicant No.1 has no other option but to manage the affairs out of her own, as they have no other source of income. Thus, the need of the applicants is bona fide and genuine and therefore, the applicants are entitled for the decree of ejectment against the defendants/appellants herein and are claiming the same from this Hon'ble Court. The applicants say and submit that as per the law settled in this regard by the Hon'ble Apex Court the applicants can claim even at this stage of the proceedings ejectment of the defendant/appellant from the suit shop and even after the death of the original plaintiff and Late Ram Shankar Gupta it cannot be said that their need for the suit accommodation has come to an end. Thus, this Hon'ble Court in this stage of proceedings also grants a decree in favour of the applicants and against the appellant seeking their ejectment from the suit shop. The plaintiffs at this stage say and submit that they will fix and install a small door in the common wall between the suit accommodation and adjacent shops in the possession of the other tenants to run the business effectively which otherwise cannot be run only by user of the suit shop only."

(12) In view of the affidavit filed by Kailash Chandra Gupta, learned counsel for the appellants/defendants and the respondents also did not press on the abovementioned applications which were dismissed on 31-07-2008 as not pressed. (13) Earlier on 29-1-1998 the appeal was admitted by the High Court of Madhya Pradesh on the following substantial question of law:

"(i) Whether in view of the evidence of the respondent Baldeo Prasad, the original plaintiff to the effect that he needed the suit shop for provision stores as well as for opening the grinding machine, the bona fide need of the respondent Baldeo Prasad was established as he had pleaded that the suit shop was required only for opening a provision stores?"

At the hearing, following questions of law were added on 31-07-2008 as under:

"(ii) Whether the lower appellate Court was justified in reversing the judgment and decree of dismissal of suit passed by the lower Court and granting a decree for eviction under Section 12(1)(f) of the Chhattisgarh Accommodation Control Act, 1961?
(iii) Whether in view of the admission by Kailash Chandra Gupta that in a family partition, which took place subsequently, the suit accommodation has fallen to his share, the bona fide need of the legal representatives of the landlord under Section 12(1)(f) of the Chhattisgarh Accommodation Control Act, 1961 comes to an end and the decree for eviction passed by the lower appellate Court can be sustained in the eye of law?"

(14) Shri Gautam Bhaduri, learned counsel for the appellants/defendants assailed the impugned judgment and decree dated 06-02-1997 passed in Civil Appeal No.106-A/1995 by the District Judge, Raipur on the ground that on facts the lower appellate Court was not justified in reversing the well reasoned finding recorded by the trial Court that bona fide requirement as projected by the landlord was not established under law. It was also urged that the availability of alternative accommodation during pendency of the suit and the fact that the landlord or Ramshankar Gupta had not started his business in the said shop vacated by "Dressco Tailors" demolished the need projected by the landlord. It was also argued that in view of death of the landlord and Ramshankar Gupta the bona fide requirement for vacation of the suit shop had ceased to exist. Lastly, it was argued that the admission by Kailash Chandra Gupta before this Court in second appeal that in a family partition the suit shop had fallen to his share completely washed away the cause of action on which the suit for ejectment was brought and the respondents were, therefore, liable to be non-suited. (15) On the other hand, Shri B.P.Sharma, learned counsel for the respondents argued that the landlord is required to establish bona fide requirement of the landlord on the date of the institution of the suit and even if a decree for eviction is passed in first appeal, death of the landlord during pendency of the first appeal will make no difference as his heirs were fully entitled to defend the estate. Once the lower appellate Court had decreed the suit in favour of the landlord on the ground that the landlord had established such bona fide requirement for the suit accommodation, a legal right crystallized in favour of the landlord which could be defended by his legal representatives. On this premise, it was urged that even though the suit shop had, in a family partition, fallen to the share of Kailash Chandra Gupta, he, being a legal representative of the deceased landlord Baldeo Prasad Gupta, was entitled to defend the estate and derive benefit from the decree passed by the lower appellate Court for eviction of the appellants/defendants. (16) Having heard rival submissions, I have perused the record.

(17) The preamble of the Chhattisgarh Accommodation Control Act, 1961 (henceforth `the Act') reads as under:

"An Act to provide for the regulation and control of letting and rent of accommodations, for expeditious trial of eviction cases on ground of "bona fide" requirement of certain categories of landlords and generally to regulate and control eviction of tenants from accommodations and for other matter connected therewith or incidental thereto."

(18) As the preamble shows, the Act has been enacted to regulate and control eviction of tenants and for expeditious trial of eviction cases on the ground of bona fide requirement of certain categories of landlords. Section 2(b) of the Act defines a "landlord" and it reads as under:

"2.(b): `landlord' means a person, who, for the time being, is receiving, or is entitled to receive, the rent of any accommodation, whether on his own account or on account of or on behalf of or for the benefit of, any other person or as a trustee, guardian or receiver for any other person or who would so receive the rent or be entitled to receive the rent, if the accommodation were let to a tenant and includes every person not being a tenant who from time to time derives title under a landlord;"

(19) Chapter III of the Act deals with control of eviction of tenants and sub-section (1) of Section 12 therein lays down that notwithstanding anything to the contrary contained in any other law or contract, no suit shall be filed in any civil court against a tenant for his eviction from any accommodation except on one or more of the grounds enumerated in the sub-section. Clause (f) of this sub-section reads as under:

"12.(1)(f): that the accommodation let for non-residential purposes is required bona fide by the landlord for the purpose of continuing or starting his business or that of any of his major sons or unmarried daughters if he is the owner thereof or for any person for whose benefit the accommodation is held and that the landlord or such person has no other reasonably suitable non-residential accommodation of his own in his occupation in the city or town concerned;"

(20) As mentioned earlier, in first appeal the original landlord Baldeo Prasad Gupta died during the pendency of his appeal. A decree for eviction of the tenant was not subsisting on the death of the landlord Baldeo Prasad during pendency of first appeal since the trial Court had dismissed the suit. The first question, which, therefore, requires consideration, is whether on account of death of the landlord during pendency of his appeal, the appeal must fail as the requirement of the suit shop by the landlord comes to an end. (21) In Shakuntala Bai and others vs. Narayan Das and others, (2004) 5 SCC 772, the Apex Court considered its several decisions on the effect of death of the landlord during pendency of the proceedings as under:

"10. The effect of death of a landlord during the pendency of the proceedings has been considered in several decisions of this Court. In Phool Rani v. Naubat Rai Ahluwalia, (1973) 1 SCC 688 the landlord filed an ejectment application under Section 14(1)(e) of the Delhi Rent Control Act and eviction of the tenant was sought on the ground that the premises were required by the plaintiff "for occupation as a residence for himself and members of his family". The Additional Rent Controller dismissed the application on a preliminary ground that the notices to quit were not valid, without examining the case on merits. The plaintiff died during the pendency of the appeal preferred by him and his heirs were substituted. The case was remanded and the Rent Controller passed an order of eviction. In appeal a contention was raised that the right to sue did not survive to the heirs of the plaintiff, which was rejected by the Rent Control Tribunal but was accepted in appeal by the High Court. This Court held that different results may follow according to the stage at which the death occurs. One of the situations considered in para 13 of the Report is as under: [SCC p. 694, para 13(i)]

13.(i) : cases in which the death of the plaintiff occurred after a decree for possession was passed in his favour; say, during the pendency of an appeal filed by the unsuccessful tenant;

10.1. With regard to this category of cases, it was held that the estate is entitled to the benefit which, under a decree, has accrued in favour of the plaintiff and, therefore, the legal representatives are entitled to defend further proceedings, like an appeal, which constitutes a challenge to that benefit. Even otherwise, this appears to be quite logical. In normal circumstances after passing of the decree by the trial Court, the original landlord would have got possession of the premises. But if he does not and the tenant continues to remain in occupation of the premises it can only be on account of the stay order passed by the appellate court. In such a situation, the well- known maxim "actus curiae neminem gravabit" that "an act of the court shall prejudice no man" shall come into operation. Therefore, the heirs of the landlord will be fully entitled to defend the appeal preferred by the tenant and claim possession of the premises on the cause of action which had been originally pleaded and on the basis whereof the lower court had decided the matter and had passed the decree for eviction.

However, in regard to the case before the court it was held that the requirement pleaded in the ejectment application on which the plaintiff founded his right to relief was his personal requirement and such a personal cause of action must perish with the plaintiff. On this ground it was held that the plaintiff's right to sue will not survive to his heirs and they cannot take the benefit of the original right to sue. In Shantilal Thakordas v. Chimanlal Maganlal Telwala, (1976) 4 SCC 417, a larger Bench overruled the decision rendered in Phool Rani v. Naubat Rai Ahluwalia, (1973) 1 SCC 688 insofar as it held that the requirement of the occupation of the members of the family of the original landlord was his personal requirement and ceased to be the requirement of the members of his family on his death. The Court took the view that after the death of the original landlord the senior member of his family takes his place and is well competent to continue the suit for eviction for his occupation and occupation of the other members of the family. Thus, this decision held that the substituted heirs of the deceased landlord were entitled to maintain the suit for eviction of the tenant. The ratio of this decision by a larger Bench does not in any manner affect the view expressed in Phool Rani (supra) that where the death of the landlord occurs after a decree for possession has been passed in his favour, his legal representatives are entitled to defend further proceedings like an appeal and the benefit accrued to them under the decree. In fact, the ratio of Shantilal Thakordas (supra) would reinforce the aforesaid view. There are several decisions of this Court on the same line. In Kamleshwar Prasad v. Pradumanju Agarwal, (1997) 4 SCC 413, it was held that the need of the landlord for the premises in question must exist on the date of application for eviction, which is the crucial date and it is on the said date the tenant incurred the liability of being evicted therefrom. Even if the landlord died during the pendency of the writ petition in the High Court, the bona fide need cannot be said to have lapsed as the business in question can be carried on by his widow or any other son. In Gaya Prasad v. Pradeep Srivastava, (2001) 2 SCC 604, it was held that the crucial date for deciding as to the bona fides of requirement of the landlord is the date of his application for eviction. Here the landlord had instituted eviction proceedings for the bona fide requirement of his son who wanted to start a clinic. The litigation continued for a long period and during this period the son joined the Provincial Medical Service and was posted at different places. The subsequent event i.e. the joining of the service by the son was not taken into consideration on the ground that the crucial date was the date of filing of the eviction petition. Similar view has been taken in G.C.Kapoor v. Nand Kumar Bhasin, (2002) 1 SCC 610. Therefore, the legal position is well settled that the bona fide need of the landlord has to be examined as on the date of institution of proceedings and if a decree for eviction is passed, the death of the landlord during the pendency of the appeal preferred by the tenant will make no difference as his heirs are fully entitled to defend the estate."

In Shakuntala Bai and others vs. Narayan Das and others (supra), it was further observed in paragraphs 13 and 14 as under:

"13. The limited question for consideration in this case was whether a decree which had attained finality would become unexecutable on account of death of the landlord and this question was answered in favour of the landlord and against the tenant basically on the principle that the executing court cannot go behind the decree. For the decision of the appeal it was wholly unnecessary to examine the question as to the effect of death of the landlord during the pendency of the appeal preferred by the tenant after a decree for eviction has been passed. The decisions rendered in Phool Rani (supra) and Shantilal Thakordas (supra) were not brought to the notice of the Bench. We are, therefore, of the opinion that the observations made in the aforesaid case that (SCC p. 322, para 18) events which take place subsequent to the filing of an eviction petition under any Rent Act can be taken into consideration for the purpose of adjudication until a decree is made by the final court determining the rights of the parties, which are more in the nature of obiter do not represent the correct legal position.
14. Sub-section (1) of Section 12 of the Act says "no suit shall be filed in any civil court against a tenant for his eviction ..". The language employed does not say "no decree shall be passed ..".

So the bar created is against filing of the suit except on one of the grounds enumerated in clauses (a) to (p) of the sub-section. Therefore what is to be seen is whether the suit was validly filed i.e. whether on the date of filing of the suit one of the grounds was made out. A suit validly filed cannot be scuttled or held no longer maintainable in absence of any specific provision to that effect. Therefore the principle that "the need of the landlord must exist till the decree for eviction is passed by the last court and attains finality" can even otherwise have no application here in view of the express language used in the section."

The observations of the Supreme Court in Shakuntala Bai and others vs. Narayan Das and others (supra) in paragraph 15 are also noteworthy and are reproduced as under:

"15. As the preamble shows, the Madhya Pradesh Accommodation Control Act, 1961 has been enacted for expeditious trial of eviction cases on the ground of bona fide requirement of landlords and generally to regulate and control eviction of tenants. If the subsequent event like the death of the landlord is to be taken note of at every stage till the decree attains finality, there will be no end to litigation. By the time a second appeal gets decided by the High Court, generally a long period elapses and on such a principle if during this period the landlord who instituted the proceedings dies, the suit will have to be dismissed without going into merits. The same thing may happen in a fresh suit filed by the heirs and it may become an unending process. Taking into consideration the subsequent events may, at times, lead to rendering the whole proceedings taken infructuous and colossal waste of public time. There is no warrant for interpreting a rent control legislation in such a manner, the basic object of which is to save harassment of tenants from unscrupulous landlords. The object is not to deprive the owners of their properties for all times to come."

(22) The legal principles discernible from the above referred decisions of the Supreme Court are as under:

"(i) The bona fide need of the landlord has to be examined as on the date of institution of the suit or proceedings.
(ii) Where a decree for eviction is passed, the death of the landlord during pendency of the appeal preferred by the tenant will make no difference as his heirs are fully entitled to defend the estate.

     (iii)      For ascertaining whether  bona
     fide     requirement    of    the    suit
accommodation by the landlord exists on the date of suit, subsequent events after the institution of the suit shall ordinarily be not taken into consideration on the ground that the crucial date for determining the bona fide requirement of the landlord is the date of suit."

(23) Reliance was placed by Shri Gautam Bhaduri, learned counsel for the appellants/defendants on Smt. Shakuntala Rao & others vs. Dr. Smt. Uma Gupta & others, 2007(2) CGLJ 97, wherein it was held that if the landlord, after the order for eviction is passed against the tenant, alienates the demised house his bona fide requirement ceases thereby disentitling the transferee from executing the decree against the tenant. In that case, reliance was placed by this Court on Ramesh Kumar vs. Kesho Ram, AIR 1992 SC 700 and Pasupuleti Venkateswarlu vs. The Motor & General Traders, AIR 1975 SC 1409 for taking the above view. In Ramesh Kumar vs. Kesho Ram (supra), it was observed as under:

"The normal rule is that in any litigation the rights and obligations of the parties are adjudicated upon as they exist at the commencement of the lis. But this is subject to an exception. Wherever subsequent events of fact or law which have a material bearing on the entitlement of the parties to relief (emphasis supplied by me) or on aspects which bear on the moulding of the relief occur, the court is not precluded from taking a cautious cognizance of the subsequent changes of fact and law to mould the relief."

In Pasupuleti Venkateswarlu vs. The Motor & General Traders (supra), it was held by the Supreme Court as under:

"For making the right or remedy claimed by the party just and meaningful as also legally and factually in accord with the current realities, the court can, and in many cases must, take cautious cognizance of events and developments subsequent to the institution of the proceeding provided the rules of fairness to both sides are scrupulously obeyed.
Where during the pendency of a proceeding under Rent Control legislation by the landlord for permission to evict the tenants, a subsequent event in the facts of the case takes place which has a material bearing on the landlord's right to evict, (emphasis supplied by me) the approach of the High Court in revision, in taking cognizance of the new development cannot be said to be wrong or illegal."

(24) The case of Smt. Shakuntala Rao & others vs. Dr. Smt. Uma Gupta & others (supra) was under

Section 23-A of the Act, which is a special provision for eviction of the tenant on the ground of bona fide requirement of special category of persons who fall under the definition of landlord under Section 23-J of the Act. Section 23-A of the Act reads as under:
"23-A: Special provision for eviction of tenant on ground of bona fide requirement. - Notwithstanding anything contained in any other law for the time being in force or contract to the contrary, a landlord may submit an application, signed and verified in a manner provided in rules 14 and 15 of Order VI of the First Schedule to the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 (V of 1908) as if it were a plaint to the Rent Controlling Authority on one or more of the following grounds for an order directing the tenant to put the landlord in possession of the accommodation, (emphasis supplied by me) namely:-
(a) that the accommodation let for residential purposes is required "bona fide" by the landlord for occupation as residence for himself or for any member of his family, or for any person for whose benefit, the accommodation is held and that the landlord or such person has no other reasonably suitable residential accommodation of his own in his occupation in the city or town concerned.

Explanation.- For the purposes of this clause, "accommodation let for residential purposes" includes-

(i) any accommodation which having been let for use as a residence is without the express consent of the landlord, used wholly or partly for any non- residential purpose;

(ii) any accommodation which has not been let under an express provision of contract for non- residential purpose;

(b) that the accommodation let for non-residential purposes is required "bona fide" by the landlord for the purpose of continuing or starting his business or that of any of his major sons or unmarried daughters, if he is the owner thereof or for any person for whose benefit the accommodation is held and that the landlord or such person has no other reasonably suitable non-residential accommodation of his own in his occupation in the city or town concerned:

Provided that where a person who is a landlord has acquired any accommodation or any interest therein by transfer, no application for eviction of tenant of such accommodation shall be maintainable at the instance of such person unless a period of one year has elapsed from the date of such acquisition."
(25) A comparison of Section 23-A of the Act with Section 12 of the Act shows that the words "for an order directing the tenant to put the landlord in possession of the accommodation" appearing in Section 23-A of the Act are omitted in sub-section (1) of Section 12 of the Act. The reason is not far to seek. Section 23-A of the Act is a speedy remedy to a special class of persons falling within the definition of "landlord" under Section 23-J of the Act. If the landlord after obtaining an order directing the tenant to put him in possession of the accommodation alienates the accommodation, it would be open to the tenant to urge before the revisional Court that the bona fide requirement on the basis of which the landlord had sought eviction of the tenant had ceased to exist because this would have a material bearing on the entitlement of the landlord to relief. However, in case of death of landlord, the situation would be different because under Section 23-A(a) of the Act the landlord may require bona fide the accommodation let for residential purposes as residence for himself or for any member of his family or for any person for whose benefit the accommodation is held.

Similarly, in a case where the accommodation is let for non-residential purpose, the landlord may require the same for continuing or starting his business or that of any of his major sons or unmarried daughters if he is the owner thereof or for any person for whose benefit the accommodation is held. In both the cases, the death of the landlord, after an order for eviction of tenant is passed, may not provide a ground to the tenant to urge before the revisional Court that the need for which the accommodation was sought to be evicted has come to an end due to death of the landlord/owner.

(26) In a similar situation, in Usha P. Kuvelkar & Ors. vs. Ravindra Subrai Dalvi, 2007 AIR SCW 7247, where the original landlord Prabhakar Govind Sinai Kuvelkar had expired after the eviction order was passed by the Additional Rent Controller under Section 23-A of the Goa Daman & Diu Buildings (Lease, Rent & Eviction) Control Act, 1968, introduced during the pendency of the eviction case, an amendment to the Section came into force with effect from 22-02-1988 providing a right to the landlord to recover immediate possession of the premises if such landlord was an employee of the State Government and had duly retired and required the premises for personal bona fide occupation for himself or any member of his family. The contention of the counsel for the respondent that since the landlord had died, the need had expired with him, was repelled by the Supreme Court inter alia on the ground that the landlord had sought possession not only for himself but also for his family members and there was a clear reference in Section 23(1)(a)(i) of the Goa Daman & Diu Buildings (Lease, Rent & Eviction) Control Act, 1968 regarding requirement for occupation of the family members of the landlord. Thus, the case of Smt. Shakuntala Rao & others vs. Dr. Smt. Uma Gupta & others (supra) is distinguishable. (27) In the present case, the suit instituted by the landlord was under Section 12(1)(f) falling under Chapter III of the Act and not under Chapter III-A of the Act. Although the trial Court had dismissed the suit, during pendency of the first appeal it was open to the legal representatives of Baldeo Prasad Gupta to show that the bona fide requirement projected by Baldeo Prasad Gupta for the suit accommodation existed on the date of suit. Death of Baldeo Prasad Gupta during pendency of the first appeal would make no difference because Ramshankar Gupta for whose bona fide requirement the suit was instituted along with his two brothers was substituted in place of the deceased appellant Baldeo Prasad Gupta. On the date of the judgment and decree by the District Judge, Raipur, Ramshankar Gupta was alive. The requirement of the suit shop by the landlord, i.e., Baldeo Prasad Gupta did not come to an end upon his death during pendency of the first appeal for another reason because the requirement projected by the landlord was not only for himself but also for his major son Ramshankar Gupta.

(28) The next question that arises for determination of this appeal is whether the lower appellate Court was justified in reversing the judgment and decree of dismissal of suit passed by the lower Court and granting a decree for eviction under Section 12(1)(f) of the Act? In other words, whether it was proved that suit shop was required bona fide by the landlord for himself and his major son Ramshankar Gupta for starting business of provisions and groceries and general goods of high and selected quality in retail and wholesale. Burden of proving the bona fide requirement under Section 12(1)(f) of the Act was on the plaintiff/landlord. The vital fact which the lower appellate Court ought to have borne in mind is that the trial Court had dismissed the suit by a well reasoned judgment from paragraphs 6 to 11. (29) In Santosh Hazari vs. Purushottam Tiwari (Deceased) by LRs., (2001) 3 SCC 179, the Supreme Court has held that while writing a judgment of reversal the appellate Court must remain conscious of two principles. Firstly, the finding of fact based on conflicting evidence arrived at by the trial Court must weigh with the appellate Court and it is only when the appraisal of the evidence by the trial Court suffers from material irregularity or is based on inadmissible evidence or on conjectures and surmises, the appellate Court is entitled to interfere with the finding of fact. The appellate Court should not interfere with the finding of the trial judge on a question of fact unless there is sufficient balance of improbability to displace the opinion of the trial Court as to the credibility of the evidence. Lastly, reversing the finding of fact the appellate Court must come into close quarters with the reasoning assigned by the trial Court and then assign its own reasons for arriving at a different finding. In the present case, the lower appellate Court did not come into close quarters with the reasoning assigned by the trial Court. The judgment of the lower appellate Court did not reflect its conscious application of mind to the reasons given by the trial Court in paragraphs 6 to 11 for granting a decree for eviction under Section 12(1)(f) of the Act.

(30) Two things weighed heavily with the lower appellate Court while reversing the trial Court's judgment. Firstly, that the tenant Ravindranath Bose D.W.1 had admitted in cross-examination paragraph 5 as under:

";g dguk lgh gS fd esjk edku ekfyd rhuks pkjks nqdkukssa dks bDBBk [kkyh djkdj Lo;a dk /ka/kk pkyq djkuk pkgrk gSA lk{kh us Lor% dgk fd ;g rks gj edku ekfyd dk dguk gksrk gS fd fdjk;k c<+kvks ugha rks nqdku [kkyh djksA"

Secondly, that the shop "Dressco Tailors" vacated by Iqbal Hassan was much smaller than the suit shop and was therefore not suitable for the requirement projected by the landlord. So far as the admission in paragraph 5 by Ravindranath Bose D.W.1 is concerned, the testimony of Baldeo Prasad Gupta itself did not show that the suit shop was required bona fide by him for carrying on his own business. All that he had stated in paragraph 3 was that he was thinking of establishing a grinding shop in the shop let out to Ram Chandra Ballaya. So far as the suit accommodation and the suit shop "Dressco Tailors" vacated by Iqbal Hassan is concerned, the testimony of Baldeo Prasad Gupta clearly projected the need only for his major son Ram Shankar Gupta. It is also pertinent to note that nowhere in the testimony of Ram Shankar Gupta the need of Baldeo Prasad Gupta for carrying on business in the suit shop or in the shop vacated by "Dressco Tailors" is projected.

(31) So far as the statement of the tenant in paragraph 30 (supra), had the tenant by making such statement meant what the lower appellate Court thought, there was absolutely no necessity for any further cross-examination or adducing evidence. In other words, if the tenant, by making such a statement, had admitted the bona fide requirement of the landlord, there would have been no necessity to proceed further in the trial. The lower appellate Court failed to notice that immediately after the said statement was recorded the witness had volunteered that every landlord makes such statement. It appears that the witness wanted to say that although the landlord was proclaiming such a need, it was usually the say of every landlord. The manner in which the entire examination-in-chief and the cross-examination of the tenant Ravindranath Bose proceeded does not rule out the possibility that the trial Court was not vigilant enough while recording the deposition of the tenant in the first line of paragraph 5. In any case, since the burden of proving requirement under Section 12(1)(f) of the Act was on the plaintiff, the lower appellate Court ought to have considered the reasonings given by the trial Court and appreciated the evidence led by the plaintiff before dislodging the finding recorded by the trial Court on the basis of such a statement. The lower appellate Court did not do so.

(32) The finding recorded by the lower appellate Court that "Dressco Tailors" was much less in dimension than the suit shop is erroneous because in the map annexed to the plaint the dimensions of the suit shop as also the shop vacated by "Dressco Tailors" are given. While the length of both the shops is 23'6", width of the shop vacated by "Dressco Tailors" is 9'3", whereas the width of the suit shop is shown to be 8'8". In paragraph 15, Baldeo Prasad was not able to explain why the shop vacated by "Dressco Tailors" was not sufficient for the business of provisions and groceries by Ramshankar Gupta. In this manner, the lower appellate Court was not justified in dislodging the finding recorded by the trial Court on this count. So far as the suit shop is concerned, in paragraph 15, he stated that it would be used as a store and their Kotha is situated behind the suit shop. If the need was genuine, Ramshankar Gupta would have started the business though at a small scale in the shop "Dressco Tailors" using the Kotha as store temporarily.

(33) The lower appellate Court also did not examine the evidence relating to bona fide requirement with objectivity with reference to the material on record. The suit for eviction was instituted on 10- 01-1985. Ramshankar Gupta, who was examined later before the trial Court, stated categorically that till 1986 he was serving on a salary of Rs.1,500/- per month in Kailash Bartan Bhandar in Sadar Bazar, which was owned by his father Baldeo Prasad Gupta. He further admitted that the salary drawn by him was sufficient to meet the expenses of his family. A perusal of paragraph 2 of his statement reveals that counsel for the landlord/plaintiff was so frustrated by the answers given by Ramshankar Gupta during examination-in-chief that he had to literally warn him that if he did not answer the questions properly he will withdraw from the case. This attitude of Ramshankar Gupta in paragraphs 1 and 2 of his testimony further projects that till he was warned by the counsel, he had not expressed his desire for starting the business of provisions and groceries and general goods of high and selected quality in retail and wholesale in the suit shop.

(34) The proceeding for eviction of Ravindranath Bose initially commenced before the Rent Controlling Authority on 10-01-1985, and subsequently got transferred to the Civil Court. Paragraph 1 of the testimony of Ramshankar Gupta, which was recorded on 07-10-1987, goes to show that till 1986 he was already serving as a paid employee in Kailash Bartan Bhandar, Sadar Bazar, Raipur. Baldeo Prasad Gupta has admitted in paragraph 5 that Kailash Bartan Bhandar was established in the year 1956 and was a very old shop in Sadar Bazar. He further admitted in paragraph 8 that Sadar Bazar is a marketing area, whereas Baijnathpara where the suit shop is situated is a residential area. On 07- 10-1987, Baldeo Prasad Gupta stated on oath that his son Ramshankar Gupta had passed LL.B. in the year 1983 and joined the office of Chartered Accountant Shri Chopra. He admitted that even on the date of his deposition Ramshankar Gupta was attending the office of Chartered Accountant Shri Chopra. Ramshankar Gupta was thus a law Graduate and on the date of suit was regularly attending the office of Chartered Accountant Shri Chopra for learning sales-tax practice. Baldeo Prasad had further admitted that Ramshankar Gupta had, after passing law, applied for a licence to practice and had never worked in a provision stores. (35) It is not disputed that during pendency of the suit, the shop "Dressco Tailors" had fallen vacant on 14-04-1987 as admitted by Baldeo Prasad Gupta on 07-10-1987 in his testimony. Baldeo Prasad Gupta further stated that since the adjacent shop "Dressco Tailors" had fallen vacant, he had asked Ramshankar Gupta to start the shop of provision stores at least on a small scale therein but Ramshankar Gupta had refused and the shop had remained closed. It is true that for seeking eviction under Section 12(1)(f) of the Act the landlord is required to prove that on the date of suit he had no other reasonably non-residential accommodation of his own in his occupation in the city or town concerned but the conduct of Ramshankar Gupta or Baldeo Prasad Gupta on the happening of a subsequent event i.e. vacation of the adjacent shop has, in my considered opinion, material bearing on the bona fide requirement projected by the landlord on the date of suit and in view of the decision of the Supreme Court in Pasupuleti Venkateswarlu (supra) can be taken into consideration for ascertaining whether the requirement projected by the landlord on the date of suit was bona fide or not. The above mentioned conduct of Ramshankar Gupta proves that he had no desire to start a business of provisions and groceries and general goods of high and selected quality in retail and wholesale in the suit shop despite the fact that the adjacent shop vacated by "Dressco Tailors" was of almost equal dimension. If the requirement was bona fide, Ramshankar Gupta would have started his business of provisions and groceries and general goods of high and selected quality in retail and wholesale in the said shop at least on a small scale till vacation of the suit shop so that he could expand his business after the suit shop was vacated.

(36) The testimony of Baldeo Prasad Gupta further depicts that on the date of suit, in a shop in the name of Kailash Bartan Bhandar situated in Sadar Bazar, Raipur, a triple storied building, Ramshankar Gupta used to work and also lived there with his family and was separate in mess from his father. In his deposition on 07-10-1987 he admitted in paragraph 21 that Kailash Chandra Gupta, who was working as a Commission Agent for Metal Non-ferrous Metal, was looking after the shop since about a year, i.e., since October, 1986. If this is to be believed, it would go to show that on the date of suit, i.e., 10-01-1985 Kailash Chandra Gupta was already working as a Commission Agent for Metal Non-ferrous Metal and Ramshankar Gupta was not only required to look after the shop Kailash Bartan Bhandar as a paid employee, but was also residing in the first floor with his family and was separate in mess with his father. Therefore, the fact that on 14-04-1987 when Baldeo Prasad Gupta obtained possession of "Dressco Tailors" and asked Ramshankar Gupta to start the business of provisions and groceries in the shop "Dressco Tailors" at a small scale, the blunt refusal by Ramshankar Gupta to do so clearly demolishes the bona fide requirement of the suit shop for Ramshankar Gupta as projected by the landlord for eviction of the tenant. Ramshankar Gupta was so much unconcerned about the suit shop and its location that in paragraph 3 he admitted in his statement on 07-10-1987 that he had only heard a whisper that the shop "Dressco Tailors" had fallen vacant. It shows that for three years after receiving possession of "Dressco Tailors" by the landlord Baldeo Prasad Gupta, Ramshankar Gupta was completely unconcerned about it.

(37) In paragraph 15, Baldeo Prasad Gupta admitted that Ramshankar Gupta was handicapped in the right hand due to Polio. The fact that despite vacation of the shop "Dressco Tailors" on 14-04-1987, it remained vacant and was never used by Ramshankar Gupta or Baldeo Prasad Gupta for starting business of provisions and groceries during their lifetime, demolishes the need projected by them in the suit in toto. I am, therefore, of the considered opinion that the lower appellate Court did not assess the evidence with objectivity and erred in reversing a well reasoned finding recorded by the trial Court that the landlord had failed to establish bona fide requirement of the suit shop for himself as also for Ramshankar Gupta as projected in the plaint. The second question of law is, therefore, answered in the negative and in favour of the appellants/defendants. (38) On the first substantial question of law, suffice it to say that upon refusal by Ramshankar Gupta to carry on the projected requirement of the suit shop in the adjacent shop "Dressco Tailors"

vacated by Iqbal Hassan even Baldeo Prasad Gupta did not start any such business in the said accommodation till he was alive. On vacation of "Dressco Tailors" by Iqbal Hassan, it remained closed till date. In paragraph 12 of his testimony, Baldeo Prasad Gupta had clearly stated that he needed the suit shop only for Ramshankar Gupta. In paragraph 9 also, he stated that since 1984 he was considering for opening of a shop for Ramshankar Gupta. It is also pertinent to note that in the entire testimony of Ramshankar Gupta, it did not appear that the suit shop was required bona fide by Baldeo Prasad Gupta. I have perused the testimony of Baldeo Prasad Gupta with utmost circumspection and also the well reasoned finding of the trial Court and am of the considered opinion that the evidence of Baldeo Prasad Gupta fails to satisfy the bona fide requirement of the suit shop for his own non-residential use. The first question of law is therefore answered in the negative.
(39) The last substantial question of law is now more or less academic in view of the finding recorded by this Court that the lower appellate Court was not justified in reversing the judgment and decree of dismissal of suit by the trial Court by granting a decree for eviction under Section 12(1)(f) of the Act. Kailash Chandra Gupta, being the legal representative of Baldeo Prasad Gupta, was also substituted in place of the original landlord/appellant Baldeo Prasad Gupta in the first appeal. The learned District Judge granted a decree for eviction under Section 12(1)(f) of the Act. It is also to be noticed that the second appeal was preferred on 27-03-1997 and the family partition between the three brothers took place on 20-04-1997, i.e., after the judgment and decree passed in the first appeal. Therefore, being the legal representative of late Baldeo Prasad Gupta, Kailash Chandra Gupta in whose favour a decree for eviction under Section 12(1)(f) of the Act was passed by the lower appellate Court was entitled to defend the estate and to benefit by such decree by seeking eviction of the tenant. Assuming for the sake of arguments that no second appeal was preferred, the decree for eviction of the tenant having been passed under Section 12(1)(f) of the Act by the first appellate Court would have attained finality. Even though Ramshankar Gupta would have died subsequently, Kailash Chandra Gupta would be entitled to defend the estate of Baldeo Prasad Gupta and to execute the decree, even after the death of the landlord or one of the major sons Ramshankar Gupta for whose bona fide requirement eviction was sought. Once such a decree attained finality, it would not become unexecutable on account of death of the original landlord or Ramshankar Gupta or on account of the fact that in a family partition, which was subsequent to the decision in the first appeal, the suit accommodation had fallen to the share of Kailash Chandra Gupta. In this view of the matter, the last substantial question of law is also answered in the negative.
(40) In the result, the appeal succeeds. The judgment and decree dated 06-02-1997 passed in Civil Appeal No.106-A/1995 by the District Judge, Raipur is set aside while affirming the judgment and decree dated 24-07-1990 passed by the IVth Civil Judge Class-I, Raipur in Civil Suit No.75-

A/1989. No order as to costs.
