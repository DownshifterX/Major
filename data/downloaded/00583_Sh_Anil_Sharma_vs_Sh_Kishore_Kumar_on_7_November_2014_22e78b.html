E. No.146/09           Sh. Anil Sharma Vs. Sh. Kishore Kumar            Page No.1 of 15
 JUDGMENT:

­

1. Vide this judgment I shall dispose of an eviction petition filed by the petitioner u/s 14 (1) (a) of Delhi Rent Control Act on the ground of non­payment of rent by the respondent.

2. Brief facts, as stated in the petition, are that the respondent is a tenant in respect of one shop bearing no. 67/3, Subhash Market Kotla Mubarakpur, New Delhi­110003 under the petitioner at a monthly rent of Rs.300/­ and w.e.f. 01.10.2005 the rate of rent was enhanced to Rs.330/­ per month. It is stated that the respondent is a habitual defaulter and he has not been paying the rent month by month and has not paid the arrears of rent despite service of the legal demand notice dated 11.08.2005 and 01.02.2007. Hence, it is stated that the respondent is liable to pay rent w.e.f. 01.03.2004 to 30.09.2005 @ Rs.300/­ p.m. and from 01.10.2005 to 30.11.2006 @Rs.330/­p.m. which he failed to pay. Hence, it has been prayed that an eviction order may be passed in favour of the petitioner and against the respondent in respect of the suit premises u/s 14 (1) (b) of Delhi Rent Control Act, 1958.

3. The respondent has contested the petition by filing the written statement in which it is stated that the petition has not come to the court with clean hands and has suppressed and concealed the material facts. It is also stated that respondent regularly paid the E. No.146/09 Sh. Anil Sharma Vs. Sh. Kishore Kumar Page No.2 of 15 rent to Sh. Jagdish Chand Sharma who is father of the petitioner till 30.11.2006. It is further stated that present petition filed without any cause of action as respondent has never made any default in making payment of rent till 30.11.2006. It is also stated that due to dispute between the petitioner and his father the respondent stopped paying the rent as he was not sure as to whom the rent should be paid. It is also stated that the respondent is ready to pay the rent in the court from 01.12.2006 under Section 15(4) of the DRC Act subject to final outcome. It is further stated that present petition is bad for non joinder of necessary parties as Sh. Jagdish Chand Sharma father of the petitioner has not been impleaded as a party. The other contents of the petition are denied and the respondent has prayed for dismissal of the petition.

4. The petitioner has filed the replication in which contents of the written statement are denied and the petitioner has reiterated and re­affirmed the averments made in the petition.

5. In order to prove his case, the petitioner examined his father Sh. Jagdish Chand Sharma as PW1 who tendered his evidence by way of affidavit and relied upon SPA Ex. PW1/1, Site Plan Ex.PW1/2, Counter foil of rent receipts Ex.PW1/3 to Ex.PW1/5, last payment receipt no.011 Ex.PW1/6, copy of the legal notice dated 11.08.2005 Ex.PW1/7, postal receipts (Colly.) E. No.146/09 Sh. Anil Sharma Vs. Sh. Kishore Kumar Page No.3 of 15 Ex.PW1/8, receipt of AD Card Ex.PW1/9 and Ex.PW1/10, notice sent at the shop received back with the report shop locked Ex.PW1/11, envelop containing notice Ex.PW1/11 Ex.PW1/12, copy of the legal notice dated 01.02.2007 Ex.PW1/13, postal receipt (Colly.) Ex.PW1/14, AD card Ex.PW1/15, another AD card Ex.PW1/16, envelop Ex.PW1/17 and UPC receipt Ex.PW1/18 and he was duly cross examined. He also examined himself as PW­2 and has filed evidence by way of affidavit in which he has reiterated the averments made in the petition. He has placed on record the SPA dated 13.03.2009 is Ex.PW1/1, site plan as Ex.PW­1/2, rent receipts are Ex.PW1/3 to Ex.PW1/6, legal notice is Ex.PW­1/7, demand notice is Ex.PW1/13.

6. On the other hand, the respondent has examined himself as RW­1 and filed evidence by way of affidavit which is Ex. RW1/1 in which he has reiterated and re­affirmed the averments made in the written statement.

7. I have heard the Ld. counsel for the parties and perused the record carefully.

Ground U/s 14(1)(a) of DRC Act

8. The ingredients required to be proved for eviction u/s 14 (1) (a) of DRC Act are:

(i) Existence of relationship of landlord and tenant between the E. No.146/09 Sh. Anil Sharma Vs. Sh. Kishore Kumar Page No.4 of 15 parties;
(ii) Existence of arrears of rent, legally recoverable rent from the date of notice of demand; and
(iii) Service of notice of demand in the manner as provided in section 106 of the Transfer of Property Act and
(iv) Failure of tenant to pay or tender the whole of the arrears of rent legally recoverable from him within two months of date of service of notice.

9. The ingredients are taken for consideration one by one:

(1) Relationship as land lord and tenant.

PW1 as well as PW2 both stated that the Sh. Anil Sharma is the land lord who authorized PW1 to collect the rent from the tenant for the suit property. But in the written statement respondent has taken the plea that the respondent is under bonafide believe and impression that Sh. Jagdish Chand Sharma is the land lord and therefore paid rent to him till 30.11.2006. It is further alleged that Sh. Jagdish Chand Sharma has told him about his dispute with the petitioner and therefore he stopped further payment of monthly rent to Sh. Jagdish Chand Sharma. In his cross examination PW1 categorically stated that his son is the owner of the suit property and there was no litigation between his son and the erstwhile owner. Even PW2 stated during cross E. No.146/09 Sh. Anil Sharma Vs. Sh. Kishore Kumar Page No.5 of 15 examination that his father was collecting rent from the respondent under his authorization and there was no dispute regarding the same between them.

10. On the other hand respondent appear in the witness box as RW1 and during his cross examination he shown his ignorance as to whether petitioner authorized Sh. Jagdish Chand Sharma to collect the rent on his behalf. But at the same time he admitted that rent was never paid to the Anil Sharma. He also admitted that petitioner and his father both are living under the same roof and he had not written any letter to either of them for issuance of rent receipts. He admitted that he has not placed any document on record to show that there was any difference between the petitioner and his father. He also admitted that neither the petitioner nor his father put any pressure upon him at any time.

11. At no point the respondent has denied that he was paying the rent to the Jagdish Chand Sharma and has also not denied that Anil Sharma is the owner/land lord but has taken a vague plea that Jagdish Chand Sharma informed him about some dispute between the petitioner and Jagdish Chand Sharma. But no evidence to show alleged dispute was produced before the Court. One Mukesh Thakur who is the witness to the alleged fact of dispute between the petitioner and Sh. Jagdish Chand Sharma was E. No.146/09 Sh. Anil Sharma Vs. Sh. Kishore Kumar Page No.6 of 15 also not produced by respondent. Thus it can be specifically concluded that there is land lord and tenant relationship between the parties as same was not denied by the respondent. (2) Rate of rent

12. As per the petitioner the rate of rent was Rs.300/­ per month w.e.f. 01.03.2004 to 30.09.2005 and thereafter by notice dated 11.08.2005 the rent was enhanced by 10% to Rs.330/­ per month w.e.f. 01.10.2005 and accordingly the respondent is stated to be in arrears of rent.

13. In the reply to the petition the respondent filed written statement wherein he admitted the rate of rent as Rs.300/­ per month but stated that the rent @300 per month was paid till 30.11.2006.

14. Under these circumstances it is duty of the petitioner to prove the rate of rent that it was initially fixed as Rs.300/­ per month and subsequently enhanced to Rs.330/­ per month.

15. In support of his claim the petitioner has filed Ex.PW1/3 to Ex.PW1/6 which are the rent receipts issued for rent of suit premises @Rs.300/­ per month. In support of the increased rate of rent the copy of the legal notice was proved as Ex.PW1/7 and the postal receipts as Ex.PW1/8 to Ex.PW1/12. Ex.PW1/11 is the unserved copy of the legal notice which received back with the E. No.146/09 Sh. Anil Sharma Vs. Sh. Kishore Kumar Page No.7 of 15 report that shop lying locked for the past 8­9 months and the addressee is not available. Further notice was given on 01.02.2007 which is Ex.PW1/13 on which RC post was served and AD Card of the receipt was filed as Ex.PW1/15 and Ex.PW1/16 at the residential address of the respondent. During cross examination PW1 and PW2 no suggestion has been put that no such notice was issued by the petitioner on the other hand PW2 denied the suggestion that the rent @Rs.300/­ per month for the period of 01.03.2004 to 30.11.2006 and was paid by the respondent. Even in his affidavit filed as RW1, no averments has been pleaded that no such legal notice was served upon him regarding enhancement of rent. Thus from the deposition of evidence and the documents produced including the rent receipts and legal notice, I am satisfied that rate of rent was initially fixed @Rs.300/­ per month for the period w.e.f. 01.03.2004 to 30.09.2005 which was subsequently enhanced to Rs.330/­ per month w.e.f. 01.10.2005.

(3) Arrears of rent.

16. Now the question before the court is whether the respondent is in arrears of rent on the date of notice. PW1 and PW2 both stated on oath that the respondent is in arrear of rent w.e.f 01.03.2004 to 30.09.2005 @Rs.300/­ per month and from 01.10.2005 @Rs.330/­ per month.

17. In reply to the contention of the petitioner the respondent denied that he was in arrear of rent and on the contrary alleged that since he had some apprehension of dispute between the petitioner and his father, he stopped paying the rent since 30.11.2006. The rent receipts filed by the petitioner shows the last payment of rent up to month of February, 2004 vide receipts no. 011 as Ex.PW1/6. He also stated as and when rent was paid, the receipts were issued. All the suggestions put forth by the respondent regarding non issuance of rent receipt was denied by PW1 and he also denied that in November, 2006 respondent came to his house and paid rent but he refused to issue the receipts on the pretext that keys of the Almira are lying with other family members who had gone outside. He denied that rent till 30.11.2006 was paid in the presence of Mukesh Thakur. PW1 also denied that there was no arrears of rent. At the same time PW2 stated that rent was collected by his father and receipts were issued. In respondent evidence even though RW1 denied that he has paid rent only up to February, 2004 and not paid rent till 30.11.2006 but admitted that he has not written any letter to the petitioner or his father for issuance of rent receipts. He has also admitted that Mukesh Thakur did not accompanied him every time when he go for payment of rent. Here again it was noted that despite opportunity the said E. No.146/09 Sh. Anil Sharma Vs. Sh. Kishore Kumar Page No.9 of 15 Mukesh Thakur in whose presence the rent up to 30.11.2006 was allegedly paid was not produced by respondent

18. It has been observed in Supreme Court Judgment "Karamchand Deojee Sanghavi Vs. Tulshiram Kalu Kumawat"

1992(1) RCR 118 that in case of eviction of rent the onus is always upon the tenant to prove that he has paid rent and there was no arrear of rent due against him as claimed in the legal demand notice which can be provided either through oral or documentary evidence. But respondent has neither produced any witness to the factum of payment of rent up to 30.11.2006 nor produce any documentary evidence to the same. Thus it is proved fact that no rent paid or received after February 2004 and as such respondent is in arrears of rent since 01.03.2004 to 30.09.2005 @300/ per month and @Rs.330/­ per month w.e.f. 01.10.2005 to 30.11.2006. That a valid legal demand notice was duly served upon the respondent:

19. As far as service of demand notice dated 11.08.2005 EX PW­1/7 same was served as per acknowledgment card received. The service of legal notice dated 11.08.2005 as well as 01.02.2007 is proved against the respondent and despite opportunity no evidence contrary to the presumption under Section 27 of General Clause Act is led by respondent no.2. No plea has been taken that E. No.146/09 Sh. Anil Sharma Vs. Sh. Kishore Kumar Page No.10 of 15 petitioner has sent legal notice at the wrong address or insufficiently stamped and service through UPC as well as registered cover is proved against the respondent. Respondent has not led any evidence to the contrary. Hence the legal notice is deemed to be served upon the respondent.

That despite the service of the legal demand notice the respondent has neither paid nor tendered the entire arrears of legally recoverable rent:

20. The respondent has admittedly not paid the rent after 30.11.2006. But he also failed to show any proof of payment of rent for the period 01.03.2004 to 30.11.2006. On the other hand petitioner denied the suggestions put forth regarding payment till 30.11.2006. The service of legal notice was proved by the petitioner. Even RW1 admitted that he had not demanded the rent receipts from either from the petitioner or Sh. Jagdish Chand Sharma.

21. Another important issue in the facts and circumstances of present case is that whether the respondent was legally required to deposit rent in court under section 27 of Delhi Rent Control Act if the petitioner refused to accept the rent or their is any confusion as to the person entitled to receive the rent.

22. At this stage it is pertinent to refer to the judgment of Honorable Supreme Court in judgment titled "Sarla Goel Vs E. No.146/09 Sh. Anil Sharma Vs. Sh. Kishore Kumar Page No.11 of 15 Kishan Chand"[2009 (7) SCC 658] wherein it was held that "..........Section 27 deals with deposit of rent by the tenant. It clearly says that where the landlord does not accept any rent tendered by the tenant within the time referred to in Section 26 or refuses or neglects to deliver a receipt referred to therein or where there is a bona fide doubt as the person or persons to whom the rent is payable, the tenant may deposit such rent with the Controller in the prescribed manner. When the words 'bona fide doubt' has been added to Section 27, the tenant may remit such rent to the Controller by postal money order. From a conjoint reading of this provision referred to herein above and particularly Section 27 of the Act, in our view, it cannot be doubted that the procedure having been made by the Legislature how the rent can be deposited if it was refused to have been received or to grant receipt for the same. If that be the position, if such protection has been given to the tenant, the said procedure has to be strictly followed in the matter of taking steps in the event of refusal of the landlord to receive the rent or to grant receipt to the tenant. It is well settled that whether the word 'may' shall be used as 'shall', would depend upon the intention of the Legislature. It is not to be taken that once the word 'may' is used by the Legislature in Section 27 of the Act, would not mean that the intention of the Legislature was only to show that the provisions under Section 27 of the Act was directory but not mandatory.

In other words, taking into consideration the object of the Act and the intention of the Legislature and in view of the discussions made herein earlier, we are of the view that the word 'may' occurring in Section 27 of the Act must be construed as a mandatory provision and not a directory provision as the word 'may' , in our view, was used by the E. No.146/09 Sh. Anil Sharma Vs. Sh. Kishore Kumar Page No.12 of 15 Legislature to mean that the procedure given in those provisions must be strictly followed as the special protection has been given to the tenant from eviction. Such a cannon of construction is certainly warranted because otherwise intention of the Legislature would be defeated and the class of landlords, for whom also, the beneficial provisions have been made for recovery of possession from the tenants on certain grounds, will stand deprived of them."

23. From the aforesaid judgment it is clear that if the landlord refuses to accept rent or their is confusion about the landlord, then the tenant is required to deposit rent in court under section 27 of Delhi Rent Control Act and in case he does not deposit rent in the court, then he cannot claim that he is protected from eviction under section 14(1)(a) of Delhi Rent Control Act because he has tendered rent to the landlord and that the landlord refused to accept the rent. The tenant/respondent admittedly has not moved any application under Section 27 of Delhi Rent Control Act and taken a plea that due to confusion as to the person to whom the rent is to be paid he did not deposited the same.

24. Thus, the court is of the opinion that even if the stand of the respondent that he was under confusion as to the person to whom rent is to be paid he is still under legal obligation to deposit rent in court under section 27 of Delhi Rent Control Act, but, the respondent failed to discharge the said obligation as per the E. No.146/09 Sh. Anil Sharma Vs. Sh. Kishore Kumar Page No.13 of 15 evidence on record.

25. Further,in the present case interest at the rate of 10% as per Delhi Rent Control Act has been specifically demanded in para 7 of the Legal Notice Ex.PW­1/7. As such the judgment of Honorable Delhi High Court in " Raghbir Singh vs. Sheela Wanti & Anr"[ 2009 (109) DRJ 19] also applies squarely to the fact of the present case and as such the respondent was under an obligation to tender interest on rent along with rent, which the respondent failed to tender.

26. In view of the above discussion the court is of the opinion that despite the service of the legal demand notice the respondent has neither paid nor tendered the entire arrears of legally recoverable rent.

Conclusion:

27. From the above said discussion, it is clear that the petitioner has proved all the ingredients of Section 14(1)(a) of DRC Act and, therefore, the present petition is decided in favour of the petitioner and against the respondent with respect of tenanted premises i.e. shop No 67/3, Subhash Market, Kotla Mubarak Pur, New Delhi depicted in red colour in the site plan Ex.PW1/2.

28. In the foregoing circumstances, the respondent is held to be a defaulter within the meaning of Section 14(1)(a) of DRC E. No.146/09 Sh. Anil Sharma Vs. Sh. Kishore Kumar Page No.14 of 15 Act.

29. Accordingly, the respondent is directed to pay the arrears of rent w.e.f.01.03.2004 to 30.09.2005 @Rs300/­per month and w.e.f. 01.10.2005 to 30.11.2006 @Rs.330/­ per month along with statutory interest @ 10% per annum thereupon in the court within one month from today .

30. If the respondent fails to deposit the aforesaid arrears within one month from today, the petitioner shall be entitled to recover the same by filing an execution in that regard.

31. Separate file be maintained for consideration as regards benefit under section 14(2) DRC Act.

32. No order as to costs. This file be consigned to Record Room. 




Announced in open court                              (Pawan Singh Rajawat)
on 07.11.2014                                       Additional Rent Controller 
                                                         (South), New Delhi
