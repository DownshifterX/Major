Calcutta High Court (Appellete Side)

Smt. Kamala Naskar & Ors vs Sri Gopal Maity & Ors on 22 December, 2017

Author: Ashis Kumar Chakraborty

Bench: Ashis Kumar Chakraborty

                            IN THE HIGH COURT AT CALCUTTA
                                 Civil Appellate Jurisdiction
                                        Appellate Side

Present :
The Hon'ble Mr. Justice Ashis Kumar Chakraborty

                                                 S.A. 37 of 2015

                                          Smt. Kamala Naskar & Ors.
                                                  Vs.
                                          Sri Gopal Maity & Ors.


For the appellants-petitioners               :   Mr. Hiranmoy Bhattacharyya
                                                 Mr. Tanmoy Mukherjee
                                                 Mr. Saunak Bhattacharya

For the respondents                         :    Mr. Asit Baran Raut
                                                 Mr. Tuhin Raut



Judgement on     :    22.12.2017


Ashis Kumar Chakraborty, J.

This second appeal is directed against the judgment and decree dated May 08, 2006 passed by the learned Additional District Judge, Fast Track Court-III, Sealdah in Title Appeal No. 41 of 2004, affirming the judgment and decree for eviction passed by the learned Civil Judge (Junior Division), 1st Court, Sealdah IN Title Suit No. 429 of 1996.

The facts relevant for deciding this second appeal are stated hereinbelow. The respondents filed the suit, being Title Suit No. 429 of 1996, against late Bhim Chandra Naskar (hereinafter referred to as "Bhim Chandra") claiming a decree for his eviction from the suit property, comprising one room, with veranda under tile shed at premises no. 3/H/17, Tarini Charan Ghosh Lane, P.S. Chitpore, Kolkata. It was the plaint case that the plaintiffs' predecessor Kanailal Maity, as the thika tenant, inducted Bhim Chandra a bharatia in respect of the suit property; after the demise of their predecessor and with the promulgation of the Calcutta Thika and other Tenancies and Lands (Acquisition and Regulation) Act, 1981 (hereinafter referred to as "the said Act of 1981"), the plaintiffs became the thika tenants of the suit property and the defendant continued to be a bharatia in respect of the suit property under them. In view of the provisions contained in Section 9 the Act of 1981 the plaintiffs, as the thika tenants claiming eviction of the defendant bharatia was governed by the provisions of the West Bengal Premises Tenancy Act, 1956 (hereinafter referred to as "the Act of 1956"). The grounds urged by the plaintiffs for eviction of the defendant Bhim Chandra were, inter alia, that the latter was a defaulter in payment of rent, he was guilty for creating nuisance and annoyance at the suit property and that the plaintiffs reasonably require the suit property for their own use and occupation. The plaintiffs claimed that they filed the eviction suit after issuance of a notice dated June 08, 1996, under Section 13(6) of the Act of 1956 to Bhim Chandra and the latter contested the suit and filed his written statement. In his written statement, Bhim Chandra alleged that the plaintiffs are not the owners of the suit property and the Government of West Bengal is the owner of the suit property. He, however, admitted to have been inducted as a tenant in respect of the suit property by Kanailal Maity and after his death the plaintiff no. 1, as the landlady collected the monthly rent for the suit property from him. Bhim Chandra further claimed that upto the month of July, 1992 he paid rent to the plaintiff no. 1 but from the month of August, 1992, the plaintiff no. 1 refused to accept the rent for the suit property from him. It was further alleged that since the plaintiff no. 1 refused to accept the rent tendered through money order, he deposited all rent for the suit property before the Rent Controller and after filing of the suit he had been depositing the rent for the suit property before the learned trial Court. Therefore, Bhim Chandra, as the defendant, denied to be the defaulter in payment of rent for the suit property. On consideration of the averments made by the plaintiffs and the defendant in their plaint and the written statement, respectively the learned trial Judge framed the following issues:

1. Is the suit maintainable either in law or in fact?
2. Is the notice to quit valid, proper, sufficient or duly served upon the defendant?
3. Is the defendant "defaulter" in payment of rent?
4. Is the defendant guilty for creating nuisance in the suit premises as alleged?
5. Have the plaintiffs any reasonable requirement of the suit premises?
6. Are the plaintiffs entitled to get decree as prayed for?

At the trial of the suit the plaintiff nos. 1 to 4 themselves and one Khagendra Narayan Bera adduced evidence and they were cross-examined. Bhim Chandra, the defendant alone adduced evidence for himself. Considering the pleadings of the respective parties and after appreciating the evidence, both oral and documentary, adduced by the respective parties, the learned trial Judge held that the defendant admitted the relationship of landlords and tenant between the plaintiffs and himself and the notice dated June 08, 1996 issued by the plaintiffs under Section 13(6) of the Act of 1956 was a valid and lawful notice and the same was duly received by the defendant. The learned trial Judge further held that the plaintiffs are the owners of the suit property and they reasonably require the suit property. The learned trial Judge, however, decided that the issues whether the defendant is a defaulter in payment of rent and whether the defendant is guilty of any act of nuisance at the suit property in the negative. By a judgment dated May 11, 2004 the learned trial Judge held since the plaintiffs proved the ground of reasonable requirement of the suit property, they are entitled to get the decree for eviction and passed the decree dated May 11, 2004 directing the defendant to quit and vacate the suit property within sixty days from date, failing which the plaintiffs would be at liberty to put the decree into execution.

Feeling aggrieved by the said judgment and decree dated May 11, 2004 Bhim Chandra, the defendant carried the same in appeal, being Title Appeal No. 41 of 2004, before the learned appellate Court.

After considering the pleadings of the parties and re-appreciating the evidence adduced by the respective parties, the learned appellate Court below found that in the instant case the relationship between the plaintiffs and the defendant that of the landlords and tenant is admitted, the notice of suit under Section 13(6) of the Act of 1956 issued by the plaintiffs is a valid notice which was received by the defendant and the plaintiffs' requirement of the suit property for their further accommodation is not only genuine but also bona fide. The learned appellate Court below further held that there is nothing to interfere with the finding of the learned trial Judge that the suit filed by the plaintiffs to be maintainable. With these findings, by the judgment and decree dated May 08, 2006 the learned appellate Court below dismissed the appeal and affirmed the judgment and decree passed by the learned trial Judge. It appears that after dismissal of the said appeal by the learned appellate Court below, Bhim Chandra, the sole defendant died and thereafter his wife, seven sons and two daughters filed the present second appeal. The appellants also filed an application under Section 5 of the Limitation Act for condonation of delay in preferring the appeal. The prayer of the appellants for condonation of delay was allowed and on July 26, 2016 the Division Bench of this Court admitted this second appeal by framing the following substantial question of law.

"Whether the Civil Court has jurisdiction to try a suit for eviction of a bharatia in a suit filed by a Thika tenant under Section 8(3) read with Section 21 of the West Bengal Thika Tenancy (Acquisition and Regulation) Act, 2001."

At the outset of the hearing of the appeal, Mr. Raut, learned advocate appearing for the plaintiffs/respondents strongly contended that none of the judgments or decrees passed by the learned Courts below suffers from any infirmity of law and this appeal does not involve any substantial question of law. According to him, although it is a fact that the learned trial Judge passed the judgment and decree for eviction dated May 11, 2004 against the predecessor of the present appellants when the West Bengal Thika Tenancy (Acquisition and Regulation) Act, 2001 had come into force, but in view of Section 27(2) of the Act of 2001 expressly providing that repealing of the Act of 1981 shall not affect any legal proceeding filed under the said Act, the learned trial Judge had the jurisdiction to decide the ejectment suit and even the learned appellate Court below had the jurisdiction to decide the appeal, being Title Appeal No. 41 of 2004.

Mr. Hiranmoy Bhattacharyya, learned advocate appearing for the appellants did not dispute the above contention raised on behalf of the respondents-plaintiffs. He, however, urged that in the instant case both the learned Courts below committed an error of law in passing the decree for eviction against the said predecessor of the present appellants when the plaintiffs/respondents failed to prove to be the owners of the suit property. It was submitted by the appellants that although it is a fact that in view of the provisions contained in Section 9 of the Act of 1981, the eviction proceeding initiated by a thika tenant against a bharatia is to be governed by the Act of 1956 before the Civil Court, but in order to succeed in a suit for eviction against a bharatia, the plaintiff thika tenant has to fulfil the conditions laid down in Section 13(1)(ff) of the Act of 1956 namely, (i) he has to prove himself to be the owner of the suit property; (ii) he reasonably requires the suit property for his own occupation and (iii) he is not in possession of any reasonably suitable accommodation.

It was argued for the appellants that although the predecessor of the present appellants, the defendant in the suit was inducted as a bharatia in respect of the suit property by the predecessor of the plaintiffs/respondents and in his written statement Bhim Chandra admitted to have paid the rent for the suit property to the appellant no. 1, but since the plaintiffs/respondents did not prove themselves to be the owner of the structure comprising the suit property, both the learned Courts below committed an error of law in decreeing the eviction suit in favour of the plaintiffs/respondents. Citing the decision of the Supreme Court in the case of Subhash Chandra vs. Mohammad Sharif & Ors. reported in AIR 1990 SC 636 and the Division Bench judgment of this Court in the case of Sambhu Nath Mitra vs. Khaitan Consultant Ltd. reported in AIR 2005 Cal 281, it was strenuously urged by the appellants that although Bhim Chandra in his written statement admitted that after the death of the predecessor of the plaintiffs he paid rent for the suit property to the plaintiff no. 1, the appellant no. 1 herein, but the provisions of Section 116 of the Evidence Act do not estop them from challenging the derivative title of the plaintiffs/respondents in respect of the suit property. It was further submitted that the onus lied on the plaintiffs/respondents to prove their ownership in respect of the suit property and since they failed to discharge such onus, the learned Court below erred in law to pass the decree for eviction against the defendant in the suit. In this regard, reliance was placed by the appellants on the decision of the Supreme Court in the case of Nagar Palika vs. Jagat Singh reported in (1995) 3 SCC 426. On these grounds, the learned advocate for the appellants strenuously urged for framing of an additional substantial question of law in this appeal that whether the learned Court below committed an error of law in passing the decree for eviction against their predecessor, when the plaintiffs/respondents did not prove their ownership of the suit property.

However, Mr. Raut, learned advocate appearing for the plaintiffs/respondents raised serious objection with regard to the framing of any substantial question of law as contended by the appellants during the hearing of this appeal. He submitted that although in paragraph 4 of his written statement Bhim Chandra, since deceased alleged that the plaintiffs are not the owner of the suit property, but in paragraphs 7 and 8 he admitted to have been inducted in the suit property as a bharatia by the said Kanailal Maity, since deceased and after the death of the latter he was paying rent for the suit property to the plaintiff no. 1. He further submitted that the rent control challans produced and exhibited before the learned trial Judge by Bhim Chandra the defendant deceased clearly show that during his lifetime, he had been depositing rent for the suit property in the name of the plaintiff no. 1 and no issue was framed in the suit with regard to the ownership of the plaintiffs/respondents in respect of the suit property. It was further argued that when it has all along been the case of the plaintiffs/respondents that they inherited the suit property through their predecessor Kanailal, but not even a suggestion was put to any of the plaintiff nos. 1 to 4 during their cross-examination to dispute their ownership in respect of the suit property and even from the judgments of both the learned Courts below it is evident that no argument was at all advanced on behalf of the defendant, either before the learned trial Judge or before the learned appellate Court below challenging the plaintiffs'/respondents' ownership of the suit property. The plaintiffs/respondents submitted that in the case of Subhash Chandra (supra) cited by the appellants the Supreme Court held that in a suit for eviction filed by a transferee from the original landlord, in view of the provisions of Section 116 of the Evidence Act though the defendant tenant cannot challenge the title of the original landlord, but he is entitled to show that the plaintiff has not as a matter of fact secured a transfer from the original landlord or that the alleged transfer is ineffective for some valid reason. According to the plaintiffs/respondents, in the facts of this case, neither the decision of the Supreme Court in the said case Subhash Chandra (supra), nor the Division Bench decision of this Court in the case of Sambhunath Mitra (supra) relied upon by the appellants has any application.

I have carefully considered the materials on record, as well as the arguments advanced on behalf of the respective parties.

The case made out by the plaintiffs/respondents in their plaint, inter alia, was that their predecessor Kanailal, as the thika tenant, inducted Bhim Chandra as a bharatia in respect of the suit property and after the death of Kanailal they themselves, as the heirs and legal representatives of Kanailal, became the thika tenants in respect of the suit property occupied by Bhim Chandra as a bharatia. Though in paragraph 4 of the written statement the defendant Bhim Chandra stated that the plaintiffs are not the owner of the suit property, but in paragraph 7 of the written statement he admitted to have been inducted in the suit property, by Kanailal and stated that after the death of Kanailal, his wife the plaintiff no. 1 as the landlady collected rent from him till July 1992. In his written statement, Bhim Chandra further claimed that since the plaintiff no. 1 refused to accept the rent tendered by him for the month of August, 1992 he has thereafter deposited the rent before the Rent Controller, Calcutta and thereafter, before the learned trial Court. In the suit, no issue was framed with regard to the ownership of the plaintiffs of the suit property. At the trial of the suit, Bhim Chandra as the defendant disclosed and proved all the Rent control challans, as well as the challans of the learned trial Court showing that he had deposited rent for the suit property in the name of the plaintiff no. 1, the wife of the original landlord Kanailal. In his examination-in-chief Bhim Chandra, as DW-1 stated that Kanailal inducted him as a tenant in respect of the suit property, Kanailal died leaving behind his three sons, three daughters and his wife, the plaintiff no. 1 and that after the death of Kanailal, he used to pay rent for the suit property to the plaintiff no. 1. In the cross-examination not even a suggestion was put to any of the plaintiff nos. 1 to 4 on behalf of the defendant disputing their ownership of the suit property. In these facts, the learned trial Judge held that the plaintiffs/respondents are the owners of the suit property and such finding has been upheld by the learned appellate Court below.

In support of the settled position of law that in a suit for eviction the defendant tenant is not estopped from assailing the derivative title of the plaintiff, the appellants relied on the decision of the Supreme Court in the case of Subhash Chandra (supra) and the Division Bench decision of this Court in the case of Sambhunath Mitra (supra). In this regard, I find it appropriate to refer to the following observations of the Supreme Court in paragraph 7 of the decision of the Supreme Court in the case of Subhash Chandra.

"It is true that the doctrine of estoppels ordinarily applies where the tenant has been let into possession by the plaintiff. Where the landlord has not himself inducted the tenant in the disputed property and his rights are founded on a derivative title, for example, as an assignee, donee, vendee, heir, etc., the position is a little different. A tenant already in possession can challenge the plaintiffs claim of derivative title showing that the real owner is somebody else, but this is subject to the rule enunciated by Section 116 of the Evidence Act. The section does not permit the tenant, during the continuance of the tenancy, to deny that his landlord had at the beginning of the tenancy a title to the property. The rule is not confined in its application to cases where the original landlord brings an action for eviction. A transferee from such a landlord also can claim the benefit, but that will be limited to the question of the title of the original landlord at the time when the tenant was let in. So far claim of having derived a good title from the original landlord is concerned, the same does not come under the protection of the doctrine of estoppel, and is vulnerable to a challenge. The tenant is entitled to show that the plaintiff has not as a matter of fact secured a transfer from the original landlord or that the alleged transfer is ineffective for some other valid reason, which non-existent in the renders the transfer to be eye of law. By way of an illustration one may refer to a case where the original landlord had the right of possession and was, therefore, entitled to induct a tenant in the property but did not have any power of disposition. The tenant in such a case can attack the derivative title of the transferee-plaintiff but not on the ground that the transferor landlord who has initially inducted him in possession did not have the right to do so. Further since the impediment in the way of a tenant to challenge the right of the landlord is confined to the stage when the tenancy commenced, he is not forbidden to plead that subsequently the landlord lost this right. These exceptions, however, do not relieve the tenant of his duty to respect the title of the original landlord at the time of the beginning of the tenancy."

From the above findings of the Supreme Court in the case of Subhash Chandra (supra) it is clear that in a case where the defendant in a suit for eviction denies the plaintiffs derivative title to the suit property, the onus is on the defendant to show that the plaintiff has not, in fact, derived any title in respect of the suit property from the original landlord. No doubt in the case of Sambhunath Mitra the Division Bench of this Court followed the ratio laid down in paragraph 7 by the Supreme Court in the said case of Subhash Chandra (supra).

However, in the present case, the plaint case of the plaintiffs/respondents, inter alia, was that Kanailal as the thika tenant inducted the defendant Bhim Chandra in the suit property, as a bharatia and after the death of Kanailal, as the heirs and legal representatives of Kanailal, became the thika tenants in respect of the suit property. In the written statement, as well as in his oral evidence Bhim Chandra admitted the plaintiffs/respondents to be the heirs and legal representatives of the original landlord, Kanailal. In the written statement, as well in his evidence Bhim Chandra also admitted after the death of his original landlord, Kanailal he not only paid rent for the suit property to the wife of Kanailal, that is, the plaintiff no. 1/ respondent no. 1 but also deposited the rent of the suit property with the Rent Controller as well as before the learned trial Court in the name of the plaintiff no. 1/respondent no. 1. Therefore, in the facts of this case as already discussed above, I find that Bhim Chandra, the predecessor of the present appellants failed to establish any defect in the plaintiffs/respondents claim of derivative title in respect of the suit property through their predecessor, Kanailal. Accordingly, the decision of the Supreme Court in the case of Subhash Chandra (supra) and the Division Bench decision of this Court in the case of Sambhunath Mitra (supra) relied upon by the appellants have no application in this case.

For all the foregoing reasons, I do not find any merit in the argument advanced on behalf of the appellants for framing any substantial question of law to assail the impugned judgments and decrees passed by the learned Courts below on the ground of any defect of the plaintiffs'/respondents' title in respect of the suit property.

Accordingly, the appeal being SA 37 of 2015 stands dismissed. However, there shall no order as to costs.

The appellants are directed to hand over vacant and peaceful possession of the suit property described in the schedule to the plaint filed in Title Suit No. 429 of 1996 within January 31, 2018 failing which the plaintiffs/respondents shall be entitled to execute the decree for eviction.

The Department is directed to forthwith send down the lower Courts records to the learned Court below.

Let urgent certified copies of this judgment, if applied for, be made available to the parties upon compliance with all requisite formalities.

[Ashis Kumar Chakraborty, J.]
