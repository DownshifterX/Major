JUDGEMENT :

1. The appellant/landlord has invoked the appellate jurisdiction of this court feeling aggrieved by the impugned order dated 30.09.2013 passed by Ld. Rent Controller, Shahdara on petition of the respondent/tenant under Section 45 of the Delhi Rent Control Act, Narender Kumar Aggarwal vs. Smt. Annu @ Kajal RCA No. 76/13 Dated 20.03.2014 Page 1 of 18 1958 (hereinafter referred as the "DRC Act") by which the appellant/landlord was directed to restore the electricity supply immediately at the tenanted premises bearing no.81, IIIrd Floor, Gopal Park, Delhi­110051. Succinctly stated, the relevant facts for disposal of this appeal are that the respondent/tenant (hereinafter referred as "tenant") has filed a petition under section 45 of the "DRC Act" for restoration of electricity supply before the Rent Controller on 17.03.2012 against the appellant/landlord (hereinafter referred as "landlord") on the ground that she is a tenant in respect of a property bearing no. no.81, IIIrd Floor, Gopal Park, Delhi­110051 shown with red colour in the attached site plan @ Rs.950/­ per month including electricity charges, and in the site plan one room on the third floor and kitchen, bath and latrine on the terrace have been shown in red colour in the site plan. The "tenant" alleged that she has been making regular payment of rent to the "landlord" and lastly the rent was paid to him for September 2010 and thereafter the "landlord" refused to accept the rent. The "tenant" was provided electricity in the tenanted premises through a sub­meter which has been withheld by the "landlord" herein and electricity supply has been cut off by electricity service provider i.e. BSES/YPL about one month prior to filing of the petition.

2. In his written statement, the "landlord", inter­alia, pleaded that Narender Kumar Aggarwal vs. Smt. Annu @ Kajal RCA No. 76/13 Dated 20.03.2014 Page 2 of 18 the "tenant" has been given only one room of the property and not the entire third floor on rent @ Rs.2000/­ per month excluding electricity charges and she has not paid rent inspite of court order under Section 15 (1) of the "DRC Act" in petition no.E­127/09 titled as "Narender vs. Annu" directing her to deposit rent @ Rs.950/­ per month w.e.f. May, 2005 and she has deposited the rent till February, 2010 only. He also pleaded that he did not provide electricity to the "tenant" as the same was never requested by her and that the "tenant" has never asked NOC from him to enable her to apply with BSES for electricity connection in her name and since he has shifted to Ashok Vihar in March, 2007 after locking his floor, he is not aware as to how the electricity was being used by the "tenant".

3. Despite opportunity, the "tenant" did not file any rejoinder to the written statement filed by the "landlord". The "tenant" has also filed an application for interim relief for restoration of electricity supply which was also replied by the "landlord". The "landlord" has filed written arguments on application for interim relief on 17.05.2013 before learned Rent Controller in which he has, inter­alia, pleaded that he has given one room on rent to the "tenant" on the third floor with facility of electricity and water charges but she never deposited the bills of electricity and water, and the BSES has cut off her electricity Narender Kumar Aggarwal vs. Smt. Annu @ Kajal RCA No. 76/13 Dated 20.03.2014 Page 3 of 18 connection in the year 2009 and the "tenant" was directed in the eviction petition no.127/09 to deposit the rent in his bank account and she has deposited one time rent in the bank account amounting Rs. 10,450/­ for the period from May 2009 to March 2010 and the "tenant" never requested him for electricity supply as she was stealing electricity and a case under Section 379 IPC for theft of electricity has been registered against her and a bill of Rs.2.5 lacs was raised against her and she is on bail.

4. Ld. Rent Controller did not grant interim relief to the "tenant" on her application for interim relief and on 29.05.2013, parties were directed to file their respective affidavits for the inquiry. The "tenant" filed her affidavit in evidence and on 31.08.2013, the "landlord" could not cross examine her and he left the court stating that he is leaving for home being suffering from dysentery and in the second call, he was proceeded ex­parte but Ld. Rent Controller granted him one more opportunity to cross examine the "tenant" on the next date of hearing i.e. 07.09.2013. On the adjourned date of hearing, the "landlord" did not appear and matter was adjourned to 27.09.2013 for arguments and the matter was decided by Ld. Rent Controller by impugned order dated 30.09.2013.

5. Alongwith the grounds of appeal, the "landlord" has filed an Narender Kumar Aggarwal vs. Smt. Annu @ Kajal RCA No. 76/13 Dated 20.03.2014 Page 4 of 18 application under Section 5 of the Limitation Act read with Section 151 CPC for condonation of delay in filing appeal. Reply of this application has not been filed by the "tenant".

6. I have heard the appellant/landlord in person and learned counsel for the respondent/tenant and very carefully perused the material available on the record.

Condonation of delay

7. Before disposing of appeal under Section 38 of the "DRC Act", I propose to adjudicate upon the application of the appellant for condonation of delay in filing the appeal. In his application, it is pleaded by the "landlord" that he is a heart patient and received the copy of impugned order from the opposing counsel by courier on 20.10.2013 and there was a delay of 12 days which is neither intentional nor deliberate.

8. Under sub­section (2) of section 38 of "DRC Act", an appeal under sub­section (1) shall be preferred within 30 days from the date of the order made by the Controller provided that the Tribunal may entertain the appeal after expiry of that period of 30 days if it is satisfied that the appellant was prevented by 'sufficient cause' from filing the appeal in time.

Narender Kumar Aggarwal vs. Smt. Annu @ Kajal RCA No. 76/13 Dated 20.03.2014 Page 5 of 18

9. It is well settled principle of law that while dealing an application for condonation of delay in filing appeal, the court shall adopt liberal approach and the expression "sufficient cause" is adequately elastic to enable the courts to apply the law in a meaningful manner which subserves the ends of justice that being the live purpose for existence of the institution of the courts and in this regard the judgment of Hon'ble Supreme Court in Collector, Land Acquisition, Anantnag vs. Mst. Katiji AIR 1987 SUPREME COURT 1353 can be referred to and para 3 of the judgment reads as under :

"The legislature has conferred the power to condone delay by enacting Section 5 "Any appeal or any application, other than an application under any of the provisions of Order XXI of the Civil Procedure Code, 1908, may be admitted after the prescribed period if the appellant or the applicant satisfies the Court that he had sufficient cause for not preferring the appeal or making the application within such period." of the Indian Limitation Act of 1963 in order to enable the Courts to do substantial justice to parties by disposing of matters on 'merits'. The expression "sufficient cause" employed by the legislature is adequately elastic to enable the Courts to apply the law in a meaningful manner which subserves the ends of justice that being the life­purpose for the Narender Kumar Aggarwal vs. Smt. Annu @ Kajal RCA No. 76/13 Dated 20.03.2014 Page 6 of 18 existence of the institution of Courts. It is common knowledge that this Court has been making a justifiably liberal approach in matters, instituted in this Court. But the message does not appear to have percolated down to all the other Courts in the hierarchy. And such a liberal approach is adopted on principle as it is realized that :­
1. Ordinarily a litigant does not stand to benefit by lodging an appeal late.
2. Refusing to condone delay can result in a meritorious matter being thrown out at the very threshold and cause of justice being defeated. As against this when delay is condoned the highest that can happen is that a cause would be decided on merits after hearing the parties.
3. "Every day's delay must be explained"

does not mean that a pedantic approach should be made. Why not every hour's delay every second's delay ? The doctrine must be applied in a rational common sense pragmatic manner.

4. When substantial justice and technical considerations are pitted against each other, cause of substantial justice deserves to be preferred for the other side cannot claim to have vested right in injustice being done because Narender Kumar Aggarwal vs. Smt. Annu @ Kajal RCA No. 76/13 Dated 20.03.2014 Page 7 of 18 of a non­deliberate delay.

5. There is no presumption that delay is occasioned deliberately, or on account of culpable negligence, or on account of mala fides. A litigant does not stand to benefit by resorting to delay. In fact he runs a serious risk.

6. It must be grasped that judiciary is respected not on account of its power to legalize injustice on technical grounds but because it is capable of removing injustice and is expected to do so."

10. In this regard another judgment of the Hon'ble Supreme Court in State of Haryana vs. Chandra Mani AIR 1996 1623 is also relevant for reference and para no. 6 and 7 of the judgment read as under :

"6. In O. P. Kathpalia v. Lakhmir Singh (dead), (1984) 4 SCC 66 : (AIR 1984 SC 1744), a Bench of three Judges had held that if the refusal to condone the delay results in grave miscarriage of justice, it would be a ground to condone the delay.

Delay was accordingly condoned. In Collector, Land Acquisition, Anantnag v. Mst. Katiji, (1987) 2 SCC 107 : (AIR 1987 SC 1353), a Bench of two Judges considered the question of the limitation in an appeal filed by the State and held that Section 5 was enacted in order to Narender Kumar Aggarwal vs. Smt. Annu @ Kajal RCA No. 76/13 Dated 20.03.2014 Page 8 of 18 enable the Court to do substantial justice to the parties by disposing of matters on merits. The expression "sufficient cause" is adequately elastic to enable the Court to apply the law in a meaningful manner which subserves the ends of the justice ­ that being the life­purpose for the existence of the institution of Courts. It is common knowledge that this Court has been making a justifiably liberal approach in matters instituted in this Court. But the message does not appear to have percolated down to all the other Courts in the hierarchy. This Court reiterated that the expression "every day's delay must be explained' does not mean that a pedantic approach should be made.

The doctrine must be applied in a rational common sense pragmatic manner. When substantial justice and technical considerations are pitted against each other, cause of substantial justice deserves to be preferred for the other side cannot claim to have vested right in injustice being done because of a non­deliberate delay. There is no presumption that delay is occasioned deliberately, or on account of culpable negligence, or on account of mala fides. A litigant does not stand to benefit by resorting to delay. In fact he runs a serious risk. Judiciary is not respected on account of its power to legalise injustice on technical grounds but because it is capable of removing injustice and is expected to do so. Making a justice­oriented approach from this perspective, there was sufficient cause for condoning the delay in the institution of the Narender Kumar Aggarwal vs. Smt. Annu @ Kajal RCA No. 76/13 Dated 20.03.2014 Page 9 of 18 appeal. The fact that it was the State which was seeking condonation and not a private party was altogether irrelevant. The doctrine of equality before the law demands that all litigants, including the State as a litigant, are accorded the same treatment and the law is administered in an even­ handed manner. There is no warrant for according a step­motherly treatment when the State is the applicant. The delay was accordingly condoned.

7. Experience shows that on account of an impersonal machinery (no one in charge of the matter is directly hit or hurt by the judgment sought to be subjected to appeal) and the inherited bureaucratic methodology imbued with the note­ making, file­pushing, and passing­on­the­buck ethos, delay on its part is less difficult to understand though more difficult to approve. The State which represent collective cause of the community, does not deserve a litigant­non­grata status. The Courts, therefore, have to be informed with the spirit and philosophy of the provision in the course of the interpretation of the expression of sufficient cause. Merit is preferred to scuttle a decision on merits in turning down the case on technicalities of delay in presenting the appeal. Delay was accordingly condoned, the order was set aside and the matter was remitted to the High Court for disposal on merits after affording opportunity of hearing to the parties. In Smt. Prabha v. Ram Parkash Kalra, (1987) Supp SCC 338 : (AIR 1987 SC 1726), this Court had held that Narender Kumar Aggarwal vs. Smt. Annu @ Kajal RCA No. 76/13 Dated 20.03.2014 Page 10 of 18 the Court should not adopt an injustice­ oriented approach in rejecting the application for condonation of delay. The appeal was allowed, the delay was condoned and the matter was remitted for expeditious disposal in accordance with law."

11. As per the order sheet of Ld. Rent Controller dated 31.08.2013, the "landlord" has submitted that he was suffering from dysentery and left the court and though he was proceeded ex­parte yet he was given another opportunity to cross examine the witness on 07.09.2013 and the "landlord" did not appear and ultimately the impugned order was passed on 30.09.2013. It is also pleaded in the grounds of appeal that on the evening of 31.08.2013, he contacted the opposing counsel on telephone who told him the next date of hearing as 06.11.2013 and meanwhile, he received the copy of impugned order from the opposing counsel by courier on 20.10.2013 and notice of execution from learned Rent Controller on 19.11.2013. He also pleaded that he is a heart patient. The "tenant" has not filed any reply to application for condonation of delay. Affidavit of the "landlord" in support of his application went unrebutted and unchallenged. The Ld. Counsel for 'tenant' admitted having sent copy of impugned order to the appellant and in written submission, it is pleaded that copy of impugned order Narender Kumar Aggarwal vs. Smt. Annu @ Kajal RCA No. 76/13 Dated 20.03.2014 Page 11 of 18 sent by him to the appellant was received by him on 18.10.2013 instead of 20.10.2013. This court is required to apply the law in a meaningful manner which subserves the ends of justice, which is the life purpose for existence of the court. In the facts and circumstances of the case, for justifiable reasons shown and to do substantial justice between the parties, as held by the Hon'ble Supreme Court in Mst. Katiji's case (supra) and Chandra Mani's case (supra), the delay in filing the appeal is hereby condoned.

Adjudication on merits

12. To appreciate the rival contentions of the parties, it is necessary to refer relevant provisions of law i.e. Section 45 of the "DRC Act"

which read as under :­ "Section 45 Cutting off or withholding essential supply or service.-(1) No landlord either himself or through any person purporting to act on his behalf shall without just and sufficient cause cut off or withhold any essential supply or service enjoyed by the tenant in respect of the premises let to him.
(2) If a landlord contravenes the provisions of sub­ section (1), the tenant may make an application to the Controller complaining of such contravention. (3) If the Controller is satisfied that essential supply or service was cut off or withheld by the Narender Kumar Aggarwal vs. Smt. Annu @ Kajal RCA No. 76/13 Dated 20.03.2014 Page 12 of 18 landlord with a view to compel the tenant to vacate the premises or to pay an enhanced rent, the Controller may pass an order directing the landlord to restore the amenities immediately, pending the inquiry referred to in sub­section (4) Explanation.-An interim order may be passed under this sub­section without giving notice to the landlord.
(4) If the Controller on inquiry finds that the essential supply or service enjoyed by the tenant in respect of the premises was cut off or withheld by the landlord without just and sufficient cause, he shall make an order directing the landlord to restore such supply or service.
(5) The Controller may in his discretion direct that compensation not exceeding fifty rupees-
a) be paid to the landlord by the tenant, if the application under sub­section (2) was made frivolously or vexatiously;
b) be paid to the tenant by the landlord, if the landlord had cut off or withheld the supply or service without just and sufficient cause.

Explanation I.- In this section, "essential supply or service" includes supply of water, electricity, lights in passages and on staircases, conservancy and sanitary services.

Explanation II.- For the purposes of this section, withholding any essential supply or service shall including acts or omissions attributable to the landlord on account of which the essential supply or service is cut off by the local authority or any Narender Kumar Aggarwal vs. Smt. Annu @ Kajal RCA No. 76/13 Dated 20.03.2014 Page 13 of 18 order competent authority."

13. A bare reading of Section 45 of "DRC Act" provides that no landlord either himself or through any person purporting to act on his behalf shall without just and sufficient cause cut off or withheld any essential supply or service enjoyed by the tenant in respect of the premises let out to him and if the Rent Controller on inquiry finds that the essential supply of service enjoyed by the tenant was cut off or withheld by the landlord without just and sufficient cause, he shall make an order directing the landlord to restore such supply or services.

14. It is argued by the "landlord" that he has filed an eviction petition bearing no.127/09 against the "tenant" on 09.01.2009 under Section 14 (1) (a) of the "DRC Act" in respect of one room situated on the third floor of the property bearing no.81, Gopal Park, Delhi­110051 and on his application, Ld. ARC under Section 15 (1) of "DRC Act" vide order dated 05.02.2010 directed her to pay the rent at the admitted rate of Rs.950/­ per month w.e.f. May 2009 uptill the day in the bank account of the "landlord" and she was further directed to continuously pay future rent month by month by 15th of each succeeding month at the said rate in the bank account. The "landlord" further argued that the "tenant" did not comply with the directions of Narender Kumar Aggarwal vs. Smt. Annu @ Kajal RCA No. 76/13 Dated 20.03.2014 Page 14 of 18 Ld. ARC and on his application under Section 15 (7) of the "DRC Act", vide order dated 06.08.2011, learned ARC found that the "tenant" has deposited the rent w.e.f. May 2009 to March 2010 total Rs.10,450/­ only on 06.03.2010 in the bank account of the appellant herein and thereafter, she did not deposit or paid any rent and hence her defence was struck off. The "landlord" further argued that the "tenant" in her affidavit tendered in evidence before the Ld. Rent Controller has falsely deposed that on 05.11.2012, the "landlord" approached her and demanded the arrears of rent and assured her that the electricity supply will soon be restored and she paid Rs.28,500/­ in cash for the period from October 2010 to March 2013 and promised her that he will send her a rent receipt. He further argued that the 'tenant' cannot be believed to have paid him Rs.28,500/­ by cash on 5.11.2012 when the present petition under Section 45 of the "DRC Act" was already pending disposal and she did not even inform Ld. Rent Controller immediately after 5.11.2012 about alleged payment. He further argued that he filed the written statement on 05.01.2013 contesting her claim and despite opportunity, the "tenant" did not file any replication to plead alleged payment dated 5.11.2012.

15. Per contra, ld. counsel for the tenant argued that the deposition of the "tenant" in her affidavit dated 16.07.2013 about payment of rent Narender Kumar Aggarwal vs. Smt. Annu @ Kajal RCA No. 76/13 Dated 20.03.2014 Page 15 of 18 went unrebutted and unchallenged and the "landlord" did not come forward to cross examine her. However, learned counsel for the tenant admitted at the bar that the defence of the "tenant" was struck off as claimed by the "landlord" vide order dated 06.08.2011. The "landlord" has placed on record certified copies of the order dated 05.02.2010 and 06.08.2011.

16. The "landlord" submitted that he was suffering from dysentery and for that reason, he informed the Ld. Rent Controller on 31.08.2013 and his request is reflected in the order sheet and subsequently, he could not appear as he is a heart patient and in the meanwhile, he received the copy of order from the opposing counsel by courier on 20.11.2013. The "landlord" points out that in para no.6 of affidavit in evidence filed by the "tenant", she has also deposed that she used to pay the electricity bills to the "landlord" separately in addition to the monthly rent as per the consumption though in her petition, she has pleaded that the electricity charges are inclusive of rent of Rs.950/­ per month and in her deposition, she has admitted that the electricity charges were in addition to the rent. Lastly, he argued that the "tenant" has been residing in the premises in question without payment of any rent or electricity charges and he may be given an opportunity to cross examine the "tenant" and adduce evidence. Narender Kumar Aggarwal vs. Smt. Annu @ Kajal RCA No. 76/13 Dated 20.03.2014 Page 16 of 18

17. Ld. Rent Controller vide impugned order dated 30.09.2013 has decided the petition on sole testimony of the "tenant" and the "landlord" could not cross examine her on 31.08.2013 as he was admittedly suffering from dysentery and thereafter on the next date of hearing, he could not appear and the petition was decided on merits. In this manner the "landlord" is deprived for an opportunity of being heard before the Ld. Rent Controller. In these circumstances to do the substantial justice between the parties, the impugned order dated 30.09.2013 is hereby set aside with the direction to the Ld. Rent Controller to give due opportunity to the "landlord" to cross examine the "tenant" and adduce his evidence subject to payment of Rs.2500/­ as cost. Ld. Rent Controller shall decide the matter afresh in accordance with law after giving opportunity to both the parties preferably within a period of one month. On 13.03.2014 Ld. Counsel for 'tenant' stated that in the presence of the bailiff and police officials, the electricity connection has been temporarily restored from the electricity pole in the premises in question and regular electricity connection has so far not been restored. In written submissions on behalf of 'tenant', it is stated that electricity supply was temporarily restored by the Bailiff of the Court in the presence of police officials pursuant to the order of Ld. Rent Controller, Shahdara, where Narender Kumar Aggarwal vs. Smt. Annu @ Kajal RCA No. 76/13 Dated 20.03.2014 Page 17 of 18 execution of impugned order is stated to be pending. It is trite that courts are meant to establish, implement and enforce rule of law. In the guise of impugned order, the 'tenant' cannot be permitted to steal electricity by having direct connection from the electric pole through bailiff or police officials. Appeal is disposed of accordingly. Trial Court record be sent back with an attested copy of the instant judgment. Appeal file be consigned to record room.
