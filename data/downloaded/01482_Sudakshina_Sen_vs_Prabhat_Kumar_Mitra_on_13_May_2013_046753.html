Calcutta High Court (Appellete Side)

Sudakshina Sen vs Prabhat Kumar Mitra on 13 May, 2013

Author: Harish Tandon

Bench: Harish Tandon

1 In The High Court At Calcutta Constitutional Writ Jurisdiction Appellate Side Present:

The Hon'ble Mr. Justice Harish Tandon.
C. O. No. 878 of 2013 Sudakshina Sen vs. Prabhat Kumar Mitra. 
For the petitioner       :       Mr. Jiban Ratan Chatterjee

For the opposite party   :       Mr. Siddhartha Mitra,
                                 Mr. Vivek Jhunjhunwala.

Heard on      :

Judgment on:
The Court:- This revisional application is directed against the order dated January 04, 2013 passed by the learned Civil Judge (Junior Division), Additional Court, Sealdah in Ejectment Suit No. 360 of 2005, by which an application challenging the maintainability of the suit is rejected.
The West Bengal Premises Tenancy Act, 1997 was promulgated and came into operation on and from July 10, 2001. By the said Act the earlier Act namely, West Bengal Premises Tenancy Act, 1956 was repealed. The Act as it stood on the date of its enactment and coming in force, the cases relating to an eviction of a monthly tenant was entrusted upon the Rent Controller and and/or Additional Rent Controller, who were competent to pass an order for eviction of a tenant on the basis of an application taken out by the landlord.
The State was empowered to appoint the Rent Controller and the Additional Rent Controller and in exercise of the said power the Civil Judge was appointed as the Additional Rent Controller for the purpose of dealing with and determining the application for eviction under the said Act.
By the West Bengal Premises Tenancy (Amendment) Act, 2005, which came into effect on March 19, 2005 the word - "the controller" was 2 deleted from Sections 6 and 7 of the said Act and was substituted by the word - "by the Civil Judge having jurisdiction".
The present suit was filed on June 06, 2005 before the Additional Court, though the plaint reveals that it was filed in the court of Additional Controller. It would be trite to mention here that the said Presiding Officer of the said Additional Court was appointed as the Additional Rent Controller by the State in exercise of the powers conferred under Section 38 of the said Act. By the time the said suit was instituted, the said amendment of 2005 was in vogue. The defendant petitioner initially entered appearance and contested the suit by filing written statement, but thereafter did not take any step and the said suit was decreed ex parte. The said ex parte decree was assailed in an application under Order 9 Rule 13 of the Code of Civil Procedure, which was eventually allowed and the said ex parte decree was set aside. The order was subsequently affirmed upon the dismissal of the revisional application before this Court.
The suit was taken up by the said Additional Court at Sealdah and an application for addition of party was taken out alleging that the person proposed to be added is intermeddling with the possession of the suit property. The said application was eventually allowed and the said added defendant subsequently filed an application for return of the plaint on the plea that the court before which the suit was instituted lacks jurisdiction and is not competent to entertain the said proceeding. The aforesaid application was rejected by the trial court.
The bone of contention of the petitioner before this Court is that the suit instituted before the Additional Court, Sealdah, who was appointed as an Additional Rent Controller cannot be entertained, as the District Judge has distributed the business and the jurisdiction has been vested upon the Civil Judge (Junior Division), 1st Court, Sealdah. From the record it appears that although the plaint shows that it has been filed in the court of Additional Controller, but on the day when the plaint was presented, there was already an amendment taken out in West Bengal Premises Tenancy Act, 1997; by virtue whereof the word - "controller" was deleted and the word - "Civil Judge with jurisdiction" was inserted.
The point which evolved in the instant case is whether the Additional Court, Sealdah, is competent to entertain an eviction suit under West Bengal Premises Tenancy Act, 1997, when the property 3 situates within the jurisdiction of the Civil Judge (Junior Division), 1st Court, Sealdah.
According to Mr. Chatterjee, learned senior advocate appearing for the petitioner, the District Judge under the Bengal, Agra and Assam Civil Courts Act, 1887 has been vested with the power to assign the business to one or more subordinate judges and, therefore, one judge cannot over- reach the jurisdiction of the other. Mr. Chatterjee further submits that the suit was instituted after the amendment of 2005 is brought, before the Additional Rent Controller and, therefore, the same is liable to be presented before the proper court. According to Mr. Chatterjee, when the court has been given specific jurisdiction, entertainment of a proceeding by another court by over-reaching the jurisdiction would result into a judicial chaos.
Lastly he submits that if a party is forced to submit to the jurisdiction of the court for proper relief if does not acquiesce to raise the issue of jurisdiction of the court as it strikes at the root of the matter and placed reliance upon the judgement of this Court in the case of Allahabad Bank vs.Ghnshyam Das Damani reported in AIR 1998 Calcutta 243.
By placing the reliance upon a judgement of this Court in the case of Tapas Biswas vs. Shyamaprasad Ghoshal & Ors. reported in 2009 (1) CHN 183, Mr. Chatterjee submits that by virtue of an amendment brought out in the year 2005 and 2006 in the said Act, the adjudicatory power was taken away from the Rent Controller and was given to the exclusive jurisdiction of the civil court. Having inspired from the aforesaid observations, Mr. Chatterjee submits that in view of the specific provisions contained under the Bengal, Agra and Assam Civil Courts Act, 1887 unless there has been an assignment of the business by the District Judge, the Additional Court cannot assume the jurisdiction as an Additional Civil Judge.
Mr. Mitra, learned advocate appearing for the plaintiff/opposite party however submits that the suit was filed after the amendment of 2005 by which the Civil Judge was conferred with the jurisdiction to deal with the eviction proceedings under the said Act. According to him though the plaint indicates that it has been filed in the court of Additional Controller, but in fact it has been filed before the Civil Judge and, therefore, the proceeding cannot be said to be incompetent. Mr. Mitra however placed before me the provisions contained under Section 4 13 of the Bengal, Agra and Assam Civil Courts Act, 1887 to contend that the State Government by notification may fix the local area limits of the civil courts and if the civil business arising out of any local area is assigned by the District Judge to one or two or more subordinate judges, the decree or order passed by the subordinate Judge shall not be invalidated by the reasons that the property was beyond the local area.

According to him, the Additional Judges are appointed if the business pending before the District Judge requires the aid and functions of the District Judge, who shall exercise the same powers as the District Judge.

Mr. Mitra placed reliance upon a judgement of this Court rendered in case of Jitendra Nath Pal vs. Birendra Krishna Roy & Anr. reported in AIR 1938 Calcutta 193 to contend that the distribution of the business does not amount the transfer of the business and, therefore, Additional Court does not lose its jurisdiction to entertain the said suit. He further placed reliance upon subsequent judgements of this Court in case of Masrab Khan vs. Debnath Mali @ Abhu Mali & Ors. reported in AIR 1942 Calcutta 321 where the said principle is reiterated. He audaciously submits that under Section 13 of the Bengal, Agra and Assam Civil Courts Act, 1887 empowers the State Government to fix and alter the local limits of the court and for administrative convenience the business has been assigned to one or more judges which does not necessarily mean that one Civil Judge cannot deal with the matter relating to the Additional Court and placed reliance upon the judgement in the case of Manish Dogra vs. Anima Rani Sinha reported in 2011 (3) CHN (Cal) 751.

Mr. Mitra heavily relies upon an unreported judgement of this Court rendered by a coordinate Bench in the case of Swapan Kumar Manna vs. Debasish Das & Ors (C.O. 2934 of 2012 decided on November 26, 2012), wherein it is held that the Additional Civil Judge (Junior Division) can hear and dispose of the eviction suit, even if the power as a Rent Controller has been taken away by amendment carried out in the year 2005.

So far as the power under Article 227 of the Constitution of India to be invoked by the High Court is concerned, Mr. Mitra submits that mere wrong decision is not capable of being assailed before the High Court and has placed reliance upon a judgement of the Apex Court in the case of Sadhana Lodh vs. National Insurance Co. Ltd. & Anr. reported in (2003) 3 SCC 524, Surya Dev Rai vs. Ram Chander Rai & Ors. reported in (2003) 6 SCC 675, Union of India & Ors. vs. Debts Recovery Tribunal Bar 5 Association & Anr. reported in (2013) 3 SCC 574 and Estralla Rubber vs. Dass Estate (P) Ltd. reported in (2001) 8 SCC 97.

Mr. Mitra further submits that the defendant/petitioner did not raise any objection as to the competence of the Civil Judge (Junior Division), Additional Court to entertain the said eviction suit in the written statement. According to him the defendant/petitioner submitted to the jurisdiction of the Civil Judge (Junior Division), Additional Court by making an application for setting aside ex parte decree and have reap the benefit of an order by which the ex parte decree was set aside. He, therefore, submits that having submitted to the jurisdiction and obtained the favourable reliefs, he cannot take a rebound and contend that the court is not competent to deal with the said ejectment suit. Lastly he submits that by conduct of the defendant/petitioner it would be apparent that they have acquiesced to raise any objection as to the competence of the court to deal with the proceeding and have in fact to waive such right of objection and placed reliance upon the judgement Bahrein Petroleum Co. Ltd. vs. P. J. Pappu & Anr. reported in AIR 1966 SC 634, Pathumma & Ors. vs. Kuntalan Kutty & Ors. reported in 1981 (3) SCC 589.

Having heard and considered the respective submissions as made above, this Court finds that the sheet anchor of the argument of the petitioner is that the Civil Judge (Junior Division), Additional Court can only entertain the proceeding specifically assigned by the District Judge in administrative capacity and cannot be treated as the original court having jurisdiction to entertain the proceeding.

There is no dispute to the fact that the said proceeding was initiated before the Additional Court, Alipur, after the amendment of 2005 brought in West Bengal Premises Tenancy Act, 1997. Section 13 of the Bengal, Agra and Assam Civil Courts Act, 1887 empowers the State Government to fix and alter the local limits of the jurisdiction of the civil courts by notification. Sub-section 2 of the said Section empowers the District Judge to assign two or more sub-ordinate Judges such civil businesses cognizable by the said Judges. Section 8 of the said Act further empowers the State Government to appoint an Additional Judge as may be requisite for speedy disposal of the cases. The cumulative effect of the aforesaid provisions indicates that the local limits of the court is to be fixed and altered by the State Government and the 6 assignment of the businesses is to be dealt with by the District Judge amongst the sub-ordinate Judges or the Munsiff as the case may be.

The Additional Judge, who is appointed for the purpose of speedy disposal of the cases is equally competent to deal with the matters relating to the local limits or jurisdiction. The Additional Judge does not have the local limits or the jurisdiction, but assumes jurisdiction on the basis of an administrative order passed by the District Judge. Broadly speaking, the administrative power is exercised by the District Judge assigning the civil business to each of the sub-ordinate officers in relation to the local limits of the concerned police station and the Additional Court has been given power to deal with the civil businesses relating to the entire local limits of the Civil Courts subject to the administrative sanction by the District Judge.

In the instant case the Additional Judge was appointed as an Additional Rent Controller by the State Government under Section 38 of the West Bengal Premises Tenancy Act, 1997. Subsequent to the amendment carried out in the year 2005, the Additional Judge, who is otherwise competent to deal with the matters capable of being dealt with by the Civil Judge, retain its jurisdiction to deal all the civil businesses assigned by the District Judge in its administrative and judicial capacity.

As has been held in the case of Jitendra Nath (supra) that distribution of the business does not amount to a transfer of a business within the meaning of Section 24 of the Code of Civil Procedure. As the local jurisdiction of a sub-ordinate Judge to which extend over the whole District is not altered by the State. The similar view is expressed in case of Masrab Khan (supra) in the following words: -

"In the present case, it is not disputed that the redistribution of local areas was made by the District Judge of Mymensingh under S. 13(2), Civil Courts Act. This was only an arrangement for distribution of civil business in that particular sub-division. It cannot and has not disturbed or in any way curtailed the jurisdiction of the First Munsif of Kishoreganj over the entire area as determined by the Local Government under S. 13 (1), Civil Courts Act. It may be contended that the Court of the Second Munsif, also having jurisdiction over the same local area could entertain an application for execution of the decree. The answer is given by Banerjee J. in 25 Cal. 315.4 The competency of the Second Munsif was not affected 7 by the order under S. 13(2), Civil Courts Act, but it was affected by S. 38, Civil P. C., which lays down that a decree could be executed only by the Court which passed it or the Court to which it was sent for execution. It is not necessary to say anything further with reference to the case in 28 Cal. 2382 which was relied on by Mr. Roy. That case followed the opinion of Garth C. J. in 6 Cal 513, 1 but expressly laid down that S. 649 (now S.37), Civil P. C., would not apply when there was no transfer of jurisdiction under S. 13 (1), Civil Courts Act, but only a redistribution of business under cl. (2) of S. 13 of the Act, This decision, therefore, far from supporting the contention of Mr. Roy is decidedly against him. Our conclusion, therefore, is that the Munsif, First Court of Kishoreganj did not cease to have jurisdiction to execute the decree by reason of the order of the District Judge under S.13(2), Civil Courts Act, and the Court of the Second Munsif could not consequently be regarded as the Court which passed the decree within the meaning of that section."

The Additional Judge or the Court has not been assigned with the specific local limits, which are constituted for the purpose of speedy disposal of the backlog cases and the assignment of business is done by the District Judge under Section 13 of the Bengal, Agra and Assam Civil Courts Act, 1887.

As Mr. Chatterjee points out that if there is local limit assigned to each of the sub-ordinate judges, one cannot overreach the other and the scrupulous litigation would be capable circumventing the same by approaching the particular judge. There is some fallacy in the arguments of Mr. Chatterjee, inasmuch as had it been a case that the local limits are assigned to a particular Judge and a party has approached another Judge, having specific local limits or jurisdiction the position is different, but in the instant case the Additional Judge has not been assigned any local limits or jurisdiction, who is capable of taking the civil matters relating to the jurisdiction of a civil court with the administrative sanction of the District Judge. Mr. Chatterjee was contemplating a situation where the sub-ordinate Judge inherently lacks the jurisdiction.

This Court finds support to the aforesaid contentions from the unreported judgement rendered by a Co-ordinate Bench of this Court in Swapan Kumar Manna (supra), wherein it is held that the Additional Judge is not denuded of the jurisdiction to hear ejectment cases, which his fellow brother Judge sitting in the next court room would decide.

8

Mr. Mitra, learned Advocate appearing for the petitioner vehemently submits that the petitioner cannot agitate the points of inherent lack of jurisdiction after having availed of the desired relief by submitting to the jurisdiction of the Additional Court. According to Mr. Mitra, the ex parte decree, which was passed by the Additional Court, was set aside on the basis of an application under Order 39 Rule 13 of the Code of Civil Procedure filed by the petitioner and, therefore, he is precluded from raising the question of maintainability of the said proceeding.

The point relating to the jurisdiction of the court of territorial or pecuniary should be taken at the earliest point of time. The defect of jurisdiction whether it is pecuniary or territorial, strikes at the root of an authority of the Court to pass a decree and cannot be cured by the consent of parties. The court which inherently lacks the jurisdiction to entertain the said suit, passes a decree, such decree is a nullity. Mere filing of an application for setting aside the ex parte decree cannot be said to be an Act to submit to the jurisdiction or bless the Court with the jurisdiction, if it did not have. Mere setting aside the ex parte decree on an application under Order 39 Rule 13 of the Code of Civil Procedure cannot be construed that the party has consented and/or submitted to the jurisdiction of the court and cannot take a plea of inherent lack of jurisdiction at a later point of time.

In my considered view the points relating to the territorial or pecuniary jurisdiction is not germane to the present dispute. It is not in dispute that the civil court is constituted by several Judges under the superintendence of the District Judge. It is not lack of jurisdiction to entertain the suit, but whether Additional Judge can take up the matter within its specific local limits of another Civil Judge. The defect in filing the said suit cannot be said to be incurable and fatal where the entire proceeding is to be thrown on the touchstone of jurisdiction. It would be the same suit before another Civil Judge of the same court having a new number where the determination would be one and the same. The proceedings initiated have consumed several years and if at this stage for such point the plaint is to be returned and/or rejected as prayed for by the defendant/petitioner, it would give him a premium to reap the benefit of the procedural hazards.

9

As indicated above, the Additional Judge is as competent as the other Civil Judges of the same court to entertain the ejectment suit. There is no impediment on the part of the Additional Judge to continue with the said matter. This Court draws inspiration of the recent judgement of the apex Court in case of Union of India & Ors vs. Debts Recovery Tribunal Bar Association & Anr. reported in (2013)2 SCC 574, wherein it is held that the High Court in exercise of the power of superintendence can oversee the administrative functioning of the sub- ordinate courts and the tribunals. This Court, therefore, feels that in the facts of the instant case, the instant suit shall be deemed to have been assigned to the Additional Judge for the purpose of disposal and the said Additional Judge would see that the same shall reach on its logical ends. This Court is informed that the defence has already been delivered by the defendant by filing the written statement and there is no impediment in disposal of the said suit.

The trial court is requested to see that the suit is disposed of on expeditious basis and preferably within 8 months from the date of communication of this order.

The revisional application is, thus, disposed of.

There will be no order as to costs.

(HARISH TANDON, J.)
