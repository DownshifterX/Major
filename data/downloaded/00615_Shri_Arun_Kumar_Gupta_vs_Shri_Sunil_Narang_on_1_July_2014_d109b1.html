JUDGMENT

1 Vide this judgment I shall decide a suit for possession, mesne profit, recovery of arrears and mandatory injunction filed by the plaintiff against the defendant. The brief fact necessitated in filing the present suit are given as under:-

2 The plaintiff is the owner of first floor of property bearing Municipal No. 7885 (51/1), built on plot No. 1, Block No. 51, Khasra No. 1660 situated at Rohtak Road, Western Extension Area, Karol Bagh, Delhi. He purchased the above suit property vide registered sale deed dated 04.06.2008.

3 That Late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang, father of the defendant was the tenant in the suit property which was let out to him for residential purpose. The tenancy of Sh. Shadi Lal Narang was terminated by the then owner during his lifetime vide legal notice dated 05.01.2001 which was duly served upon him. Sh. Shadi Lal Narang died in the year 2002 and his wife has also expired in the year 2010. Neither the defendant nor any son or daughter of Late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang had been residing with him nor they were financially dependent on the deceased on the date of his death.

4 As per the Delhi Rent Control Act after the demise of late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang, his successor acquired a right in the tenancy for the period of one year and thereafter the defendants right to occupy the suit property extinguished and the occupation of the defendant in the suit property is of an un-authorized occupant and not as a tenant.

5 The plaintiff after becoming the owner of the suit property issued legal notice dated 25.08.2008 calling upon the defendant that the market rate of rent of the premises was approximately 20,000/- per month, therefore, he is liable to pay the same after the period of one year from the death of his father along with interest @ 15% per annum as provided under Delhi Rent Control Act. In spite of repeated demands, the defendant has failed to handover the possession of the suit premises, therefore, defendant is liable to hand over the possession and also liable to pay damages @ of Rs. 20,000/- per month. On the basis of the aforesaid averment, present suit has been filed for adjudication.

6 Pursuant to the summons, defendant appeared and filed his written Suit No. 186/12 Page no. 2/16 statement taking preliminary objection that suit is barred under Delhi Rent Control Act; that the suit has not been properly valued; that there no relationship of landlord and tenant between the plaintiff and the defendant.

7 On merit it is averred that Sh. Shadi Lal Narang was a tenant in the suit property for the last more than 40 years and he had been paying rent @ of Rs. 250/- per month to the erstwhile landlord. After the demise of Sh. Shadi Lal Narang, defendant stepped into his shoes and become tenant of the suit premises. It is denied that plaintiff become the owner of the suit property. It is also denied that he purchased the suit property vide registered sale deed. It is also denied that the tenancy of Sh. Shadi Lal Narang was determined/ terminated during his life time vide notice dated 05.01.2001. It is also denied that his successor only acquired rights for one year.

8 It is stated that no notice was served upon the father of the defendant. Moreover, the said notice was never acted upon and the father of the defendant remained tenant continuously till his death and thereafter his wife and sons have inherited the right of tenancy in the suit premises. It is denied that plaintiff after becoming owner of the suit property had issued legal notice dated 25.08.2008 and informed the defendant that market rent of the property was Rs. 20,000/- per month. It is further stated that plaintiff cannot asked for damages @ Rs. 20,000/- per month along with interest because defendant is residing in the suit property as a tenant and paying rent @ Rs. 275/- per month. It is denied that defendant is liable to pay an amount of Rs. 5,80,000/- as damages w.e.f. from 01.10.2008 till 28.02.2011. All other averments have also been denied. It is prayed that suit be dismissed with cost.

9 Plaintiff has filed replication to the written statement thereby reiterated the averment made in the plaint and denied the averment made in the written statement.

10 On the basis of the pleadings of the parties, the Ld. Predecessor of this court vide order dated 21.02.2012, framed the following issues for adjudication.

1. Whether the defendant has inherited the tenancy right of his father, who was the tenant paying rent @ Rs. 250/- per month and thus the suit is barred by the provision of Delhi Rent Control Act? OPD

2. Whether the father of the defendant was a statutory tenant in the premises bearing No. 7885 (51/1) Khasra No. 1660, Rohtak Road, WEA Karol Bagh, Delhi? OPD

3. Whether the plaintiff is entitled to a decree of possession of the premises in question? OPP

4. Whether the plaintiff is entitled for a decree of suit amount? OPP

5. Whether the plaintiff is entitled for a decree of mandatory injunction as prayed in para-c of the prayer clause of the suit? OPP

6. Whether the plaintiff is entitled for pendentlite and future interest, if yes on what rate? OPP

7. Whether the defendant if held to be a tenant is liable to retain the premises without payment of the market value? OPP

8. Relief.

13 I have heard plaintiff in person and counsel for the defendant and perused the pleading, evidence and other material placed on record. My issue wise findings is as under:-

14 ISSUE NO. 1 Whether the defendant has inherited the tenancy right of his father, who was the tenant paying rent @ Rs. 250/- per month and thus the suit is barred by the provision of Delhi Rent Control Act? OPD ISSUE NO. 2 Whether the father of the defendant was a statutory tenant in the premises bearing No. 7885 (51/1) Khasra No. 1660, Rohtak Road, WEA Karol Bagh, Delhi? OPD ISSUE NO. 3 Whether the plaintiff is entitled to a decree of possession of the premises in question? OPP 15 Issue No. 1 to 3 are taken together as they are interconnected. The onus to prove issue no. 1 is upon the defendant, whereas onus to onus to prove issue no. 2 & 3 should be upon the plaintiff. The onus of issue no. 2 has Suit No. 186/12 Page no. 5/16 been wrongly put upon the defendant because it is the plaintiff who has raised the plea that father of the defendant was statutory tenant. Thus the onus to prove the said issue should be upon the plaintiff.

16 The plaintiff has filed the present suit against the defendant on the ground that he is the owner of suit property which he purchased vide registered sale deed dated 04.06.2008. Further plea of the plaintiff is that late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang, father of the defendant was the tenant in the suit property whose tenancy was determined / terminated by the then owner of the suit premises vide legal notice dated 05.01.2001 which was duly served upon late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang, who remained statutory tenant under the Protection of Delhi Rent Control Act and on his death his successor acquired a right in tenancy only for a period of one year. As per the plaintiff, late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang left behind his wife who also expired in the year 2010. The defendant was not financially dependent upon Sh. Shadi Lal Narang and occupying the suit premises as an unauthorized occupant and not as a tenant. The defendant has failed to handover the possession of the suit premises to the plaintiff despite the legal notice dated 25.08.2008. As per the plaintiff, defendant is unauthorized occupant in the suit premises and is liable to handover peaceful and vacant possession of the suit premises to the plaintiff.

17 The defendant has taken a plea that his father was a tenant in the suit premises for the last more than 40 years @ Rs.250 per month and after the demise of his father the defendant stepped into the shoes of his father and become tenant. The rent of the premises is less than Rs.3,500/-, therefore, the suit is barred under Delhi Rent Control Act. As per the defendant, he occupying the suit premises not as unauthorized occupant but as a tenant on Suit No. 186/12 Page no. 6/16 a monthly rent of Rs. 275/- per month, therefore, the instant suit filed by the plaintiff is barred under Delhi Rent Control Act.

18 In order to substantiate their plea, both the parties have led their respective evidence.

19 An examination of the pleadings and the evidence led on record shows that plaintiff has filed present suit for possession against the defendant on the ground that he is the owner of the suit property. The defendant has disputed the factum of ownership of the plaintiff. To substantiate the said plea, the plaintiff has placed on record certified copy of the registered sale deed dated 04.06.2008, Ex.PW1/2. The plaintiff has also reiterated the factum of the ownership in para-3 of his examination in chief. PW-1 has been cross examined by Ld. Counsel for the defendant but the plea regarding the ownership of the plaintiff qua the suit property has not been disputed. The plaintiff has thus proved on record that he is the owner of the suit property vide sale deed Ex.PW1/2.

20 The plaintiff has sought recovery of possession of the suit property against the defendant on the ground that late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang, the father of the defendant, was the tenant in the suit premises whose tenancy was determined vide legal notice dated 05.01.2001 Ex..PW1/3. As per the plaintiff, late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang was a statutory tenant at the time of his death and his legal heirs were entitled to retain the possession of the suit premises only for a limited period. The defendants has controverted the said plea of the plaintiff in the written statement and pleaded that his father was a tenant in the suit premises and he remained so till his death and after his demise the Suit No. 186/12 Page no. 7/16 defendant has inherited the tenancy right in the suit premises and occupying the same as a tenant and not as unauthorized occupant.

21 The sum and substance of the plea taken by the plaintiff is that late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang was statutory tenant at the time of his death therefore, after his demise his legal heir has acquired only limited right to occupy the suit premises for one year only. The onus to prove the fact that late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang was a statutory tenant whose tenancy was determined by the previous owner vide legal notice Ex.PW1/3 dated 05.01.2001 is upon the plaintiff.

22 The plaintiff has simply averred in para-4 of the plaint that the tenancy of late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang was determined by the then owner during his lifetime vide notice dated 05.01.2001. But he has nowhere stated in the plaint as to who determined the tenancy of late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang or how and in what manner the plaintiff has come to know about the said determination. He has also not averred a single word in the entire plaint as to how he has got the notice dated 05.01.2001. The defendant in para-4 of the written statement has disputed the said averment and denied that the tenancy of his father was terminated by the then owner vide notice dated 05.01.2001. The defendant has made a positive averment that no notice was served upon the father of the defendant.

23 The plaintiff is thus required to establish on record that the tenancy of late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang was terminated by the previous owner by serving legal notice Ex.PW1/3, dated 05.01.2001. The plaintiff was also required to establish on record that the said legal notice was duly served upon late Sh.

Shadi Lal Narang. To establish the said plea the plaintiff has examined himself as PW-1 and averred in para-5 of his examination in chief that the tenancy of late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang was determined by the then owner during his lifetime vide notice dated 05.01.2001 which was duly served upon late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang and the office copy of the legal notice is annexed herewith as Ex.PW1/3 and original receipt of registered AD is annexed herewith as Ex.PW1/4.

24 The notice dated 05.01.2001 Ex.PW1/3 was not given by the plaintiff as plaintiff had purchased the suit property only on 04.06.2008. The claim of the plaintiff is that the previous owner had served the notice dated 05.01.2001 upon late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang. The plaintiff, however has not mentioned either in the plaint, replication or in his examination in chief as to how he has acquired knowledge or information that previous owner had terminated the tenancy of late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang vide notice dated 05.01.2001 Ex.PW1/3. The plaintiff has also not averred as to what was the name of previous owner who served the notice dated 05.01.2001. The plaintiff has also not averred single word either in the pleading or in his evidence as to how he acquired possession of notice Ex.PW1/3 and who informed him about termination of tenancy of late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang vide legal notice Ex.PW1/3.

25 The legal notice Ex.PW1/3 could have been proved only by the person who served the same upon late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang or by the counsel who drafted the said legal notice on the instructions of such person. But the plaintiff has not made any endeavour to examine either the previous owner who stated to have terminated the tenancy of late Sh. Krishan Kumar, the previous owner of the suit premises. The plaintiff has also not made any averment as to whether Sh. Krishan Kumar, the previous owner, who instructed Sh. Sandeep Garg, Advocate to serve legal notice Ex.PW1/3 upon late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang is alive or dead.

26 No endeavour whatsoever has been made by the plaintiff to examine the previous owner or the counsel who served legal notice Ex.PW1/3 upon late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang. In the absenceof non-examination of previous owner who stated to have terminated the tenancy of late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang vide legal notice Ex.PW1/3 or the counsel Sh. Sandeep Garg who drafted the said notice under the instructions of Sh. Krishan Kumar the previous owner, the notice Ex.PW1/3 remained unproved.

27 The defendant has examined himself as DW-1 and averred in para 3 of his examination in chief that the legal notice dated 13.05.2002 shows that legal notice dated 05.01.2001 neither received nor acted upon by the owner of the property. The defendant has been cross examined by the plaintiff but he has not carried out any cross examination or given any suggestion to DW-1 that the previous owner of the suit property determined / terminated the tenancy of his father late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang vide legal notice dated 05.01.2001, Ex.PW1/3. He has also not given any suggestion to DW-1 that the said legal notice was duly served upon the father of the defendant. In the absence of any cross examination or confrontation of DW-1 on this material particulars, the plea of the plaintiff that the previous owner terminated the tenancy of late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang vide legal notice Ex.PW1/3 remained unsubstantiated and unproved.

28 The plaintiff has placed on record certified copy of the sale deed in his favour Ex.PW1/2. Clause 2 of the sale deed at page 11 stipulates that late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang was the tenant in the suit premises @ Rs. 275/- per month and now, his widow and son of Sh. Sunil Narang are in possession of property. The said sale deed nowhere stipulates that tenancy of late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang was terminated by the previous owner or that late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang was occupying the suit premises as a statutory tenant at the time of his death. If the tenancy of late Shadi Lal Narang was terminated by the previous owner then the reference of the same was required to be there in clause 2 of the sale deed Ex.PW1/2. But there is no mention of the said fact in clause 2.

29 The aforesaid fact thus clearly shows that plaintiff has sought recovery of possession of the suit premises from the defendant on the ground that tenancy of his father late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang was terminated vide legal notice dated 05.01.2001 Ex.PW1/3 by the previous owner. But the plaintiff has failed to prove on record that the previous owner of the suit premises had terminated the tenancy of late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang during his lifetime vide legal notice Ex.PW1/3. The plaintiff has failed to prove on record the legal notice Ex.PW1/3, on the basis of which he claimed that late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang was a statutory tenant at the time of his death and on his demise his legal heirs have acquired limited right to acquire the suit premises as per law. The consequences of said fact is that the plaintiff has failed to establish on record that late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang was a statutory tenant at the time of his death. The consequences of the same would be that the defendant, who is the legal heir of late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang, who was the tenant in the suit property, is not occupying the suit premises as an unauthorized occupant but Suit No. 186/12 Page no. 11/16 as a tenant on a monthly rent of Rs. 275/- per month i.e. less than 3,500/- per month.

30 In view of my above discussion my issue wise findings is as under:-

31 ISSUE NO. 1 Whether the defendant has inherited the tenancy right of his father, who was the tenant paying rent @ Rs. 250/- per month and thus the suit is barred by the provision of Delhi Rent Control Act? OPD In view of the above facts and circumstances, I am of the considered view that plaintiff has failed to prove on record that the tenancy of late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang was terminated by legal notice Ex.PW1/3. The plaintiff has also failed to prove on record that late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang was a statutory tenant at the time of his death. Late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang was thus a tenant in the suit premises at the time of his death and after his demise the tenancy right in the suit premises has been inherited by the defendant and his mother being his legal heir. The occupation of the defendant in the suit premises is thus of a tenant on a monthly rent of Rs. 275/- per month. The rent of the suit premises is thus less than Rs. 3500/- per month, therefore, the suit is barred under Section 50 of Delhi Rent Control Act. The defendant has successfully discharged the onus of Issue No. 1, same is accordingly decided in favour of Issue No. 1.

32 ISSUE NO. 2 Whether the father of the defendant was a statutory tenant in the premises bearing No. 7885 (51/1) Khasra No. 1660, Rohtak Road, WEA Karol Bagh, Delhi? OPD In view of the above facts and circumstances, I am of the considered view that plaintiff has failed to prove on record that the father of the defendant was a statutory tenant in the suit premises at the time of his death. The Suit No. 186/12 Page no. 12/16 plaintiff has thus failed to prove on record that late Sh. Shadi Lal Narang was a statutory tenant at the time of his death. The plaintiff has failed to discharge the onus of Issue No. 2, same is accordingly decided against the plaintiff.

33 ISSUE NO. 3 Whether the plaintiff is entitled to a decree of possession of the premises in question? OPP In view of my finding on Issue NO. 1 & 2, I am of the considered view that plaintiff is not entitled for decree of possession in the suit premises because the premises falls within the exclusive jurisdiction of Delhi Rent Control Act as the rent of suit premises is Rs. 275/- per month and the civil court cannot decree for possession with respect to such premises. The plaintiff has thus failed to discharge the onus of Issue No. 3 same is accordingly decided against the plaintiff.

34 ISSUE NO. 4 Whether the plaintiff is entitled for a decree of suit amount? OPP ISSUE NO.5 Whether the plaintiff is entitled for a decree of mandatory injunction as prayed in para-c of the prayer clause of the suit? OPP ISSUE NO.6 Whether the plaintiff is entitled for pendentlite and future interest, if yes on what rate? OPP In view of my finding on Issue no. 1 to 3, I am of the considered view that plaintiff is neither entitled for the amount as prayed by him nor he entitled for mandatory injunction as prayed in para-C of the prayer nor he is entitled for pendentlite and future interest as claimed by him. The plaintiff has thus failed to discharge the onus of Issue No. 4, 5 & 6, same are accordingly decided against the plaintiff.

35 ISSUE NO. 7 Whether the defendant if held to be a tenant is liable to retain the premises without payment of the market value? OPP The plaintiff has taken a plea in para 4 of the replication that if the plea Suit No. 186/12 Page no. 13/16 of the defendant is accepted that he is the tenant in the suit premises than as per the Judgment of Hon'ble Supreme Court Mohd. Ahmad v. Atma Ram Chauhan & Others DLT 2011 "the rent of the suit premises to be fixed as per the market rate if defendant pays the market rent then he is entitled for the protection otherwise he shall not be entitled for the protection."

36 The defendant has not got any opportunity to answer the said plea as the plaintiff has not taken the said plea in the plaint but in replication.

37 The Delhi Rent Control Act was enacted to provide for control of rents and evictions in the Union Territory of Delhi. Section 50 barred jurisdiction to Civil Court from entertaining any suit in so far as it related to fixation of standard rent in relation to premises to which the Act applied and / or to any other matter which the Rent Controller was empowered by or under the said Act to decide.

38 An amendment to the Delhi Rent Control Act was made w.e.f. 01.12.1988. the premises, monthly rent whereof exceeded Rs. 3,500/- were taken out of the purview of the Act; Section 6A was incorporated enabling the landlord to have the rent increased by 10 % every three years by issuing a notice under Section 8 intimating to the tenant of his desire to so have the rent increased and the increased rent became due and recoverable after expiry of 30 days from the date on which the notice was given.

39 The plaintiff claimed that the rent of the premises be fixed as per the marked rate. A similar question came for consideration before a Devision Bench of Hon'ble High Court in case titled "Santosh Vaid & Anr. v. Uttam Suit No. 186/12 Page no. 14/16 Chand and Amrit Ghai v. Darshan Singh reported as 188 (2012) DLT (DB) in para 11 of the said judgment the Hon'ble Delhi High Court has framed the following legal question to be answered:-

"Whether in the case of premises fetching rent of less than Rs. 3,500/- per month, the owner / landlord can claim increase in rent other than as provided under Sections 6A & 8 of the Act or have the rent increased in proportion to the rate of inflation or devaluation of money and if so on what basis and / or to what extent."

40 After examining each and every aspect, the Hon'ble Division Bench in para 29 of the said judgment has answered the said question which is reproduced as under:-

Pare 29- "We accordingly answer the question framed by us hereinabove as under: A landlord of a premises governed by the Delhi Rent Control Act, 1958 is entitled to have increase(s) in rent only in accordance with Section 6A and 8 thereof and not otherwise; such a landlord cannot approach the Civil Court contending that the rent stands increased or should be increased in accordance with the inflation or cost price index; the jurisdiction of the Civil Court in this regard is barred by Section 50 of the Delhi Rent Control Act."

41 The judgment relied upon by the plaintiff in para-4 of the replication was also referred before the Hon'ble High Court and the Hon'ble High Court in Para-23 (III) has held that the said judgment was passed in the context of UP Rent Act and same have no application to the position as prevailing in Delhi.

Suit No. 186/12 Page no. 15/16

42 The above legal preposition laid down by the Division Bench of the Hon'ble High Court makes it absolutely clear that the rent with respect to the premises falling within the purview of Delhi Rent Control Act can only be increased as per Section 6 A & 8 of Delhi Rent Control Act and not otherwise. Moreover, the Civil Court is not vested with any jurisdiction to fix the rent as per the market rate. The plaintiff has thus failed to discharge the onus of Issue No. 7, same is accordingly decided against the plaintiff.

43 RELIEF In view of the above facts and circumstances, the suit filed by the plaintiff against the defendant is dismissed. No order of cost. Decree sheet be accordingly prepared. File be consigned to record room after due compliance.
