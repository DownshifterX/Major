 JUDGMENT

1. Brief facts of the plaintiffs/appellants' case: Plaintiffs in the trial court are the appellants herein. Plaintiff no. 1 and 2, RCA No. 108 /2013 Shri Padam Singh & Anr. Vs. Shri Ramesh Chand & Ors. Page1/23 alongwith Defendant no.2 are the heirs of late Gulab Singh (who died in the years 1972, leaving behind the LRs as above and a widow Bhagwati Devi). It is contended that late Gulab Singh is the owner of a property bearing no. 60, Gali Jagbar, Mohalla- Kundewalan, Ajmeri Gate, Delhi. Bhagwati Devi also expired on 16.10.1997.

2. One Bishanswaroop S/o Giridhari Lal was the tenant of late Gulab Singh on a monthly rent of Rs. 8/-. The said Bishanswaroop died on 12.4.2004, leaving behind the defendant no.1 as the heir who is in possession of the tenanted premises.

3. Plaintiffs also contended that their mother, late Bhagwati Devi got served a legal notice terminating the tenancy of Bishanswaroop on 06.09.1993 contending that the contractual tenancy expired on 31.10.1993 and thereafter he remained a statutory tenant. Plaintiffs also contended that late Bhagwati Devi Demanded arrears of rent Rs. 7,342/- for the period 01.01.1991 till 12.4.2004 at rate of Rs. 8/- per month and thereafter at rate of Rs. 10/- per day from 13.04.2004 till 15.02.2006. The plaintiffs being the heirs of late Gulab Singh had filed the suit for possession and recovery of sums above noted.

4. Case of the Defendant no. 1 in the trial court: IN the trial court the defendant no.1 denying all the allegations contended that plaintiff no.1 is the land lord and no notice of arrears of rent RCA No. 108 /2013 Shri Padam Singh & Anr. Vs. Shri Ramesh Chand & Ors. Page2/23 and terminating the tenancy was served by the plaintiffs nor by late Bhagwati Devi. The alleged notice dated 06.09.1993 is false. Rents were paid up to December, 1998 against the receipts. The suit is barred by time. Upon the death of Gulab Singh, the defendant no. 2 also succeeded. They also questioned the jurisdiction of the civil court since section 50 of the DRC Act bars the same as the rent is below Rs. 3500/- p.m. They also contended that the property is in slums area and the plaintiffs have not sought permission u/s 19 of the Slums Clearance Act. The suit is also bad for non joinder of Smt. Raj Rani, daughter of Bishanswaroop who is the co-tenant.

5. Case of D-2 in the trial court: The defendant no.2 who is also the co-owner of the suit property contended that the plaintiffs without informing her had filed the present suit to grab the property from themselves without impleading Smt. Raj Rani, D/o late Gulab Singh. The plaintiff no.1 issued the rent receipt even after the death under the name of his father to the plaintiff no.1. D-2 also questioned the jurisdiction of the court u/s 19 of the DRC Act.

6. Issues in the trial court: The court below had framed four issues as under:

1. Whether the plaintiff is entitled to a decree for possession of the suit premises as prayed for? OPP.
2. Whether the plaintiff is entitled to a decree for recovery of Rs. 7342/- as claimed? OPP
3. Whether the plaintiffs have no locus standi to file the present RCA No. 108 /2013 Shri Padam Singh & Anr. Vs. Shri Ramesh Chand & Ors. Page3/23 suit? OPD
4. Whether the suit for the plaintiff is barred by the provisions of section 50 of DRC Act ? OPD
5. Relief claimed.

7. Findings of the trial court: By the impugned judgment and decree in suit no. 1015/2006 on the above pleadings, the court below has answered the legal issue arising out of section 50 of DRC Act held that since the rent is below Rs. 3500/- per month, the suit is not maintainable. After clubbing all the issues together and after appreciating the oral and documentary evidence, the court below found that late Gulab Singh had become the owner of the property under a sale deed dated 17.12.1974 but the plaint had pleaded that Gulab Singh died in the year 1972. Since this fact of pleading is in conflict with the sale deed, the court disbelieved the contentions of the plaintiff. It also noted that the legal notice terminating the tenancy dated 06.09.1993 was never acted till the filing of the suit i.e. 20.02.2006. Considering the Limitation Act, the suit is held as bad and barred by limitation. Thus, the ld.court below had dismissed the suit.

8. Grounds in the Appeal in brief: Plaintiffs assail the above said findings on the ground that the trial court ignored the provisions of law of inheritance and the DRC Act. The signatures of Bishansarup and documents at Ex. P-2 to P-5 having not been traversed amounts to admissions. The well settled dictum that the RCA No. 108 /2013 Shri Padam Singh & Anr. Vs. Shri Ramesh Chand & Ors. Page4/23 tenant could not challenge the title of the land lord is ignored. There has been misreading with regard to provisions of section 50 of the DRC Act. There was a misreading by the trial court as regards the admitted relationship of landlord and tenant between the parties. Even after issuance of notice, it is nobody's case that there is a fresh tenancy. The evidence of the parties was not appreciated especially Ex. P-2. It is also urged that the court below erred in holding that the production of documents to show the ownership of the landlord is not discharged. The owner need not prove the title. The court below also gravely erred in observing of law of limitation applies. The non-impleadment of Raj Rani, sister of respondent no.1 is not required since she is living separately in Aligarh. Even during the lifetime of Bishan Sarup.

9. The respondents have filed their reply contended that the order of the trial court is justified especially in the wake of observation that Gulab Singh died in 1972 but title shows his name only in the year 1974. They have also relied on the additional evidence by filing an application u/o 41 rule 27 to bring in the deposition of cross examination of P.K. Narayan examined in suit no. 73/12/12. the same was allowed since the appellant has no objection.

10. During the course of arguments, the ld. counsel for the appellant submits that after death of the tenant LRs have become RCA No. 108 /2013 Shri Padam Singh & Anr. Vs. Shri Ramesh Chand & Ors. Page5/23 the statutory tenants and right to continue remained there up to one year only and the findings of the trial court on Ex. P-2 is perverse. The ld. counsel Shri S.K. Bhalla vehemently argued that the notice was issued in the year 1993 and the finding of the trial court that the same is barred by limitation is incorrect since the cause of action arose only in the year 2004 when Bishan Sarup died. He further vehemently argued that section 50 of the DRC Act does not apply in this case since the tenancy rights do not devolve on defendants since the service of termination of tenancy was already served on Bishansarup by Bhagwati Devi. The ld. counsel had relied on several rulings in support of his contentions.

11. Shri Mahabir Singh, Ld. counsel for the respondent has vehemently contended that the appellant has no locus to maintain the suit for eviction since no where the name of the appellant is shown in the title or in the mutation. The mutation till shows the name of Giridhari Lal who died in the year 1974 and the rent receipts are issued in his name. He also submits the notice of termination of tenancy is without of cause of action. He further urges that the respondent was financially dependent on the original tenant and therefore section 2 (1) protects his tenancy. He points out there were four LRs of the landlord and the defendant no.2 in the trial court being one of them never authorised her mother as the landlord. He submits that the defendant is entitled RCA No. 108 /2013 Shri Padam Singh & Anr. Vs. Shri Ramesh Chand & Ors. Page6/23 for protection u/s 50 of Delhi Rent Control Act. The same were replied by the ld. counsel for the appellant the authorisation of the LRs is not pleaded and is coming for the first time. He further submits that section 50 of the DRC Act has no role since Explanation II found in section 2(l) of the DRC Act would come into play.

12. Issues before this court: In view of the submissions and the contentions of the parties, the following issues arise for consideration before this court.

1. Whether the suit for eviction was valid for non-joinder of all the LRs of the deceased landlord/tenant?

2. Whether the impugned judgment of the ld. court below suffers from the legal infirmities, non application of mind rendering it to be set aside or not ?

3. If so what relief.

13. Findings:

Issue no.1: It is admitted case that late Gulab Singh, the landlord had left behind him one son and two daughters namely Padam Singh, Sita Rani and Birmo Devi ( D-2 in the trial court). The defendant no.2 had contended that she is also the co-owner and also contended that plaintiff no.1 Padam Singh has received the rents after the death of his father. She also contends that suit is barred u/s 50 of the DRC Act. The non-joinder of one of the LRs of RCA No. 108 /2013 Shri Padam Singh & Anr. Vs. Shri Ramesh Chand & Ors. Page7/23 the landlord do not come in the way of filing the eviction petition. It is settled law that one of the co-owners can alone in his own right may file the eviction petition and this shall not be a defence for the tenant as held in Kanta Goel Vs. V.P. Pathak, 1977 (2) SCC 814 and also by our Hon' High Court in Surjeet Singh Vs. Pahilaj 65 (1977) DLT 22. Likewise, the contention of the tenants that non-joinder of married daughters of the deceased-tenants debars the other co-
owner is also not bad in law as held in Rani Devi Vs. Bhole Nath & Ors. reported in 1991 (2) of All India Rent Control Journal 556.
Thus, the suit for eviction cannot be held as bad in law as contended by the ld. counsel for the respondents.

14. Issue no.2. This issue is fraught with technical interpretations of the provisions of DRC Act which glaringly confounds when read with the provisions of Limitation Act. The mixed question of facts and law concerning the service of notices issued by the deceased land-lady to the deceased-tenant also scrambles up in the wake of the arguments advanced. The written arguments filed by the appellant is also is perused. The respondent no. 1 has not filed any written argument.

15. The ld. court below holds that section 50 of the DRC Act comes into play since the rent is below Rs. 3500/-. The findings of the ld. court below is that the suit is barred u/s 50 of the DRC Act. The respondent is vehemently submitting the same. It is no doubt RCA No. 108 /2013 Shri Padam Singh & Anr. Vs. Shri Ramesh Chand & Ors. Page8/23 that u/s 50 of the DRC Act, the jurisdiction of the civil court are barred in the matters of eviction where the act applies. This section is to be understood with the definitions found in section 2 (l) of the Act. Tenant means any person by whom the rent of any premises would be payable which includes a sub tenant or any person continuing any possession after the termination of his tenancy. Section 2 (l) is being reproduced as under:

(l) tenant means any person by whom or on whose account or behalf the rent of any premises is or, but for a special contract, would be payable, and includes
(i) a sub- tenant;
(ii) any person continuing in possession after the termination of his tenancy; and
(iii) in the event of the death of the person continuing in possession after the termination of his tenancy, subject to the order of succession and conditions specified, respectively, in Explanation I and Explanation II to this clause, such of the aforesaid person's-
(a) spouse,
(b) son or daughter, or, where there are both son and daughter, both of them,
(c) parents,
(d) daughter-in-law, being the widow of his pre-deceased son, as had been ordinarily living in the premises with such person as a member or members of his family up to the date of his death, but does not includes,- (A) any person against whom an order or decree for eviction has been made, except where such decree or RCA No. 108 /2013 Shri Padam Singh & Anr. Vs. Shri Ramesh Chand & Ors. Page9/23 order for eviction is liable to be re-opened under the proviso to Section 3 of the Delhi Rent Control (Amendment ) Act, 1976, (18 of 1976).
(B) any person to whom a licence as defined by Section 52 of the Indian Easement Act, 1882 (5 of 1882) has been granted.

Explanation I- The order of succession in the event of the death of the person continuing in possession after the termination of his tenancy shall be as follows:-

(a) firstly, his surviving spouse;
(b) secondly, his son or daughter, or both, if there is no surviving spouse, or if the surviving spouse did not ordinarily live with the deceased person as a member of his family up to the date of his death;
(c) thirdly, his parents, if there is no surviving spouse, son or daughter of the deceased person, or if such surviving spouse, son or daughter or any of them, did not ordinarily live in the premises as a member of the family of the deceased person up to the date of his death; and
(d) fourthly, his daughter-in-law, being the widow of his pre-deceased son, if there is no surviving spouse, son, daughter or parents of the deceased person, or if such surviving spouse, son, daughter or parents, or any of them, did not ordinarily live in the premises as a member of the family of the deceased person up to the date of his death.

Explanation II- If the person, who acquires, by succession, the right to continue in possession after the termination of the tenancy, was not financially dependent on the deceased person on the date of his death, such successor shall acquire such right for a RCA No. 108 /2013 Shri Padam Singh & Anr. Vs. Shri Ramesh Chand & Ors. Page10/23 limited period of one year; and on the expiry of that period, or his death, which ever is earlier, the right of such successor to continue in possession after the termination of the tenancy shall become extinguished.

Explanation III- For the removal of doubts, it is hereby declared that:

(a) where, by reason of Explanation II, the right of any successor to continue in possession after the termination of the tenancy becomes extinguished, such extinguishment shall not affect the right of any other successor of the same category to continue in possession after the termination of the tenancy; but if there is no other successor of the same category, the right to continue in possession after the termination of the tenancy shall not, on such extinguishment, pass on to any other successor specified in any lower categories, as the case may be;
(b) the right of every successor, referred to in Explanation I to continue in possession after the termination of the tenancy, shall be personal to him and shall not, on the death of such successor, devolve on any of his heirs.
(m) "urban area" has the same meaning as in the Delhi Municipal Corporation Act, 1957 (66of 1957).

16. It is contended that the tenant Bishansarup expired on 12.4.2004 who was a statutory tenant. His rights according to section 2 (l) in the event of his death after termination of tenancy passes on as detailed above. In this case, it is pleaded in the suit that after the death of Bishansarup, the defendant no.1 is the only legal heir and his possession is akin to a tress passer since he could not inherit as he was not dependent on his father during his RCA No. 108 /2013 Shri Padam Singh & Anr. Vs. Shri Ramesh Chand & Ors. Page11/23 lifetime. The tenancy was terminated under notice to the tenant during his lifetime. The ld. counsel for the appellant argues that as per Explanation III of Section 2 (l), the respondent is not entitled to remain in occupation after the period of one year from the date of death of his father. After the death of the said tenant Bishansarup, it is seen that respondent no.1 succeeded for a period of one year to enjoy such tenancy rights as found in Explanation II. If he was financially dependent, the right to enjoy the property is perpetuated. If not, it continued only for a period of one year. After expiry of that period, the rights to continue in possession after terminating of the tenancy gets extinguished. According to the ld. counsel for appellant, the respondent no.1 was not dependent financially on his father. It is already mentioned in para 3 of the plaint, such an assertion is made. In the W.S. filed by the defendant no.1 in the court below while dealing with para 3 of the plaint, the defendant no.1 has taken the stand that he himself is a regular tenant and his father was also a regular tenant. His father's tenancy was not terminated. He had been paying the rents even after the death of his father but no rent receipt was issued. Again, it is asserted that defendant no. 1 is a lawful tenant. The D-1 has not traversed the allegation that the defendant no.1 was not financially dependent. The ld. counsel for the appellant submits that non meeting of this pleading would amount to express RCA No. 108 /2013 Shri Padam Singh & Anr. Vs. Shri Ramesh Chand & Ors. Page12/23 admission as provided u/o 8 rule 5 of the CPC. Therefore by necessary implication, it is urged that this fact be treated as deemed admission.

17. As regards this financial dependency or otherwise of the respondent, the evidence adduced by DW-1 is perused. According to him, plaintiff no.1 is the landlord and no notice was served by the plaintiff to his father Bishansarup. He has not testified that he was financially dependent on his father. The evidence of other witnesses DW2 and 3 is not helpful to arrive at the financial dependency. Therefore the evidence reveals that he was not financially dependent on late Bishansarup since no such denial is found from the written statement. If there is no evidence to this effect the status of Ramesh Chand can be read only as a tenant at sufferance. DW-1-Ramesh Chand who also contended that he is an independent tenant has relied on Ex. DW1/B to Ex. DW1/D. He has produced 36 rent receipts between 1962 to 1998 marked as Ex. PW1/K1. These are issued by Gulab Singh in the name of Giridhari Lal which was collected by Padam Singh. The MO was sent by Bishansarup to Bhagwati Devi. The name of Ramesh Chand does not figure in the receipts to prove his independent tenancy. Ex. DW3/1 to 3 are all municipal records which are not helpful to arrive at the financial dependency or otherwise. Therefore presuming the implication from the pleadings, it is to be held that it is a deemed RCA No. 108 /2013 Shri Padam Singh & Anr. Vs. Shri Ramesh Chand & Ors. Page13/23 admission u/o 8 rule 5 of CPC and further not proved by any cogent evidence.

18. It is also contended by the respondent counsel that the collection of rents even after the death of Bishansarup created a fresh tenancy. The same is countered by the ld. counsel for the appellant urging that mere acceptance of rents would not create a fresh tenancy. He relies on in C. Albert Morris Vs. K. Chandrasekharan (2006) 1 SCC 228. It is held that mere acceptance of rent by the landlord, after the tenancy is terminated would not create any fresh lease. The first respondent herein continued in possession after the lease has been determined either by efflux of time or by notice to quit would not create a tenancy afresh.

19. In view of the above, the status of Ramesh Chand becoming only a tenant in sufferance is confirmed since he could not lead any evidence and prove that there was a fresh tenancy. Thus, his status becomes a tenant in sufferance. After termination of the tenancy of Bishansarup, his son who had succeeded but not dependent financially on his father could enjoy protection only for a period of one year as per Explanation II found in section 2 (l). Bishansarup expired on 12.04.2004. Therefore, it is contended that his son is not tenant entitled for any protection under the DRC Act much less section 50 would apply. Considering the provisions and the law as RCA No. 108 /2013 Shri Padam Singh & Anr. Vs. Shri Ramesh Chand & Ors. Page14/23 noted above, there is no impediment to hold that the respondent no.1 remained only as tenant in sufferance and therefore section 50 would not come into play at all. The ld. court below has not looked into this aspect, the nature of the possession of defendant no.1 before it.

20. Limitation aspect: The next limb of argument of the ld. counsel for the respondent is that there is no perversity in the findings of the ld. court below as regards limitation. 21 Ld. counsel for the appellant submits that the suit was well within the time and the cause of action arose in the year 2004 after the death of Bishansarup who was the original tenant. While adducing additional evidence, which is also admitted by the landlord, it is clear from the cross examination of Shri Kailash Narayan, the counsel who was engaged by the landlord then, that he issued the legal notice. The said legal notice is also produced in the trial court which is marked as Ex. P-2. The said notice is dated 06.09.1993 and from the contents of the notice, it is made clear that the tenancy of Bishansarup is determined by Bhagwati Deiv, widow of Gulab Singh with effect from 31.10.1993. The findings of the court below as regards the above point-though not a specific issue is framed, the ld. court below held that " according to limitation act 1963 suit for possession by the landlord from the tenant shall be filed within 12 years". Article-67 of Schedule to the RCA No. 108 /2013 Shri Padam Singh & Anr. Vs. Shri Ramesh Chand & Ors. Page15/23 Limitation Act prescribes a period of 12 years for recovery of possession from a tenant. The time from which the period begins to run is " when the tenancy is determined." From the admitted facts and evidence that is from Ex. P-2 which service is flayed by the respondents, it is very clear that the tenancy is determined with effect from 31.10.1993. Therefore, the limitation to file the suit for recovery runs from this date though the date of notice is 06.09.1993. The innocuous reading of section 67 without any implications of the special act namely the DRC Act would lead anyone to hold that the suit for possession is hit by limitation. In this case one cannot wink at the provisions of DRC Act which had prescribed certain provisions regarding the death of tenant and the rules of succession of such rights in favour of the survivors of such tenants. It is made clear u/s 2 (l) that the successor who is not dependent on the tenant financially would have the right to continue for a period of one year after the death of such tenant. After the expiry of such period the right to remain gets extinguished. It is clear from the facts undisputed that Bishansarup died on 12.04.2004. After his death, his son could have continued for a period of one year only since the pleadings by necessary implication reveals that he was not financially dependent. Therefore he could have continued in possession till 12.04.2005. Thereafter, it becomes very difficult to hold that he was in lawful possession. RCA No. 108 /2013 Shri Padam Singh & Anr. Vs. Shri Ramesh Chand & Ors. Page16/23 The plaintiff had pleaded that the cause of action to the suit arose on 12.04.2005 after the period of one year and lastly on 10.02.2006 while denying these allegations, no proper Explanation is forthcoming from the written statement of the defendant no.1. More over, the DRC Act having created a clog to institute the suit for recovery of possession, the Limitation Act simplicitor would not be the yard stick to reckon the limitation. The possession of the tenant in sufferance having become akin to the tress passer as held C. Albert Morris (supra), he cannot be treated as a tenant so as to invite the provisions of article 67 of the Limitation Act. It is to held that this aspect has escaped the attention of ld. court below while dealing with the aspect of limitation.

22. Service of notice on Bishansarup. From the additional evidence adduced by the respondent no.1, it becomes very clear that one Kailash Narayan, advocate had addressed a notice which is Ex. P-2. There is some over writing at Ex. P-5 which the postal acknowledgment card wherein there over writings on the name of addressee. It is mentioned in the notice that the notice is Ex. P-2 is being sent by RPAD as well as certificate of posting. The Ld. counsel for the respondent had argued that the counsel who sent the notice while deposing before the court in connected matter was unable to recall the client who approached him and that he could not identify the photographs of his clients confronted to him. This RCA No. 108 /2013 Shri Padam Singh & Anr. Vs. Shri Ramesh Chand & Ors. Page17/23 additional evidence now marked as Ex. C-1 before this court is read carefully. It is found in the cross examination of the said witness- Kailash Narayan that the notice was sent for posting to the post office through his clerk. A suggestion was made that the Bhagwati Devi did not come before him for getting the notice served on Bishansarup. This suggestion is denied as wrong. He also denies a suggestion that he cannot identify Bhagwati Devi by photograph but he further stated that he cannot identify the photograph shown to him without any spectacle. The ld. counsel for the appellant relied on the ruling of Meghwanti Vs. Surender Mohan ILR 1972 (1) Delhi 714 wherein our high Court held a civil court is not entitled to hold an identification parade in order to see whether the plaintiff could identify the defendant. Keeping in mind of the above the non identification of the photo by the witness who is an aged counsel cannot be held to be fatal because in the civil proceedings the preponderance and probabilities are to be looked into and not the bone test to arrive at, sans any doubt. Suffice it to say that the non identification of the photograph is not fatal. Coming to the scoring off-over writing found at Ex. P-5, it is to be seen that the Ex. P2 is also supported by Ex. P-5 the certificate of posting also which bears the date as 08.09.1993. It is also supported by receipt dated 06.09.1993 addressed to Bishansarup which is at Ex. P-3.

23. Ex. P-2 is a notice terminating the tenancy of Bishansarup. RCA No. 108 /2013 Shri Padam Singh & Anr. Vs. Shri Ramesh Chand & Ors. Page18/23 The non service of such notice is also very much pleaded and vehemently argued by Shri Mahavir Singh, ld. counsel for the respondent no.1. In this aspect as regards the presumption available to the sender of the notice comes into play. In Skyland International Pvt. Ltd. Vs. Kavita P. Lalwani 191 (2012) DLT 594, his lordship Justice J. R. Midha has observed "26.3 Even assuming that the notice of termination was not served upon the appellant, though there is legal presumption of since as held above, the tenancy stood terminated on the filing of the suit in terms of the judgments of the Supreme Court in Nopany Investments ( P) Ltd. Vs. Santokh Singh (HUF) (supra) and of this Court in Jeevan Diesels & Electricals Ltd. VS. M/s Jasbir Singh Chandha (HUF) (supra), Rabinder Nath Saha VS. Sushma Jain (supra) and Shri Radhkrishan Temple Trust Maithan, Agra Vs. M/s Hindco Rotatron Pvt. Ltd. (supra) in this regard". Apart from this, In Union Bank of India Vs. Sushila Goela and others, 2005 VIII AD ( Delhi) 541, the division Bench of our Hon' High Court held that the object of a notice under Section 106 of the Transfer of Property Act, 1872 is to inform the other party as to the intention of the person issuing the notice that he wants the premises back. Section 106 Transfer of Property Act, 1872 is not to be scrutinized by hair splitting precision. It is not a pleading but a mere communication of the intention to the recipient." In view of the above the changing RCA No. 108 /2013 Shri Padam Singh & Anr. Vs. Shri Ramesh Chand & Ors. Page19/23 aspects of law concerning the service of notice section 106 of the TP Act, the same cannot be taken up as a hair splitting issue. The ld. court below while dealing with this aspect of service of notice has not dealt while dealing issue no.1 framed.

24. The ld. court below was carried away by the testimony of DW3 who said that the suit property stood in the name of Suraj Singh prior to the mutation letter Ex. DW3/1. He further stated that basis of mutation is 17.12.1974. He further testified that Gulab Singh continuous to the owner of his portion. The ld. court below was baffled to know how that Gulab Singh who died in the year 1972 could have been a party to the sale deed of the year 1974. I have perused Ex. DW3/1 to DW3/3. It is only a municipal inspection form and not the change of mutation entry except Ex. DW3/1 which intimates one Dropadi Devi that property no. 1/2668 of ward no. VIII is mutated into her name and the date of mutation is 02.02.1984. This aspect would have never bothered the ld. court below that since the settled position of law is that a tenant cannot challenge the title of his landlord. Further more, from the pleadings, it is clear that the tenant Ramesh Chand had pleaded at one place that he is the regular tenant and also has taken up an alternate plea that he is the dependent on the original tenant namely the Bishansarup. Thus, the tenant cannot challenge the title of the landlord howsoever it be doubtful. The court which is to adjudicate upon the RCA No. 108 /2013 Shri Padam Singh & Anr. Vs. Shri Ramesh Chand & Ors. Page20/23 eligibility of the prayers of eviction of a landlord in a given situation where the relationship is not disputed could have never overshadowed the lis by looking into the side lights which are not the part of the lis.

25. In view of the above, this court is of the opinion that the impugned judgment and decree of the court below is lacking in properly appreciating the entire conspectus of the matter as regards the legalities arising out of the status of the defendant no. 1 after the death of Bishansarup, the legal position u/s 2 (l) of the DRC Act, the non applicability of section 50 of the DRC Act to the case on hand, the law of presumption operating concerning the issuance of notice u/s 106 of TP Act and paddling the way in the conflicted provisions of law arising out of the DRC Act as well as article 67 of the Limitation Act.

26. Hence, it is concluded the ld. court below had not applied its mind rendering the impugned judgment to be set aside in the background of above reasons. Thus, issue no.2 is answered accordingly.

The defendant no. 1 in the trial court who had taken an stand that the suit is not maintainable in view of Slum Clearance Act, did not lead any evidence to establish that the suit property is a declared slum and that thus a statutory clog is created against the plaintiff in the trial court for his eviction. Thus, the ground urged RCA No. 108 /2013 Shri Padam Singh & Anr. Vs. Shri Ramesh Chand & Ors. Page21/23 regarding the same in the appeal is only superfluous and not supported by any cogent evidence.

27. Issue no.3: Having answered the above issues that framed by this court as above, this court is constrained to pass a decree of eviction against the respondent no.1. It was argued by the ld. counsel for the respondent no.1 that a reasonable time of one year may be given for vacating the premises since the respondent no.1 had lived there for two generations and it becomes very difficult to search alternate accommodation. In view of the peculiar circumstances of this case, respondent no.1 is given a reasonable time of 10 moths to vacate the premises in question.

28. In the peculiar circumstances of the case, the prayer of the appellant to treat the mesne profit at rate of Rs. 10/- per day is not allowed since the LRs of Gulab Singh had been accepting the earlier rent. No evidence is led as regards the mesne profits in the court below, except stating that he is entitled for Rs. 10/- per day in the testimony of plaintiff Hence, the following order:

ORDER The appeal is allowed. The impugned judgment and decree of the court below in suit no. 1015/2006 dated 29.02.2012 is hereby set aside. There shall be a decree of grant of recovery of possession in favour of the plaintiff in the trial court ( appellants) concerning the suit premises of one Room and half veranda on the RCA No. 108 /2013 Shri Padam Singh & Anr. Vs. Shri Ramesh Chand & Ors. Page22/23 first floor of the property bearing no. 668, Gali Jatwara, Mohalla Kundewalan, Ajmeri Gate, Delhi-110006 as described in the site plan. The defendant no.1 in the trial court is directed to quit and deliver the vacant possession of the same within 10 months from today subject to paying accepted rents regularly to the landlord or by depositing the same in the trial court.
Parties shall bear their own costs. The decree be drawn accordingly. The TCR be sent back alongwith a copy of this judgment. File be consigned to Record Room.
