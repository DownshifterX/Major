Madhya Pradesh High Court

Madhya Pradesh Housingh Board & Anr. vs Malwa It Park Ltd.& Ors. on 30 July, 2015

Author: P.K. Jaiswal

Bench: Tarun Kumar Kaushal, P.K. Jaiswal

                         ORDER

(Passed on this 30th day of July, 2015) Per P.K. Jaiswal, J. 

           Heard on IA        No.5807/2014            and       IA
No.561/2015.

Respondents No.1 to 6, who are landowners of certain lands of village Khajarana Jagir, Tahsil & District Indore, as mentioned in paragraph No.5.2 of the writ petition, have filed IA No.5808/2014 for direction to the appellants to pay compensation, as per the provisions of the Right to Fair Compensation and Transparency in Land Acquisition, Rehabilitation and Resettlement Act, 2013 (in short, New Act of 2013).

2. Brief facts of the case are that a notification under Section 4 (1) of the Land Acquisition Act, 1894 (in short, the Act of 1894) was issued on 4th April, 2008, 2 WA No.330/2009 whereby the land of respondents No.1 to 6 was proposed for acquisition for a public purpose, which was indicated as "to provide plots and for the Housing Scheme under Jawaharlal Nehru National Urban Renewal Mission (JNNURM). The Executive Engineer of the Housing Board was indicated as the Authorized Officer in terms of Section 4 (2) of the Act of 1894. Urgency provisions under Section 17 (1) of the Act of 1894 were invoked, thereby dispensing with the provisions of Section 5-A of the Act of 1894 and taking away the right of the landowners to file objections thereunder.

3. Respondents No.1 to 6 aggrieved by the issuance of the aforesaid notification, challenged the same by filing Writ Petition No.2146/2009 before this Court by challenging the inaction of the appellants.

4. A preliminary grievance was raised that there being no urgency in the matter, the provisions of Section 17 (1) of the Act of 1894 had wrongly been invoked. Learned Writ Court took up all the identical writ petitions together for hearing and by a common order dated 25th June, 2008 passed in Writ Petition No.2696/2008 disposed of the matter with liberty to the landowners to raise objections under Section 5-A of the Act of 1894, on or before 18th July, 2008, in the Office of the Land Acquisition Officer. In compliance to the order dated 25th June, 2008 passed by this Court in Writ Petition No.2696/2008, respondents No.1 to 6 raised their objections before respondent No.8 (Commissioner, Indore 3 WA No.330/2009 Revenue Division, Indore). Respondent No.8, in exercise of his powers appropriate Government, after examining the report and other aspect of the matter, vide order dated 24th March, 2009, granted approval to the recommendations made by the Land Acquisition Officer. After according approval to the report of respondent No.8, a declaration / notification under Section 6 of the Act of 1894 was signed by the State Government on 31st March, 2009 and was sent for publication in the official gazette and newspapers. The declaration under Section 6 of the Act of 1894 was published only in one newspaper on 1st April, 2009.

5. Respondents No.1 to 6 challenged the said action by filing Writ Petition No.2146/2009. The Writ Court, after hearing the arguments of the learned counsel for the parties, vide order dated 7th August, 2009 passed in Writ Petition No.2146/2009 allowed the writ petition and set aside the recommendations made by the Land Acquisition Officer and also the approval granted by the appropriate Government and remanded the case to the Land Acquisition Officer for hearing afresh. Relevant part of the order dated 7th August, 2009 passed in Writ Petition No.2146/2009 reads as under: -

"Consequently, in view of the aforesaid discussion, the present petitions are allowed. The report dated March 19, 2009, forwarded by the Collector, Indore, recommending the rejection of objections filed by the land owners under Section 5-A of the Act, and the order dated March 24, 2009, passed by the 4 WA No.330/2009 Commissioner, Indore Division, Indore, are hereby set aside. The objections filed by the land owners under Section 5-A of the Act shall be deemed to be alive before the Collector. The said objections shall now be decided afresh by the Collector, in accordance with law. Needless to say that while undertaking the aforesaid process, the law laid down by this Court and the Apex Court in various judgments, including the judgment in Hindustan Petroleum's case (supra) and also the observations made by this Court in present judgment, on the issues of law, shall be kept in view.
Before parting with this order, it must be clarified that, however, any observations made by this Court in this order, which may have the effect of touching the merits of the objections, in any manner, shall not be treated to be an expression of opinion by this Court on the said objections. The objections filed by the land owners shall be adjudicated, in accordance with law, independently, by the appropriate Authorities."

6. As per the notification, the purpose of acquisition was "to provide plots and for Housing Scheme under JNNURM" and as per the appellants, the entire acquisition in question was for twin purposes; firstly, to make available plots under JNNURM; and secondly, for execution of the Housing Scheme. Indore Municipal Corporation had been indicated as the Nodal Agency.

7. The appellants - Madhya Pradesh Housing Board aggrieved by the order dated 7th August, 2009 passed by the learned Writ Court in Writ Petition No.2146/2009 challenged the same by filing this intra court appeal.

5 WA No.330/2009

8. During pendency of this writ appeal, an application (IA No.5807/2014) for appropriate directions has been filed by respondents No.1 to 6 on the ground that the learned Writ Court by the impugned order dated 7th August, 2009 observed that since no valid notification under Section 6 of the Act has been issued, hence, no directions qua the said notification are required and directed that the objections filed by the answering respondents No.1 to 6 under Section 5-A of the Act, shall be deemed to be alive before the Collector, who shall decide it afresh, in accordance with law.

9. It is submitted by the learned counsel for respondents No.1 to 6 that during pendency of the present writ appeal, Union of India promulgated the Right to Fair Compensation and Transparency in Land Acquisition, Rehabilitation and Resettlement Act, 1013, which has been brought into force with effect from 1st January, 2014 and the provisions of Section 24 of the New Act of 2013 have categorically provide that as regards land acquisition proceedings where no award under Section 11 of the Act of 1894 has been made, then all provisions of this New Act of 2013, relating to determination of compensation shall apply. Section 24 of the New Act of 2013 reads, as under: -

"24. (1) Notwithstanding anything contained in this Act, in any case of land acquisition proceedings initiated under the Land Acquisition Act, 1894, -
(a) where no award under section 11 of the said Land Acquisition Act has been made, then, all provisions of this Act 6 WA No.330/2009 relating to the determination of compensation shall apply; or
(b) where an award under said section 11 has been made, then such proceedings shall continue under the provisions of the said Land Acquisition Act, as if the said Act has not been repealed.
(2) Notwithstanding anything contained in sub-section (1), in case of land acquisition proceedings initiated under the Land Acquisition Act, 1894, where an award under the said section 11 has been made five years or more prior to the commencement of this Act but the physical possession of the land had not been taken or the compensation has not been paid the said proceedings shall be deemed to have lapsed and the appropriate Government, if it so chooses, shall initiate the proceedings of such land acquisition afresh in accordance with the provisions of this Act;

Provided that where an award has been made and compensation in respect of a majority of land holdings has not been deposited in the account of the beneficiaries, then all beneficiaries specified in the notification for acquisition under section 4 of the said Land Acquisition Act, shall be entitled to compensation in accordance with the provisions of this Act."

10. The Apex Court in the case of Pune Municipal Corporation and another v. Harakchand Misirimal Solanki & others reported in (2014) 3 SCC 183 held, as under: -

"10. Insofar as sub-section (1) of Section 24 is concerned, it begins with non obstante clause. By this, Parliament has given overriding effect to this provision over all other provisions of 2013 Act. It is provided in clause (a) that where the land acquisition proceedings have been initiated under the 1894 Act but no award 7 WA No.330/2009 under Section 11 is made, then the provisions of 2013 Act shall apply relating to the determination of compensation. Clause (b) of Section 24 (1) makes provision that where land acquisition proceedings have been initiated under the 1894 Act and award has been made under Section 11, then such proceedings shall continue under the provisions of the 1894 Act as if that Act has not been repealed.
11. Section 24 (2) also begins with non obstante clause. This provision has overriding effect over Section 24 (1). Section 24 (2) enacts that in relation to the land acquisition proceedings initiated under 1894 Act, where an award has been made five years or more prior to the commencement of the 2013 Act and either of the two contingencies is satisfied, viz;
(i) physical possession of the land has not been taken or (ii) the compensation has not been paid, such acquisition proceedings shall be deemed to have lapsed. On the lapse of such acquisition proceedings, if the appropriate government still chooses to acquire the land which was the subject matter of acquisition under the 1894 Act then it has to initiate the proceedings afresh under the 2013 Act. The proviso appended to Section 24 (2) deals with a situation where in respect of the acquisition initiated under the 1894 Act an award has been made and compensation in respect of a majority of land holdings has not been deposited in the account of the beneficiaries then all the beneficiaries specified in Section 4 notification become entitled to compensation under 2013 Act."

11. The Apex Court in the case of Bharat Kumar v. State of Haryana & another reported in (2014) 6 SCC 586 held that sub-section (2) of Section 24 commences with a non obstante clause. It is a beneficial provision. In view of this provision, if the physical 8 WA No.330/2009 possession of the land has not been taken by the acquiring authority though the award is passed and if the compensation has not been paid to the landowners or has not been deposited before the appropriate forum, the proceedings initiated under the 1894 Act is deemed to have been lapsed.

12. Paragraph No.5 in the case of Pune Municipal Corporation and another v.

Harakchand Misirimal Solanki & others (supra), the Apex Court has observed that "the acquisition proceedings were also held bad in law for non-compliance with Section 7 and other statutory breaches. Inter alia, the High Court has quashed the acquisition proceedings and gave certain directions including restoration of possession." The Apex Court rejected the arguments of Pune Municipal Corporation and held that legal fiction under Section 24 (2) comes into operation as soon as conditions stated therein are satisfied. The applicability of Section 6 of the General Clauses Act being subject to Section 24 (2), there is no merit in the contention of the Corporation.

13. By ordinance No.9 of 2014 dated 31st December, 2014; No.4 of 2015 dated 3rd April, 2015; and No.5 of 2015 dated 30th May, 2015, the Act of 2013 has been amended. By the aforesaid ordinances, in the New (Principal) Act of 2013, in Section 24, in sub-section (2), after the proviso, the following proviso has been inserted, which reads, as under: -

9 WA No.330/2009
"Provided further that in computing the period referred to in this sub-section, any period or periods during which the proceedings for acquisition of land were held up on account of any stay or injunction issued by any Court or the period specified in the award of a Tribunal for taking possession or such period where possession has been taken but the compensation lying deposited in a court or in any account maintained for this purpose shall be excluded."

14. The Apex Court in the case of M/s. Radiance Fincap (P) Limited and others v. Union of India & others Civil Appeal No.4283/2011 after considering the amended ordinance 2014 has held, as under: -

"The right conferred to the landholders / owners of the acquired land under Section 24 (2) of the Act is the statutory right and, therefore, the said right cannot be taken away by an Ordinance by inserting proviso to the above-said sub-section without giving retrospective effect to the same. Taking into consideration the fact that we have allowed similar matters in C.A. No.4284/2011 titled M/s. Magnum Promoters P. Ltd. v. Union of India & others, vide order dated 27.11.2014 and in other appeals also by following the earlier decisions of this Court with regard to taking physical possession of the acquired land by the Land Acquisition Collector, which are extensively referred in the above judgment, promulgation of the Right to Fair Compensation and Transparency in Land Acquisition, Rehabilitation and Resettlement (Amendment) Ordinance, 2014 was made on 31.12.2014, which is prospective in nature and, therefore, it cannot be applied to their cases."
15. Learned Counsel for respondents No.1 to 6

submits that as per Section 26 of the New Act of 2013 read 10 WA No.330/2009 with Schedule-I, the amount of compensation of the land in question will be calculated, as under: -

"26. Determination of market value of land by Collector.
1. The Collector shall adopt the following criteria in assessing and determining the market value of the land, namely:--
a. the market value, if any, specified in the Indian Stamp Act, 1899 for the registration of sale deeds or agreements to sell, as the case may be, in the area, where the land is situated; or b. the average sale price for similar type of land situated in the nearest village or nearest vicinity area; or c. consented amount of compensation as agreed upon under sub-section (2) of section 2 in case of acquisition of lands for private companies or for public private partnership projects, whichever is higher:
Provided that the date for determination of market value shall be the date on which the notification has been issued under section 11. Explanation 1.--The average sale price referred to in clause (b) shall be determined taking into account the sale deeds or the agreements to sell registered for similar type of area in the near village or near vicinity area during immediately preceding three years of the year in which such acquisition of land is proposed to be made. Explanation 2.--For determining the average sale price referred to in Explanation 1, one-half of the total number of sale deeds or the agreements to sell in which the highest sale price has been mentioned shall be taken into account.
Explanation 3. --While determining the market value under this section and the average sale price referred to in Explanation 1 or 11 WA No.330/2009 Explanation 2, any price paid as compensation for land acquired under the provisions of this Act on an earlier occasion in the district shall not be taken into consideration.
Explanation 4.--While determining the market value under this section and the average sale price referred to in Explanation 1 or Explanation 2, any price paid, which in the opinion of the Collector is not indicative of actual prevailing market value may be discounted for the purposes of calculating market value.
2. The market value calculated as per sub-

section (1) shall be multiplied by a factor to be specified in the First Schedule.

3. Where the market value under sub-

section (1) or sub-section (2) cannot be determined for the reason that--

a. the land is situated in such area where the transactions in land are restricted by or under any other law for the time being in force in that area; or b. the registered sale deeds or agreements to sell as mentioned in clause

(a) of sub-section (1) for similar land are not available for the immediately preceding three years; or c. the market value has not been specified under the Indian Stamp Act, 1899 by the appropriate authority, the State Government concerned shall specify the floor price or minimum price per unit area of the said land based on the price calculated in the manner specified in sub- section (1) in respect of similar types of land situated in the immediate adjoining areas:

Provided that in a case where the Requiring Body offers its shares to the owners of the lands (whose lands have been acquired) as a part compensation, for acquisition of land, such shares in no case shall exceed twenty-five per cent. of the value so calculated under sub-

section (1) or sub-section (2) or sub-section (3) as the case may be:
Provided further that the Requiring Body shall in no case compel any owner of the land (whose land has been acquired) to take its shares, the value of which is deductible in the value of the land calculated under sub-section (1): Provided also that the Collector shall, before initiation of any land acquisition proceedings in any area, take all necessary steps to revise and update the market value of the land on the basis of the prevalent market rate in that area: Provided also that the appropriate Government shall ensure that the market value determined for acquisition of any land or property of an educational institution established and administered by a religious or linguistic minority shall be such as would not restrict or abrogate the right to establish and administer educational institutions of their choice."
16. He has also placed on record the Collector Guidelines (Annexure MA-2) issued as per provisions of the Indian Stamp Act and submitted that scheme has been brought by the appellants for providing plots under JNNURM and residential scheme whereby Housing Board intended to provide plots to the economically weaker sections of the society. The MP Housing Board has requested for acquisition of the land without preparing scheme and without budgetary allocation and after coming into force of the New Act of 2013, it is practically impossible for the Housing Board to pay the amount of compensation to acquired said land. He submits that even otherwise, JNNURM has been closed by the Union of India and now no such department exists; consequently, 13 WA No.330/2009 the alleged public purpose and land acquisition have also come to an end. Thus, the appellants cannot sit tight over the matter and they may be directed to pay compensation as per the provisions of the New Act of 2013 for the land in question and necessary directions be issued to the appellants for payment of compensation accordingly.
17. On 11.12.2014, a short reply has been filed by the appellants, stating therein that they have forwarded the same to their Head Office at Bhopal. Since it being a policy matter of the Madhya Pradesh Housing Board, therefore, it is required to be placed in the meeting of the Board, which is likely to be held in the month of January, 2015 and this issue shall also be kept in the Agenda of the meeting; and prayed for further time to file reply.
18. Considering the aforesaid, this Court allowed the prayer for grant of time by order dated 10.12.2014 and directed for listing the matter in the second week of January, 2015. On 16.01.2015, vide IA No.229/2015, a prayer for grant of further time to file reply to application (IA No.5807/2014) was allowed.
19. On 28.01.2015, reply to IA No.5807/2014 has been filed by the appellants, which is supported by the affidavit of Shri Praveen Kumar Majumdar, Officer-in-

Charge of the case, in which the appellants very categorically admitted that the Board shall make compensation as would be decided by the Land Acquisition Officer, be it to be under the Land Acquisition Act, 1894, or under the New Act of 2013. Paragraphs No.2 14 WA No.330/2009 to 4 of the reply dated 28.01.2015 reads, as under: -

"2. That, during the pendency of the appeal, the Respondent No.1 on 31.10.2014 moved an application for appropriate directions, wherein it has been mainly prayed that the directions be issued to the Housing Board to inform as to whether they are ready and willing to pay compensation as per provision of Act of 2013 for acquisition of land in question.
3. That, the matter was placed in the Board's meeting held on 16.01.2015, wherein it has been decided that acquisition of land in question would be in the interest of public at large and the Board has capacity and all the arrangements to meet the financial requirements for the acquisition of land in question. It would not be out of place to mention here that the Board shall make the payment of compensation as would be decided by the Land Acquisition Officer be it be under the Land Acquisition Act, 1894 or under the Right to Fair Compensation and Transparency in Land Acquisition, Rehabilitation and Resettlement Act, 2013.
4. That, this writ appeal and other connected appeals are the matters of 2009 and pending for final disposal. The appellants are ready and willing to argue these appeals finally at the earliest."

20. On 02.02.2015, IA No.561/2015 has been filed by the appellants for withdrawal of the reply to IA No.5807/2014 filed on 28.01.2015 and for taking the corrected new reply on record. Along with this application, they have also filed another reply dated 02.02.2015 to IA No.5807/2014. Relevant paragraph No.3 of reply dated 02.02.2015 reads as under: -

"3. That, the matter was placed in the Board's meeting held on 16.01.2015, wherein it has been decided that acquisition of land in question 15 WA No.330/2009 would be in the interest of public at large and the Board will make all the arrangements to meet the financial requirements for the acquisition of land in question. It would not be out of place to mention here that the proceeding for acquisition of land was started way back in the year 2008, when the Land Acquisition Act, 1894 was very much in force. But on account of litigation, the process of acquisition could not be completed. In these circumstances, it is requested that the payment of compensation of land in question be ordered as per the provisions of Land Acquisition Act, 1894."

21. The sole contention of the learned counsel for respondents No.1 to 6 is that compensation as to the said acquisition was neither paid to them nor deposited before appropriate forum.

22. In reply, learned Senior Counsel for the appellants has drawn our attention to the decisions of the Apex Court in the case of Basavan Jaggu Dhobi v. Sukhnandan Ramdas Chaudhary (dead) through L.Rs. & others reported in 1995 Supp. (3) SCC 179 and Gautam Sarup v. Lela Jetly and others reported in (2008) 7 SCC 85 and submits that the law is well settled on the point that admission made in earlier reply dated 28.01.2015 can be withdrawn by taking a contrary stand in the subsequent reply.

23. On the other hand, learned counsel for respondents No.1 to 6 submits that the appellants cannot be approbate and reprobate, and cannot resile from admission made in the earlier reply. The said practice has been deprecated by the Apex Court in the case of S. Malla 16 WA No.330/2009 Reddy v. Future Builders Cooperative Housing Society & others reported in (2013) 9 SCC 349.

24. Though both replies have been filed by the same Officer-in-Charge of the case, but even if we permit the appellants to withdraw certain amendments made in the reply to the application filed on 28.01.2015, that will not make any difference, because in the present case, the land of respondents No.1 to 6 has been acquired by notification dated 4th April, 2008 but till today, no compensation has been paid and with effect from 27th September, 2013 the New Act of 2013 came into force and thus, respondents No.1 to 6 are entitled for compensation as per the provisions of the New Act of 2013.

25. In the instant case, admittedly, compensation was not paid to the landowners i.e. Respondents No.1 to 6 and no award under the Act of 1894 was made, when the New Act of 2013 came into force with effect from 27th September, 2013, and thus, there is no force in the contention of the learned Senior Counsel for the appellants that respondents No.1 to 6 are entitled for compensation under the old Act i.e. The Act of 1894.

26. On due consideration of the aforesaid, we are of the view that respondents No.1 to 6 - landowners are entitled for compensation for the acquisition of land in question under the provisions of the New Act of 2013.

27. For the foregoing reasons, IA No.5807/2014 filed by respondents No.1 to 6 and IA No.561/2015 filed by the appellants on 02.02.2015 are allowed and 17 WA No.330/2009 consequently, necessary exercise for awarding the compensation to respondents No.1 to 6 under the New Act of 2013 be completed within a period of three months from today.
