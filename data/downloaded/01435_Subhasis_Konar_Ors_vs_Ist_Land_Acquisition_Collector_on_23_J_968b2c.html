Calcutta High Court (Appellete Side)

Subhasis Konar & Ors vs Ist Land Acquisition Collector on 23 July, 2009

Author: Jayanta Kumar Biswas

Bench: Jayanta Kumar Biswas

                                   In the High Court at Calcutta
                                  Constitutional Writ Jurisdiction
                                           Appellate Side
Present:
The Hon'ble Mr. Justice Jayanta Kumar Biswas.

                                   W.P. No. 2272 (W) of 2007
                                     Subhasis Konar & Ors.
                                               v.
                           Ist Land Acquisition Collector, Calcutta & Ors.
                                              with
                                     CAN No. 2804 of 2009

Mr Ramapati Roy and Ms Miru Hazra, advocates, for the petitioners. Mr A.N. Banerjee, Ms Husn
Ara Begum, Mr K.N. Nabi and Mr Sk. Golam Gous, advocates, for the state. Mr Debabrata
Banerjee and Mr Koushik Bhattacharyya, advocates, for the housing board.

Heard on: July 22 & 23, 2009.

Judgment on: July 23, 2009.

       The Court: - The seven petitioners in this writ petition dated January (sic) 5, 2007 are
seeking the following final reliefs:
        "(a) A Writ of and/or in the nature of Mandamus commanding the State Respondents, their

men, agents, and subordinates as to why the possession of the disputed lands being Plot Nos. 4266 of Mouza Kasba, P.S. Jadavpur, now Kasba, District South 24 Parganas, purchased by your petitioners should not be restored to the petitioners in its original position and if require by demolishing all structure and building constructed thereon.

(b) A Writ of and/or in the nature of Mandamus, commanding the Respondents, their men, agents and subordinates why the alleged acts and actions of the Respondents should not be declared bad, illegal and why the possession of the lands should be restored to its original position in view of the judgment and order passed by Hon'ble Mr. Justice N.K. Mitra and affirmed by the Appeal Court presided over by Hon'ble Mr. Justice Altamas Kabir and Hon'ble Justice Ranjan Kumar Majumdar in M.A.T. No. 2964 of 1997;

(c) A declaration that the Judgment and order of the Hon'ble Supreme Court of India dated 18th. September, 2001 passed in Special Leave (Civil) No.16525 of 2001 is not binding upon the petitioners as the said judgement and orders was passed behind the back of the petitioners without serving any notice and without giving any opportunity of being heard.

(d) A Writ of and/or in the nature of - Certiorari, calling upon the Respondents their men, and subordinates to transmit and certify the records of the proceedings to this Hon'ble Court so that the conscionable justice may be done."

By an order dated March 11, 1993 made by the First Land Acquisition Collector, Calcutta under s.3(1) of the West Bengal Land (Requisition and Acquisition) Act, 1948 approximately 18 acres of land, particulars whereof were given in the schedule to the order, were requisitioned "for the purpose of creation of better living condition in Urbab/Rural area by the construction of accommodation of dwelling places in such area & for the purpose connected therewith incidental thereto namely for the purpose of construction of Housing Complex." Out of the total 18 acres (approximately), the petitioners were interested only in 0.36 acre (approximately). On March 14, 1993 the collector gave possession of the entire requisitioned area to the Housing Department of the State Government. On March 31, 1997 the West Bengal Land (Requisition and Acquisition) Act, 1948 expired. Under the circumstances, by the Land Acquisition (West Bengal Amendment) Act, 1997, the Land Acquisition Act, 1894 was amended for concluding the requisition and acquisition proceedings initiated under the West Bengal Land (Requisition and Acquisition) Act, 1948. The amendment published in the official gazette on May 2, 1997 was given retrospective effect from April 1, 1997.

The petitioners claim that they made a representation to the authority concerned for return of the lands. Then questioning the requisition order they moved this court by filing a writ petition (counsel are unable to give the number of the case) that was disposed of by an order dated June 10, 1997. The order is set out below:-

"It appears that the disputed lands were requisitioned under the West Bengal Act II of 1948 namely the West Bengal Land (Requisition and Acquisition) Act 1948, in 1993, vide the order dated 11th March, 1993 being annexure 'G' to the writ petition, by the Requisitioning Authority. Since that Act is no longer alive and has lost its force, the respondents are directed to give back possession of the disputed requisitioned property to the petitioners positively within three months from the date of communication of the order and the time limit so fixed above is peremptory and mandatory.
With these observations, the writ petition is disposed of without any order as to costs. Let plain copy of the order countersigned by the Asstt. Registrar (Court) be given to the learned advocates for both the sides."

Feeling aggrieved by the single bench order dated June 10, 1997 the West Bengal Housing Board lodged an appeal (MAT No.2964 of 1997) with the division bench of this court. It has been submitted that at the appellate stage the petitioners amended the writ petition in which the single bench made the order June 10, 1997; and that by amendment they challenged the vires of the Land Acquisition (West Bengal Amendment) Act, 1997. In view of the state amendment of the Land Acquisition Act, 1894 inserting sub-ss.(3A) and (3B) into s.9 thereof, the land acquisition collector issued the requisite s.9(3A) notice dated July 16, 1999 to the petitioners.

The provisions of sub-s.(3A) read as follows:-

"(3A) The Collector shall also serve notice to the same effect on all such persons known or believed to be interested in any land, or to be entitled to act for persons so interested, the possession whereof has already been taken on requisition under section 3 of the West Bengal Land (Requisition and Acquisition) Act, 1948 (hereinafter referred to in this section as the said Act), as re-enacted by the West Bengal Land (Requisition and Acquisition) Re-enacting Act, 1977, and, in every such case, the provisions of sub-section (1) of section 4, section 5, section 5A, section 6, section 7, and section 8 of this Act shall be deemed to have been complied with:
Provided that the date of notice under this sub-section shall be the date of reference for the purpose of determining the value of such land under this Act:
Provided further that when the Collector has made an award under section 11 in respect of any such land, such land shall, upon such award, vest absolutely in the Government, free from all encumbrances."

By a judgment and order dated January 2, 2001 the appeal, MAT No. 2964 of 1997, was dismissed. Their Lordships were pleased to hold that the requisition proceedings initiated under the West Bengal Land (Requisition and Acquisition) Act, 1948 "had lapsed along with Act.II of 1948 after 31st March 1997." Feeling aggrieved, the board lodged an appeal (Civil Appeal No. 6550 of 2001) with the Supreme Court. Feeling aggrieved by the same decision of the division bench dated January 2, 2001 quite a substantial number of appeals were lodged with the Supreme Court. All the appeals were taken up for hearing by their Lordships together, and they all were disposed by an order dated September 18, 2001.

In the order dated September 18, 2001 their Lordships recorded as follows:-

"At the time of hearing of this matter learned counsel for the respondents (original petitioners) sought leave to withdraw their challenge to the vires of the Act. They have submitted that the respondents would be satisfied if reasonable compensation as provided under the Act is paid within the time frame as agreed. Learned counsel appearing on behalf of the State, after obtaining instructions from the concerned officers, submitted that reasonable compensation under the provisions of the Act would be determined and paid.
Admittedly in the present case, possession of the land was taken by the authority on 14th March, 1993 under the West Bengal Land (Requisition & Acquisition) Act, 1948 (Act II of 1948) (for short "the Requisition Act") and thereafter the Collector has issued notices under Sections 9(3)(a) of the Land Acquisition (West Bengal Amendment) Act, 1997. Keeping these two dates in mind, the compensation is required to be determined."

After recording the above-noted submissions of counsel for the parties and noticing the provisions of the statutes, their Lordships ordered as follows: -

"In view of the above, we direct that (a) The Collector shall determine the market value of the land as on 14.3.1993 and shall also calculate interest @ 12% thereon from 14.3.1993 to 15.7.1999. (b) The Collector shall make an award comprising the following: (i) The market value of the land as on 16.7.1999 i.e. the date of notice as stated above. (ii) Interest @ 12% p.a. on such market value from 16.7.1999 till the date of the award as provided in section 23(1A) of the Act, as it stands amended in its application to the State of West Bengal. (iii) In addition to the market value of the land as determined, additional amount of 30% on such market value in consideration of the compulsory nature of amount shall be awarded as provided under Section 23(2) of the Land Acquisition Act. (c) The Collector shall determine the above within a period of two months from today and shall pay the said amount to the claimants within a period of two months thereafter. (d) Learned counsel appearing for the State of West Bengal, after obtaining instructions, states that the time prescribed above would be strictly adhered to by the concerned officers and non-compliance to the same would entail appropriate action by the Court.
In view of the aforesaid directions, the impugned order passed by the High Court would not survive. The appeals are disposed of accordingly. There shall be no order as to costs."

Thereupon the collector sent a requisition dated September 24, 2001 to the petitioners under s.10 of the Land Acquisition Act, 1894 requiring them to make or deliver to him at his office at 2, Church Lane, Calcutta on October 12, 2001 at 12 noon "a statement containing, so far as may be practicable, the name of every person possessing any interest in the land or any part thereof, referred to in the notice" under s.9 served on them. Counsel are unable to tell me what steps the petitioners took in compliance with the s.10 requisition served on them.

Suddenly in 2004 they moved this court by filing W.P. No.9144 (W) of 2004 seeking an order restraining the housing board from entering the lands. At the time of admission hearing of the writ petition counsel for the housing board produced the order of the Supreme Court dated September 18, 2001. Faced with the situation, counsel for the petitioners stated his total ignorance of the order of the Supreme Court. Finally by an order dated July 16, 2004 the writ petition was disposed of saying:-

"Be that as it may when the matter has been dealt with by the supreme court by its aforementioned order, I am incompetent to pass any order contrary to what has been directed by the supreme court. In the event the aforementioned order has been obtained by the W.B. Housing Board behind the back of the petitioners herein, it shall be open to the petitioners, if they are so advised, to approach the supreme court for proper redressal. With the above directions, this writ petition stands disposed of."

Under the circumstances, the petitioners filed an application, IA No.2 of 2005, before the Supreme Court in connection with Civil Appeal No. 6550 of 2001. By an order dated May 3, 2005 their Lordships of the Supreme Court were pleased to dismiss the application.

Thereupon all of a sudden they moved this court by filing W.P. No. 19319 (W) of 2006. They questioned the s.10 requisition dated September 24, 2001 and contended that since the order of the Supreme Court dated May 3, 2005 was passed "dismissing the review application without going any reason whatsoever", in terms of the single bench order dated June 10, 1997 and the division bench judgment and order dated January 2, 2001 the respondents were under an obligation to return the lands. By an order dated September 4, 2006 I dismissed the writ petition. The order is set out below: -

"Since no cause of action has been stated anywhere in the writ petition for taking it out, counsel for the petitioners prays for leave to withdraw the writ petition with liberty to file an appropriate writ petition. Counsel for the respondents leaves the matter to the court.
In my view leave as prayed for should be granted. Hence I dismiss this writ petition without any order for costs in it, on the ground that no statement has been made regarding the cause of action. It is made clear that this order shall not prevent the petitioners from taking out appropriate writ petition for the relief for which this writ petition was taken out."

The petitioners claim that after dismissal of W.P. No. 19319 (W) of 2006 they made a representation dated November 23, 2006 calling upon the respondents to return the lands; and that since no was step taken for returning the lands in terms of the single bench order dated June 10, 1997 and the division bench judgment and order dated January 2, 2001, they were compelled to take out this writ petition.

Mr Roy, counsel for the petitioners, has argued as follows. The order of the Supreme Court dated September 18, 2001, made behind the back of the petitioners who were not served with notice of the appeal and who never engaged the counsel who appeared for the other respondents in the other appeals and withdrew the challenge to the vires of the Land Acquisition (West Bengal Amendment) Act, 1997, is not binding on them. The review application filed by the petitioners was dismissed by the Supreme Court by circulation and without giving any opportunity of hearing to the petitioners. As will appear from the order dated May 3, 2005 the review application was dismissed without giving any reasons. Under the circumstances, the petitioners can file a writ petition under art.226 of the Constitution of India seeking a declaration that the order of the Supreme Court dated September 18, 2001 is not binding on them. Since the order of the Supreme Court is not binding on the petitioners, they have rightly called upon the respondents to return the lands in terms of the single bench order dated June 10, 1997 and the division bench judgment and order dated January 2, 2001 whereby both the petitioners and the respondents are bound. The housing board has not filed any opposition stating that notice of its appeal lodged with the Supreme Court was ever served on the petitioners.

Mr A. Banerjee, counsel for the state, has raised the question of maintainability of the writ petition itself. His submission is that this court has no jurisdiction and power to make an order declaring that the order of the Supreme Court dated September 18, 2001 is not binding on the petitioners. Mr D. Banerjee, counsel for the West Bengal Housing Board, has submitted that from the facts stated in the writ petition and the documents produced with it, it is evident that the petitioners were fully aware of the Supreme Court order, because even after receipt of the s.10 requisition dated September 24, 2001 they never questioned its validity, and on the contrary, waited till 2004 when they suddenly filed a writ petition in this court. According to him, if the petitioners had no knowledge of the order of the Supreme Court dated September 18, 2001, then their ordinary reaction on receipt of the s.10 requisition dated September 24, 2001 would have been to challenge it at once citing the division bench judgment and order dated January 2, 2001 under which they were entitled to return of the lands, especially when the collector issuing the s.10 requisition, instead of taking steps for return of the lands, called upon them to submit the requisite statement for proceeding further with the proceedings for the acquisition of the lands according to provisions of the Land Acquisition Act, 1894 as amended by the Land Acquisition (West Bengal Amendment) Act, 1997. He has said that the writ petition filed in 2006 was dismissed for absence of cause of action, and that in the representation made thereafter the petitioners conveniently suppressed the facts concerning the order of the Supreme Court dated September 18, 2001 and dismissal of their application by order dated May 3, 2005. He has prayed for dismissal of the writ petition with exemplary costs.

The questions for decision are: (i) whether seeking a declaration that the order of the Supreme Court dated September 18, 2001 is not binding on them on the ground that it was made behind their back, the petitioners can file this writ petition under art.226 of the Constitution of India; and (ii) assuming that they can file the writ petition under art.226 seeking a declaration that the order of the Supreme Court dated September 18, 2001 is not binding on them, whether, on the facts, it can be said that the order is not binding on them.

In my opinion, the writ petition is totally misconceived, and by filing it the petitioners have grossly abused the process of court. A writ petition under art.226 of the Constitution of India cannot be filed seeking a declaration that an order of the Supreme Court in a case the petitioners in the writ petition were parties is not binding on them. There can be absolutely no doubt that a party to a case in which the Supreme Court makes an order is bound by the order. The party cannot contend that since the order was made without serving notice on him, it is not binding on him. A party to a case remains bound by a decision in the case so long as the decision remains in force, even if the decision is given without serving a notice on the party. In such a case, his remedy is to apply for rehearing of the case after setting aside of the order.

The principle laid down by the Supreme Court in Shivdeo Singh & Ors. v. State of Punjab & Ors., AIR 1963 SC 1909 cannot be applied to a case where a writ petition under art.226 is filed seeking a declaration that an order of the Supreme Court in a case between the parties is not binding on the ground that the petitioner was not served with the notice of the case. The principle applies only when the high court makes an order in a case under art.226. In such a case the affected party has the choice to file either an application in the case seeking an order setting aside the order in exercise of inherent power of the writ court and directing rehearing of the case in his presence, or a writ petition under art.226 seeking the same relief. Hence the present writ petition is just not maintainable.

Assuming that I am wrong in my opinion on the question of maintainability of the writ petition, now I propose to examine the question whether, on the facts, it can be said that the petitioners are not bound by the order of the Supreme Court dated September 18, 2001. The admitted position is that the order was made by the Supreme Court in Civil Appeal No.6550 of 2001 lodged by the housing board that lost its appeal, MAT No.2964 of 1997, lodged with the division bench of this court from the single bench order dated June 10, 1997 made in the petitioners' writ petition to which the housing board was a party; and that the petitioners were respondents in Civil Appeal No.6550 of 2001. Hence there can be no doubt that they are bound by the order of the Supreme Court dated September 18, 2001.

Whether notice of the appeal was served on them is a question that cannot be raised by the petitioners before any court except the Supreme Court. I am unable to accept their counsel's argument that the advocate-on-record who represented the respondents in the civil appeal was never engaged by them. It is of no consequence that the advocate-on-record has been made a respondent in this writ petition, and that he has chosen not to enter appearance in the case. It is to be noted that they moved the Supreme Court by filing I.A. No.2 of 2005 that was dismissed by their Lordships by an order dated May 3, 2005. The argument that the application was dismissed by an unreasoned order just cannot be made before this court. On these facts, I hold that so long as the order dated September 18, 2001 remains in force, the petitioners shall remain bound by it.

For these reasons, I hold that the writ petition is liable to be dismissed with exemplary costs. Accordingly, I dismiss it ordering the petitioners to pay the State of West Bengal and the West Bengal Housing Board at the rate of Rs.20,000 costs within four weeks. CAN No. 2804 of 2009, not appearing, is treated as on the day's list by consent of counsel for the parties, and it shall be deemed to be disposed of.

Urgent certified xerox of this order, if applied for, shall be supplied to the parties within three days from the date of receipt of the file by the section concerned. 

S.r.                                                          (Jayanta Kumar Biswas, J.)
