JUDGMENT:

The present appeal has been filed on behalf of the appellants against the impugned judgment/decree dated 09.11.2009 passed by the Ld. Trial Court in Suit No. 1062/02 titled as 'Sh. Gauri Shankar ( since deceased) Through LRs Vs. Sh.Har Narain ( since deceased) Through LRs & Ors.

It is pertinent to mention here that earlier the present appeal was RCA No. 10/14/10 4/41 5 dismissed vide order dated 05.7.2010 passed by one of the Ld. Predecessors of this court.

Appellants herein preferred an appeal against the above said order dated 05.7.2010 and vide order dated 03.4.2014 passed in RSA No. 206/2010, the Hon'ble High Court of Delhi set aside the above said impugned order and remanded back this matter .

The operative portion of the above said order dated 03.4.2014 passed by the Hon'ble High Court of Delhi reads as under:­ " After arguments, it is agreed that the appeal be and is allowed and disposed of by setting aside the impugned order of the first appellate Court dated 5.7.2010, and the respondents who were defendants in the trial Court but were not added as respondents in the first appeal, are added only as proforma respondents ( or if they are dead then their legal heirs) in the appeal noting that their service will not be required.

As per the request made on behalf of Mr. Deepak Gupta/ respondent No.1(a), who is arguing in person in this Court, the first appellate Court is requested to dispose of the appeal within a period of four months of receipt of the copy of the present order. It is also ordered that the appellant/plaintiff under no circumstances and for no reasons whatsoever including personal reasons, will seek any adjournment in the first appeal and if it is sought, the appellate Court can impose very heavy costs for an exceptional requirement of adjournment.

Parties to appear before the District & Sessions Judge ( West) Tis Hazari Court, Delhi on 29th April, 2014, and the District & Sessions Judge will mark the first appeal for disposal to a competent Court in accordance with law RCA No. 10/14/10 5/41 6 noting that the appeal has to be disposed of within four months of receipt of copy of the present order."

Thereafter, the present case/appeal was assigned to this court vide order dated 29.4.2014 passed by the Ld. District & Sessions Judge (West) Tis Hazari Court, Delhi in TP No. 37/14.

2. Brief facts necessary for the disposal of the present appeal are that a suit for recovery of possession was filed on behalf of the plaintiff Sh. Gauri Shankar Gupta ( since deceased ) in the year 1970 against the defendant Sh. Har Narain (since deceased) & Anr. before the Ld. Trial Court. It is pertinent to mention here that the said plaintiff Sh. Gauri Shankar has expired and he is being represented by his legal representatives namely ­Sh. Deepak Gupta, Ms. Madhu Aggarwal, Ms.Shobhana Gupta and Ms. Mridula Gupta (respondent nos. 1(a) to (d) herein). Further, defendant no.1 Sh. Har Narain and defendant no.2 Sh. Nathu Ram Mehra have also expired and they were being represented by their respective legal representatives before the Ld. Trial Court.

In the aforesaid plaint, it has been stated that the plaintiff was the sole owner of the land bearing Khasra No. 466 and 805/407 vide jamabandi for the year 1960­61 in the revenue estate of Patti Jahanuma, Delhi and this land measuring 7.5 bighas is a part of the property earlier known as 'Bagh Barafkhana' in the locality Nai Basti in Kishan Ganj, Delhi. It is further stated that property in Bagh Barafkhana was one of the properties owned by the plaintiff's father namely Lala Manohar Lal, who died in the year 1946 and plaintiff's Chacha namely Lala Krishan RCA No. 10/14/10 6/41 7 Lal, who expired in the year 1968. It is stated that in July, 1947 the said Lala Krishan Lal filed a partition suit bearing no. 74 of 1947 in the court of Civil Judge, Agra against the two sons of late Lala Manohar Lal i.e. plaintiff ­Sh. Gauri Shankar and his younger brother Sh. Hari Shankar and the widow of Lala Monohar Lal, who also died in the year 1955. It is also stated that Bagh Barafkhana was one of the properties forming the subject matter of the said suit for partition and the main dispute in the said partition suit was the extent of share of Lala Krishan Lal in Bagh Barafkhana. It is further stated that by virtue of compromise dated 28th March,1958 entered into by the parties to the said partition suit, each one of them was interalia allotted a distinct portion out of the property Bagh Barafkhana and the said partition suit was decided in terms of this compromise vide judgment dated 26.05.1958, whereby Lala Krishan Lal was ordered to take steps for preparation of the final decree. It is stated that a formal partition decree regarding the above said partition was prepared on 23rd September, 1961 in the Suit No. 74 of 1947 and on 9th March, 1967 in RFA No. 93­D/1960 in the Hon'ble High Court of Delhi. It is stated that during the pendency of the partition Suit No. 74 of 1947, Lala Krishan Lal without any authority or consent of the plaintiff or his younger brother started granting improvident leases of the vacant land in Bagh Barafkhana to his employees etc and the plaintiff sought appointment of a Receiver and Hon'ble High Court of Allahabad vide order dated 05.05.1955, appointed Lala Krishan Lal as Receiver, who had to deposit all the income derived from the said property (Bagh Barafkhana) and was also to submit half yearly accounts in the court. It is stated that the said suit was compromised vide compromise deed dated 28.03.1958 and RCA No. 10/14/10 7/41 8 accordingly, the Hon'ble High Court of Allahabad terminated the said receivership vide order dated 02.05.1958. It is further stated that while assuming exclusive possession of his portion in Bagh Barafkhana after the above said decision on 26.05.1958, the plaintiff found that sometime earlier Lala Krishan Lal had allowed the defendant no.1( who was working as his Rent Collector) and defendant no.2 (who was also his employee) to occupy the land in this suit and on the said land, the defendants have surreptitiously erected super structure bearing the municipal no. 535, Nai Basti, Delhi and the assessed annual rateable value of the land in suit and the super structure thereupon was Rs. 1,400/­ in 1966­67. It is stated that since improvident lease was neither sanctioned by the court administrating the receiver nor consented to by the plaintiff or his younger brother, the plaintiff served the notice that he did not recognize the alleged tenancy and demanded the possession and the defendants claimed tenancy by a written deed under Kishan Lal and disputed the plaintiff's right to possession. It is further stated that during scrutiny of receiver accounts filed in Agra Court in October, 1959 it was confirmed that defendants had been let in when the property was custodia legis and the plaintiff therefore by his application dated 15.2.1960 requested the court to summarily dispossess the defendants and two others, who had been similarly let in. It is stated that while considering the said application , Civil Judge, Agra vide his order dated 23.4.1960 recorded an admission by Lala Krishan Lal to the effect that the defendants had been let in by him during Receivership and accordingly the court ordered issuance of notice to the defendants & others and in pursuance thereof, the defendants appeared and resisted the said application for summary dispossession RCA No. 10/14/10 8/41 9 on the ground of jurisdiction and above said application was rejected by the said court on 01.5.1969.

It has been stated that that a formal warrant of possession was also granted by the Civil Judge of Agra on 30.11.1968 in favour of the original plaintiff and his brother ( decree holders) against the legal representatives of Lala Krishan Lal and during the execution thereof, the delivery of actual possession of the land in suit was resisted by and on behalf of the defendant and even after these proceedings, defendant did not attorn to the plaintiffs and thus electing to the abandon the claim if any as tenants. Thereafter, a notice dated 20.04.1970 was got served upon the defendants by the plaintiff, however no reply to the said notice was received which resulted in filing of the present suit before the Ld. Trial court, wherein it has been prayed that a decree of possession of the above said suit property may be passed in favour of the plaintiff and against the defendants. It was further prayed that decree for recovery of Rs.500/­ with future interest @ 6% per annum till realization may be passed in favour of the plaintiff and against the defendants and it has also been prayed that a decree of mesne profit @ Rs. 50/­ per month may be passed against the defendants from the date of suit till the delivery of the possession of land in suit.

3. The defendants contested the above said suit and filed the written statement, wherein it was stated that vide order passed in Appeal No. 149/74, the Hon'ble High Court of Allahabad held that the defendants were tenants of the plaintiff in the land under the suit property and were not liable to be dispossessed RCA No. 10/14/10 9/41 10 by the plaintiff and the said order/judgment operates as res­judicata between the parties. It is further stated that plaintiff was not the owner of the suit land or that the said property Bagh Barafkhana was owned by Sh. Manohar Lal, father and Sh. Krishan Lal, uncle of the plaintiff. It is stated that defendants have been and are tenants in portion of the property situated at Bagh Barafkhana , Kishan Ganj, Delhi under Sh. Krishan Lal. It was denied that after the alleged partition was mutated in the revenue records vide mutation no. 1549­1550 dated 25.11.1959 or that the plaintiff was given exclusive share in khasra no. 466 and 805/467. It is stated that the defendants constructed the building on the suit land within the knowledge of Sh. Krishan Lal & the plaintiff and defendants have been paying rent to Sh. Krishan Lal . It is further stated that Lala Krishan Lal was managing the properties and was entitled to give property on lease and the said lease was never objected by the plaintiff or his younger brother.

In the additional pleas in the W.S, it has been stated that the suit was barred by section­47 of the CPC. It was further stated that if it was held that defendant was not a tenant in the suit property or that Sh. Krishan Lal was not authorized to let the property in suit to the defendants, then the defendants and their predecessors have been using the property as owner and their possession is adverse for a period of more than 12 years. It is also stated that the suit of the plaintiff was time barred . All other averments made in the plaint were also denied on behalf of the defendants and it was prayed that the aforesaid suit filed on behalf of the plaintiff may be dismissed with cost.

Thereafter, the replication was filed on behalf of the plaintiff, wherein RCA No. 10/14/10 10/41 11 the averments made in the written statement were denied as incorrect and those made in the plaint were reiterated as correct.

4. On the basis of the pleadings, issues were framed and the trial in the suit was conducted by the Ld. Trial Court and vide impugned judgment/ decree dated 9.11.2009, the aforesaid suit was decreed for possession in favour of the plaintiff and against the defendant on payment of Rs. 4 lacs as compensation within four months from the date of decree, in respect of construction raised, to the LRs of defendants. The said suit was also decreed for mesne profit and damages in favour of the plaintiff and against the defendant @ Rs. 600/­ per annum (Rs. 50/­p.m.) w.e.f 1.5.1970 to 31.12.1979, mesne profit and damages @ Rs. 1600/­ per annum w.e.f 01.1.1980 till 31.12.1989, further @ Rs. 4000/­per annum w.e.f 1.1.1990 to 31.12.1999 and Rs.10,000/­per annum w.e.f 1.1.2000 till the date of passing of the decree and handing over the possession or two years from the date of decree whichever is earlier. In case of non handing over of the possession within two years of passing of the decree, the plaintiff shall be entitled to file fresh the suit for claiming the mesne profit and damages at the higher rate, if deemed proper by the plaintiff. Costs of the suit has also been awarded to the plaintiff and it has been held that the defendants shall not be liable to pay any mesne profit & damages in case they hand over the possession within four months from the date of decree.

5. Aggrieved by the aforesaid impugned judgment/decree dated 09.11.2009, the appellants have filed the present appeal, wherein it has been prayed RCA No. 10/14/10 11/41 12 that the aforesaid impugned judgment/decree dated 09.11.2009 passed by the Ld.Trial Court may be set aside.

6. It is pertinent to note here that earlier the present appeal was dismissed vide order dated 05.7.2010 passed by one of the Ld. Predecessors of this court.

Appellants herein preferred an appeal against the above said order dated 05.7.2010 and vide order dated 03.4.2014 passed in RSA No. 206/2010, the Hon'ble High Court of Delhi set aside the above said impugned order and remanded back this matter .

On receipt of the present appeal, Trial Court Record ( TCR) was also summoned and the same has also been received.

7. I have heard the arguments at length put forward on behalf of both the parties and have carefully gone through the record of the case. 8 . It has been submitted on behalf of the appellants that the impugned judgment/decree dated 09.11.2009 passed by the Ld. Trial Court was contrary to the facts of the case and material on record. It is further submitted that the suit filed by the plaintiff/respondent before the Ld. Trial Court was not properly valued. It is submitted that in the plaint, the plaintiff has valued the suit for recovery of possession at Rs.205/­ i.e. ten times of land revenue which has been wrongly assessed. It is further submitted that the plaintiff has mentioned the value of the suit RCA No. 10/14/10 12/41 13 premises at Rs.1,400/­ in the year 1966­67 and the suit was filed in the year 1970 and value would have been much more at that time. It is submitted that Ld. Trial Court has directed the plaintiff/respondent to make up the deficiency in the court fee and thereafter the plaintiff/respondent filed the court fee which was again deficient. It is also submitted that since the requisite court fee was not filed on behalf of the plaintiff/respondent, the suit of the plaintiff before the Ld. Trial Court was liable to be dismissed. It is submitted that the Ld. Trial Court had failed to appreciate that suit property was HUF in nature and only Karta of HUF can sue and be sued and the present suit before the Ld. Trial Court by Sh. Gauri Shankar was without any right or authority and as such the said suit was not maintainable.

It has been further submitted that the Ld. Trial Court had failed to appreciate that the suit filed on behalf of the plaintiff was hit by Article 66 of the Limitation Act as the said suit was filed by the plaintiff after the expiry of twelve years as according to records, the defendant was in possession prior to 01.01.1958 in his capacity as tenant and the arrears of rent up to 01.01.1958 were to be collected by late Lala Kishan Lal only from him. It is submitted that the Ld. Trial Court has failed to appreciate that the status of the appellants was of a tenant and not of unauthorized occupant and appellants have been in possession of the property prior to the year 1958 and the plaintiff admitted the same in the notice of 1970.

It has been submitted on behalf of the appellants that the Ld. Trial Court failed to consider the fact that the suit of the plaintiff/respondent was barred by Section ­50 of the Delhi Rent Control Act as the relationship between the plaintiff and the defendant was of landlord & tenant and the monthly rent was below RCA No. 10/14/10 13/41 14 Rs.3,500/­. It is further submitted that contention of the plaintiff/respondent that the property let out was an open land and therefore Delhi Rent Control Act and Slum Act did not apply was not tenable as the construction/superstructure on the land was raised with the consent and within the knowledge of earlier landlord Lala Kishan Lal, who had let out the premises to the appellants herein. It is submitted that from the correspondence between the parties, it is evident that the construction on the suit land was within the knowledge of Lala Kishan Lal & the plaintiff/ respondent herein and they never objected to the said construction and as such by way of waiver or acquiescence, the appellants are the tenant in the suit property and they had also paid the rent in this regard to Lala Kishan Lal and the proof thereof is on record. It is submitted that even in Rashid Kiraya Jameen Milkiyat Krishan Lal & Sons (Ex. PW­1/DC­9), the word used was rent and as such the relationship between plaintiff & defendant therein was that of landlord & tenant and accordingly the jurisdiction was vested in the Rent Controller to try the dispute between the plaintiff and defendant therein. It is further submitted that the Ld. Trial Court failed to consider the fact that the valuation of the suit property was done on the basis of letting value which implies that the appellants were tenants in the suit property and therefore the suit for possession was not maintainable as the appellants were not the unauthorized occupants and as such the jurisdiction of the Trial Court was also barred by Section 50 of the DRC Act. It is submitted that the Ld. Trial Court failed to consider the fact that Lala Kishan Lal admitted throughout that the defendants therein were tenants settled by him in the suit property and were not unauthorized occupants and the same was also upheld by the Hon'ble Allahabad RCA No. 10/14/10 14/41 15 High Court on 28.8.1975. It is further submitted that the Ld. Trial Court failed to appreciate the fact that no notice u/s 106 of the Transfer of Property Act was given to the defendant/appellant by the plaintiff/respondent before filing of the present suit before the Ld. Trial Court. It is submitted that the Ld. Trial Court failed to consider the fact that no notice was given to the defendant/appellant u/s­ 27 of General Clauses Act before filing the suit. It is further submitted that the Ld. Trial Court failed to consider the fact that the plaintiff therein used the word agricultural land in his plaint, however no notice under Delhi Land Reforms Act was given to the defendant therein before filing the suit. It is also submitted that the suit filed by the plaintiff before the Ld. Trial Court was barred by the provisions of Delhi Land Reforms Act .

It has been further submitted on behalf of the appellants that the Ld. Trial Court failed to consider the fact that no title document was filed by the plaintiff in the present case to show his right, title or interest over the suit property and as such the ownership of the suit property qua plaintiff therein has not been proved in accordance with law before the Ld. Trial Court. It is further submitted that partition decree or mutation record filed on behalf of the plaintiff/respondent are not the proof of ownership and they do not confer any right or title qua the suit property , upon the plaintiff/respondent . It is further submitted that the Ld. Trial Court failed to appreciate that the judgment/order and formal partition decree passed in the partition suit bearing no.74/1947 and RFA No. 93­D/1960 were not passed on merits and as such the plaintiff before the Ld. Trial Court could never claim the ownership of the suit property on the basis of the suit decree as the same RCA No. 10/14/10 15/41 16 was not passed on merits. It is further submitted that the Ld. Trial Court failed to appreciate that the suit property falls within the jurisdiction of the Slum Area and no permission was taken from the Competent Authority by the plaintiff/respondent, before initiating eviction proceedings against the defendant before the Ld. Trial Court. It is also submitted that the Ld. Trial Court failed to consider the fact that the exclusive right of the plaintiff/respondent qua the suit property was not proved on record and therefore the suit filed on behalf of the plaintiff before the Ld. Trial Court was not maintainable.

It has further been submitted on behalf of the appellant that the Ld. Trial Court failed to appreciate the fact that the LRs of Lala Krishan Lal were not impleaded in the array of parties and as such the suit filed on behalf of the plaintiff /respondent before the Ld. Trial Court was also bad for non­ joinder of the parties. It is submitted that the said Lala Kishan Lal or his legal heirs were necessary parties without whom no order could have been made effectively and their presence was also necessary for a complete and final adjudication of the question involved in this case. It is further submitted that the plaintiff/respondent have not intentionally impleaded Lala Kishan Lal or his LRs as parties to the suit as the said Lala Kishan Lal has consented to the raising structures on the plot and was having full knowledge of the complete facts, which would have demolished the case of the plaintiff/respondent. It is submitted that the Ld. Trial Court failed to consider the fact that the market value of the suit property exceeded more than Rs.25,000/­ at the time of filing the suit and hence subject matter was beyond the pecuniary jurisdiction of the Ld. Trial Court. It is further submitted that the RCA No. 10/14/10 16/41 17 Ld. Trial Court failed to appreciate that the partition suit was not proved as no certified copy of relevant documents was filed and even the concerned file was not summoned from the court at Agra and in the absence of the same, the shares of the parties to that suit were not defined. It is submitted that even the alleged partition suit was not decided on merits and the alleged compromise between the parties therein was collusive. It is further submitted that the suit filed on behalf of the plaintiff before the Ld. Trial Court was pre­mature as the partition with the other LRs of Late Sh. Gauri Shankar Gupta and the present plaintiff was decided in 1986 and the suit was filed before the Ld. Trial Court in the year 1970 without any right, title or interest over the suit property.

It has been submitted on behalf of the appellants that the Ld. Trial Court failed to appreciate that the defendant was having exclusive right upon the superstructure, which was built/existed prior to 01.01.1958 and the plaintiff therein had no locus­ standi to file the present suit against the defendant before the Ld. Trial Court specially in view of the fact that there was no cause of action in favour of the plaintiff and against the defendant therein, at any point of time. It is further submitted that the Ld. Trial Court failed to consider the fact that the site plan was not proved in accordance with the site and the same does not describe the suit property completely. It is submitted that the Ld. Trial Court failed to appreciate the fact that the possession of the appellant was not unauthorized in the suit property and the tenancy of the defendant therein has been upheld by the Hon'ble High Court of Allahabad. It is further submitted that the Ld. Trial Court failed to consider that the damages were neither claimed nor proved by the plaintiff therein and whatever RCA No. 10/14/10 17/41 18 damages were allowed by the Ld. Trial Court were without jurisdiction, imaginary and fanciful. It is further submitted that the Ld. Trial Court has erred in holding that since the appellant/defendants have failed to pay or deposit the arrears of rent during the pendency of the litigation, they are presumed to have forfeited their right of tenancy and conversely it could also have been interpreted that the landlords have forfeited their ownership/ landlordship rights as they failed to claim or collect arrears of rent prior to 1.1.1958 and also thereafter and onwards till 30.4.1970 i.e the date of filing of the suit and Ld. Trial Court failed to consider and hold that the appellants herein, in view of the aforesaid circumstances, had become owners being in adverse possession for a period of more than 12 years. It is further submitted that the Ld. Trial Court failed to appreciate the fact that the plaintiff therein had failed to discharge the onus to prove the issues framed by the Ld. Trial Court. It is submitted that the Ld. Trial Court failed to consider the fact that the suit filed on behalf of the plaintiff therein was not maintainable and same was hit by Section 11 CPC, Section 47 CPC and order II rule 2 CPC. It is further submitted that the Ld. Trial Court failed to appreciate the fact that the appellant/defendant therein were not bound by the consent decree as they never got opportunity to assail the same.

It has been further submitted on behalf of the appellant that impugned judgment/decree passed by the Ld. Trial Court was based upon conjecture & surmises and was in contradiction to the fact, law documents and evidence on record. It has also been submitted that the impugned judgment/decree passed by the Ld. Trial Court was based upon the presumption & assumptions and was not RCA No. 10/14/10 18/41 19 sustainable in law and it has been prayed that the impugned judgment/decree dated 09.11.2009 passed by the Ld. Trial Court may be set aside.

In support of his contentions , Ld. Counsel for the appellants have relied upon the case­law cited as AIR 1972 MP 157, 180 (2011) DLT 75, AIR 2003 Kar. 15, AIR 2013 Uttrakhand 42, AIR 1977 Orissa 42, 191 (2012) DLT 247, AIR 2008 P&H 27, 1999 (1) KLT 47, AIR 1937 PC 251, AIR 1970 Madras 396, AIR 2008 (NOC) 2229 (Bom.), 105 (2003) DLT 987, 187 (2012) DLT 73, (1998) 2SCC 510, 2004 VI AD ( Delhi) 388 & AIR 1995 Orissa 270.

9. On the other hand, it has been submitted on behalf of the respondents that there was no illegality or irregularity in the findings of the Ld. Trial Court in respect of the various issues except Issue no. 10. It is further submitted that the issue regarding deficiency in the court fee was raised at the initial/preliminary stage before the Ld. Trial Court as it was not found that the court fee has not been paid on the relief for mesne profits claimed pendente lite and thereafter Ld. Trial Court granted time for payment of deficient court fee and accordingly requisite court fee as required by the Ld. Trial Court was paid/filed as is evident from order sheets of the Ld. Trial Court. It is submitted that after completion of pleadings, the Ld. Trial Court framed the issues in the suit and at that time no issue on court fee was pressed/framed in as much as the court fee was paid or found by all including counsels for parties to be sufficient. It is further submitted that according to the appellant, the deficient court fee paid on the plaint is deficient by Rs.5 or Rs. 6 only and the said plea is fallacious as the requisite court fee has already been paid by RCA No. 10/14/10 19/41 20 the plaintiff/respondent and the question of court fee is no more in issue. It is also submitted that in any case, the plaintiff/respondents are even otherwise, willing to pay the deficiency, if any in the court fee in accordance with the provisions of 12 of the Court Fee Act. It is submitted that the plea on the suit is barred by Section 50 of Delhi Rent Control Act as also by Section 19 of the Slum Areas (Improvement & Clearance) Act 1956 is no more res integra. It is submitted that according to the plaintiff, what was demised to them was merely an 'open plot of land' on which they had raised superstructure out of their own resources and funds without municipal sanction and consequently, the Delhi Rent Control Act would not be attracted to the tenancy in as much as there was no 'premises' that were allegedly demised by Lala Kishan lal to the predecessors­in­interests of the appellants herein.
