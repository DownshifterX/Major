   JUDGMENT

1. This is a suit for possession, recovery of money and for recovery of damages for the use and occupation and permanent injunction and mandatory injunction.

2. It is claimed that the husband of the plaintiff No.2, late Sh. V.N. Sarna, the sole-proprietor of the plaintiff No.1, was the lessee of the premises bearing No. 59, Okhla Industrial Estate, Phase-III, New Delhi (hereinafter referred to as the "suit property"). Further that Sh. K.C. Bajaj, Sole proprietor of the defendant entered into an agreement on 1st October, 1975 with late Sh.V.N. Sarna, vide which plaintiff No.1 was to provide the space along with other amenities like power, electricity and water at the cost of defendant No.1 and that plaintiff No.1 was also to provide the technical skill, guidance, assistance at the suit property. It is pleaded in the plaint that the defendant No.1 herein were to give the commission equivalent to 10% of the value of their manufactured goods subject to minimum of Rs.1,200/- per month but inadvertently it was typed as "maximum of Rs.1200/-" It is also pleaded in the plaint that the defendant No.1 is in default of the payment of commission as agreed upon in terms of the agreement dated 1st of October, 1975 and it was stipulated that the said agreement could be revoked in the event of defendant No.1 would not be able to fulfill the conditions of the payment of commission, as envisaged in clause 8 of the said agreement.

3. It is also stated in the plaint that Sh. V.N. Sarna died on 22.03.1986 leaving behind Smt. Vinay Sarna wife, Sh. Vipan Sarna Son and Vibha Jaggi, Daughter, as his legal heirs. It is also pleaded in the plaint that late Sh. V.N. Sarna left behind a Will, thereby bequeathing all his rights, title and interest in plaintiff No.1 to Smt. Vinay Sarna, the plaintiff No.2 and thus she became the owner/proprietor of plaintiff No.1. It is also pleaded in the plaint that the defendants have now denied the access to the plaintiff in the aforesaid premises, whereby also denying the plaintiffs the information regarding the quantum of the goods manufactured in the premises which forms the basis of the commission in pursuance of the agreement dated 01.10.1975.

4. It is further pleaded in the plaint that the defendant No.1 has allowed the defendants No.2,6,7,8, and 9 to use the premises for their own use and benefit without any consent in writing or otherwise of the plaintiffs. It is also pleaded in the plaint that it has come to the knowledge of the plaintiffs that the defendants are causing an illegal construction in the premises bearing No. 9 Okhla Industrial Estate, Phase-III, New Delhi and also on 19.06.1969, the defendants have broken the wall in the factory premises and have also prevented the plaintiff and her agents/employees from entering into the office. It is also pleaded that the defendants are wanting to install generator for the running of the machinery and equipments and that it will cause a noise and air pollution and shall disturb the functioning of the plaintiffs. It is also claimed that another occupant of the premises M/s Industrial Packers have also caused certain illegal construction in the suit property for which the department of Industries have given a notice to the plaintiffs to remove all such unauthorised construction, failing which the lease of the plaintiffs is sought to be cancelled. It is also claimed that, thereafter, vide letter dated 19.09.1995, the Office of the Commissioner of Industries, NCT have directed them to remove or get removed the illegal construction from the suit premises and, thereafter, also vide its letter, dated 19.09.1995, they have also demanded a sum of Rs.15,23,336.00 as charges for the use and occupation. Thereafter, plaintiff vide legal notice dated 03.04.1996 had called upon the defendants to remove the illegal construction caused in the premises and also to hand over the peaceful and physical vacant possession of the said area in premises bearing No. 59, Okhla Industrial Estate, Phase III, New Delhi.

5. Thus, the suit of the plaintiff praying for decree of possession, decree for recovery of Rs.17,03,336.00, decree of permanent injunction restraining the defendants, their employees etc. from causing any construction and from preventing the plaintiff or her agents from entering into the premises No.59, Okhla Industrial Estate, Phase III, New Delhi. Plaintiff also prayed for decree of mandatory injunction thereby directing the defendants to remove the unauthorised construction caused by the defendants, at suit property.

6. To the contrary, defendants No. 1 to 5 have filed their written statement while taking preliminary objections as to the fact that suit framed is misconceived and the plaintiff has suppressed material facts; that the jurisdiction of this Court is barred by Section 50 of the Delhi Rent Control Act; that the suit is bad for misjoinder of parties; that there is no cause of action against the defendants.

7. On merits, it is pleaded that the suit premises was taken on lease at a monthly rent of Rs.1200/- by Sh. K.C. Bajaj, Proprietor of the defendant from late Sh. V.N Sarna, vide agreement dated 01.10.1975. It is also claimed that, immediately, after execution of the said agreement, Sh. V.S. Sarna, also issued a rent receipt dated 02.10.1975, which fact has been concealed from this Hon'ble Court. Defendants have denied that any commission was agreed to be given to the plaintiffs.

8. Thereafter, plaintiffs filed replication, in which they have reiterated the facts of the plaint and denied the averments in the written statement.

9. From the pleading of parties, following eleven issues were framed on 15.04.2005:-

"1). Whether the agreement dated 01.10.75 is a lease agreement?

OPD.

2. Whether defendant No.1 has breached the conditions of that agreement dated 01.10.75?

3. Whether the jurisdiction of this Court is barred under Section 50 of Delhi Rent Control Act? OPD.

4. Whether the plaintiff No.2 has become the owner/proprietor of plaintiff No.1 by virtue of Will executed by Sh. V.N. Sarna? OPP.

5. Whether the suit is bad for mis-joinder of defendant No.6 to 9? OPD.

6. Whether defendant No.2,6,7,8,9 are occupying and using the suit premises without consent of the plaintiff, if so, its effect? OPP.

7. Whether the defendants are liable to pay a sum of Rs.17,03,336/-

payable to the office of Commissioner of Industries? OPP.

8. Whether the defendants are liable to pay a sum of Rs.1,80,000/- as damages for using and occupation of suit premises as claimed in the plaint? OPP.

9. Whether the plaintiff is entitled to pendente lite and future mesne profits/damages for use and occupation of premises, if so, at what rate? OPP.

10. Whether the plaintiff is entitled to permanent injunction as prayed in clause C and E of the prayer clause in the plaint? OPP.

11.Whether the plaintiff is entitled to mandatory injunction? OPP.

12. Relief".

10. In support of its case, the plaintiff examined Sh. Vipan Sarna, son of late Sh V.N.Sarna as PW-1. Besides reiterating the averments of the plaint, PW1 proved documents Ex.PW-1/1 to 1/28. This witness proved on record partnership deed, dated 15.10.1998, as Ex.PW-1/1; dissolution deed, as Ex.PW-1/2; site plan as Ex.PW-1/3; the letters dated 19.09.1995 & 29.01.1996 issued from the Office of Commissioner of Industries as Ex.PW-1/4 & 5; letter of allotment dated 22.04.1961 as Ex.PW-1/6; letter dated 09.11.1963 for execution of the lease deed as Ex.PW-1/7; copy of legal notice as Ex.PW-1/8; its postal receipts as Ex.PW-1/9 to PW-1/17; speed post receipts as Ex.PW-1/18 to 26 and the U.P.C. receipt as Ex.PW-1/27.

11. This witness was cross-examined at length by ld. counsel for the defendant. During cross-examination, he deposed that he did not sign the partnership deed in the presence of the Notary Public and that the said deed of partnership was not the registered one, but this witness has deposed that the firm was Income Tax Assessee and had Sales Tax Number, which was issued afresh in the year 1998. This witness has further deposed that the suit property was alloted by Directorate of Industries in the name of M/s P&S (India) Corporation.

12. In its defence, the defendants have also examined one witness, namely, Sh.K.C. Bajaj, as DW-1. This witness has deposed on the lines and averments of written statement and also exhibited certain documents viz. the agreement dated 01.10.1975 as Ex.DW-1/1; the letter dated 02.10.1975 as Ex.DW-1/2; vouchers for rent for different dates as Ex.DW-1/3 A to Ex.DW-1/3 I; letter dated 02,.01.1976 as Ex.DW-1/4; undertaking dated 24.08.1994 given by Sh. Vipan Sarna as Ex.DW-1/6 and undertaking dated 25.10.1993 given by Sh. Vipan Sarna as Ex.PW-1/D2; contempt petition as Ex.DW-1/8; order dated 20.05.2009 of Hon'ble High Court as Ex.DW-1/9; report of Local Commissioner as Ex.DW-1/10 and reply to notice as Ex.DW-1/11.

13. During cross-examination, this witness has, vehemently, deposed that he was the sole proprietor of M/s Premier Tools, at the time, when Ex.DW-1/2 was executed and thereafter, it was a converted to a partnership firm. This witness had denied the suggestions of ld. counsel for the plaintiff to the effect that when Mr. V.N. Sarna was alive, he used to visit suit property or that he used to supervise the business or that he used to inspect the books of accounts or that he used to receive the commission. This witness further denied the suggestion to the effect that he had an agreement of commission with the plaintiff or that the property was never let out by the plaintiff to him. This witness deposed that he was not aware that the plaintiff received any notices from the Commissioner of Industries or that any penalty was imposed on the plaintiff on account of misuse or sub-letting.

14. I have heard Ld. Counsels for the parties and have gone through the records carefully.

15. My issue wise findings are as follows :-

16. Issue No.1.

"i). Whether the agreement dated 01.10.75 is a lease agreement?

OPD.

17. The onus to prove this issue was on the defendant.

18. A lease agreement is a contract between a lessor and lessee that allows the lessee rights to the use of a property owned or managed by the lessor for a period of time. The agreement does not provide ownership rights to the lessee; however, the lessor may grant certain allowances to modify, change or otherwise adapt the property to suit the needs of the lessee. During the lease period, the lessee is responsible for the proper condition of the property.

19. There is clearly a dispute between the parties, regarding the nature of document, dated 01.10.1975, whether it is a licence or lease. Section 52 of Easements Act, 1882 defines "licence" whereas Section 105 of the Transfer of Property act defines a "lease". A lease is transfer of right to enjoy premises, whereas a licence is a privilege to do something on other's premises which otherwise would be unlawful. In the case titled as Board of Revenue etc. Vs. A.M. Ansari, AIR 1976 SC 1813, it has been held that:

"It is the creation of an interest in immovable property or a right to possess it that distinguishes a lease from a licence. A licence does not create an interest in the property to which it relates while a lease does. There is, in other words, transfer of a right in lease. As to whether a particular transaction creates a lease or a licence is always a question of intention of the parties which is to be inferred from the circumstances of each case. For the purpose of deciding whether a particular grant amounts to a lease or a licence, it is essential, therefore, to look to the substance and essence of the agreement and not to its form"

The following guiding principles have been reiterated:-

(1) To ascertain whether a document creates a licence or lease, the substance of the document must be preferred to the form; (2) the real test is the intention of the parties, whether they intended to create lease or a licence; (3) if the document creates an interest in the property, it is a lease;

but, if it only permits another to make use of the property, of which the legal possession continues with the owner, it is a licence; and (4) if under the document a party gets exclusive possession of the property, 'prima facie' he is considered to be a tenant, but circumstances may be established which negative the intention to create a lease."

20. In the present agreement, deceased Sh. V.N. Sarna is described as "manufacturing agent" and the defendant is described as "Principal" and the said agreement is an agreement of agency. As per clause 2 of Ex.DW-1/1, it is provided that the manufacturing agents shall provide the necessary space i.e. the factory premises No.59, Okhla Industrial Area, New Delhi along with power, electricity and water provided therein, by clauses 1 and 5. As per Ex.DW-1/1, the principal shall provide at his own cost the machinery, its parts, other accessories, raw material and the labour required for manufacture of the principal's products, charges for water, electricity and power are also his liability. As per clause 3 and 4 of the said agreement, the manufacturing agent is to give technical skill, guidance, assistance and know how etc. and shall supervise the manufacturing of the products of the principals personally or through his representative but under the guidance, desire and directions of the Principal and as per Clause 8, the manufacturing agents shall get commission @10% of the value of goods manufactured but subject to maximum of Rs.1,200/- per month. In Clause 9 of the said agreement, it is provided that the principal shall pay Rs.12,500/- as security deposit which shall bear interest of Rs.100/- per month. Even there is no specific period of duration agreed in the agreement. However, the agreement is revocable in the event when the principal fails to pay the commission as envisaged in Clause 8.

21. Defendant has placed on record documents Ex.PW-3/A to 3/I, showing receiving of rent of Rs.1,200/- monthly. This agreement has been in operation for over 20 years and throughout the defendant has been paying a fixed amount of Rs.1,200/-per month and also taking credit of Rs.100/- on account of interest against security deposit of Rs.12,500/-. It is not the case of the plaintiff that deceased Sh. V.N. Sarna or the plaintiff had ever before claimed the inspection of the accounts of the defendants to find out what was the value of the total goods manufactured in the premises or that commission was ever demanded and paid as was provided in the agreement. DW-1, in his cross-examination has denied the suggestion of ld. Counsel for the plaintiff to the effect that, when Sh. V.N. Sarna was alive, he used to visit suit property or that he used to supervise the business or that he used to inspect the books of accounts or that he used to receive commission from them. No oral or documentary evidence to the contrary was proved on record by the plaintiff. Thus, it is not proved on record that any control and supervision had ever been exercised by them in the business at any time or they have knowledge and experience in that business for that purpose. It has come on record that the defendant had installed his own machinery and had been doing manufacturing business of his own without any sort of control by the plaintiff. It is clear from the record that the defendant is in possession of the premises and has been doing his business without any interference of the plaintiff and her husband and thus, it cannot be said that the defendant No.1 is a licensee and is not a lessee.

22. On perusal of Ex.DW-3/A to 3/I, some documents bears signatures of Sh.V.N. Sarna (the signatures were admitted by the plaintiff during admission/denial of documents), it is clear that he receives a sum of Rs. 1,200/- per month as rent. Vide Ex.DW-1/2, it is also clear that a rent of Rs. 1,200/- per month, was paid by the defendant to the plaintiff. This document is also admitted by the plaintiff, at the time of admission/denial of documents.

23. Thus, in the facts and circumstances of the case, it is proved on record that the said agreement was a lease agreement and the defendants were tenants of the plaintiff.

24. In view of aforesaid discussion, issue No.1 is decided in favour of the defendant and against the plaintiff.

25. Issue No.3.

"Whether the jurisdiction of this Court is barred under Section 50 of Delhi Rent Control Act? OPD.

26. The onus to prove this issue was on the defendant.

27. Sub-section (1) of Section 50 of Delhi Rent Control Act provides that no civil court shall entertain any suit or proceedings in so far as it relates to the fixation of standard rent in relation to any premises to which this Act applies or to eviction of any tenant there from or to any other matter which the controller is empowered by or under this Act to decide, and no injunction in respect of any action taken or to be taken by the Controller under this Act shall be granted by any civil court or other authority.

28. It is claimed by the defendants that the property was taken on rent of Rs.1,200/-. DW-1, Sh.K.C. Bajaj, had proved on record documents Ex.DW-1/2 & Ex.DW-1/3A to 3I. The document Ex.DW-1/2 is a letter dated 02.10.1975, wherein Mr.V.N. Sarna, being proprietor of M/s P & S (India) Corporation had written as follows:

"You are requested to deposit a sum of Rs.1100/-(Rupees eleven hundred only) in the State Bank of India, Okhla Industrial Estate, New Delhi-110020, every month with effect from 01.10.1975 towards our rent of factory shed No. 59 against our manufacturing commission which is due to us @ Rs.

1200/-less interest Rs.100/- (i.e. Rs.100/-)".

29. Vide Ex.DW-3/A to 3I, it has also proved on record that the payment of Rs. 1,200/- paid by the defendant to the plaintiff, was towards rent. It is not the case of the plaintiff that the documents Ex.DW-1/2 & 1/3A to 3I were the forged and fabricated documents. Even no documentary evidence was produced by the plaintiff to the contrary. Thus, in view of the aforesaid facts and circumstances, it is proved on record that there were landlord tenant relationship between the plaintiff and the defendant.

30. In view of the above discussion, I am of the considered view that the defendants have proved on record that they have been occupying the suit premises as a tenant at a monthly rent of Rs.1,200/- per month and said rent has not been enhanced. The premises in question, thus, falls within the jurisdiction of the Rent Control Tribunal as the rent of the suit premises is less than Rs. 3500/- per month. Thus, the defendant can be evicted only as per the provisions contained in the Delhi Rent Control Act. The defendant has, thus, proved on record that the present suit filed by the plaintiff is barred under Section 50 of Delhi Rent Control Act. The defendant has successfully discharged the onus of Issue No. 3, same is decided in favour of the defendant.

31. ISSUES NO.2, 4 to 11

2. Whether defendant No.1 has breached the conditions of that agreement dated 01.10.75?

4. Whether the plaintiff No.2 has become the owner/proprietor of plaintiff No.1 by virtue of Will executed by Sh. V.N. Sarna? OPP.

5. Whether the suit is bad for mis-joinder of defendant No.6 to 9? OPD.

6. Whether defendant No.2,6,7,8,9 are occupying and using the suit premises without consent of the plaintiff, if so, its effect? OPP.

7. Whether the defendants are liable to pay a sum of Rs.17,03,336/- payable to the office of Commissioner of Industries? OPP.

8. Whether the defendants are liable to pay a sum of Rs.1,80,000/- as damages for using and occupation of suit premises as claimed in the plaint? OPP.

9. Whether the plaintiff is entitled to pendente lite and future mesne profits/damages for use and occupation of premises, if so, at what rate? OPP.

10. Whether the plaintiff is entitled to permanent injunction as prayed in clause C and E of the prayer clause in the plaint? OPP.

11.Whether the plaintiff is entitled to mandatory injunction? OPP.

32. In view of my findings on Issue No.1 & 2, the tenancy of the defendant is still subsisting and has not been terminated. The jurisdiction of Civil Courts is barred under Section 50 of Delhi Rent Control Act.

33. Accordingly, in view of the above discussions, the jurisdiction of the Civil Courts is held to be barred. It may be mentioned here that, in view of the fact that jurisdiction of Civil Court is barred under Section 50 of the DRC Act, I do not deem it proper and fit to give any specific findings on issues No. 2 and 4 to 11, as that may adversely effect the rights of the parties before Rent Control Tribunal. Thus, these issues are left open.

34. Relief.

Therefore, it is held that the suit is barred under section 50 of Delhi Rent Control Act, 1958. Hence, the suit is dismissed. No order as to cost. Decree sheet be prepared, accordingly.

File be consigned to Record Room.
