1. Vide this Judgment, I shall decide a suit for recovery of possession, rent use and occupation charges as well as permanent injunction filed by the plaintiff.

It is further submitted that late Shri Sohan Singh, the grand father of Suit No. 56/2014 Page No. 2 of 29 the plaintiff herein was the owner of various properties. It is further submitted that the defendant was in possession of the suit property as a tenant under the earlier owner; however, after the suit property fell into the share of late Shri Surat Singh, the defendant became tenant under Shri Surat Singh. It is further submitted that at the time when the suit property fell into the share of Shri Surat Singh, Shri Surat Singh was leading a retired life. Shri Surat Singh thought to run a business in the suit property, he may lead a reasonable life and hence Shri Surat Singh asked the defendant to vacate the suit premises. On this asking, the defendant alongwith two respectable persons of the locality namely Shri Mahesh Kumar and Dr. Parvinder Khanna, came to the residence of Shri Surat Singh and persuaded him to allow the defendant to continue to use and occupy the suit property. To this officer/ persuasions, late Shri Surat Singh desired that the rent of the suit property be fixed at higher rate so that he may survive with the help of the amount received as pension and also of rental income. 11. Issue No. 2 Whether this court has no jurisdiction to try the Suit No. 56/2014 Page No. 12 of 29 present suit? OPD Issue no 2 is taken up first since same is a legal issue. The contention of the defendant in this regard that the rate of rent is Rs. 70/­ per month and, therefore, Delhi Rent Controller, under the Delhi Rent Control Act, has jurisdiction. It is also stated that since the suit property lies in village Dhaka, Delhi and Revenue records are prepared in respect of the said property so as such the suit is triable by the Revenue Authority and this suit is barred U/s 185 of Delhi Land Reforms Act. On the other hand, it is denied by the plaintiff that this court lacks jurisdiction.

12.As regards, the plea that the present suit is barred under the provisions of Delhi Land Reforms Act , it is admitted case between the parties that the defendant is running a shop for carrying on the business of tailoring and therefore, obviously no agriculture activity is being carried out in the suit property. In view of the foregoing discussion clearly, the present case is not barred under the provisions of Section 185 of the Delhi Land Reforms Act, 1954.

13. As regards the contention of the defendant was a tenant in the suit property at a monthly rate of Rs. 70/­ per month, counter argument of the plaintiff to it is that the defendant was paying rent at Rs. 3,000 to the father of the plaintiff till the end of 1999 Suit No. 56/2014 Page No. 13 of 29 and thereafter the rent was enhanced in January 2000 to Rs. 3750/­ per month which the defendant paid till March, 2001 and thereafter has not been paying rent w.e.f 01.04.2001. The burden to prove that the defendant was a tenant at Rs. 70/­ per month was on the defendant. The defendant relied on EX. DW2/1 to DW2/29 which are the MCD reports viz­a­viz the property tax assessment with respect of the suit property wherein the defendant was shown as a tenant at Rs. 50/­ per month. On perusal of these reports, it is made out that these documents pertain to the year 1970-1971. Nothing has been brought on record by the defendant to show that lastly rent of Rs. 70 per month was paid to the plaintiff/ ancestors of the plaintiff. Merely, by depositing rent at Rs. 70/­ per month would not show and prove that the defendant was paying rent at Rs. 70/­per month. In view of the, foregoing discussion clearly the defendant has failed to discharge the burden of proving this issue. Therefore, this issue is decided in favor of the plaintiff and against the defendant.

14.Issue no. 3 Whether the suit is not properly valued for the purpose of court fee and jurisdiction? OPD The contention of the defendant in respect of the present issue is that the plaintiff has not properly valued the present suit for the Suit No. 56/2014 Page No. 14 of 29 purpose of court fees and jurisdiction. It is stated that the plaintiff should have assessed the present suit and affixed ad­ valorem court fees on the market value, which was more than Rs. 10 lakh, at the time of filing of present suit. However, neither the plaintiff has placed on record any document to show and prove that the suit property valued more than Rs. 10 lakh at the relevant time nor has otherwise proved the same. As per the provisions under the suit valuation Act and the Court Fee Act a suit for possession and other reliefs filed by a landlord against the tenant is to be valued at the annual rent payable. As per the case of the plaintiff the annual rate of rent at the time of filing of the present suit was Rs. 3750/­ per month and the plaintiff has accordingly valued the present suit and affixed the court fees thereupon. The defendant has neither led any evidence to disprove the same nor has otherwise proved this issue and thus has failed to discharge the burden to prove this issue. Accordingly, this issue is decided in favor of the plaintiff and against the defendant.

24.At this juncture it is worthwhile to reproduce provisions under S. 106 of the Transfer of Property Act which is as under:

Section 106 of the Transfer of Property Act
106. Duration of certain leases in absence of written contract or local usage Suit No. 56/2014 Page No. 20 of 29 In the absence of a contract or local law or usage to the contrary, a lease of immovable property for agricultural or manufacturing purposes shall be deemed to be a lease from year to year, terminable, on the part of either lessor or lessee, by six months' notice expiring with the end of a year of the tenancy; and a lease of immovable property for any other purpose shall be deemed to be a lease from month to month, terminable, on the part of either lessor or lessee, by fifteen days' notice expiring with the end of a month of the tenancy.

Every notice under this section must be in writing, signed by or on behalf of the person giving it, and either be sent by post to the party who is intended to be bound by it or be tendered or delivered personally to such party, or to one of his family or servants at his residence, or (if such tender or delivery is not practicable) affixed to a conspicuous part of the property.

27.Issue no. 6 Whether the plaintiff is entitled for decree of Rs. 12,750/­ as rent fro the period 01­04­01 to 30­06­01 @ Rs. 3750/­ per month? OPP Issue No. 7 Whether the plaintiff is entitled for damages, if yest, at what rate and for what period? OPP The issue nos. 6 and 7 being inter­related are being taken up and decided together.

The case of the plaintiff is that the defendant lastly paid rent @ Rs. 3750/­ per month uptill March 2001 and is in arrears of rent wef 01/04/2001. It is the case of the defendant that the defendant has been paying rent upto date by depositing it in the court @ Rs. 70/­ per month. To prove his case the plaintiff examined PW2, 3 and 4 in this regard who have supported the case of the plaintiff. The contention of the defendant in this regard is that these witnesses are neighbours of the plaintiff and thus are not reliable. This contention is without any merits as these witnesses are not only neighbours of the plaintiff but also of the defendant. The other contention of the defendant that Suit No. 56/2014 Page No. 22 of 29 because the other plaintiff witnesses have witnessed the rent agreement of the property of PW3 would not mean that these witnesses are not reliable witnesses. Obviously, the known persons witness a document of a friend or relative and not of an unknown person. Further, these witnesses have proved that as per the oral agreement between Sh. Surat Singh and the defendant, the rent was enhanced from Rs. 2,000/­ to Rs. 3,000/­ and then to Rs. 3, 750/­. Merely because PW2 to 6 did not see the defendant paying these enhanced rents would not mean that they are unreliable or that the rent was not enhanced to Rs. 3,750/­ per month. Thus, clearly, the plaintiff has proved that the rate of rent was lastly enhanced to Rs. 3,750/­.

28.To rebut this the defendant has pleaded that the rate of rent was Rs. 70/­. The Defendant relied on EX. DW17, Ex. D/W­18 which are the MCD reports viz/a viz the property tax assessment with respect of the suit property bare in the defendant washroom as a tenant at Rs. 50 per month. On perusal is report it ;is made out that these documents pertain to the year 1970 - 1971. With the passage of time and rise of inflation obviously the rent had enhanced and between 1971 and 2001 i.e. a lapse of 30 years it cannot be presumed that the rent remained stagnent @ Rs. 70/­ per month especially in the light of the evidence of various Suit No. 56/2014 Page No. 23 of 29 plaintiff witnesses. Clearly, the defendant has failed to rebut the case of the plaintiff in this regard.

29.Furthermore, the plaintiff has prayed for the recovery of arrears of rent @ Rs. 3,750/­ per month for the period 01/04/2001 to 30/6/2004. Admittedly, the defendant has been depositing upto date rent in the court @ Rs. 70/­ per month. Thus, clearly, the defendant is liable to pay rent @ Rs. 3750/­ per month to the plaintiff.

30.Further, it is the case of the defendant that it has not been proved by the plaintiff that the defendant is in the arrears of rent since 01/04/2001. In this regard, it has been stated by the plaintiff witnesses that the defendant is in the arrears of rent and admittedly no rent receipt was issued to the defendant. However, none of the witnesses have proved that the defendant is in arrears of rent since 01/04/2001. Also, as per para no. 4 on merits of the written statement the defendant has been depositing rent since 1/9/2003. Neither the plaintiff has been able to prove that the defendant is in arrears of rent since 01/04/2001 nor the plaintiff has been able to disprove the case of the defendant that defendant has been depositing rent since 1/9/2003 @ Rs. 70/­ per month, meaning thereby that the defendant is in arrears of rent since 01/09/2003 since the Suit No. 56/2014 Page No. 24 of 29 defendant has been depositing rent @ Rs. 70/­ per month instead of Rs. 3,750/­ per month.

31.Also, it is the case of the defendant that SH. Surat Singh expired in August 2003 and prior thereto the plaintiff had no right to take rent from the defendant. This contention of the defendant is also without any merits as a tenant cannot escape from his duty of paying rent as agreed. The plaintiff is entitled to recover rent for the period when the defendant did not pay the rent not only in his personal capacity but also as the legal heir of Surat Singh. Whether the plaintiff will get the complete share in the arrears of rent from 01/04/2001 till August 2003 is not a concern of the defendant as same is a matter between the legal heirs of the deceased Sh. Surat Singh.

In view of the foregoing discussion, the plaintiff is entitled to a decree of recovery of arrears of rent for the period from 01/09/2003 till 30/6/2004 @ Rs. 3,750/­ per month.

32.The case of the plaintiff in this regard is that the defendant despite service of legal notice did not vacate the suit property and thus is liable to pay damages @ Rs. 7,500/­ per month. As discussed in issue no. 5 herein above since the legal notice dated 06.05.2004 EX. PW1/16 has admittedly been received by the defendant and considering the fact that admittedly there is Suit No. 56/2014 Page No. 25 of 29 no written rent agreement between the parties then as per Section 106 of the Transfer Property Act the tenancy between the parties was a month to month tenancy which the plaintiff terminated vide legal notice Ex. PW1/16 and accordingly, on the 16th day thereafter the plaintiff was liable to hand over the possession of the suit property to the plaintiff. The plaintiff witnesses PW3 to 6 have proved the current market rate of letting out of the premises in the vicinity of the suit property. Also, having regard to the size, location and extent of the suit property, Rs. 7,500/­ is also the fair rate of mesne profits. Even if the plaintiff had not produced any witness other than himself to support his claim, this can be inferred by taking judicial notice of the phenomenal rise in rents in Delhi. That the court is competent to take judicial notice of the rise in rental values follows from the decision of Vinod Khanna v. Bakshi Sachdev AIR 1996 Delhi 32, wherein the Hon'ble High Court of Delhi noted:

(21)The learned Counsel for the appellants also urged before us that the learned Trial Court was not justified in taking a judicial notice of the fact of increase of rents like the suit property and also in providing Rs.10,000.00 per month as fair amount towards damages mesne profits in favor of the plaintiffs. It is true that no substantial evidence has been led by the plaintiffs in respect of the increase of rent in the properties like that of the suit property. However, it is a well known fact that the amount of rent for various properties in and around Delhi has been rising staggeringly and we cannot see why such judicial notice could not be Suit No. 56/2014 Page No. 26 of 29 taken of the fact about such increase of rents in the premises in and around Delhi which is a city of growing importance being the capital of the country which is a matter of public history. At this stage we may appropriately refer to the Court taking judicial notice of the increase of price of land rapidly in the urban areas in connection with the land acquisition matters. Even the Apex Court has taken judicial notice of the fact of universal escalation of rent and even raised rent of disputed premises by taking such judicial in case of D.C. Oswal v. V.K. Subbiah AIR 1992 SC 184."

33. Similarly in the case of Rattan Arya etc. v. State of Tamil Nadu and another AIR 1986 SC 1444, the Apex Court laid down that the Court can take judicial notice of enormous manifold increase of rents throughout the country particularly in urban areas.

In my opinion, taking judicial notice of the escalation in rents in Delhi and evidence led by the plaintiff, the sum of Rs. 7, 500/­ per month does reflect the market rate of rent. Thus, the plaintiff is entitled to recovery of mesne profits/damages @ Rs. 7,500/­ per month from 1/7/2004 till the vacation of the suit property by the defendant and handing over of the peaceful possession of the suit property by the defendants to the plaintiff.

According to me, considering the current market rate the plaintiff is entitled to pendente­lite and future interest @ 9 % p.a. on the decreetal Suit No. 56/2014 Page No. 27 of 29 amount of arrears of rent for the period from 01/09/2003 till 30/6/2004 @ Rs. 3,750/­ per month and on the damages @ Rs. 7,500/­ per month from 1/7/2004 from filling of the suit till the actual realization on the decreetal amount.
