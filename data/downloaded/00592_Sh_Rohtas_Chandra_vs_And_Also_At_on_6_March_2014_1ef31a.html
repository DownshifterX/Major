Sh. Rohtas Chandra vs Sh. Anup Singh on 6 March, 2014

                     IN THE COURT OF MS. RACHNA TIWARI LAKHANPAL

                                                                   JUDGMENT

1. Vide this judgment, I shall dispose off the suit for ejectment and recovery of Rs. 24,330/­ filed by the plaintiff Sh. Rohtash Chandra. CS No.­ 385/10 Page..... 1/18

2. The necessary facts for disposal of the present suit as per the plaint are here as under;

The defendant was inducted as a tenant of the plaintiff in respect of 87 sq. yards of vacant land forming part of the property no. 2365, Khampur, opposite West Patel Nagar, New Delhi­08 at a monthly rate of Rs. 585/­ st excluding other charges. The month of tenancy used to be reckoned from 1 day of each English Calender month to the last of that month. The defendant was irregular in making the payment of rent and has failed to pay the rent w.e.f 01.09.2009.

As the tenancy of the defendant was in respect of vacant land and was not covered under the DRC Act, the plaintiff served a legal notice of termination of tenancy dated 03.05.2010 upon the defendant to vacate the disputed land after the expiry of 15 days of the receipt of the notice. The said notice was duly served upon the defendant on or before 06.05.2010 and as such the possession of the defendant over the land in dispute became unauthorized after 21.05.2010 and the defendant is liable to deliver the possession of the disputed land to the plaintiff.

3. In the prayer clause, the plaintiff has prayed for decree of ejectment and decree for recovery of Rs. 24,330/­. He has further prayed for decree for future damages w.e.f 03.06.2010 at the rate of Rs. 1600/­ per day till delivery of possession.

CS No.­ 385/10 Page..... 2/18

4. The suit was contested by the defendant and the written statement was filed by him and accordingly, the contents of the plaint were denied by him. It was inter­alia contended that the present suit was barred by law in view of the Section 50 Delhi Rent Control Act, 1958 as the defendant was lawful tenant in the suit property at monthly rate of Rs. 585/­ per month. It was further contended that the plaintiff has suppressed the material fact that at the time of giving the premises on rent to the defendant, there was a gate already installed besides a tin shed.

5. The plaintiff made a request to the defendant for getting the structure constructed to avoid any mishap. Hence, the defendant constructed the premises i.e a hall. Further, the plaintiff did not pay the expenses of the construction i.e Rs. 1 lac. The defendant had also paid house tax of the tenanted premises to the MCD.

6. The plaintiff had further concealed the fact that he had received a bank draft of Rs. 5265/­ prepared from Canara Bank in favour of the plaintiff.

7. It was further contended that the defendant was paying the rent regularly to the plaintiff but the plaintiff did not accept the same and did not get the cheque encashed deliberately and thereafter, issued a frivolous notice dated 03.05.2010 to the defendant. Reply to that notice was sent by the defendant to the plaintiff alongwith original bank draft of Rs. 5265/­. The defendant also sent the sum of Rs. 2340/­ through money order towards the CS No.­ 385/10 Page..... 3/18 rent for the period from June 2010 to September 2010 but the plaintiff did not accept the same.

8. It was further contended that the tenancy has also not been terminated by the legal notice. In fact, the ground of termination of tenancy is non payment of rent, whereas the rent was being paid by the defendant. Hence, the status of the defendant can be unauthorized.

9. Thereafter, replication was filed by the plaintiff. The contents of the written statement were denied. It was submitted that money order was refused by the plaintiff as the case was pending before the court. It was further submitted that only reply of the legal notice was refused but no bank draft was annexed with it and therefore, the defendant did not give the particulars of the bank draft also. It was further submitted that the super structure was received by the defendant and hence, it cannot be said that the status of premises as of vacant land is changed because of construction of super structure later on. The construction belongs to the defendant and the plaintiff is asking for the rent towards the land only.

10. Thereafter, following issues were framed:­

(i) Whether the plaintiff is entitled for decree for ejectment, as prayed for? OPP

(ii)Whether the plaintiff is entitled to recovery of the arrears of rent, as prayed for ? OPP CS No.­ 385/10 Page..... 4/18

(iii)Whether the plaintiff is entitled for future damages, if yes, at what rate and for what period? OPP

(iv)Whether the plaintiff is entitled to any interest, if yes, at what rate and for what period? OPP

(v)Whether the suit is barred by Section 50 of the Delhi Rent Control Act? OPD

(vi)Relief.

11. To prove his case, the plaintiff has examined himself as PW­1 and has relied upon Ex. P­1/1 to P­1/5 and C­1 to C­5.

12. The defendant has also led his evidence and in support of his defefence, he has examined himself as DW­1 and Sh. Mahender Kumar, Assistant Grade­I, Tata Power Delhi Distribution Ltd. as DW­2.

13. I have heard the parties and perused the records.

14. Now, my issue­wise findings are as here under:

As the issue no. 5 is related to the jurisdiction of this court, hence, I shall decide the issue no. 5 first. The issue no. 5 reads as whether the suit is barred by Section 50 of the Delhi Rent Control Act? OPD The onus to prove this issue was upon the defendant. The main defence of the defendant had been that this court has no jurisdiction to try the present case as the suit premises is covered under the Delhi Rent Control Act as the admitted rent is less than Rs. 3,500/­.
CS No.­ 385/10 Page..... 5/18

15. It was the defence of the defendant that the suit premises was a building. Hence,the protection of Section 50 of Delhi Rent Control Act be given to him and accordingly, the suit be dismissed as this court has no jurisdiction to try this case.

16. The case of the plaintiff had been that the suit premise was the vacant land and hence, the suit is triable by the Civil Court. Prior to decide this controversy, the law in this regard has been reproduced as here under;

The relevant portion of Section 50 of DRC Act 1950 reads as here under;

"Jurisdiction of civil courts barred in respect of certain matters:­
(i) Save as otherwise expressly provided in this Act, no civil court shall entertain any suit or proceedings in so far as it relates to the fixation of standard rent in relation to any premises to which this Act applies or to eviction of any tenant thereform or to any other matter which the Controller is empowered by or under this Act to decide and no injunction in respect of any action taken or to be taken by the Controller under this Act shall be granted by any civil court or other authority.
(ii) If, immediately before the commencement of this Act, there is any suit or proceeding pending in any civil court for the eviction of any tenant from any premises to which this Act applies and the construction of which has been completed after the 1st day of June 1951, but before the 9th day of June 1955, CS No.­ 385/10 Page..... 6/18 such suit or proceeding shall, on such commencement, abate.
(iii) If, in pursuance of any decree or order made by a court, any tenant has been evicted after 16th day of August 1958, from any premises to which this Act applies and the construction of which has been completed after 1st day of June 1951, but th before the 9 day of June 1955, then, notwithstanding anything contained in any other law, the Controller may, on an application made to him in this behalf by such evicted tenant within six months from the date of eviction, direct the landlord to put the tenant in possession if the premises or to pay him such compensation as the Controller thinks fit.
(iv) Nothing in sub­section (i) shall be construed as preventing a civil court from entertaining any suit or proceeding for the decision of any question of title to any premises to which this act applies or any question as to the person or persons who are entitled to receive the rent of such premises. "

17. Now, the Section 2(i) talks about the definition of 'premises', which reads as;

" 'premises' means any building or part of a building which is, or is intended to be, let separately for use as a residence or for commercial use or for any other purpose and includes;­
(i) the garden, grounds and outhouses, if any, appertaining to such building or part of the building;
(ii) any furniture supplied by the landlord for use in such building or part of the building; but does not include a room in a hotel or lodging house. "
CS No.­ 385/10 Page..... 7/18

18. In the written statement filed by the defendant in para no. 7 of preliminary objections, the defendant submitted that at that time when he took the premises on rent, there was a gate already installed besides a tin shed. The plaintiff made a request to the defendant for getting the structure constructed to avoid any mishap and hence, the defendant constructed the premises i.e hall.

19. The reply given by Ld. counsel for the defendant in response to the legal notice of the plaintiff is Ex. P­6. The reply to the legal notice was also admitted by the defendant. In Para­C of this legal notice, it was mentioned that the defendant had constructed the structure on the vacant land when it was given to the defendant and he spent huge amount on the construction made on the piece of land.

20. The defendant had filed his evidence by way of affidavit Ex. DW­1/A. In his affidavit, the defendant deposed that at the time of letting out the tenanted premises, the premises consisted of a boundary wall and a gate having a tin shed as roof and the same was given to him for doing commercial activities i.e manufacturing of aluminum doors and window. It was denied that vacant plot of land was given to him contrary to the stand taken by the defendant's counsel in reply to legal notice Ex. P­6.

21. In the cross­examination of the defendant, the defendant deposed that it was wrong to suggest that the vacant land was let out to him. He further CS No.­ 385/10 Page..... 8/18 deposed that he had raised construction, which now exist there and he voluntarily deposed that the tin shed was there before raising the construction.

22. The defendant further tried to prove his line of defence by summoning the witness from Tata Power Delhi Distribution Ltd. (T.P.D.D.L), who brought the summoned record of the electricity meters of the suit premises in the name of the defendant, which were installed in the year 1975. The complete record in respect of both the meters were exhibited as Ex. DW­ 2/1 and DW­2/2 (collectively). Record of payment of electricity bills was exhibited as Ex. DW­2/3. The defendant had relied upon these records of electrical department to show that in all the records of erstwhile DESU at the coloum of address "House 2365" or "H in plot no. 2365, Khampur" has been mentioned, which shows that there was a building at the premises. This record pertains to the year 1975 and thereafter.

23. Further, the defendant has admitted Ex. C­1 and C­2. These are rent receipts. The defendant has also filed certain rent receipts. However, the defendant has not relied on those receipts in his affidavit. But, as the defendant has himself filed these rent receipts, this court can take it into consideration in evidence against the defendant as admission of the defendant.

24. Now, as far as the documents of electrical department are concerned, I have perused the documents. In the documents in coloum of CS No.­ 385/10 Page..... 9/18 address, at some places "H.No. 2365" is mentioned, at some places "H.Plot no. 2365" is mentioned, at some places "House in Plot no. 2365" is mentioned and at some places "plot no." is mentioned.

25. In the cross­examination of DW­2, the witness deposed that 'H' means 'house number'. After perusal of all these documents of electrical department, I am of the view that no conclusive implication can be raised that a building was existing at the plot no. 2365 as somewhere "house in plot no. 2365" is mentioned and somewhere "H.Plot No. 2365" is mentioned or somewhere even "plot no." is mentioned. Relying upon those documents, it cannot be conclusively held that these meters were installed at the building in plot no. 2365. Even otherwise, the reference to house in these documents cannot be construed as a reference to 'premises' within the meaning of Rent Control Act. It is not incumbent for the electrical department to first have a survey whether the building is constructed or not and after survey to give finding on the existence of building and then, meter is installed in the premises. Further more, it is the case of the defendant himself, as per para no. 7 of preliminary objection in the written statement, wherein it was submitted that at the time of giving suit premises to him, there was only a gate besides a tin shed. His stand is further confirmed by his admission to his reply to the legal notice. In para no. 2 on merit of written statement, he admitted his reply to the legal notice. The reply to the legal notice is Ex. P­6. In Para­C of reply to CS No.­ 385/10 Page..... 10/18 notice to the preliminary objection, there was a clear cut admission on behalf of the defendant that the defendant has constructed the structure on the vacant land. Further, on 21.02.11, the defendant himself had filed photocopies of rent receipts of various dates and years ranging from the year 1983 to 2009. In all the rent receipts except the one rent receipt dated 23.10.83 against the coloum of 'type of accommodation', 'vacant land' has been mentioned. In the rent receipt dated 23.10.83 Ex. PW­1/D­1, the word 'commercial' has been mentioned. Apart from this, in all the rent receipts, there is mentioning of coloum of 'plot/house number' and in all the rent receipts, the word 'house' was cut off, meaning thereby; the rent receipts were issued with regard to the plot no. 2365. The photocopies of these rent receipts were filed by the defendant himself, which again go to show that rent was being paid with regard to the vacant land and it was a plot. The word mentioning of 'commercial' does not itself imply that the rented premises was the constructed building. The purpose of rented premises for commercial use is not in dispute.

26. The defendant has taken contradictory stand in the written statement. He categorically mentioned that at the time of letting out the premises, it was a plot with iron gate and a tin shed. In the reply to the legal notice, it was specifically admitted that it was a vacant land and the construction was raised later on. In his cross­examination, defendant again admitted that he had given instruction for giving reply to this legal notice and CS No.­ 385/10 Page..... 11/18 this reply to the legal notice has further been admitted by him in the written statement. Hence, contradictory stand taken by him at the time of filing of affidavit in evidence is surrounded by suspicion when the defendant deposed that the premises was consisted of a boundary wall and a gate having a tin shed as roof.

27. Further, in view of the rent receipts filed by the defendant himself, it is apparent that the rent receipts were being issued qua the plot no. 2365 not qua the house. These rent receipts are pertaining as old as year 1983 also. Had the defendant any grievance about the rented premises not being vacant plot of land, he could have objected to striking off the word 'house' on the rent receipts. However, till date he never objected to the rent receipts issued qua the plot no. 2365. From the rent receipts also, it is being proved that rented premises was a plot of land.

28. Even otherwise, in the evidence, the defendant deposed that the tenanted premises was consisting of boundary wall, a gate and tin shed, which again is not covered under the term 'premises' as provided under Delhi Rent Control Act. The existence of boundary wall is not decisive of matter as boundary wall may be erected to demarcate the boundaries or to support a shed for any other purpose.

29. It was further held by the Hon'ble High Court in 1982, Rajdhani Law Report (Note) 8 that "property let out to a tenant is a plot, then it does not CS No.­ 385/10 Page..... 12/18 become premises, if tenant raises construction even if it is with permission of landlord."

30. It is also well settled that temporary shed also does not come under the term of 'premises'. Ld. defendant's counsel had argued on the point that tin shed was permanent in nature. However, the defendant has failed to prove this aspect. Not only this, Ld. defendant's counsel himself put a question to PW­1 in the cross­examination, wherein he deposed that it was correct that a 'pakka' structure was built on the suit land by the defendant in the year 1980. In that case, it implies that there was a temporary structure having tin shed on the suit land prior to the year 1980.

31. In para­7 of preliminary objections of written statement, the defendant himself mentioned that the plaintiff made a request to him for getting the structure in solid state to avoid any mishap. Then, it again implies that the tin shed was temporary in nature. Hence, it is proved that the rented premises was the vacant plot of land to which bar of Section 50 Delhi Rent Control Act does not apply and this court is competent to try the present case.

32. Therefore, in view of the above discussion, this relief is decided in favour of the plaintiff and it is held that this court has jurisdiction to try the present suit.

33. Issue No. 1: Whether the plaintiff is entitled for decree for ejectment, as prayed for? OPP CS No.­ 385/10 Page..... 13/18 In view of the above discussion in issue no. 5, it is already held that the present suit is maintainable and the jurisdiction of this court is not barred. The plaintiff has prayed for decree of ejectment of suit premises in respect of 87 sq. yards of vacant land forming part of the property no. 2365, Khampur, opposite West Patel Nagar, New Delhi­08 as per the site plan Ex. P­

1. It is the case of the plaintiff that the month of tenancy used to be reckoned st from 1 day of each English Calender month to the last of that month. The defendant was irregular in making the payment of rent. The plaintiff got served a legal notice of termination of tenancy upon the defendant to vacate the land occupied by the defendant. The notice was duly served upon the defendant. The possession of the defendant over the land in dispute after the expiry of 15 days of the receipt of notice is trespasser and hence, the plaintiff is liable to recover the possession of the land in dispute.

34. The defense of the defendant had been that he was regularly making the payment of rent and the monthly rent was Rs. 585/­ per month.

35. Admittedly, there is no written rent agreement was executed between the parties. The defendant basically submitted that he was making the payment of rent regularly and after notice also, he sent the payment of rent and hence, this legal notice terminating the tenancy has no value because the main ground of the plaintiff for terminating the tenancy was non payment of monthly rent, which was denied by the defendant. However, service of legal CS No.­ 385/10 Page..... 14/18 notice was not in dispute.

36. I have perused the legal notice issued by Ld. counsel for the plaintiff. The relevant portion of this notice reads as here under:

"That you are very irregular in payment of rent and have failed to pay rent w.e.f 01.09.2009. You are called upon to pay the rent w.e.f 01.09.2009 at the rate of Rs. 585/­ per month.
That my client no longer wants to keep you as his tenant in respect of the aforesaid vacant land and you are called upon to handover the vacant land after removing the super structure raised by you on the said vacant land to my client within 15 days of the receipt of this notice failing which my client will take appropriate legal proceedings for possession against you in a court of law at your risk and costs which please note.
That in case of your failure to deliver the possession within 15 days of the receipt of this notice, you will be liable to pay damages/ mesne profit at the rate of Rs. 50,000/­ per month which the land in question can now reasonably fetch if let out and appropriate legal procedure for the recovery of the amount of mesne profit/damage shall be taken against you in a court of law as your risk and costs which please note. "

37. After perusal of contents of the said notice, it cannot, at all, be summed up that because of non­payment of rent, notice was issued and this was the main ground for termination of notice. Perusal of the contents of the notice show the intention of the plaintiff to terminate the tenancy. It is well CS No.­ 385/10 Page..... 15/18 settled that in such a like tenancy as in the present case, where no written agreement is executed, the tenancy is reckoned as month to month tenancy and it can be terminated by issuing legal notice; thereby giving 15 days time to the tenant after receipt of legal notice to vacate the premises. It does not require existence of any specific reason at all. Hence, in view of the legal notice dated 03.05.10 issued to the defendant, it is apparent that the plaintiff was not interested in continuation of tenancy. Even otherwise, filing of the case by the plaintiff against the defendant seeking vacation of the premises itself is a notice of termination of the tenancy to the defendant and hence, the plaintiff has been able to prove that the tenancy was terminated w.e.f. 21.05.10 and he is entitled for decree of ejectment for the suit property no. 2365, Khampur, opposite West Patel Nagar, New Delhi­08.

38. Issue no. 2 : Whether the plaintiff is entitled to recovery of the arrears of rent, as prayed for ? OPP In view of the findings in the issue no. 1, the plaintiff has proved that the defendant was the tenant at the rate of Rs. 585/­ per month. It was the case of the plaintiff that the defendant has not paid the rent from the period 01.09.2009 to 21.05.2010 amounting of Rs. 5130/­. It was the defence of the defendant that he issued cheques to the plaintiff but the same was not encashed by the plaintiff. Be that as it may, it is not in dispute that the rent for the abovesaid period was not paid. Hence, the plaintiff is entitled to recovery of CS No.­ 385/10 Page..... 16/18 Rs. 5130/­ for the period 01.09.2009 to 21.05.2010 without any interest.

The plaintiff has further prayed for rent @ 1600/­ from the 22.05.2010 to 02.06.2010 amounting to Rs. 24330/­. It is held that the plaintiff is entitled to rent @ 585/­ per month till this period and is not entitled to any interest.

37. Issue no. 3: Whether the plaintiff is entitled for future damages, if yes, at what rate and for what period? OPP In view of the findings in the abovesaid issues, it is proved that the defendant is still in possession of the property after termination of tenancy. The onus to prove this issue was upon the plaintiff. He has prayed for decree for the future damages w.e.f 03.06.2010 @ 1600/­ per day till delivery of possession. However, he has not adduced any proof to prove rent at this rate as damages and hence, I pass the decree of damages w.e.f 03.06.2010 till the date of passing this order @ 1000/­ per month and further @ 1200/­ per month till delivery of the possession from the date of passing of order.

38. Issue No. 4: Whether the plaintiff is entitled to any interest, if yes, at what rate and for what period? OPP Plaintiff has sought interest @ 15% on the rent due from 01.09.2009 to 21.05.2010. It was the case of the defendant that he issued the cheques, which were not encashed by the plaintiff towards the rent of the above­said period. After filing of the case, money order was issued by the CS No.­ 385/10 Page..... 17/18 defendant, which was admittedly refused by the plaintiff. Hence, in view of the above, the plaintiff is not entitled for the interest on this amount.

39. Issue No. 6 Relief:

In view of the above discussion, the suit of the plaintiff is decreed. The plaintiff is entitled for decree of ejectment qua the suit property no. 2365, Khampur, opposite West Patel Nagar, New Delhi­08, admeasuring 87 sq. yards as shown in red colour in the site plan. He is further entitled to recovery at the rate of 585/­ per month from the period 01.09.2009 to 02.06.2010. He is further entitled to recover rent at the rate of 1000/­ per month as damages from the period 03.06.10 till the date of passing of this order and rent at the rate of 1200/­ per month till the delivery of the possession from the date of passing this order. Rent paid, if any, during pendency of the case will be adjusted in the decreetal amount. Suit is decreed with cost. Decree sheet be prepared accordingly.
File be consigned to record room after necessary compliance.
